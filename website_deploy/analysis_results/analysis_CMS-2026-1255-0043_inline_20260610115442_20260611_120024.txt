## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports CMS efforts to reduce prior authorization barriers and focuses on DMEPOS access for people with disabilities. She recommends expedited review for replacement or modification of previously approved equipment, multi-year authorization for durable equipment tied to permanent disabilities, transparent denial explanations, and continued engagement with disability advocates, providers, and social workers.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** None.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: DMEPOS prior authorization burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Delays in wheelchairs, prosthetics, orthotics, and assistive technologies can reduce mobility, independence, employment, and health.
- **Specific request:** Add protections for individuals needing DMEPOS and long-term supports.

**Topic 2: Expedited review for previously approved equipment**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeated proof of medical necessity for substantially unchanged needs adds burden with limited program-integrity value.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited review for replacement or modification of previously approved DME when medical necessity remains substantially unchanged.

**Topic 3: Multi-year authorization periods**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Permanent disabilities often involve lifelong equipment needs that do not require repeated reassessment.
- **Specific request:** Permit multi-year authorization periods for certain DME categories used by individuals with permanent disabilities.

**Topic 4: Denial transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Clear denial reasons help beneficiaries and providers understand documentation requirements and pursue appeals.
- **Specific request:** Increase transparency in PA decisions and denial explanations.

**Topic 5: Stakeholder engagement**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Disability advocates, providers, and social workers have practical knowledge of PA impacts.
- **Specific request:** Continue engaging these stakeholders during implementation.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to the DMEPOS RFI by describing patient-care impacts, recommending expedited review and multi-year authorizations, supporting transparency, and asking CMS to engage disability stakeholders.
- **S16:** By analogy, the commenter supports clear denial explanations, though the rule's S16 is specific to drug PA denials.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish expedited review for replacement or modification of previously approved DME when need is unchanged.
2. Create multi-year authorization periods for DME used by individuals with permanent disabilities.
3. Require clear denial explanations and documentation requirements.
4. Engage disability advocates, healthcare providers, and social workers during implementation.
5. Incorporate added protections for individuals needing DME and long-term supports.

To Whom It May Concern:<br/>I respectfully submit the following comments regarding CMS-0062-P. I support CMS&#39;s efforts to improve prior authorization processes and reduce barriers to healthcare access. As a social work professional serving individuals with disabilities and chronic health conditions, I have observed firsthand the challenges that prior authorization requirements can create for vulnerable populations.<br/>I am specifically commenting on the section addressing prior authorization requirements for Durable Medical Equipment, Prosthetics, Orthotics, and Supplies (DMEPOS). CMS appropriately recognizes that prior authorization processes may delay access to medically necessary equipment and seeks public feedback on ways to reduce these burdens.<br/>Individuals with disabilities often depend on wheelchairs, prosthetic devices, orthotic equipment, and other assistive technologies to maintain independence, employment, community participation, and health. Delays in obtaining these items can have significant consequences. When prior authorization decisions are delayed, beneficiaries may experience decreased mobility, increased risk of injury, deterioration of health conditions, and reduced quality of life.<br/>I recommend that CMS establish an expedited review process for replacement or modification of previously approved durable medical equipment when medical necessity has already been documented and remains substantially unchanged. Many beneficiaries must repeatedly demonstrate medical necessity for equipment that has long been recognized as essential to daily functioning. This requirement creates administrative burdens for beneficiaries, providers, and payers while offering limited additional value in preventing inappropriate utilization.<br/>Additionally, I recommend that CMS consider multi-year authorization periods for certain categories of durable medical equipment used by individuals with permanent disabilities.
