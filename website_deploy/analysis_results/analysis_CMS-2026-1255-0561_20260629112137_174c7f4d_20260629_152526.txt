# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Community Pharmacists Association (NCPA); signed by Steve Postal, JD, Senior Director, Policy & Regulatory Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association; PBMs & Pharmacies; small business/community pharmacy representative.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents 18,900 independent community pharmacies, including many long-term care pharmacies; members employ 235,000 individuals and provide pharmacy and clinical services.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NCPA supports CMS's proposal to require NCPDP Formulary & Benefit v60, RTPB v13, and SCRIPT 2023011 for pharmacy-benefit drug prior authorization, but urges CMS not to mandate newer NCPDP versions in the final rule. NCPA asks CMS to extend the pharmacy-benefit compliance date to no earlier than 24 months after the final rule effective date, clarify the medical-vs-pharmacy benefit pathway, define actionable workflow responses and PA clock rules, and ensure pharmacists can participate in and initiate prior authorization workflows where appropriate.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; GENERAL/RFIs; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.205(b), 45 CFR 170.205(c), 45 CFR 170.205(u); references to CMS-0057-F and CMS-0056-F.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT Standard Version 2023011; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit Standard Version 60; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit Standard Version 13; FHIR-based APIs; Da Vinci implementation guides; payer-to-payer APIs; ADT/event notifications.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: NCPDP standards and version adoption**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** NCPA supports adoption of the proposed NCPDP standards but says the industry is already transitioning to the named versions for Part D and is not prepared for a subsequent version mandate.
- **Specific request:** Finalize no NCPDP versions newer than SCRIPT 2023011, F&B v60, and RTPB v13.

**Topic 2: Pharmacy benefit implementation timeline**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** For non-Part D Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP payers, NCPDP SCRIPT ePA is net-new functionality requiring integrations, trading partner onboarding, testing, training, and operational changes.
- **Specific request:** Extend the October 1, 2027 pharmacy-benefit compliance date to no earlier than 24 months after the final rule effective date.

**Topic 3: Medical vs pharmacy benefit pathway clarity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** CMS proposes two separate pathways, but pharmacists and other stakeholders need to know which pathway applies and receive actionable next steps to avoid misrouting, duplicate submissions, delays, and administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Clarify payer expectations for identifying the correct authorization pathway, producing actionable responses, and orchestrating workflows across FHIR and NCPDP processes.

**Topic 4: Real-time benefit information and workflow integration**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Plan-level formulary files and real-time benefit responses are not operationally interchangeable; real-time responses should reflect adjudication processes that produce the same patient cost-sharing or utilization management determination as pharmacy claims.
- **Specific request:** Clarify expectations for actionable, patient-specific benefit information in workflows and ensure modernization produces workflow outcomes rather than merely endpoints.

**Topic 5: PA timeframes and the operational clock**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Without clear rules for when a request is received, complete, stopped, corrected, or rerouted, public reporting may misstate operational performance and shift accountability to pharmacists who do not control documentation completeness.
- **Specific request:** Define when the PA clock starts and stops, how incomplete requests are handled, and how iterative workflows, appeals, and peer-to-peer reviews should be treated.

**Topic 6: Pharmacist role and patient participation**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacists may be downstream dispensers who depend on prescriber documentation, but also may be clinical service providers responsible for documentation and submission.
- **Specific request:** Recognize pharmacists' dual role, include pharmacists as participants in real-time benefit and PA workflows, and do not prevent pharmacists from initiating or submitting PA when they are enrolled providers and have the required information.

**Topic 7: RFIs on event notifications, cybersecurity, API testing, step therapy, and lab/DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** NCPA supports broader interoperability improvements but warns that small community pharmacies have different infrastructure than large systems and that step therapy and lab/DMEPOS workflows can create fragmentation.
- **Specific request:** Include pharmacists in event-notification policy, account for small pharmacy cybersecurity capacity, strengthen API usability/testing, support step-therapy history portability, and apply PA modernization lessons to lab/DMEPOS.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S2/S60-S65:** Supports the proposed standards versions and advises CMS not to name newer NCPDP versions because the industry is already transitioning to the proposed versions.
- **S14/S19/S22:** Supports NCPDP standards for non-Part D impacted payers but states implementation is net-new and costly for Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP payers.
- **S21/S25/S31:** Requests a longer timeline for pharmacy benefit requirements and emphasizes RTPB/F&B operational differences.
- **S15:** Requests clarity on routing between medical-benefit FHIR workflows and pharmacy-benefit NCPDP workflows.
- **S20/S43:** Requests specificity on when PA timing clocks start, stop, and pause for incomplete, corrected, or rerouted requests.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Encourages CMS to make API and PA workflow measures meaningful and not merely endpoint-availability measures.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on event notifications, resiliency/cybersecurity, API testing and certification, step therapy, and lab/DMEPOS, with emphasis on pharmacist-enabled coordination and small-pharmacy feasibility.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize NCPDP SCRIPT 2023011, F&B v60, and RTPB v13, but do not mandate newer versions in this final rule.
2. Extend the pharmacy-benefit drug PA compliance date to at least 24 months after the final rule effective date.
3. Clarify how payers identify and communicate the correct medical-benefit vs pharmacy-benefit pathway.
4. Define what constitutes an actionable PA or benefit response.
5. Clarify the PA clock, including receipt, completeness, additional-information requests, corrected submissions, and rerouting.
6. Recognize pharmacists' dual dispenser/provider roles in PA workflows.
7. Ensure real-time benefit information is patient-specific and consistent with pharmacy claims adjudication.
8. Support patient visibility and appropriate patient participation in PA workflows without adding administrative burden.
9. Allow pharmacists to initiate or submit PA when they are enrolled providers and have necessary information.
10. Use RFIs to incorporate pharmacists in ADT/event notifications, step therapy portability, and future lab/DMEPOS PA modernization.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Premature version mandates; insufficient implementation time; disconnected FHIR and NCPDP workflows; unclear PA clock rules; lack of pharmacist-specific workflow recognition.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Administrative burden, misrouted requests, duplicative submissions, delayed access to medications, inaccurate reporting, and downstream accountability placed on pharmacists.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** New integrations, trading partner onboarding, testing protocols, staff training, fragmented multi-vendor ecosystem, small pharmacy infrastructure limitations, and overlapping CMS/HHS transaction mandates.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** NCPA represents 18,900 independent community pharmacies; nearly half provide long-term care services; members employ 235,000 individuals.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Cites Federal Register rule publication and CMS electronic prior authorization pledge/use case materials.
- **Examples provided:** Part D standards transition dates; need to avoid implementing SCRIPT 2017071 when 2023011 becomes required; pharmacist roles as both downstream dispenser and provider of clinical services.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Community and LTC pharmacies, pharmacists, Medicaid/CHIP and QHP payers, providers, patients, PBMs, vendors, and care teams.
- **Economic implications:** New version or rushed implementation mandates would impose administrative burden and cost on pharmacies and other stakeholders.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS preserves proposed versions, extends timeline, and clarifies interoperable workflow routing and actionable responses.
- **Patient care impact:** Properly implemented real-time benefit and ePA workflows could reduce delays and improve medication access; poor routing or non-actionable responses could worsen delays.
