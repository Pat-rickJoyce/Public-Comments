- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Patient advocate and healthcare provider; oncology nurse credentials; LBBC is a national breast cancer nonprofit reaching around 2 million individuals annually.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to protect oncology patients from prior authorization delays and denials for medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing. She recommends allowing retroactive authorization after specimen collection, prohibiting denials based solely on authorization timing, avoiding repeat blood draws when valid specimens exist, requiring expedited oncology lab/biomarker review, and protecting patients from surprise administrative costs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to expedited review; PATIENT-ACCESS.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Section III.E.
- **Standards referenced:** None.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Oncology laboratory and biomarker PA timing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker and specialty lab results guide cancer treatment selection, treatment changes, monitoring, and clinical trial eligibility; rigid pre-collection timing rules can delay care.
- **Specific request:** Allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected.

**Topic 2: Denials based on authorization timing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose/Modify
- **Rationale:** Denying otherwise valid testing based solely on timing can require repeat blood draws and delay urgent care.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing and prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.

**Topic 3: Expedited oncology review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cancer care is time-sensitive and lab results may support life-saving decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.

**Topic 4: Patient cost protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not bear costs caused by administrative delays.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative.

To Whom It May Concern: 
My name is Caroline Koffke, and I am writing as a patient advocate and healthcare provider to 
urgently request that CMS protect patients from critical care delays and denials caused by rigid 
prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing. 
Living Beyond Breast Cancer (LBBC) is a national breast cancer non-profit that reaches around 
2 million individuals each year. Many of our constituents are on either Medicare and Medicaid 
and depend on frequent bloodwork for the short- and long-term management of their breast 
cancer.  
We commend CMS for its ongoing efforts to modernize and streamline prior authorization 
processes. We also deeply appreciate the opportunity to provide specific feedback via the 
Request for Information regarding the impact of prior authorization on timely patient access to 
medically necessary laboratory testing, as referenced in Section III.E of CMS-0062-P. 
I urge CMS to protect oncology patients from delays and denials caused by prior authorization 
requirements for medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing. 
In cancer care, timing is critical. Biomarker and specialty lab results often guide treatment 
selection, treatment changes, disease monitoring, and clinical trial eligibility. When prior 
authorization rules require approval before or on the exact specimen collection date, valid 
specimens may be delayed, rejected, or discardedâ€”even when the patient, physician, and 
laboratory acted in good faith. This can force repeat blood draws and delay urgent cancer care. 
CMS should require health plans to: 
1. Allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected. 
2. Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing. 
3. Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available. 
4. Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing. 
5. Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative. ...
