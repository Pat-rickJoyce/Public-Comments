# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Movement Disorders Policy Coalition (MDPC), with multiple co-signing patient and advocacy organizations.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Coalition representing stakeholders, including health care providers and patients, focused on movement disorders such as Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, tardive dyskinesia, Tourette Syndrome, dystonia, ataxia, and Huntington's disease.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MDPC supports CMS' effort to modernize prior authorization and step therapy protocols, especially electronic prior authorization for drugs covered under the medical benefit. The commenter asks CMS to ensure the framework is patient-centered, supports expedited review, protects continuity of therapy, accommodates clinically justified off-label use, and includes stronger Medicare Advantage step therapy guardrails.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY/GLOBAL prior authorization reporting; GENERAL RFI on step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited beyond the rule title and CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR, NCPDP, HL7, CRD, DTR, or PAS implementation guides named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for medical-benefit movement disorder drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Many high-cost movement disorder treatments are administered in clinical settings and billed under the medical benefit. Current manual PA processes rely on calls, faxes, paper forms, appeals, and peer-to-peer discussions, delaying access to therapy and increasing provider burden.
- **Specific request:** Finalize an electronic PA framework that reduces administrative burden and helps treatment begin quickly once a clinical decision has been made.

**Topic 2: Expedited pathways and decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Movement disorder patients may experience severe and swift consequences from treatment delays, including worsened symptoms and reduced independence.
- **Specific request:** Create expedited review pathways that explicitly apply to medical-benefit drugs and support immediate or same-day decisions where clinically necessary.

**Topic 3: Continuity of established therapy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Reauthorization burdens during transitions between health insurance plans or coverage periods can disrupt effective ongoing therapy.
- **Specific request:** Ensure patients already established on therapy do not face disruptions or duplicative reauthorization burdens during coverage transitions.

**Topic 4: Clinically justified off-label use**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Off-label drug use is common in movement disorders, particularly rare movement disorders with fewer on-label options.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that ePA standards and clinical documentation workflows can capture and accommodate appropriate off-label use.

**Topic 5: Full care-team access and coordination**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Many movement disorder patients have physical or cognitive limitations and depend on caregivers and multiple providers to coordinate access to care.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that the PA framework extends to the full care team.

**Topic 6: Step therapy guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can force patients to fail insurer-preferred drugs before accessing clinician-prescribed therapy, delaying appropriate care and increasing burden.
- **Specific request:** Require plain-language public step therapy protocols during open enrollment, timely exception and appeal pathways including emergency review, and data sharing that recognizes prior treatment failures across plans.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Responds indirectly by describing manual medical-benefit drug PA workflows as burdensome and emphasizing the need for ePA to work for clinically administered movement disorder therapies.
- **S20:** Supports timely decision-making and requests expedited or same-day pathways for medical-benefit drugs where clinical circumstances demand it.
- **S21:** Supports the direction of the proposed timeframes but asks CMS to design implementation around urgent clinical circumstances for movement disorder patients.
- **S29/S35:** Supports public reporting requirements as a transparency and accountability mechanism, though the comment does not provide detailed metric specifications.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the step therapy RFI by recommending public disclosure of step therapy protocols, timely review and emergency pathways, and use of data sharing to recognize prior treatment failures.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for drugs covered under the medical benefit.
2. Create expedited review pathways for medical-benefit drugs, including immediate or same-day decision-making when clinically necessary.
3. Prevent therapy disruptions and unnecessary reauthorization when patients transition plans or coverage periods.
4. Ensure ePA documentation supports clinically justified off-label drug use.
5. Clarify that the PA framework supports caregivers and the full care team.
6. Require Medicare Advantage plans to publicly disclose step therapy protocols in plain language during open enrollment.
7. Require timely step therapy review, accessible appeals, and emergency review pathways.
8. Use data sharing to recognize prior treatment failures from previous plans so patients do not have to fail the same therapies repeatedly.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Treatment delays, care disruption, opaque step therapy protocols, and manual PA burden.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Worsened symptoms, reduced quality of life, loss of independence, delayed medication access, and increased burden on patients and clinicians.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Manual PA workflows, lack of transparency, plan transitions that trigger reauthorization, and insufficient recognition of prior treatment failures.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Patients with movement disorders often face waits of several months to see a neurologist; 98% of physicians in an Alliance for Patient Access survey identify step therapy as a significant barrier.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Lin CC et al., Neurology 2025 on neurologist wait times; Alliance for Patient Access Physician Burnout & Utilization Management Survey.
- **Examples provided:** Movement disorder treatments administered in clinical settings; off-label use in rare movement disorders; patients requiring caregivers due to cognitive or physical limitations.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Movement disorder patients, caregivers, neurologists, clinician offices, Medicare Advantage beneficiaries, plans, and broader care teams.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced manual PA work could save clinician time and reduce administrative burden; the comment does not quantify cost impacts.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter supports digitization but emphasizes that technology is a means to faster, more transparent patient access, not the end goal.
- **Patient care impact:** High; delays can lead to severe symptom worsening, reduced quality of life, and loss of functional independence.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive
- **Tone:** Professional, patient-centered, collaborative
- **Priority level:** High
