# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Chronic Care Policy Alliance (CCPA); signed by Elizabeth Helms, CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Network of state and regional advocacy organizations advancing policy for people living with chronic conditions and diseases.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CCPA supports CMS's effort to modernize interoperability and prior authorization, especially electronic PA and faster decision-making. It recommends stronger transparency, clinically expert review, plan-level public reporting, portability of step therapy and PA history across plans, protections for stable patients, and robust exceptions and appeals.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI step therapy; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED indirectly through interoperability tools
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB) standard; electronic prior authorization; interoperability tools; no specific FHIR IG cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Improving information sharing**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Efficient and accurate information exchange improves care coordination, reduces duplication and administrative burden, and supports decisions based on complete information.
- **Specific request:** Finalize reforms in a way that improves data sharing and removes barriers that delay care.

**Topic 2: Electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Manual PA creates major administrative burden and delays treatment for days, weeks, or months.
- **Specific request:** Expand electronic PA and require payers to use the NCPDP RTPB standard to indicate whether a drug is covered under a medical benefit.

**Topic 3: Faster decision-making requirements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delayed access to medications, tests, or therapies can worsen symptoms, accelerate disease progression, create avoidable complications, and increase healthcare utilization.
- **Specific request:** Establish clear, timely PA decision timelines that create accountability and certainty for patients, caregivers, and providers.

**Topic 4: Transparency around coverage determinations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients and providers need enough information to understand a denial and determine next steps.
- **Specific request:** Denial notices should explain unmet requirements, additional information needed, and pathways for reconsideration, exception, or appeal.

**Topic 5: Specialty-appropriate clinical review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA decisions for complex conditions should reflect the patient's condition, treatment history, and clinical needs.
- **Specific request:** Require or promote review by individuals with relevant clinical expertise in the disease area and treatment under consideration.

**Topic 6: Utilization management transparency and metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Public reporting helps identify whether PA and step therapy are delaying or restricting medically necessary care.
- **Specific request:** Report approvals, denials, appeals, and step therapy requirements at the plan level at least annually, preferably biannually or quarterly.

**Topic 7: Portability of step therapy and treatment continuity**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / RFI Step Therapy
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not repeat step therapy after a plan change when they have already completed requirements or are stable on effective therapy.
- **Specific request:** Use interoperability tools to carry treatment history, PA records, and step therapy determinations across coverage transitions; protect stable patients from unnecessary disruption.

**Topic 8: Exceptions and appeals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Standard utilization management rules cannot account for every patient's clinical circumstances.
- **Specific request:** Establish timely, understandable, and effective exceptions and appeals processes.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14 / S19 / S22 / S25:** Supports electronic PA for drugs and specifically asks CMS to require NCPDP RTPB use to indicate medical-benefit drug coverage.
- **S16:** Responds to denial-reason issues by asking for clear explanations of unmet requirements, needed information, and appeal/exception pathways.
- **S20 / S43:** Supports timely decision-making requirements for PA to reduce delays for chronic-condition patients.
- **S29-S35:** Supports plan-level public reporting of PA approvals, denials, appeals, and step therapy requirements at least annually and preferably more often.
- **S66-S82 / Step Therapy RFI:** Supports portability of step therapy determinations and treatment history so patients do not restart step therapy after changing plans.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize interoperability and electronic PA reforms to reduce delays and burden.
2. Require payers to use NCPDP RTPB to identify whether a drug is covered under a medical benefit.
3. Establish clear, timely PA decision timelines.
4. Require denial notices to explain what requirement was unmet, what information is needed, and how to seek reconsideration, exceptions, or appeal.
5. Promote specialty-appropriate clinical review of PA requests.
6. Require plan-level public reporting of approvals, denials, appeals, and step therapy use.
7. Publish utilization management metrics at least annually, with biannual or quarterly reporting preferred.
8. Ensure prior PA and step therapy history follows patients across plans.
9. Protect stable patients from treatment disruption and repeated step therapy.
10. Establish timely, understandable, and effective exceptions and appeals processes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative requirements can delay treatment, interrupt established care, force patients to repeat UM requirements, and undermine patient-provider decision-making.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Worsening symptoms, disease progression, avoidable complications, increased healthcare utilization, reduced trust, inappropriate denials, and treatment disruption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Manual PA workflows, lack of clear denial communication, insufficient clinical expertise, lack of plan-level transparency, and non-portable treatment history.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics cited.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** Complex cardiac conditions, autoimmune diseases, neurological disorders, cancer, and patients changing health plans after completing step therapy.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with chronic diseases, caregivers, providers, health plans, and policymakers.
- **Economic implications:** Delays can increase healthcare utilization; electronic PA and better information sharing could reduce administrative burden and duplication.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports use of interoperability tools and NCPDP RTPB; assumes treatment history, PA records, and step therapy determinations can move with patients.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment frames treatment continuity and timely access as central to chronic disease management, independence, and quality of life.

On behalf of the Chronic Care Policy Alliance (CCPA) we appreciate the opportunity to submit comments 
on the proposed rule by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services on Interoperability Standards and 
Prior Authorization for Drugs. 
 
CCPA is network of state and regional advocacy organizations advancing public policy that improves the 
lives of those living with chronic conditions and diseases. We work to advance policies that strengthen 
patient-centered care, improve access to medically necessary treatments, and reduce barriers that 
interfere with effective disease management. 
 
For patients with chronic conditions, access to treatment is often one of the most important tools available 
to maintain their health, independence, and quality of life. Delays in care, interruptions in treatment, and 
administrative barriers can have significant consequences for patients managing ongoing and complex 
health needs. Patients should have access to timely, understandable information about prior authorization 
requirements, coverage determinations, and treatment options so they can serve as informed participants 
in decisions affecting their care. 
 
Support for the Proposed Rule 
 
CCPA supports CMS’s efforts to improve interoperability and modernize prior authorization processes. We 
believe the proposed rule represents an important opportunity to reduce administrative burden, improve 
communication across the healthcare system, and help patients receive medically necessary care more 
quickly.
