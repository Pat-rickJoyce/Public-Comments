---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Civitas Networks for Health supports the rule's expansion of electronic prior authorization, mandatory FHIR IGs, and API reporting requirements, but raises two major concerns: the HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS proposal (X12 278 replacement with FHIR) is architecturally consequential and the Da Vinci IGs may not be sufficiently mature for the full universe of covered entities; and the payer API framework has not adequately recognized HIEs and HDUs as preferred intermediary infrastructure. Civitas urges CMS to formally recognize HIE/HDU infrastructure in payer API, event notification, and TEFCA frameworks, resolve the Medicaid legal barrier to HIE integration, and address patient matching gaps.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS, PA-DENIAL-REASONS, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, GENERAL (HIE infrastructure, event notifications, cybersecurity, payer API implementation)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 431.306(d) and 42 CFR 457.1110(b) (Medicaid/CHIP beneficiary information sharing); MATCH IT Act (H.R. 2002)
- **Standards referenced:** Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS IGs; NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, RTPB; X12 278; FHIR Subscriptions; Da Vinci PDex IG; HL7 v2 ADT; TEFCA; HITRUST; SOC 2; Patient-Centered Data Home (PCDH) framework

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs (FHIR and NCPDP Tracks)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Da Vinci IG suite is mature and increasingly well-tested; extending to medical benefit drugs is consistent with broader interoperability direction. State Medicaid systems face operational readiness challenges given concurrent HR1 implementation demands and the October 2027 deadline.
- **Specific request:** Provide clear technical guidance on CRD/DTR/PAS in drug-specific workflows; clarify HIE/HDU role for under-resourced providers; provide early detailed technical assistance to state Medicaid agencies; consider phased compliance approach; publish conformance testing framework with production data quality standards.

**Topic 2: Mandatory FHIR IGs and API Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Moving from recommended to required status backed by testing moves payer APIs toward genuine accountability. Public endpoint directory would allow HIEs/HDUs to independently validate payer compliance. Attribution remains a persistent gap conformance testing can help surface.
- **Specific request:** Publish conformance testing framework; clarify compliance includes real-world data quality in production environments; encourage use of standardized PA data in care coordination and quality workflows.

**Topic 3: Specific Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Vague denials create documentation gaps cascading through care coordination workflows. Structured, coded denial data enables systematic PA burden analysis and supports longitudinal care coordination, quality measurement, and value-based care.
- **Specific request:** Align denial reason coding with existing clinical terminology standards to support downstream quality measurement use cases.

**Topic 4: HIPAA PA Standards (X12 278 Replacement)**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Oppose (current timeline and approach)
- **Rationale:** This is a compliance reset for every covered entity conducting PA transactions—not a technical upgrade. X12 278 is deeply embedded in clearinghouses, EHR vendors, billing systems, and intermediary networks. 24-month timeline is insufficient. FHIR-based PA transactions have not been tested at scale across the full diversity of HIPAA-covered entities.
- **Specific request:** Conduct thorough operational readiness assessment for all covered entity categories; consider extending timeline beyond 24 months with structured transition pathway; clarify clearinghouse compliance obligations; solicit documented implementation community evidence on Da Vinci PAS/CRD/DTR IG maturity before finalizing.

**Topic 5: Electronic Event Notifications (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (with HIE-centric approach)
- **Rationale:** Civitas members route millions of ADT notifications annually. Payer-centric notification architecture would replicate connectivity in expensive, difficult-to-govern ways; HIEs provide neutral, multi-payer infrastructure enabling efficient routing. PCDH proves HIE-driven event notification works at national scale.
- **Specific request:** Formally recognize HIE/HDU as preferred intermediary model; extend notification standards beyond ADT to high-value clinical events; use PCDH as cross-state architecture model; address three structural gaps: fragile direct alerting model, predatory pricing, litigation risk discouraging HIE participation.

**Topic 6: Health Care Resiliency and Cybersecurity (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** PRIVACY-SECURITY
- **Position:** Collaborative
- **Rationale:** 2024 Change Healthcare attack demonstrated systemic risks of centralized processing and value of distributed HIE infrastructure as emergency backup. No documented instances of data breaches at or by an HIE. QIOs/RHICs help smaller providers assess cybersecurity posture.
- **Specific request:** Recognize HIE/HDU as national resiliency infrastructure; include QIOs/RHICs as recognized cybersecurity technical assistance resources; develop accessible provider guidance on evaluating health IT vendor cybersecurity posture (HITRUST, SOC 2).

**Topic 7: Improving Payer API Implementation (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Collaborative
- **Rationale:** Key gap is between technical API availability and operational data quality. Many providers lack capacity to connect directly to payer APIs. Medicaid legal ambiguity (42 CFR 431.306(d)) blocks HIE integration. Patient matching remains a critical unresolved production gap.
- **Specific request:** Recognize HIEs/HDUs as authorized intermediaries; clarify 42 CFR 431.306(d) and 457.1110(b) do not prohibit Medicaid/CHIP beneficiary info sharing with HIEs for Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs; address patient matching via MATCH IT Act; leverage QIOs/RHICs for provider-facing payer API adoption.

**Topic 8: HIE/HDU Role in Federal Interoperability Architecture**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** HIE/HDU infrastructure built over two decades with trusted governance frameworks and community accountability. The payer API bilateral framework has generated uneven adoption and last-mile gaps precisely because it bypasses the networked intermediary infrastructure HIEs already provide.
- **Specific request:** Formally recognize HDUs within TEFCA with defined participation pathways, data use expectations, and governance standards.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** (1) HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS is a compliance reset for all covered entities—24-month timeline is insufficient and Da Vinci IGs have not been tested at scale across the full HIPAA covered entity universe. (2) Payer API framework bypasses existing HIE/HDU infrastructure, creating last-mile access gaps for small and rural providers. (3) Medicaid legal barrier (42 CFR 431.306(d)) blocks HIE integration with Medicaid payer APIs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** 24-month HIPAA transition would disproportionately harm smaller providers, rural hospitals, FQHCs, behavioral health organizations, and clearinghouses; state Medicaid systems face compressed timeline given concurrent HR1 implementation; patient matching failures have highest clinical stakes.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** X12 278 deeply embedded in existing infrastructure; predatory pricing and litigation risk undermine HIE participation; alert fatigue from undirected notification expansion; Medicaid legal ambiguity; patient matching unreliability in production environments.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Civitas members route millions of notifications annually; nearly all current payer API use in member markets tied to value-based care contracts; no documented instances of data breaches at or by an HIE.
- **Studies or research referenced:** References 2024 Change Healthcare cyberattack; MATCH IT Act (H.R. 2002); PCDH framework; California Data Exchange Framework; Connecting for Better Health Community Sandbox.
- **Examples provided:** HIE networks serving as emergency backup during Change Healthcare outage; single HIE connection enabling multi-payer access for providers; PCDH as cross-state event notification architecture proof of concept; Community Sandbox as implementation testing model.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** HIEs, HDUs, APCDs, QIOs, RHICs; small and rural providers, FQHCs, behavioral health organizations; state Medicaid agencies; clearinghouses and billing system vendors; all HIPAA covered entities conducting PA transactions.
- **Economic implications:** HIPAA transition would require systemic rework across clearinghouses, EHR vendors, billing systems, and health plans; smaller entities face disproportionate costs; predatory notification pricing undermines HIE sustainability.
- **Technical feasibility:** Core concern about HIPAA PA Standards—Da Vinci IGs not tested at scale for full HIPAA covered entity universe; persistent gap between technical API availability and production data quality.
- **Patient care impact:** Indirect but significant—better notification and API infrastructure improves care coordination and reduces avoidable utilization; patient matching failures create clinical risk for most complex patients.
