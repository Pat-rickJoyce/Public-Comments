## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports CMS efforts to make prior authorization more timely, transparent, and interoperable, but warns that electronic PA must not merely make denials faster or more difficult to challenge. The comment asks CMS to require meaningful denial explanations, accessible PA records, patient-impact metrics, safeguards for automated systems, and special attention to services where delays can cause functional loss.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Clear Denial Explanations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that patients are harmed when denials are generic, difficult to understand, or hard to correct and appeal.
- **Specific request:** Require clear, specific, clinically meaningful denial explanations rather than generic documentation-insufficient language.

**Topic 2: Accessible Prior Authorization Records**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients need access to the factual record to understand what happened, correct errors, and pursue appeals.
- **Specific request:** Require accessible PA records showing request date, submitted documents, status changes, decision date, denial reason, appeal pathway, and final outcome.

**Topic 3: Patient-Impact Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Metrics should capture whether care reaches patients in time, not merely whether electronic transactions occur.
- **Specific request:** Measure time to care, appeal time, repeat-request burden, abandoned care, and delays affecting function.

**Topic 4: Safeguards for Automated or Electronic Systems**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Faster electronic systems can still harm patients if they generate opaque denials, hidden delays, or uncorrectable records.
- **Specific request:** Add safeguards ensuring electronic PA systems are transparent, correctable, and do not simply accelerate harmful denials.

**Topic 5: Time-Sensitive Functional Services**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** For rehabilitation, imaging, therapy, lab testing, and DMEPOS, timing and continuity can determine whether a patient regains or preserves basic function.
- **Specific request:** Give special attention to PA policies affecting rehabilitation, imaging, therapy, laboratory testing, and DMEPOS.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require clear, specific, clinically meaningful denial explanations.
2. Prohibit reliance on generic denial language such as documentation insufficient without meaningful detail.
3. Require accessible PA records with request, submission, status, decision, denial, appeal, and outcome information.
4. Track patient-impact metrics such as time to care, appeal time, repeat-request burden, abandoned care, and functional delays.
5. Add safeguards for automated or electronic PA systems to prevent opaque denials and hidden delays.
6. Pay special attention to rehabilitation, imaging, therapy, laboratory testing, and DMEPOS because delays can cause functional loss.
