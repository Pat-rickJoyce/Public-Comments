## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This individual commenter supports interoperability and prior authorization reform in principle but raises four specific concerns: a conflict-of-interest allegation regarding CMS leadership (Chris Klomp's former role at Collective Medical/PointClickCare), inadequate access controls and scope disclosure for the proposed FHIR endpoint registry, data governance gaps across expanded APIs, and the absence of explicit prohibitions on non-healthcare secondary use of patient data. The commenter requests formal CMS responses to each concern in the final rule preamble.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Privacy Act of 1974, HIPAA, Social Security Act (referenced in context of prior CMS data-sharing violations)
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR (endpoint registry, Payer-to-Payer API, Prior Authorization API, Patient Access API)

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Conflict of Interest — CMS Leadership**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Chris Klomp, overseeing CMS operations, is the former CEO of Collective Medical (sold for ~$650M to PointClickCare) and retains investments through Endurance Companies. The rule expands health data-sharing infrastructure directly benefiting his former employer.
- **Specific request:** Disclose whether Klomp recused himself, whether a conflict-of-interest waiver was obtained, and which officials made the final determination to publish.

**Topic 2: FHIR Endpoint Registry — Undisclosed Scope**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** The registry constitutes new permanent federal infrastructure without defined access controls; the proposed rule does not address who can access it, whether it can be shared with DHS or immigration enforcement, audit mechanisms, or whether it is subject to HIPAA limitations.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly prohibit registry data from being repurposed for non-healthcare uses without Congressional authorization; address access controls in the final rule.

**Topic 3: Data Governance Gaps**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The rule expands multiple APIs without establishing data minimization requirements, prohibitions on secondary commercial use, patient notification for Payer-to-Payer data flows, or individual remedies for misuse.
- **Specific request:** Add explicit data minimization requirements, commercial-use prohibitions, patient notification requirements, and individual remedies.

**Topic 4: Transparency and Accountability Requests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Given documented prior CMS violations (Senators Schiff and Padilla letters regarding Medicaid PII sharing with DHS), the commenter argues the final rule requires higher justification and explicit oversight mechanisms.
- **Specific request:** CMS must respond on the record to: all COI reviews, private-sector consultation relationships, whether Payer-to-Payer data can be accessed by law enforcement/immigration, and what independent oversight body will monitor compliance.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S23 (API endpoint reporting — security concerns):** Commenter raises concern that the new FHIR endpoint registry lacks defined access controls and could be accessed by non-healthcare federal agencies, including immigration enforcement, without explicit prohibition.
- **S24 (what to report):** Implicitly addressed — commenter argues the scope of the registry and who may access reported endpoint data is undisclosed and inadequate.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Disclose whether Chris Klomp recused himself from all CMS-0062-P decisions and whether a formal COI waiver was obtained.
2. Identify all senior CMS officials with financial interests in entities benefiting from expanded interoperability and disclose COI reviews.
3. Identify private-sector entities consulted in drafting the rule and any financial relationships to the FHIR endpoint registry or health data infrastructure.
4. Explicitly prohibit registry data from being repurposed for non-healthcare uses (including immigration enforcement) without Congressional authorization.
5. Add data minimization requirements to all expanded APIs (Payer-to-Payer, Prior Authorization, Patient Access).
6. Prohibit secondary commercial use of data flowing through interoperability APIs.
7. Require patient notification when their data flows through Payer-to-Payer channels.
8. Establish individual remedies for data misuse.
9. Confirm whether Payer-to-Payer API data can be accessed under existing memoranda of understanding with law enforcement or immigration agencies.
10. Identify an independent oversight body to monitor compliance with data use restrictions.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a private citizen & a constituent of/within communities affected by medicaid/medicare. I write neither in opposition to interoperability Standards as a goal nor to prior authorization reform, both of which, relatively speaking, I support in principle. I write because this proposed rule raises specific, documented concerns that must be addressed in the final rule before implementation.

1. CONFLICT OF INTEREST — CHRIS KLOMP

The individual currently overseeing CMS operations, Chris Klomp, is the former CEO of Collective Medical — a health data sharing company he built & sold for approximately $650 million in 2020 to PointClickCare, which now operates with 27,000 senior care facilities, over 2,700 hospitals, 180 health plans, & 75 state and government agencies. Mr. Klomp remained a Senior Advisor to PointClickCare through January 2024 & retains investments in related health technology companies through Endurance Companies.

This proposed rule directly expands the health data sharing infrastructure that Mr. Klomp's former company pioneered & from which his former employer continues to profit...
