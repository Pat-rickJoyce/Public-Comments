## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Not provided

- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Retail pharmacist working directly with patients, doctors, and insurance companies.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports CMS-0062-P and moving drug prior authorization to a fully electronic process. They recommend patient notifications when PA is initiated, real-time provider access to patient-specific formularies before prescribing, and simpler API reporting oversight that does not add penalty-driven administrative burden.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PATIENT-ACCESS/GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Docket No. CMS-2026-1255.
- **Standards referenced:** APIs generally; CoverMyMeds mentioned as an example platform. No FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards are named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Fully electronic drug PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Current pharmacy PA workflows require repeated calls between pharmacy and prescriber and leave patients waiting without information.
- **Specific request:** Move drug PA to a fully electronic process.

**Topic 2: Patient notification**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients often do not know PA has been initiated or how to follow up.
- **Specific request:** Require automatic patient notification when a PA is initiated.

**Topic 3: Real-time formulary access before prescribing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Prescribers could avoid pharmacy-to-office back-and-forth if they knew coverage and PA requirements before sending prescriptions.
- **Specific request:** Give providers real-time access to patient-specific drug formularies and allow PA initiation before prescriptions reach the pharmacy.

**Topic 4: Reporting and penalties**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Penalty-backed reporting may create a new compliance burden and divert attention from patient care.
- **Specific request:** Use a simpler monitoring approach that preserves accountability without adding administrative load.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S23-S24:** The commenter raises concern about API reporting requirements and associated penalties, asking CMS to avoid unnecessary administrative burden.
- **S29-S30:** The comment addresses monitoring burden in general, but does not propose specific metrics or privacy protections.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to broader workflow and patient notification issues by requesting patient-facing PA status notification and provider access to formulary information.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic drug prior authorization modernization.
2. Require automatic patient notification when PA is initiated.
3. Provide prescribers real-time patient-specific formulary and coverage access before prescribing.
4. Permit PA initiation before the prescription is sent to the pharmacy.
5. Simplify API reporting and compliance monitoring to avoid new provider burden.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Pharmacy staff burden, lack of status visibility, patient uncertainty, provider formulary gaps, and penalty-driven reporting burden.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays in medication access, staff time diverted from patient care, repeated calls, and provider focus shifting to compliance.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of pharmacy visibility into PA status and lack of prescriber access to coverage information before prescribing.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CMS estimate that prior authorization costs providers around $34,000 and 700 hours per year in administrative work.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Pharmacy calling the doctor's office, repeated follow-up, CoverMyMeds notifications that do not include patients, and patients waiting without status information.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, retail pharmacists, pharmacy staff, prescribers, providers, and payers.
- **Economic implications:** Electronic PA and formulary access could reduce staff time spent on calls and follow-up; reporting penalties could increase compliance burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter assumes electronic PA, patient notifications, and real-time formulary access are feasible enhancements.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster status awareness and pre-prescribing formulary checks could reduce waiting and improve medication access.
