# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Maryland Department of Health (MDH), Maryland Medicaid Administration
- **Stakeholder type:** Government; State Medicaid/CHIP FFS; Medicaid/CHIP Managed Care
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** State Medicaid agency responsible for Medicaid administration, PBM/MCO contracting, procurement, and implementation of federal interoperability and prior authorization requirements.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MDH supports CMS' goals of interoperability, prior authorization streamlining, and better information exchange, but objects to several proposed requirements as technically undefined, operationally burdensome, and infeasible on the October 1, 2027 timeline. MDH asks CMS to withdraw or reassess machine-readable clinical criteria and internal audit requirements, delay drug PA metrics/public reporting until CY2028 data with publication in 2029, and extend or revise Track 2 pharmacy benefit drug requirements tied to NCPDP, RTPB, PDL mapping, ePA workflows, and API reporting.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; TRANSPARENCY-related general requirements.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Docket CMS-2026-1255-0001 / CMS-0062-P; no specific CFR citations in the comment text.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT, NCPDP Formulary & Benefit (F&B), NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB), FHIR, API endpoint reporting, API usage metrics, PDL mapping, ePA workflows.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Machine-readable PA clinical criteria**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** MDH states clinical criteria are maintained in narrative policies, vendor systems, proprietary rule sets, decision trees, PDFs, and free text, not standardized computable artifacts. Criteria change frequently, may be vendor-owned/licensed, may include subjective judgment, and CMS has not defined a required model, vocabulary, logic representation, or handling of exceptions and clinical judgment.
- **Specific request:** Withdraw the machine-readable clinical criteria requirement until a national standard for computable clinical criteria exists.

**Topic 2: Internal audit framework, monitoring, and 10-year retention**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** MDH argues the proposed daily/weekly monitoring, exception reporting, vendor oversight, FHIR conformance tracking, rapid remediation, and 10-year log retention require new enterprise data platforms, real-time pipelines, dashboards, archival infrastructure, staffing, and contractual authority that do not exist today.
- **Specific request:** Withdraw the internal audit framework requirements as written.

**Topic 3: Drug PA metrics, public posting, and API usage reporting timeline**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MDH does not currently capture the required drug PA data; relevant information sits in PBM systems and requires custom data files, contract modifications, validation, and reconciliation. Contract modifications require 12-18 months and cannot produce reliable CY2027 data.
- **Specific request:** Use CY2028 as the base year for drug PA metrics and publish CY2028 data by March 31, 2029.

**Topic 4: Track 2 pharmacy benefit drug operational and technical requirements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify/Oppose in part
- **Rationale:** MDH states NCPDP ePA, F&B, RTPB, PDL mapping, prescriber-EHR/pharmacy/PA adjudication integration, and carved-out pharmacy benefit responsibilities require PBM and MCO contract modifications and system builds that cannot be completed by October 1, 2027.
- **Specific request:** Reassess the 10/1/2027 compliance timeline and remove RTPB therapeutic alternatives as a requirement.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** MDH addresses non-Part D pharmacy benefit applicability by explaining that state pharmacy benefit implementation depends on PBM systems, PDL mapping, NCPDP workflows, and carved-out pharmacy arrangements.
- **S19:** MDH directly raises state readiness concerns, including procurement, budget, PBM/MCO contracting, and system build timelines.
- **S22:** MDH identifies significant NCPDP-related costs and implementation work for SCRIPT ePA, F&B, RTPB, and PDL mapping.
- **S25:** MDH questions RTPB functionality by requesting removal of the therapeutic-alternatives RTPB requirement.
- **S29/S34:** MDH responds to API/drug PA metric collection and reporting frequency by explaining the necessary data are not currently captured and requesting a later baseline year.
- **S36/S38:** MDH's state implementation concerns are relevant to extension criteria and duration, particularly for state FFS programs needing more time.
- **S37:** MDH describes implementation challenges across FHIR conformance, audit, reporting, vendor oversight, and staffing.
- **S43:** MDH discusses PA timeframe monitoring and exception reporting, objecting to the operational infrastructure required.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Withdraw the requirement to publish machine-readable PA clinical criteria until a national computable clinical criteria standard exists.
2. Define any future machine-readable criteria requirement with a clear data model, vocabulary, logic representation, and approach to narrative or subjective clinical judgment.
3. Withdraw the internal audit framework requirements as written.
4. Extend implementation timelines to account for state procurement, legislative approval, PBM/MCO contract amendments, and vendor build cycles.
5. Make CY2028 the base year for drug PA metrics and require publication by March 31, 2029.
6. Remove the RTPB requirement to return therapeutic alternatives in real time.
7. Recognize vendor-controlled systems and state limits over PBM, UM vendor, and MCO data and logs.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Undefined technical requirements, infeasible timeline, lack of computable criteria standards, vendor ownership/licensing limits, insufficient state staffing, lack of monitoring infrastructure, long procurement/contract cycles.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Misleading or outdated clinical criteria, recipient/provider confusion, increased state costs, inaccurate public metrics, inability to meet audit expectations, vendor failures imputed to states.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** PBM/MCO contract modifications, 12-18 month procurement lifecycle, new EDP/data pipelines, dashboards, archival storage, FHIR expertise, vendor log access, legislative/budget approval.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Contract modifications take 12-18 months; procurement cycles can take 18-36 months; requested CY2028 baseline and March 31, 2029 publication.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Not addressed.
- **Examples provided:** Narrative medical policies, PBM/UM vendor rule sets, proprietary decision trees, embedded vendor logic, free-text reviewer criteria, and PDF PA forms as examples of non-computable criteria sources.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** State Medicaid agencies, Medicaid beneficiaries/recipients, providers, PBMs, MCOs, UM vendors, IT contractors, data warehouse vendors.
- **Economic implications:** Multi-million-dollar state implementation and ongoing infrastructure costs; additional staffing, licensing, governance, QA, storage, and contract costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** MDH views several requirements as not feasible without standards, data pipelines, new platforms, and extensive vendor changes.
- **Patient care impact:** MDH frames risks primarily around recipient confusion and inaccurate/outdated criteria, rather than direct clinical outcome data.
