# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Association of Medicaid Directors (NAMD); signed by Stuart Portman, NAMD Board President and Georgia Medicaid Director, and Lee Grossman, NAMD Board President Elect and Iowa Medicaid Director
- **Stakeholder type:** Government / Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Nonpartisan professional community of Medicaid leaders serving nearly 70 million people through Medicaid and CHIP across states, D.C., and territories; supports over 500 Medicaid policy leaders.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NAMD supports CMS's goals for interoperability, streamlined prior authorization, and improved information exchange, but argues that the proposed timeline is not feasible for Medicaid agencies facing major system, budget, procurement, legislative, and vendor-capacity constraints. NAMD asks CMS to extend implementation by at least three years, prioritize core functionality before reporting, provide enhanced systems match and technical assistance, allow flexible compliance pathways, account for PBM/vendor market limitations, create open-source Medicaid reference implementations, and revise 24-hour requirements to align with real-world Medicaid operations.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; X12; NCPDP; Patient Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization APIs; health information network connectivity.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medicaid Implementation Timeline**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Medicaid agencies are simultaneously implementing prior CMS interoperability rules, H.R. 1/Working Families Tax Cut Legislation changes, state legislative requirements, procurement changes, budget constraints, and major systems modernization.
- **Specific request:** Extend implementation by at least three years, prioritize core functionality first, and delay reporting requirements until implementation experience matures.

**Topic 2: Funding and Enhanced Federal Match**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Implementation requires Medicaid enterprise system changes, vendor contracts, staff capacity, and technical resources.
- **Specific request:** Provide enhanced systems match to support implementation to the fullest extent possible.

**Topic 3: Alternative Compliance Pathways for Existing Networks**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Existing health information networks already use FHIR, X12, and NCPDP connections and may already support electronic PA workflows.
- **Specific request:** Clarify whether pharmacy vendor integrations with health information networks satisfy NCPDP requirements and whether agencies may rely on vendor documentation instead of payer-specific documentation.

**Topic 4: PBM Infrastructure Exemption, Extension, and Reference Implementation**
- **Provision ID:** STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PBM vendor markets are concentrated; smaller Medicaid agencies may lack vendor responses or appropriations for mid-procurement changes.
- **Specific request:** Establish exemption/extension processes that account for vendor market availability, provide targeted technical assistance and enhanced match clarification, assess vendor capacity before final compliance dates, and develop an open-source Medicaid pharmacy-benefit reference implementation.

**Topic 5: Managed Care Reporting Pathway**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Medicaid agencies need to know whether MCOs must build their own Prior Authorization APIs or report through the state Medicaid API; uncertainty could cause duplicate builds and contract amendments.
- **Specific request:** Allow state/territory Medicaid agencies to designate a consolidated reporting pathway through the Medicaid API and provide local-market flexibility.

**Topic 6: Data Reporting and 24-Hour Compliance Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Overnight batch processing, PBM data exchange, MMIS updates, weekends, holidays, and late-day requests may make one-business-day or 24-hour requirements operationally infeasible.
- **Specific request:** Incorporate business-day standards and consider two-business-day timeframes across applicable provisions; adopt a principles-based compliance framework based on practical implementation realities and demonstrated progress.

**Topic 7: Technical Assistance, Guidance, and Open-Source Tools**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** States need support on workforce/vendor readiness, implementation guidance, training, cross-state learning, and compliance questions.
- **Specific request:** Provide technical assistance; partner with vendors; offer FHIR training; move technical details into sub-regulatory guidance where possible; pair guidance with model open-source software; create reference implementations for Patient Access API and Payer-to-Payer API; hold office hours, listening sessions, FAQs, and supplemental guidance.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12 / S14 / S19:** NAMD identifies Medicaid-specific implementation challenges for drug PA standards, especially procurement, vendor contracts, PBM readiness, and operational capacity.
- **S21:** NAMD argues the proposed timeline is not achievable for Medicaid agencies and requests at least a three-year extension.
- **S22:** NAMD raises cost and procurement concerns related to NCPDP and PBM infrastructure, especially for smaller agencies.
- **S25:** NAMD seeks clarity on whether existing health information network connections can satisfy NCPDP requirements.
- **S29-S35:** NAMD asks CMS to delay reporting requirements until core functionality matures, account for data latency, publish existing API usage, and allow consolidated Medicaid reporting pathways.
- **S36 / S38:** NAMD supports extension/exemption processes that account for state/territory readiness and vendor market availability, with targeted technical assistance and federal match clarification.
- **S37:** NAMD describes implementation challenges including procurement, contracts with MCOs/PBMs/fiscal agents, vendor capacity, workforce constraints, FHIR training, and overlapping federal/state initiatives.
- **S43:** NAMD requests revised 24-hour timeframes and recommends business-day or two-business-day standards to accommodate batch processing and clinical complexity.
- **S60-S65:** NAMD supports sub-regulatory guidance, open-source resources, and reference implementations to manage technical details and future standards changes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend implementation timelines by at least three years.
2. Prioritize core functionality first and delay reporting requirements until experience matures.
3. Provide enhanced systems match to support Medicaid implementation.
4. Clarify whether existing health information network/vendor integrations satisfy NCPDP standard requirements.
5. Allow agencies to reference vendor documentation where appropriate rather than maintaining redundant payer-specific materials.
6. Structure the final rule with outcomes-based flexibility rather than a single prescribed technical pathway.
7. Issue sub-regulatory guidance, guidance letters, and open-source resources before compliance deadlines.
8. Establish exemption and extension processes accounting for PBM/vendor market availability.
9. Assess vendor capacity before finalizing compliance dates.
10. Develop or facilitate a Medicaid reference implementation for pharmacy benefits.
11. Allow consolidated reporting through state/territory Medicaid APIs where appropriate.
12. Build flexibility into reporting expectations to account for data latency and vendor/MCO/PBM challenges.
13. Replace rigid 24-hour requirements with business-day standards, potentially two business days where operationally appropriate.
14. Provide technical assistance on workforce readiness, vendor readiness, implementation guidance, training, cross-state learning, and operational questions.
15. Partner with vendors and facilitate FHIR training.
16. Develop reference implementations for Patient Access API and Payer-to-Payer API.
17. Establish office hours, listening sessions, FAQs, supplemental guidance, and other ongoing state engagement mechanisms.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Timeline infeasibility, insufficient state and vendor capacity, procurement and contract constraints, PBM vendor concentration, duplicative MCO infrastructure, data latency, and lack of operational clarity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Increased denials if 24-hour decisions are rushed, provider frustration, delayed access to care when additional clinical information is needed, duplicative builds, costly contract amendments, and underutilized APIs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** H.R. 1 implementation, state legislative requirements, budget constraints, procurement requirements, MCO/PBM/fiscal agent contracts, MMIS modifications, batch processing, PBM data exchange delays, holidays/weekends, workforce shortages, and vendor capacity.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** NAMD represents Medicaid programs covering nearly 70 million people and supports more than 500 Medicaid policy leaders.
- **Studies or research referenced:** CMS Medicaid and CHIP Policy Implementation Roadmap; CMS software reuse policy and open-source software strategy; Eligibility Made Easy (Emmy) tool as an example of open-source implementation support.
- **Examples provided:** Procurement delays, contract amendments with MCOs/PBMs/fiscal agents, states requesting additional time for current interoperability rules, concentrated PBM vendor market, overnight batch processing, and pharmacy data received from PBMs often taking two business days.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** State and territory Medicaid agencies, Medicaid members, CHIP agencies, MCOs, PBMs, fiscal agents, pharmacy vendors, health information networks, CMS, state legislatures, and Medicaid IT staff.
- **Economic implications:** Significant implementation costs from procurement, contract amendments, vendor builds, MMIS changes, workforce training, and potential duplicative infrastructure; enhanced federal match could mitigate costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible only with longer timelines, flexible pathways, technical assistance, open-source reference implementations, vendor capacity assessments, and realistic data-reporting timeframes.
- **Patient care impact:** NAMD supports timely access and administrative burden reduction, but warns rigid decision deadlines could increase denials or delay care when additional clinical information is needed.
