ORIGINAL COMMENT (COMPLETE)
------------------------
As a healthcare leader responsible for clinical informatics and operational workflows, I strongly support the intent of this proposed rule to modernize prior authorization through improved interoperability and electronic processes. Prior authorization delays are not simply administrative inefficiencies—they directly impact patient care. When approvals are delayed, patients experience postponed treatments, interruptions in medication access, and, in some cases, worsening clinical outcomes. For this reason, the proposed decision timeframes are not just operational benchmarks; they are critical patient safety safeguards. However, the effectiveness of this rule will depend on meaningful enforcement. If payers are not held accountable to consistently meet these timelines, the burden of delay will continue to fall on patients despite the transition to electronic systems.
Additionally, it is essential that payers bear the primary responsibility for the costs and operational lift associated with integration and implementation. Prior authorization requirements originate from payer policies, and the administrative complexity that this rule seeks to address has largely been driven by payer processes. While providers will need to interact with enhanced digital workflows, shifting the cost burden onto health systems would further strain organizations already managing significant clinical and financial pressures. True reform requires clear accountability—both in terms of adherence to required timelines and investment in the infrastructure needed to support interoperability. Without enforceable standards and appropriate allocation of responsibility, there is a risk that these changes will not achieve their intended impact. Ensuring payer accountability will be key to delivering meaningful improvements in efficiency, transparency, and, most importantly, patient care.
