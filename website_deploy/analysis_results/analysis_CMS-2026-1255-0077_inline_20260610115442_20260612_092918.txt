## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A California neurologist supports CMS prior authorization and step therapy reform but emphasizes critical implementation needs for transparency in denial reasons. The commenter documents that over 50 patients have been harmed by PA denials since a February policy change, including a detailed case of a patient whose effective Botox treatment was denied without specific reasons, resulting in 10+ ER visits and deterioration from 2 to 25 migraine days per month. The commenter also objects to step therapy requirements forcing young women to try medications with serious safety concerns.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior Authorization Reform (General)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support

- **Specific request:** Implement the proposed prior authorization reforms.

- **Rationale:** Current denial letters do not list actual reasons for denial, only general requirements, making appeals impossible to address effectively.

**Topic 4: Continuity of Care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Over 50 patients stable on Botox for years experienced disruption in care due to a February policy change.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a neurologist and headache specialist practicing in California. Thank you to CMS for reforming prior auth reform and step therapy.

Prior authorization harms my patients by delaying treatment for my patients. Since the Botox rule change in February I've had more than 50 patients impacted by difficulty with prior authorization and denials. These are patients who have had Botox for migraine for years and now have a disruption in care. I have a patient that used to have 30 days of headache and migraine per month but with Botox had 2 migraine attacks per month. But in March her Botox was denied through prior auth. We submitted appeal after appeal and each time the denial letter doss not list the actual reason for denial but rather just the requirements in general which we have always documented. This patient still has not been able to get Botox and now has 25 migraine days per month and has been to the emergency room more than 10 times. This is horrible for the patient and costly to the system. We need more transparency in prior auth reform.

Similarly step therapy causes harmful delays for patients. It requires patients to try medications that are harmful. I have young women of reproductive age that are being forced to try depakote which may harm possible fetus, or amitriptyline which will cause brain fog and problems concentrating at school and work.
