## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HIMSS endorses the goals of the proposed rule but raises significant concerns about implementation readiness, calling the October 1, 2027 compliance date premature. HIMSS recommends a glidepath starting 18 months after the final rule effective date, prioritization of a CMS payer directory, mandatory testing at scale, IG version standardization, and a delayed ONC standards expiration date of January 1, 2029. On NCPDP vs. FHIR for drug ePA, HIMSS members are divided and recommend CMS convene stakeholders to find short- and long-term solutions. HIMSS also provides detailed cybersecurity recommendations in response to the Healthcare Resiliency RFI.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Compliance Timeline (October 1, 2027)**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Oppose (as proposed; request modification)
- **Rationale:** ePA FHIR APIs have not been tested at scale; CMS payer directory does not yet exist; payers on different IG versions; software development takes more than a year even absent other barriers.
- **Specific request:** Glidepath of implementation milestones starting 18 months after final rule effective date (or after prerequisites are met).

**Topic 2: CMS Payer Directory**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify (prerequisite must be resolved first)
- **Rationale:** Payer directory essential for ePA payer look-up; its absence makes testing at scale near impossible.
- **Specific request:** Prioritize payer directory development before compliance deadlines are imposed.

**Topic 3: IG Version Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payers use different IG versions due to lack of ONC certification requirements; health plans built on earlier versions for two years, creating compliance conflict.
- **Specific request:** CMS should set a benchmark for a single IG version for industry alignment.

**Topic 4: NCPDP vs. FHIR for Drug ePA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify (divided membership; recommends convening)
- **Rationale:** NCPDP advocates say FHIR is premature for drug ePA; FHIR advocates say long-term alignment is less burdensome; HIMSS members agree NCPDP is effective short-term; FHIR testable in future.
- **Specific request:** CMS should convene a diverse group of healthcare constituencies to identify short- and long-term solutions.

**Topic 5: ONC Standards Expiration Date**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** EHR and certified developers are still implementing current standards; January 1, 2028 expiration not reasonable; HIMSS recommends 18-month–2-year cycles for major standard changes.
- **Specific request:** Voluntary shift January 2028; mandatory compliance January 1, 2029.

**Topic 6: Cybersecurity / Healthcare Resiliency (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Recommend
- **Rationale:** Healthcare organizations face asymmetric cyber threats; small/rural health systems under-resourced; legacy systems create technical debt; clearinghouses rely on outdated centralized methods; AI growth is emerging cybersecurity risk.
- **Specific request:** Align new regulations with NIST Cybersecurity Framework; use continuous micro-learning; mandate decentralized FHIR API integrations for clearinghouses; fund small/rural practices; leverage existing Stark Law waivers for preparedness; CMS + OCR joint convening for scalable security guidance.

- **S21 (Timeline achievable):** October 1, 2027 explicitly called premature; recommends 18-month glidepath from final rule effective date.
- **S12 (Technical challenges — medical benefit drugs):** ePA FHIR APIs untested at scale; payer directory absence makes testing near impossible.
- **S14/S22 (NCPDP costs, non-Part D applicability):** Members divided on NCPDP vs. FHIR; recommends CMS convene stakeholders; NCPDP effective short-term.
- **S60 (ONC versioning approach):** Recommends voluntary January 2028, mandatory January 1, 2029 for new standards/IGs.
- **S61 (Coordination):** Recommends CMS convene diverse constituencies on NCPDP vs. FHIR.
- **S62 (Testing criteria):** Explicitly recommends requiring at-scale testing with lessons learned from January 2027 existing API implementation.
- **S64 (Stakeholder input):** Recommends convening diverse group for NCPDP vs. FHIR decision.
- **S65 (Industry adoption rates):** Health plans built on earlier IG versions for two years; alignment needed.
- **S23/S24 (API endpoint reporting/security):** Payer directory development would support endpoint reporting and payer look-up.
- **S66-S82 (Healthcare Resiliency RFI — Cybersecurity):** Extensive response: NIST alignment, small/rural practice support, continuous micro-learning, clearinghouse decentralization via FHIR, AI as cybersecurity risk, leveraging Stark Law waivers.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend the October 1, 2027 compliance date to a glidepath starting 18 months after the final rule effective date or after prerequisites are met.
2. Prioritize development of the CMS payer directory before compliance deadlines are imposed.
3. Require testing at scale with lessons learned from January 2027 existing API infrastructure.
4. Set a benchmark for a single IG version to establish industry alignment.
5. Convene a diverse group of healthcare constituencies to identify short- and long-term solutions for NCPDP vs. FHIR drug ePA standards.
6. Set January 2028 as voluntary and January 1, 2029 as mandatory compliance date for new ONC standards/IGs.
7. Align new CMS cybersecurity regulations with NIST Cybersecurity Framework.
8. Fund and support small/rural practices when mandating new security measures; consider shared-service models.
9. Mandate standardized, secure, decentralized API integrations (HL7 FHIR) for clearinghouses.
10. Use continuous, contextual micro-learning and diagnostic phishing simulation for cybersecurity training.
11. CMS and OCR together convene diverse regulated entities for scalable MFA, encryption, and risk assessment guidance.
12. Promote existing Physician Self-Referral Exceptions and Anti-Kickback Act Waivers for ecosystem-wide cybersecurity preparedness.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** October 1, 2027 timeline premature; ONC January 2028 standards expiration too soon; NCPDP vs. FHIR member division; absent payer directory; untested at-scale FHIR ePA.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Compliance conflicts for health plans on earlier IG versions; ineffective ePA without testing and payer directory; small/rural practice bankruptcy from unfunded cybersecurity mandates.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No at-scale FHIR ePA testing; no CMS payer directory; divergent IG versions in use; software development time over one year; asymmetric cybersecurity resources for small/rural entities; legacy medical device technical debt.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Health plans have built on earlier IG versions for two years; HIMSS recommends 18-month–2-year implementation cycles for major standard changes (consistent policy position).
- **Studies or research referenced:** 2025 HIPAA Security Rule; HIMSS prior comments on CMS-0057.
- **Examples provided:** Network micro-segmentation to isolate legacy medical devices; high-profile clearinghouse incidents (implied) demonstrating centralized system vulnerability.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, EHR developers, certified electronic developers, health IT vendors, health systems, small/rural practices, clearinghouses, patients.
- **Economic implications:** Premature compliance creates cost burdens; unfunded cybersecurity mandates could bankrupt small/rural clinics; shared-service models could reduce costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Significant concerns — at-scale testing not done, payer directory absent, IG version divergence; recommends phased approach.
- **Patient care impact:** Proper implementation could accelerate care access; premature mandates could disrupt care delivery; small/rural practice bankruptcy reduces patient access.
