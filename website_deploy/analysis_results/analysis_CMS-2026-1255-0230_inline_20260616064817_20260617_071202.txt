ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a registered nurse with 5.5 years of clinical experience across multiple disciplines and an independent clinical AI governance consultant. I am submitting this comment in connection with primary-source research I have conducted on CMS-0057-F prior authorization disclosures.<br/><br/>Under CMS-0057-F, impacted payers were required to publicly report prior authorization denial rates for non-drug items and services beginning March 31, 2026. In reviewing these disclosures directly from payer websites, I identified a material disclosure quality problem: UnitedHealthcare&#39;s HTML disclosure pages present &quot;PA rate&quot; &mdash; the percentage of claims requiring prior authorization &mdash; in the same visual format as the CMS-required denial rate, without clear labeling distinguishing these metrics. These are mathematically different measures with different denominators. This distinction is particularly important when the disclosures are used for cross-payer comparison, benchmarking, or future analytic applications. A reader relying solely on the HTML disclosure page may not readily identify the CMS-required denial rate without consulting additional disclosure materials. This finding is documented as Infrastructure Finding IF-002 in a publicly available primary-source dataset I have published at https://github.com/sole-healthtech/cms-0057f-prior-auth.<br/><br/>CMS-0062-P proposes to extend the prior authorization transparency framework to drugs. I strongly support this expansion. However, I urge CMS to address disclosure quality standards alongside disclosure requirements. If impacted payers can satisfy the transparency requirement by publishing metrics that conflate different measures &mdash; as documented in the non-drug context &mdash; extending the framework to drugs will replicate the same structural problem at greater scale. I recommend that CMS specify in the final rule that publicly reported prior authorization metrics must include clear labeling distinguishing PA rate from denial rate, and that raw counts sufficient to verify reported rates must be included alongside percentage figures.
