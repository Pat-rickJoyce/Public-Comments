**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
This brief comment, submitted as "supporting documentation," highlights concerns about data integrity in automated prior authorization systems and proposes an "Audit-Proofing Protocol" for payers. The commenter emphasizes the gap between high approval rates (95%) and high overturn rates on appeal (80.7%), characterizing this as a "Speed-to-Error" trap. The document focuses on three areas: the 24-hour drug authorization mandate, audit-proofing protocols to address RADV overpayment risks, and the need for a "Clinical-Technical Handshake" bridging NCPDP SCRIPT and FHIR standards.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**
- Specific sections/provisions addressed: CMS-0062-P (24-hour urgent drug authorizations, 72-hour standard drug authorizations); proposed rule published April 10, 2026
- Regulatory citations mentioned: Docket CMS-2026-1255; comment period closes June 15, 2026; March 31 Reporting Cycle; CAA 2026 (Consolidated Appropriations Act) transparency requirements
- Standards referenced: NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011; FHIR PAS (Prior Authorization Support); FHIR Observation resources; RTPB v13; NCPDP XML; JSON; LOINC codes implied (A1C, GFR lab values)

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

**Topic: Automated Decision Engine Accuracy**
- Position: Opposed / Critical
- Rationale: 80.7% of appealed denials are overturned, proving automated decision engines are failing. Describes fast denials as "administrative errors waiting to be exposed." Characterizes payer claims of 95% approval rates as misrepresenting systemic data integrity failures.
- Specific request: Implied need for better data integrity and clinical provenance verification before automated decisions.

**Topic: 24-Hour Drug Authorization Mandate**
- Position: Support with Technical Concerns
- Rationale: Acknowledges mandate for 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard drug authorizations. Success requires bridging NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011 and FHIR PAS standards.
- Specific request: Focus on "Clinical-Technical Handshake" and "Decision-Ready" standard before 24-hour clock commences.

**Topic: Audit-Proofing Protocol**
- Position: Strongly Advocate for Implementation
- Rationale: Following Elevance's $935 million accrual (April 22, 2026), audit-proofing is "no longer optional." Every automated approval must be backed by clinical provenance.
- Specific request: Implementation of pre-submission verification protocols, particularly for RADV overpayment risks.

**Topic: RADV Overpayment Prevention**
- Position: Support Technical Solution
- Rationale: RADV (Risk Adjustment Data Validation) audits pose significant financial risk to payers (evidenced by Elevance's $935M accrual).
- Specific request: Pre-submission verification of Lab Provenance (A1C, GFR) via FHIR Observation resources.

**Topic: PBM Transparency (CAA 2026)**
- Position: Support Transparency Requirements
- Rationale: CAA 2026 requires elimination of hidden PBM "Spread Pricing."
- Specific request: Use RTPB v13 and v2023011 standards to ensure transparency.

**Topic: NCPDP to FHIR Interoperability ("Handshake Crisis")**
- Position: Concerned - Requires Technical Solution
- Rationale: Gap exists between NCPDP XML payloads (pharmacy systems) and JSON medical records (clinical systems). Refers to this as "Handshake Crisis."
- Specific request: Mapping NCPDP XML payloads to JSON medical records to ensure "bilingual consistency."

**Topic: Clinical-Technical Handshake**
- Position: Advocate for Standard
- Rationale: Need for "Decision-Ready" standard before 24-hour authorization clock commences.
- Specific request: Establish clear standards for when data is sufficiently complete and structured for automated decision-making to begin.

**5. RECOMMENDATIONS**
1. Implement "Audit-Proofing Protocol" to ensure automated approvals are backed by clinical provenance
2. Establish pre-submission verification of lab provenance (A1C, GFR) via FHIR Observation resources for RADV compliance
3. Use RTPB v13 and v2023011 standards to eliminate hidden PBM spread pricing
4. Create mapping protocols between NCPDP XML payloads and JSON medical records for "bilingual consistency"
5. Define "Decision-Ready" standard to clarify when 24-hour authorization clock commences
6. Bridge NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011 and FHIR PAS standards for drug authorization success
7. Address "Speed-to-Error" trap by improving data integrity before automation
8. Implement "Clinical-Technical Handshake" to ensure data quality before automated decision-making
