# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Infusion Access Foundation; signed by Alicia Barron, LGSW, Executive Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Advocates for patients with complex, chronic, and rare conditions who rely on specialty medications, clinician-administered infusions, specialty injections, and home infusion or infusion-center care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Infusion Access Foundation supports CMS and ONC efforts to modernize prior authorization for drugs and improve electronic data exchange, especially for patients needing infusion and injection therapies. It urges CMS to ensure electronic prior authorization reduces treatment delays rather than digitizing existing barriers, with clear coverage/documentation criteria, durable approvals, continuity protections, specific denial reasons, public metrics, timely decisions, and step therapy safeguards.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED by implication through the Prior Authorization API; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS/GENERAL public prior authorization metrics; GENERAL/RFI step therapy and continuity of therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P. No CFR sections are cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API and electronic data exchange generally; no specific Da Vinci IG, NCPDP, HL7, or HIPAA standard is named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Incorporating drugs into the Prior Authorization API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients using infused and injected medications face complex and repetitive PA requirements that can delay initiation or continuation of treatment. Standard electronic workflows could reduce paperwork and improve communication.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to include drug coverage and documentation requirements in the Prior Authorization API and design workflows to improve access, not merely digitize existing barriers.

**Topic 2: Clear coverage and documentation requirements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients and providers need predictable information about coverage criteria, required documentation, approval duration, and reauthorization requirements.
- **Specific request:** Ensure electronic PA tools clearly present coverage criteria, required documentation, approval duration, and reauthorization requirements.

**Topic 3: Continuity of therapy and durable approvals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Chronic disease management often requires continuing therapy and clinically appropriate dose, frequency, or schedule adjustments; new authorization demands for each adjustment may disrupt care.
- **Specific request:** Require approvals to remain valid for the approved course of treatment when clinically appropriate and avoid new authorization requirements for reasonable adjustments within an ongoing treatment plan.

**Topic 4: Denial reasons and transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Vague denial notices impede correction, additional documentation, appeals, and communication with patients.
- **Specific request:** Require clear, specific denial reasons and hold payers accountable for providing them.

**Topic 5: Decision timeframes and enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** PA delays can disrupt planned infusion and injection schedules, causing postponed appointments, treatment gaps, and symptom worsening.
- **Specific request:** Adopt shorter and more consistent drug PA decision timeframes and enforce payer compliance.

**Topic 6: Public reporting of drug PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients, providers, and policymakers need visibility into how payer PA policies affect access to drugs for chronic and complex conditions.
- **Specific request:** Publicly report drug PA approvals, denials, appeals, overturns, delays, and where feasible drug-specific or therapy-category-specific data.

**Topic 7: Step therapy and plan transitions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify/Oppose disruptive use
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can force patients to fail insurer-preferred options and is particularly disruptive when stable patients change plans or experience administrative transitions.
- **Specific request:** Use interoperability tools so PA approvals, step therapy history, and treatment documentation follow the patient between plans; provide timely exceptions and appeals when step therapy is clinically inappropriate.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The comment supports incorporating medical-benefit drugs into electronic PA workflows but asks CMS to address chronic-disease realities, durable approvals, and documentation clarity.
- **S15:** The comment focuses on infused and injected therapies, reinforcing the need for PA processes that accommodate clinician-administered drugs and ongoing treatment plans.
- **S16:** The commenter supports requiring specific denial reasons and explains that vague notices prevent providers from correcting errors, adding documentation, or pursuing appeals.
- **S20:** The comment supports shorter, consistent drug PA timeframes because delays can interrupt infusion and injection schedules.
- **S21:** The commenter supports implementation of drug electronic PA but conditions success on workflows that actually reduce delays and administrative burden.
- **S29:** The commenter recommends reporting approval, denial, appeal, overturn, and delay metrics, with more granular drug-specific or therapy-category reporting where feasible.
- **S35:** The comment supports public reporting in a form useful to patients, providers, and policymakers.
- **S43:** The commenter supports consistent PA decision timeframes to prevent avoidable gaps in care.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to step therapy and continuity-of-therapy RFIs by urging CMS to prevent repeat step therapy or duplicative PA when stable patients change plans.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Incorporate drug coverage and documentation requirements into the Prior Authorization API for impacted payers.
2. Design electronic PA workflows to reduce administrative burden and treatment delays rather than digitizing existing barriers.
3. Require clear coverage criteria, required documentation, approval duration, and reauthorization requirements.
4. Ensure PA approvals remain valid for the approved course of treatment when clinically appropriate.
5. Avoid new PA requirements for reasonable dose, frequency, or schedule adjustments within ongoing treatment.
6. Require clear, specific denial reasons.
7. Adopt shorter and more consistent PA decision timeframes for drugs.
8. Enforce payer compliance with decision timeframes and denial-reason requirements.
9. Publicly report drug PA approvals, denials, appeals, overturns, and delays.
10. Consider drug-specific or therapy-category-specific public reporting where feasible.
11. Build interoperability processes that allow PA approvals, step therapy history, and treatment documentation to follow patients between plans.
12. Provide timely exceptions and appeals when step therapy is clinically inappropriate or threatens continuity of care.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Treatment delays, digitization of existing barriers, unclear criteria, excessive reauthorization, vague denials, weak enforcement, duplicative PA and step therapy after plan transitions.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Interrupted infusion/injection schedules, postponed appointments, treatment gaps, worsening symptoms, patient stress, confusion, and reduced quality of life.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Repetitive PA requirements, unclear documentation expectations, payer accountability gaps, plan transitions, and inability of current data exchange processes to carry authorization and treatment history.
