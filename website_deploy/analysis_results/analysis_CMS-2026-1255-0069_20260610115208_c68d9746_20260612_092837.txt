# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** NHMH - No Health without Mental Health, joined by multiple behavioral health, disability, addiction treatment, clinical social work, marriage and family therapy, and psychiatric nursing organizations.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group; Professional Association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Florence C. Fee, J.D., M.A.; other signatories include public policy directors, clinical social workers, legal counsel, and psychiatric mental health nursing leaders.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter broadly supports CMS interoperability modernization but focuses on medical-behavioral health data exchange rather than the rule's drug prior authorization mechanics. It recommends making interoperability a funded infrastructure requirement in CMS payment models, prioritizing complex behavioral and medical populations, strengthening FHIR/API-based behavioral health data exchange, and requiring EHR vendors to support unified physical and behavioral health records.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR Part 2; ONC HTI-1 Rule; 21st Century Cures Act information blocking provisions.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, APIs, HL7 DS4P Implementation Guide, ONC certification standards, HIEs, EHRs, PGHD, PROMs, PREMs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medical-behavioral interoperability as infrastructure**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Integrated care and value-based payment depend on shared longitudinal medical, behavioral, social, quality, and cost data.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require and prospectively fund interoperable medical-behavioral data systems as core infrastructure in APMs and integration models.

**Topic 2: FHIR/API standardization for integrated records**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The commenter views state-of-the-art FHIR standards and APIs as necessary to support integrated medical-behavioral EHRs, patient-generated health data, and outcome measurement.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ASTP/ONC should prioritize rules deploying FHIR data standards and APIs across integrated medical-behavioral records.

**Topic 3: Privacy segmentation and patient governance**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Behavioral health and SUD data require careful consent-based sharing that preserves privacy while supporting integrated care.
- **Specific request:** HHS should continue BHIDS work, use DS4P and 42 CFR Part 2 safeguards, and create an Integration Patient Advisory Council.

**Topic 4: Vendor support for unified data systems**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that some EHR vendors do not meet FHIR API interoperability expectations and that behavioral health functionality is often missing from certified systems.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ASTP/ONC should require vendors to support unified behavioral and physical health data or seamless interoperability, including behavioral health certification and stronger information blocking enforcement.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Indirectly supports requiring FHIR/API standards where needed for integrated records, but does not address specific Da Vinci IG mandate details.
- **S60-S65:** Indirectly supports ONC standards updates and certification changes to include behavioral health functionality and FHIR API interoperability.
- **S66-S82:** Provides general interoperability recommendations for behavioral health integration, complex populations, privacy segmentation, patient governance, and vendor obligations.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Add a rigorous evaluation component to the IBH model once implementation begins.
2. Treat interoperability as required, prospectively funded infrastructure in CMS payment models.
3. Prioritize unified data and interoperability for high-cost, high-need complex populations.
4. Deploy FHIR standards and APIs for integrated medical-behavioral records, PGHD, PROMs, and PREMs.
5. Establish an Integration Patient Advisory Council.
6. Continue building federal policy architecture for unified medical-behavioral payment and care delivery.
7. Require EHR vendors to support unified physical and behavioral health data or seamless interoperability.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Fragmented medical and behavioral data, inadequate vendor support, privacy and consent for sensitive behavioral/SUD information, and excessive automation without patient trust.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Continued fragmented accountability, duplicated infrastructure, misaligned incentives, unconnected data systems, and weakened patient-care team relationships.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** EHR vendor limitations, need for HIE integration, behavioral health certification gaps, sensitive data segmentation, and funding for HIT modernization.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** High-cost complex populations are described as 5% of the national patient population accounting for 50% of healthcare expenditures.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AHRQ/MEPS 2024; Samal 2016; Remers et al. 2022; Chang et al. 2023; Bipartisan Policy Center 2021; NASEM 2021; CMS Behavioral Health Strategy 2026; CMS dQM Strategic Roadmap 2024; Milliman 2018; Pew Research EHR Usability 2021-2023.
- **Examples provided:** IBH model, BHIDS pilot, HIE use, behavioral health EHR certification, DS4P, 42 CFR Part 2 safeguards.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with behavioral health and medical comorbidities, dually eligible beneficiaries, behavioral health providers, medical providers, EHR vendors, CMS model participants, and advocacy organizations.
- **Economic implications:** Prospective infrastructure funding could increase near-term federal/model investment but support TCOC accountability and value-based payment.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter sees FHIR/API approaches as feasible but dependent on vendor compliance, certification, and integration of behavioral health records.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved data sharing could support integrated care, reduce fragmentation, improve adherence and outcomes, and better serve high-need patients.
