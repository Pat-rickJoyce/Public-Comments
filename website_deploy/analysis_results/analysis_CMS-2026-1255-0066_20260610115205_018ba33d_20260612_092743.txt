# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Organization of Rheumatology Management (NORM); signed by Michelle A. Owen, CPC, President
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** NORM represents rheumatology practice administrators; contact Andrea Zlatkus, CMPM, CRMS, CRHC; signer Michelle A. Owen, CPC.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NORM supports CMS and ONC efforts to improve electronic drug prior authorization, denial transparency, API data exchange, and usage metrics, but argues the proposal does not sufficiently coordinate drugs that move between medical and pharmacy benefit structures. It requests more granular drug-level reporting, retention of formulary information in APIs, future 24-hour response standards for rheumatology therapies, and interoperability solutions that reduce duplicated step therapy and reauthorization requirements.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT issues within PA-DRUG-MEDICAL/PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL RFI on step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed
- **Standards referenced:** Patient Access API, Provider Access API, Payer-to-Payer API; generally references standards and implementation guides across medical and pharmacy benefits.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic drug prior authorization across benefit structures**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Drugs with infused, injected, and self-administered formulations require practices to query separate medical and pharmacy benefit platforms for coverage, documentation, PA, step therapy, and cost-sharing information.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should move toward stronger coordination across medical and pharmacy benefit systems rather than only streamlining each system separately.

**Topic 2: Denial reasons and prior authorization metrics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons and public metrics could help identify excessive denials, repeated authorization requirements, step therapy delays, and utilization management barriers.
- **Specific request:** Require more granular drug-level reporting of prior authorization and step therapy metrics by therapy or therapeutic category.

**Topic 3: Prior authorization decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Rheumatology practices face multiple payer response standards, and treatment delays can lead to irreversible joint damage, worsening disability, disease progression, and higher downstream costs.
- **Specific request:** Pursue future rulemaking to establish a 24-hour maximum response timeframe for rheumatology therapy prior authorizations across all impacted payers and benefit categories.

**Topic 4: API formulary information and usage metrics**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** NORM supports including drug PA information in Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs and reporting API usage metrics, but says formulary and utilization management data are needed during plan transitions and benefit shifts.
- **Specific request:** Retain formulary information within Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 5: Step therapy continuity and formulary adequacy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeated step therapy, reauthorization, and payer-specific utilization management policies disrupt continuity for clinically stable rheumatology patients.
- **Specific request:** Explore interoperability solutions that recognize prior determinations and treatment history, reduce duplicative requirements, and develop a formulary adequacy standard for preferred or step-therapy drugs.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** NORM identifies technical and operational challenges for drugs that can shift between medical and pharmacy benefits, including the need to query separate platforms.
- **S15:** NORM directly addresses confusion between medical and pharmacy benefits for multi-formulation rheumatology therapies and requests cross-benefit coordination.
- **S16:** NORM supports requirements to provide specific denial reasons because they would help practices identify utilization management barriers.
- **S20:** NORM supports alignment of decision timeframes and seeks a future 24-hour maximum response standard for rheumatology therapies.
- **S29:** NORM supports API usage metrics and asks for more granular drug-level reporting of PA and step therapy metrics.
- **S30:** Not directly addressed; no privacy-specific metric safeguards are discussed.
- **S43:** NORM supports aligning prior authorization timeframes across impacted payers and benefit categories.
- **S66-S82:** The step therapy RFI response asks CMS and ONC to reduce repeated step therapy, recognize prior determinations and treatment history, and consider formulary adequacy.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Coordinate medical and pharmacy benefit prior authorization systems for multi-formulation drugs.
2. Retain formulary and utilization management information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
3. Require drug-level reporting of prior authorization and step therapy metrics.
4. Consider future rulemaking for a 24-hour maximum PA response timeframe for rheumatology therapies.
5. Develop interoperability approaches that recognize prior payer determinations and treatment history.
6. Explore a formulary adequacy standard for preferred or step-therapy drugs.
7. Ultimately reduce or eliminate step therapy requirements that delay physician-recommended treatment.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Lack of cross-benefit coordination, administrative burden, delayed therapy initiation, repeated step therapy, and removal of formulary data from APIs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, disrupted continuity of care, patient confusion, irreversible joint damage, worsening disability, avoidable pain, disease progression, and increased downstream healthcare costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Disparate payer benefit platforms, different response standards, separate medical and pharmacy benefit tools, and repeated utilization management requirements.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Rheumatology practices, practice administrators, patients with chronic rheumatologic diseases, impacted payers, CMS/ONC, and vendors supporting API and utilization management workflows.

- **Technical feasibility:** NORM views the proposed within-benefit improvements as positive but insufficient; it implies additional standards and implementation guides are needed to coordinate across benefit structures.
- **Patient care impact:** The comment emphasizes timely access to specialty therapies, continuity of care, and avoidance of disease progression and disability.

The National Organization of Rheumatology Management (NORM) represents rheumatology practice administrators who manage the day-to-day operations of rheumatology practices across the country. On behalf of our members, we appreciate the opportunity to provide feedback on the aforementioned proposed rule.
