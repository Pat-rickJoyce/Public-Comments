# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Bleeding Disorders Foundation (NBDF) and Hemophilia Alliance (HA)
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** NBDF represents individuals with inheritable bleeding disorders; HA represents federally recognized hemophilia treatment centers; signed by Nathan Schaefer, NBDF Sr. VP for Public Policy & Access, and Jeff Blake, HA President and CEO.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NBDF and HA support finalizing the proposed rule because efficient drug PA is critical for patients with bleeding disorders who require uninterrupted access to high-cost, life-preserving therapies. They ask CMS to strengthen payer accountability through denial reasons, metrics, appeals standardization, expansion of PA standards to labs and DMEPOS, substantive step therapy guardrails, and additional interagency rulemaking to address alternative funding program abuses.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFIs for lab tests, DMEPOS, step therapy, and payer practices beyond the rule.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 89 Fed. Reg. 8758 (Feb. 8, 2024); NPRM 19942; Safe Step Act references S. 2903/H.R. 5509.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; Access APIs; prior authorization metrics; no specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for All Drugs Requiring PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Bleeding disorder patients need uninterrupted access to infused clotting factors and injectable factor mimetics, and repeated or opaque PA can cause avoidable bleeding and advanced medical issues.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to support electronic PA for all drugs requiring PA, whether covered under the medical or pharmacy benefit.

**Topic 2: Timeframes, Denial Reasons, and Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Defined decision timeframes, denial reasons, and approval/denial reporting would reduce delays, improve accountability, and support patient plan choices.
- **Specific request:** Finalize decision timeframes, denial-reason requirements, and public reporting of metrics, including specialty and high-cost drugs.

**Topic 3: Appeals Process Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients and providers face generic denials, opaque paper or fax-based appeals, reviewers without relevant expertise, and other obstacles when drugs are excluded or denied.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to improve and standardize appeals processes for medically necessary treatments.

**Topic 4: Extend PA Standards to Lab Tests and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Bleeding disorder care depends on specialized lab tests, inhibitor testing, infusion supplies, port supplies, orthotics, and mobility supports, often on expedited timelines.
- **Specific request:** Extend PA standardization requirements to lab tests and DMEPOS.

**Topic 5: Step Therapy Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can be dangerous for bleeding disorder patients because treatment failure can cause serious or irreversible harm and patients may remain stable on the same biologic therapy for years.
- **Specific request:** Conduct separate rulemaking to require evidence-based protocols, standardized electronic exception processes, defined exceptions, and continuity for stable patients who change plans.

**Topic 6: Alternative Funding Program PA Abuses**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** The commenters state that AFPs manipulate PA processes to force predetermined denials and shift patients toward manufacturer assistance programs, causing delays and gaps in treatment.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with sister agencies to end AFP abuses of drug PA, including outside CMS's direct jurisdiction.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The comment supports electronic PA for medical-benefit drugs because uninterrupted access to physician-administered or specialty therapies is critical for bleeding disorder patients.
- **S14:** The comment supports electronic PA for all drugs requiring PA, including drugs covered under the pharmacy benefit.
- **S16:** NBDF and HA support requiring denial reasons and ask CMS to further standardize appeals processes.
- **S20:** The comment supports defined and reasonable timeframes for drug PA decisions, given the risk of bleeding episodes from delayed treatment.
- **S29:** The commenters support reporting PA metrics, including approval and denial rates and drugs subject to PA.
- **S35:** They support public reporting on payer websites, especially for specialty and high-cost drugs, to improve accountability and inform plan choice.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to broader RFI issues by urging CMS to extend PA standardization to labs and DMEPOS, adopt step therapy guardrails, and pursue interagency action on alternative funding programs.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize the proposed rule extending PA standards to prescription drugs.
2. Require electronic PA for all drugs requiring PA, regardless of medical or pharmacy benefit coverage.
3. Require defined PA decision timeframes, denial reasons, and public reporting of approval and denial metrics.
4. Ensure specialty and high-cost drugs are included in public reporting.
5. Standardize appeals processes, including electronic and time-limited procedures.
6. Extend PA standardization to laboratory tests and DMEPOS.
7. Adopt substantive step therapy guardrails through separate rulemaking.
8. Protect stable patients from repeating step therapy when they change plans or payers.
9. Coordinate with other federal agencies to address alternative funding program manipulation of PA.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Treatment delays, opaque PA and appeals, denied specialty drugs, step therapy harm, lab and DMEPOS access barriers, AFP manipulation of PA.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Avoidable bleeding episodes, permanent disability, death, gaps in treatment, ER and clinic visits, loss of intended PAP resources, patient costs and time burdens.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Paper/fax appeals, generic denials, reviewers lacking expertise, plan exclusions, AFP arrangements, payer-specific step therapy protocols.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Hemophilia affects about 30,000 Americans; von Willebrand disease affects more than three million Americans; up to 96% of total health care costs for severe hemophilia A patients may be medication costs; AFP-related hemophilia medication access delays average 83.7 days.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Chen et al., Journal of Managed Care & Specialty Pharmacy 2023; NBDF MASAC Document 236; Wong et al., Journal of Managed Care & Specialty Pharmacy 2024.
- **Examples provided:** Inhibitor testing; specialized bloodwork; infusion and port supplies; crutches, walkers, wheelchairs; AFP predetermined PA denials and manufacturer PAP redirection.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with hemophilia and other bleeding disorders, HTCs, specialty providers, payers, employer plans, manufacturers, PAPs, DMEPOS suppliers, labs, CMS and sister agencies.
- **Economic implications:** High medication costs make therapies frequent PA targets; PA delays can drive ER visits, clinic visits, extra DME costs, and inappropriate use of charitable PAP resources.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment supports electronic PA and Access APIs but focuses more on process standardization and accountability than implementation specifications.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays or denials can cause bleeding episodes, irreversible harm, disability, or death; uninterrupted therapy is central to patient safety.
