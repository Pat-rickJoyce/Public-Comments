# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Genentech, Inc.; signed by Dan Neves, Senior Director, U.S. Policy and Evidence.
- **Stakeholder type:** Other / Manufacturer
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Genentech describes itself as the original biotech company with 50 years of history, more than 40 approved U.S. medicines, 66 new molecules in clinical development, and 43 FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designations. It incorporates by reference PhRMA's broader comments and focuses this letter on the step therapy RFI.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Genentech focuses on step therapy and argues that opaque, inflexible utilization management increasingly delays access to clinically appropriate drugs, worsens disparities, and imposes administrative costs. It recommends public step therapy metrics, a ban on embedded step therapy disguised as PA, therapeutic-area disaggregation, active use of Payer-to-Payer API history, recognition of prior step therapy determinations, clear exceptions, a 365-day look-back period, prohibition of off-label step therapy, evidence-based criteria, and human peer review for AI-driven denials.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** API-USAGE-METRICS; Payer-to-Payer API aspects of FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL/RFI on step therapy; PA-DENIAL-REASONS/PA reform indirectly; PATIENT-ACCESS and TRANSPARENCY themes.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 Fed. Reg. 19890; RIN 0938-AV44; CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** Payer-to-Payer API; artificial intelligence/algorithms in claims management. No specific FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, or CFR sections are named in the substantive recommendations.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Public step therapy metrics and embedded step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Plans may hide treatment-sequence mandates inside standard PA criteria, making formularies opaque and blocking access to therapies.
- **Specific request:** Require public reporting of step therapy metrics with the rigor proposed for PA metrics and categorically prohibit embedded step therapy protocols within ordinary PA criteria.

**Topic 2: Therapeutic-area disaggregation of utilization management data**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Aggregate reporting can obscure localized inequities and disease-specific access problems.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to disaggregate utilization management data by specific therapeutic areas.

**Topic 3: Payer-to-Payer API use for continuity of care**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Passive availability of API-transmitted history is insufficient if payers do not ingest and apply it to identify therapies already tried and failed.
- **Specific request:** Require payers across public and commercial markets to actively ingest and apply Payer-to-Payer API data to programmatically identify prior treatment failures and auto-grant corresponding authorizations.

**Topic 4: Honor historical step therapy determinations**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Forcing a stable patient to repeat a failed or intolerable drug after changing plans undermines patient safety and can cause irreversible harm.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to honor step therapy determinations completed under a prior health plan.

**Topic 5: Step therapy exception standards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Exceptions are needed when a protocol is clinically inappropriate, harmful, or disruptive to stability and functioning.
- **Specific request:** Require exceptions when the patient already tried and failed the protocol or it is contraindicated; when the protocol is expected to be ineffective and delay would cause severe or irreversible consequences; when it prevents daily or occupational functioning; or when the patient is stable on current medication.

**Topic 6: Look-back period for “new start” status**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A short look-back period can cause stabilized patients to be treated as new starts and forced into repeat step therapy.
- **Specific request:** Extend the Part D look-back period from 108 days to 365 days and apply it across all segments, including commercial plans.

**Topic 7: Prohibition on off-label step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Oppose off-label step therapy
- **Rationale:** Plans should not require patients to try unapproved alternatives when an FDA-approved on-label medicine is prescribed.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit off-label step therapy.

**Topic 8: Evidence-based coverage criteria**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Coverage criteria should be clinically grounded rather than driven by internal pharmacy committee financial directives.
- **Specific request:** Anchor criteria in consensus-based, peer-reviewed clinical guidelines.

**Topic 9: AI-driven claims management**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Algorithmic systems may generate high-volume denials without adequate clinical oversight.
- **Specific request:** Require regulatory scrutiny of AI claims management; permit AI/algorithms only to approve care or predict clinical pathways, and require meaningful documented human clinical review by a peer specialist for any denial.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S29/S35:** Recommends public reporting of step therapy metrics and disaggregation by therapeutic area, analogous to PA transparency requirements.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the step therapy RFI extensively, including transparency, continuity of care, exceptions, look-back periods, clinical guidelines, off-label step therapy, and AI-driven denials.
- **S53/S54/S55 (indirect):** Although framed as step therapy, the request for active Payer-to-Payer API use and human review of AI-driven denials implicates implementation and enforcement mechanisms for interoperable payer data exchange.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require public reporting of step therapy metrics.
2. Prohibit embedding step therapy protocols within standard prior authorization criteria.
3. Require utilization management data to be disaggregated by therapeutic area.
4. Require payers to actively ingest and apply Payer-to-Payer API data to identify prior failed therapies.
5. Honor historical step therapy determinations from preceding health plans.
6. Grant step therapy exceptions when prior failure, contraindication, expected ineffectiveness, severe or irreversible harm, impaired daily/occupational function, or patient stability is present.
7. Extend the Part D “new start” look-back period from 108 days to 365 days and apply it to all segments, including commercial plans.
8. Prohibit off-label step therapy.
9. Anchor coverage criteria in consensus-based, peer-reviewed clinical guidelines.
10. Scrutinize AI-driven claims management and require meaningful documented human peer-specialist review before any denial.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Opaque step therapy, embedded restrictions disguised as PA, failure to honor clinical history across payers, off-label step therapy, non-evidence-based criteria, and AI-generated denials without clinical review.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, clinical deterioration, rapid disease progression, health inequities, patient financial exposure, benefit abandonment, clinician burden, burnout, and administrative costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Passive rather than active use of Payer-to-Payer API data, lack of public step therapy reporting, formulary opacity, coverage criteria extending beyond FDA labels, and algorithmic denial systems.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Up to 56% of Part D brand drugs are subject to utilization controls; commercial insurers deny initial coverage for three out of four patients across key chronic disease states; treatment gaps average three to five weeks; some patients must cycle through up to ten alternative therapies; 94% of MA providers report step therapy restricts access to preferred physician-administered Part B treatments; 56% of Black Americans and 60% of Hispanic Americans experience UM restrictions versus 36% of white Americans; 93% of physicians report care delays and 89% report burnout; estimated $93 billion annual ecosystem cost to navigate and contest UM; Part D sponsors required PA for certain breast cancer treatments 97% of the time and embedded step therapy in up to 88% of those cases.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Avalere analyses on Part D and MA step therapy; IQVIA reports; Rucker et al. Health Affairs 2025; PhRMA patient experience data; AMA prior authorization surveys; Howell et al. Health Affairs 2021; American Cancer Society Cancer Action Network analysis; National Pharmaceutical Council roundtable / Karmarkar et al. JMCP 2021.
- **Examples provided:** Breast cancer treatments, commercial chronic disease therapies, preferred physician-administered Part B treatments, severe/progressive/autoimmune conditions, FDA-approved on-label treatments, and AI/algorithmic PA denials.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients using complex medicines, oncology and chronic disease patients, Medicare Part D and MA beneficiaries, commercial plan members, prescribers, payers, manufacturers, and marginalized populations.
- **Economic implications:** Genentech argues current UM imposes large administrative costs and can shift costs to patients when coverage barriers force out-of-pocket payment.
- **Technical feasibility:** Depends on payers actively ingesting and using Payer-to-Payer API data rather than merely making it available; also depends on enforceable reporting and AI oversight.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the letter frames step therapy as causing delayed appropriate treatment, disease progression, and inequitable access.

- **Tone:** Critical, evidence-driven, policy-focused
