**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
Commenter recommends CMS expand electronic event notification requirements to include non-transport emergency medical services (EMS) encounters where patients are evaluated by 9-1-1 responders but not transported to a hospital. Nearly 10 million such encounters occur annually (17.3% of all EMS encounters) and are completely invisible to primary care providers and care teams, creating a critical care gap for a high-risk population that experiences significant adverse outcomes without follow-up intervention.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**
- Specific sections/provisions addressed: CMS-0062-P Request for Information on Electronic Event Notifications for Value-Based Care and Care Coordination (Section III.A)
- Regulatory citations mentioned: 42 CFR 482.24(d) (current electronic event notification framework for hospital-based events)
- Standards referenced: FHIR-based event notification frameworks, health information exchange (HIE) infrastructure, EMS electronic patient care record (ePCR) to EHR interfaces, National EMS Information System (NEMSIS)

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

Topic: Electronic Event Notifications for Non-Transport EMS Encounters
- Position: Add New Requirement
- Rationale: Current 42 CFR 482.24(d) framework only captures hospital-based events (ED registration, inpatient admission, discharge), missing nearly 10 million annual non-transport EMS encounters; these patients are clinically vulnerable with high risk of deterioration, repeat utilization, and death; encounters are not billable so don't appear in claims data; primary care providers and care teams have zero awareness of these acute events
- Specific request: Establish requirement for EMS organizations to send electronic event notifications when 9-1-1 encounter does not result in transport to emergency department

Topic: Implementation Approach
- Position: Support Multiple Pathways
- Rationale: EMS agency is only party with knowledge of event; without requirement on EMS agency, care team has no way of knowing event occurred
- Specific request: Implement through existing HIE infrastructure, direct EMS ePCR-to-EHR interfaces, or FHIR-based event notification frameworks

**5. RECOMMENDATIONS**
1. Establish requirement for EMS organizations to send electronic event notifications when a 9-1-1 encounter does not result in transport to an emergency department
2. Follow same logic as 42 CFR 482.24(d), placing notification obligation on the entity that holds the encounter data (EMS agency)
3. Implement through existing HIE infrastructure, direct EMS ePCR-to-EHR interfaces, or FHIR-based event notification frameworks
4. Close critical care gap for nearly 10 million patients annually through low-cost, high-impact intervention

**6. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS**

Primary Concerns:
- Non-transport EMS encounters are completely invisible to providers managing chronic and longitudinal care
- 9,974,179 annual non-transport encounters (17.3% of all EMS encounters) represent patients with acute needs serious enough to prompt 9-1-1 call
- These encounters are not billable and do not appear in claims data
- Primary care providers, care coordinators, geriatricians, and care team members have zero awareness of these acute events
- Encounter data remains siloed in EMS ePCR and never reaches patient's longitudinal medical record
- Patients evaluated only by EMT/paramedic who cannot provide diagnosis or definitive treatment
- More serious than ED discharge without PCP notification because patient hasn't been evaluated by physician capable of diagnosing, ruling out life threats, or providing definitive treatment
- Patient left in community with undiagnosed, untreated condition without PCP knowledge

Potential Negative Impacts (if not addressed):
- One third of geriatric patients declining transport after fall-related EMS call require repeat 9-1-1 response within 30 days
- 71% of repeat calls result in ED transport
- 66% of transported patients require hospitalization
- Nearly half of all non-transported fall patients require healthcare within two weeks
- Patients often have serious underlying conditions (infection, cancer, neurological disease) manifesting as falls
- Without notification, patients remain at high risk of deterioration, repeat utilization, and death

Implementation Barriers:
- Current framework limited to hospital-based events
- EMS ePCR systems may need integration with HIE or EHR systems
- No existing requirement for EMS agencies to transmit event data

The Care Gap: Non-Transport EMS Encounters Are Invisible to the Health System
I am writing in response to the Request for Information on Electronic Event Notifications for
Value-Based Care and Care Coordination published in CMS-0062-P. I appreciate the opportunity
to comment and respectfully recommend that CMS expand the scope of electronic event
notification requirements to include non-transport emergency medical services (EMS)
encounters, specifically 9-1-1 responses where a patient is evaluated by EMS but not transported
to an emergency department.
The current electronic event notification framework under 42 CFR 482.24(d) is limited to
hospital-based events such as ED registration, inpatient admission, and discharge. This
framework does not capture EMS 9-1-1 encounters that end without transport to a hospital.
According to the National EMS Information System (NEMSIS) 2024 national report, there were
9,974,179 responses to 9-1-1 calls with patient contact where EMS returned to service without
transporting the patient, representing 17.3% of all EMS encounters. These are patients with acute
needs serious enough to prompt a 9-1-1 call, yet the event is completely invisible to the providers
managing their chronic and longitudinal care. Because these encounters are not billable, they
also do not appear in claims data. The result is that primary care providers, care coordinators,
geriatricians, and other care team members have zero awareness that their patient experienced an
acute event in the community.
Re: CMS-0062-P: Request for Information on Electronic Event Notifications for Value-Based

Clinical Evidence: Non-Transport Patients Face Significant Risk of Adverse Outcomes
Why Notification to the Primary Care Team Is Essential
The published literature demonstrates that non-transported EMS patients, particularly older
adults evaluated after a fall, are a clinically vulnerable population at high risk of deterioration,
repeat utilization, and death. The following evidence supports the urgency of closing this
notification gap:
Approximately one third of geriatric patients who decline transport after a fall-related EMS call
require a repeat 9-1-1 response within 30 days. Of those repeat calls, 71% result in transport to
the ED, and 66% of those transported require hospitalization (Moore et al., 2025). Nearly half of
all non-transported fall patients required healthcare within two weeks of the initial event (Paul et
al., 2016). Patients who are hospitalized after a recent non-transport fall encounter are frequently
diagnosed with serious underlying conditions such as infection, cancer, or neurological disease,
indicating that falls in this population are often a symptom of broader clinical deterioration
(Leggatt et al., 2017).
