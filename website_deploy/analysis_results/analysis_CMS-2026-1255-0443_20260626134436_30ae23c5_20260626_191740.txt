## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Artificer Health supports CMS-0062-P and specifically endorses mandatory CRD, DTR, PAS, CDex, drug prior authorization requirements, and the October 1, 2027 compliance target. The comment asks CMS to strengthen the final rule by preserving treating-clinician medical-legal authority, requiring payer API conformance testing with escalation triggers, routing step therapy determinations through Payer-to-Payer exchange with safeguards, requiring patient-and-drug-level benefit routing signals, phasing in laboratory and DMEPOS prior authorization reform, and aligning ONC/EHR certification with payer timelines.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act; HIPAA Administrative Simplification provisions. No specific CFR section is cited.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR Da Vinci CRD 2.2.1, DTR 2.2.0, PAS 2.2.1, CDex, PDex; US Core; X12N 278; FHIR R4 CapabilityStatement; FHIR DSTU2 Conformance resource; ONC Inferno; ONC Lantern Payer FHIR endpoint catalog; Payer-to-Payer API; DTR questionnaire response; C-CDA; PDF and text attachments; CRD CoverageInformation extension; NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP RTPB; NCPDP F&B; Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG; TEFCA.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Mandatory Adoption of CRD, DTR, PAS, and CDex**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that voluntary Da Vinci IG status has produced uneven payer adoption and shifted payer variation into clinician-side integration cost, deployment time, and workflow burden. It supports mandatory CRD, DTR, PAS, and CDex because fallbacks to manual lookup, payer portals, fax, and X12N 278 preserve burden.
- **Specific request:** Finalize mandatory CRD 2.2.1, DTR 2.2.0, PAS 2.2.1, and CDex; resist weakening mandates, delaying beyond October 1, 2027, or carving out payer subsets.

**Topic 2: Treating Clinician Medical-Legal Authority**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Artificer Health argues prior authorization is a medical-legal act and that automation should not remove the treating clinician's responsibility for clinical determinations submitted on the clinician's behalf.
- **Specific request:** Clarify in the final rule preamble and, where appropriate, regulatory text that nothing in CRD, DTR, PAS, or CDex diminishes the treating clinician's medical-legal responsibility; avoid testing incentives for touchless autonomous submission.

**Topic 3: Payer API Testing and Certification**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter presents endpoint scan results showing significant variation and no full Da Vinci PAS conformance among 134 publicly discoverable endpoints, arguing that voluntary adoption and one-time attestation are insufficient.
- **Specific request:** Adopt Tier 1 mandatory Inferno conformance testing for all impacted payers, publish scores, support Tier 2 sandbox environments with realistic production behavior, and trigger Tier 3 ONC certification only after at least 12 months of data showing persistent non-compliance.

**Topic 4: Step Therapy Determinations Through Payer-to-Payer Exchange**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with safeguards
- **Rationale:** The commenter says redundant step therapy after plan changes causes preventable adverse events, treatment delays, and care discontinuation, especially in eating disorders, pediatric psychiatry, and obesity medicine.
- **Specific request:** Adopt Payer-to-Payer routing of step therapy determinations; require transferred data to include authorization dates, documented regimen, clinical justification, patient response markers, and prescriber identity/credentials; require receiving payers to document a patient-level clinical change before re-imposing step therapy.

**Topic 5: Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with phased implementation
- **Rationale:** Artificer Health says specialty lab and DMEPOS prior authorization were excluded from the 2024 final rule and now create disproportionate clinician burden in oncology, rheumatology, endocrinology, orthopedics, pain management, post-surgical recovery, and home health.
- **Specific request:** Phase expansion with specialty laboratory tests in year 1 and DMEPOS in year 2, to reduce clinician burden without overwhelming payer or clinician implementation capacity.

**Topic 6: Medical-Benefit vs Pharmacy-Benefit Drug Routing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports the dual-track architecture but says the unresolved problem is determining, at the point of order, whether a specific patient-drug combination belongs under medical benefit, pharmacy benefit, or both. Existing CRD, RTPB, and F&B standards were not designed to supply this routing signal.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers by October 1, 2027 to publish a machine-readable patient-and-drug-level benefit-determination signal retrievable by clinician-side tools at order time, indicating benefit path, split coverage scenarios, and conditions determining routing.

**Topic 7: Event Notifications and Resiliency RFIs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Artificer Health supports expanding ADT/event notification infrastructure for care continuity, especially in behavioral health level-of-care transitions, and says interoperability infrastructure should degrade gracefully during outages and service disruptions.
- **Specific request:** Explore TEFCA integration and Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications as candidate paths; design and test FHIR-based infrastructure for graceful degradation.

**Topic 8: Compliance Timeline and EHR Coordination**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE-related GENERAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with interim milestones
- **Rationale:** The commenter says October 1, 2027 gives sufficient runway while maintaining urgency, but large payers can move faster and EHR vendors may be unevenly prepared.
- **Specific request:** Maintain October 1, 2027 as the end state, consider earlier interim milestones for the top 25 payers by enrollment, and coordinate with ONC so EHR certification updates align with payer API timelines.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize mandatory CRD 2.2.1, DTR 2.2.0, PAS 2.2.1, and CDex.
2. Do not delay mandatory IG requirements beyond October 1, 2027 or carve out payer subsets.
3. Clarify that the rule does not diminish treating-clinician medical-legal responsibility for prior authorization submissions.
4. Avoid incentives for touchless autonomous prior authorization submission without clinician review.
5. Adopt Tier 1 mandatory Inferno conformance testing for all impacted payers and publish scores.
6. Permit Tier 2 shared sandbox infrastructure for smaller payers and require sandbox behavior to reflect production behavior, with at least quarterly refreshes.
7. Trigger Tier 3 ONC certification only after persistent measured non-compliance, using score thresholds and complaint patterns.
8. Adopt Payer-to-Payer routing of step therapy determinations with required clinical data elements.
9. Require receiving payers to document a specific patient-level clinical change before re-imposing step therapy after a transferred determination.
10. Phase prior authorization reform into specialty lab testing first, then DMEPOS.
11. Require patient-and-drug-level machine-readable benefit-determination signals by October 1, 2027.
12. Consult Da Vinci Project members and clinician-side tool developers on the resource and field structure for benefit routing.
13. Explore TEFCA and Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications for event notifications.
14. Design FHIR infrastructure for graceful degradation during outages and third-party disruptions.
15. Consider earlier interim milestones for the top 25 payers by enrollment and align ONC EHR certification updates with payer timelines.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Payer-side variation; voluntary IG adoption; clinician workflow burden; autonomous submissions undermining clinician authority; insufficient payer API conformance; redundant step therapy after plan transitions; unresolved medical/pharmacy benefit routing; EHR readiness gaps.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Lost clinical time, integration cost, delayed deployment, patient safety risks, liability uncertainty, reduced clinician trust, preventable adverse events, care discontinuation, pediatric psychiatric deterioration, eating disorder care delays, and obesity-related clinical setbacks.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of full Da Vinci PAS endpoint conformance, unreachable/erroring payer endpoints, DSTU2/R4 inconsistency, small payer sandbox capacity, poor sandbox-production fidelity, unclear benefit routing, weak EHR certification alignment, and added complexity from lab/DMEPOS expansion.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA 2023 Prior Authorization Physician Survey estimates 13 hours per clinician per week lost to PA burden. Artificer Health's June 12, 2026 scan probed 134 publicly discoverable payer-side FHIR endpoints: 73 returned a capability statement, 33 returned an error response, 28 were unreachable, zero achieved full Da Vinci PAS conformance, 35 declared partial Da Vinci profiles without CRD/DTR/PAS, 38 exposed only baseline FHIR resources, and 29 endpoints intermittently returned DSTU2-era Conformance resources rather than R4 CapabilityStatement resources.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA 2023 Prior Authorization Physician Survey; Academy for Eating Disorders position on anorexia nervosa as relapsing-remitting.
- **Examples provided:** Manual coverage-rule lookup when CRD is absent; hand-mapping documentation when DTR is absent; portal/fax/X12N 278 fallback when PAS is absent; pediatric eating disorder level-of-care denials; pediatric psychiatry step therapy retrials; GLP-1 therapy disruption in pediatric obesity medicine; specialty lab and DMEPOS workflow examples.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Clinicians, clinician-side technology vendors, EHR vendors, payers, smaller and regional payers, patients undergoing prior authorization, pediatric behavioral health patients, eating disorder patients, obesity medicine patients, lab and DMEPOS users, CMS, ONC, and Da Vinci/standards developers.
- **Economic implications:** Mandatory IGs and testing may reduce clinician labor and vendor integration costs, while phased scope expansion and shared sandboxes may reduce implementation burden for smaller payers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS requires conformance testing, realistic sandboxes, defined escalation triggers, benefit routing signals, and ONC/EHR timeline alignment.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment ties prior authorization automation and step therapy routing to patient safety, continuity of care, treatment delays, and clinical deterioration.
