## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Altera Digital Health; signed by Leigh C. Burchell, Vice President, Policy & Government Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Provides clinical, business, and interoperability solutions across ambulatory, acute, and other settings; reports more than three decades deploying software to thousands of providers.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Altera generally supports CMS and ONC's interoperability and drug prior authorization proposals, especially standardization of required standards and IG versions across payers and health IT vendors. Its main concerns are timing, uneven certification requirements, payer endpoint discoverability, medical-versus-pharmacy workflow differences, and the need for standardized denial-reason placement and authentication approaches.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.215; 45 CFR 170.299; 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31); 42 CFR 422.120(a); 42 CFR 431.70(a); 42 CFR 438.210(c); 42 CFR 438.242; 42 CFR 457.760(a); 42 CFR 457.1233(d); 45 CFR 156.223(i)(1); 29 CFR 2560.503-1(m)(1)-(2).
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR; Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS, and PDex; CARIN IG for Blue Button; PDex US Drug Formulary; PDex Plan Net; US Core; USCDI v4; SMART App Launch; OpenID Connect Core; NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB; X12N 278.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Standardized IG versions and transition timing**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Altera says interoperability depends on all sending and receiving entities using the same standards and IG versions in the same timeframes. It supports expiration dates and transition periods but warns that the current timeline does not reflect development, certification, and deployment realities.
- **Specific request:** Keep transition periods, clarify the required US Core version, and push the PI measure date out one year while CMS and ONC coordinate industry readiness.

**Topic 2: Payer API certification**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The company argues that requiring EHRs to adopt standards while other exchange partners use proprietary approaches has increased cost, slowed exchange, and added user complexity.
- **Specific request:** ONC should issue certification criteria for payer API technology, and CMS should require payer certification without a voluntary-only component.

**Topic 3: Provider Directory API authentication**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Standardized authentication is needed for consistent payer API integration.
- **Specific request:** Finalize SMART App Launch and OpenID Connect Core for the Provider Directory API authentication method.

**Topic 4: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with clarification
- **Rationale:** Immediate denial reasons help providers know the next step, but the two electronic pathways for medical and pharmacy benefit drugs create implementation ambiguity.
- **Specific request:** Clarify where the denial reason must appear in each electronic response message for medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit drug PA.

**Topic 5: API endpoint publication**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Early and usable endpoint publication is necessary for health IT vendors to connect client databases to payer databases.
- **Specific request:** Publish payer endpoints through a FHIR API-enabled directory, update changes within one week, and verify published information quarterly rather than annually.

**Topic 6: Medical versus pharmacy benefit drug workflows**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** EHR prescription workflows differ from medication or vaccine administration workflows, and payer variation in benefit classification will add administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Do not require medical-benefit drug ePA on the same timeframe as prescription-benefit drugs; standardize benefit categorization quickly; consider the development and workflow impact on providers and vendors.

**Topic 7: HIPAA prior authorization standard**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Requiring X12N 278 inside the FHIR Prior Authorization API would be duplicative and burdensome.
- **Specific request:** Finalize replacement of X12N 278 by adopting the Prior Authorization API as the HIPAA standard for prior authorization-related transactions.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Altera supports requiring impacted payers to use unexpired versions of required and newly required IGs.
- **S2:** Altera requests clarification on the US Core version, noting that a 2028 version may require USCDI v4 before vendors are required to support it.
- **S3:** Altera supports adding expiration dates with transition periods rather than abrupt replacement, while warning that timing must align with vendor development and deployment.
- **S6:** Altera supports requiring unexpired CARIN IG, PDex, PDex US Drug Formulary, and Plan Net IGs for applicable APIs.
- **S12:** Altera says medical-benefit drug PA creates a large development and workflow effort because EHR medication ordering workflows differ from prescribing workflows.
- **S15:** Altera states that payer variation between prescription benefit and medical benefit coverage will create major interoperability challenges unless standardized.
- **S16:** Altera supports immediate denial reasons so providers know the next step.
- **S17:** Altera asks ONC to clarify where denial reasons appear in electronic response messages for each drug PA pathway.
- **S18:** Altera supports mandatory IG use but emphasizes synchronized obligations across payers and EHR vendors.
- **S20:** Altera supports the shortest feasible PA response timeframes because faster processing is part of the justification for ePA.
- **S21:** Altera questions whether the October 1, 2027 date is realistic for medical-benefit drug PA and recommends more time.
- **S22:** Altera describes burdens in aggregating F&B/RTPB data from multiple sources and updating client systems many times per year.
- **S23:** Altera recommends standardized authentication approaches, including SMART App Launch and OpenID Connect Core.
- **S24:** Altera supports collecting and publishing payer endpoint information needed for integration.
- **S25:** Altera supports unexpired NCPDP RTPB use but suggests CMS work with NCPDP toward a faster transition to FHIR.
- **S26:** Altera recommends a FHIR API-enabled directory format for endpoint publication.
- **S27:** Altera supports one-week updates for changes and recommends quarterly verification.
- **S33:** Altera says CMS should publish endpoint information through a FHIR-enabled directory usable by health IT vendors.
- **S37:** The comment identifies implementation challenges involving version transitions, certification asymmetry, vendor deployment timing, and benefit-category variation.
- **S43:** Altera supports the shortest feasible drug PA response timelines generally, though it seeks more implementation time for the new medical-benefit workflow.
- **S44:** Altera supports adopting the Prior Authorization API as the HIPAA standard for PA transactions.
- **S46:** Altera supports replacing X12N 278 rather than requiring duplicative X12 use within the FHIR API.
- **S47:** Altera's timeline concerns suggest CMS should align HIPAA/API deadlines with certification and deployment realities.
- **S60:** Altera supports ONC's versioning approach using unexpired adopted standards and expiration dates.
- **S61:** Altera repeatedly asks CMS and ONC to coordinate standards, certification, and compliance timelines.
- **S62:** Altera supports certification criteria for payer API technology and mandatory payer certification.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require all relevant sending and receiving entities to comply with the same standards and IG versions in the same timeframes.
2. Retain transition periods for updated standards and expiration dates.
3. Clarify the US Core implementation guide version and USCDI implications.
4. Push the PI measure date out one year and coordinate CMS/ONC timelines with vendor deployment.
5. Require ONC certification criteria for payer API technology, with CMS-mandated payer certification.
6. Finalize standardized authentication using SMART App Launch and OpenID Connect Core where applicable.
7. Clarify denial-reason placement in response messages for both medical and pharmacy benefit drug PA.
8. Publish payer endpoints early through a FHIR API-enabled directory, with one-week updates and quarterly verification.
9. Allow additional time for medical-benefit drug ePA and standardize benefit-category distinctions.
10. Work with NCPDP to accelerate movement toward FHIR-based approaches.
11. Finalize adoption of the Prior Authorization API as the HIPAA standard replacing X12N 278.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Misaligned compliance timelines, uneven certification, lack of payer standardization, unclear denial-reason implementation, and variation between medical and pharmacy benefit drug workflows.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Added provider burden, increased health IT development cost, slowed exchange, avoidable complexity for users, and insufficient time for certified functionality deployment.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Standards version transitions, US Core/USCDI version dependencies, payer endpoint discovery, authentication variation, F&B/RTPB aggregation, and different EHR workflows for prescriptions versus administered drugs.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Altera cites thousands of providers served and more than three decades of deployment experience, but no quantitative study results.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Not addressed.
- **Examples provided:** EHRs must adopt standards while exchange partners may use proprietary approaches; provider workflows differ for prescriptions versus onsite medication or vaccine administration; F&B/RTPB data may require aggregation from multiple sources.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health IT vendors, payers, providers, patients, CMS, ONC, NCPDP, and app/EHR developers.
- **Economic implications:** Altera says uneven standard adoption adds extensive cost and thousands of hours of work; repeated F&B/RTPB aggregation creates recurring operational burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** The proposals are feasible if standards, certification, endpoint discovery, and timelines are aligned across the industry.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster and standardized PA responses can improve access, but rushed or inconsistent implementation may slow provider workflows.
