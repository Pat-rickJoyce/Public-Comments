# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** HealthEdge Software, Inc.; signed by Bettina Vanover, Sr. Director, Regulatory Compliance.
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Healthcare digital platform company serving Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, Marketplace, and commercial health plans through administrative, clinical, care management, and member engagement systems.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HealthEdge supports CMS's interoperability and prior authorization direction, including extending PA APIs to medical benefit drugs, requiring FHIR IGs, centralized endpoint reporting, usage metrics, and a long-term HIPAA transition from X12N 278 to FHIR. It asks CMS to provide clearer technical guidance, stable versioning, testing infrastructure, phased reporting, privacy guidance, and vendor engagement to make implementation achievable by October 1, 2027.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.215; 42 CFR Part 2; HIPAA; references CMS-0057-F and X12N 278 administrative simplification.
- **Standards referenced:** Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS IGs; FHIR; X12N 278; NDC; Inferno; HL7; Da Vinci Project; Patient Access, Provider Directory, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization APIs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medical benefit drug Prior Authorization API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with clarification.
- **Rationale:** CRD, DTR, and PAS are technically sound and align with existing non-drug PA implementation, but medical benefit drug formulary data may be incomplete, unstructured, or not stored at drug level.
- **Specific request:** Issue technical guidance on minimum data requirements and acceptable data structures for medical benefit drug coverage rules.

**Topic 2: Mandatory IGs and versioning**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Standardizing IG versions is useful, but unexpired-version flexibility creates planning uncertainty and possible functional incompatibility.
- **Specific request:** Publish a minimum supported IG version matrix, give at least 18 months' notice before expiration, ensure stable IGs at least 12 months before compliance dates, require supported-version disclosure, and support conformance testing.

**Topic 3: HIPAA transition from X12N 278 to FHIR**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with clarification.
- **Rationale:** A FHIR HIPAA PA standard would resolve uncertainty after CMS-0057-F, but clearinghouses, trading partners, state Medicaid FFS programs, and small plans need transition clarity.
- **Specific request:** Maintain 24-month and 36-month compliance windows, avoid duplicative retesting for already compliant plans, require trading-partner notice, and publish a consolidated standards timeline.

**Topic 4: Centralized API endpoint registry**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Endpoint discoverability is a major barrier to Payer-to-Payer data exchange and API adoption.
- **Specific request:** Publish reporting specs six months before the 60-day deadline, require structured submissions with IG version support, create update SLAs, and include vendors in registry design.

**Topic 5: API usage metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Metrics can help CMS evaluate impact, but reporting across five APIs and multiple plan clients is technically non-trivial and can become non-comparable without precise definitions.
- **Specific request:** Define transaction-level metrics, aggregation rules, denominators, partial-year rules, flexible reporting cycles, and a 12-month ramp for newly captured metrics.

**Topic 6: Privacy in multi-indication drug PA workflows**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PRIVACY-related GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify.
- **Rationale:** Drug PA data can imply sensitive diagnoses when drugs have multiple indications, especially across mental health, SUD, weight management, and cardiovascular conditions.
- **Specific request:** Clarify whether HIPAA, mental health parity, 42 CFR Part 2, and minimum necessary rules are sufficient or whether additional consent and segmentation requirements are needed.

**Topic 7: QHP PA decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS where QHP operations are implicated
- **Position:** Support with clarification.
- **Rationale:** Consistent timeframes across product lines are helpful, but systems need transaction-level clocks, audit logs, and rules for non-API submissions.
- **Specific request:** Clarify when the clock starts, how non-electronic decisions are documented, and whether metrics are transaction-level auditable or aggregate only.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports moving CRD, DTR, and PAS from recommended to required to advance standardization.
- **S2/S3:** Requests a supported IG version matrix, 18-month expiration notice, and 12 months of stable IG availability before compliance dates.
- **S12:** Identifies medical benefit drug data complexity, including compound drugs and missing NDC-level formulary records.
- **S15:** Asks CMS to clarify edge-case drugs that vary between medical and pharmacy benefits and how benefit category should appear in API workflows.
- **S21:** States the October 1, 2027 timeline is aggressive and asks CMS to monitor readiness and allow targeted extensions where documented constraints exist.
- **S23-S27:** Supports endpoint reporting and recommends structured formats, IG version metadata, update processes, SLAs, and vendor involvement.
- **S29-S35:** Supports usage metrics but asks for precise transaction-level definitions, aggregation rules, reporting-cycle flexibility, and phased implementation for new metrics.
- **S36/S39:** Notes operational complexity for state Medicaid FFS extension policies and asks for a consolidated timeline aligning interoperability and HIPAA standards dates.
- **S44-S55:** Supports a long-term FHIR HIPAA PA standard replacing X12N 278, with 24/36-month timelines, no duplicative retesting, transition guidance, testing, certification, and implementation clarity.
- **S60-S65:** Supports certification/testing infrastructure, Inferno test kits, sandbox environments, voluntary ONC certification, and multi-party Connectathon testing.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to payer API implementation RFI topics by recommending pre-compliance attestation, sandboxes, module-level certification, and end-to-end testing.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide technical guidance for medical benefit drug data structures and coverage rules in the Prior Authorization API.
2. Publish a minimum supported IG version matrix and require adequate notice before IG expiration.
3. Coordinate with HL7/Da Vinci so stable IG versions are available at least 12 months before compliance dates.
4. Require API support and documentation for all unexpired IG versions to prevent incompatibility.
5. Build IG-specific Inferno test kits, sandbox environments, conformance thresholds, and public conformance scores.
6. Clarify HIPAA FHIR transition timing, X12N 278 coexistence, trading partner notice, and state Medicaid FFS implications.
7. Implement a centralized CMS API endpoint registry with version metadata, update SLAs, and vendor input.
8. Define API usage metrics at the transaction level and allow a phased ramp for new data elements.
9. Address privacy and consent issues for multi-indication drug PA workflows.
10. Clarify QHP PA timeframe clocks, non-electronic submission documentation, and audit requirements.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Implementation feasibility, IG version uncertainty, medical benefit drug data gaps, dual-standard classification, clearinghouse transition, endpoint discoverability, metric comparability, privacy implications, and auditability.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Building to moving targets, functionally incompatible compliant systems, delayed vendor readiness, claim adjudication errors, non-comparable metrics, implied sensitive diagnosis disclosure, and check-the-box IG adoption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Core system configuration, health plan-specific variations, lack of structured formulary data, concurrent testing and version finalization, shared vendor infrastructure, legacy X12N workflows, and sandbox/testing gaps.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative external statistics cited; proposes timing thresholds such as 18-month expiration notice, 12-month stable IG availability, and 12-month metric ramp.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Compound medical benefit drugs without NDC-level records, bupropion as a multi-indication privacy example, clearinghouse X12N 278 workflows, and multi-plan payer metric aggregation.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health plans, core administrative system vendors, providers, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, trading partners, state Medicaid FFS programs, QHP issuers, small health plans, CMS/ONC, and patients whose drug PA data may reveal sensitive information.
- **Economic implications:** Compliance requires vendor development, plan configuration, testing, reporting infrastructure, sandboxes, and transition support; public conformance scores may reduce integration risk and vendor selection uncertainty.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if standards are stable, testing tools are available, data models are defined, vendors are engaged, and phased implementation is allowed for new metrics.
- **Patient care impact:** Better electronic PA can reduce friction, but privacy gaps or implementation failures could undermine trust and workflow reliability.
