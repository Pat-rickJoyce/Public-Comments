- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Laboratory/genomic testing company with experience submitting prior authorizations for genomic tests and working with payer billing/coding requirements.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

GeneDx responds primarily to CMS's RFI on laboratory tests and DMEPOS prior authorization. It argues that current lab PA rules, especially pre-collection authorization and specimen collection date-of-service policies, misalign with genomic testing workflows, burden providers and patients, delay diagnoses, and restrict access for rare disease and pediatric populations; GeneDx recommends allowing laboratories to initiate PA, accepting test requisition forms as medical documentation where sufficiently detailed, automating documentation and payer communications, and aligning PA systems with claims and laboratory workflows.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY by analogy; API-USAGE-METRICS indirectly.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization in pharmacy benefit space by analogy; no specific FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Lab PA burden and date-of-service rules**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring ordering providers to obtain PA before specimen collection forces additional visits, delays testing, and may prevent testing entirely.
- **Specific request:** Do not treat specimen collection date as the operative service date for PA timing; allow workflows where samples are collected and held while authorization is pursued.

**Topic 2: Laboratory-initiated PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Laboratories understand payer-specific coding and billing requirements and bear financial risk if authorization is denied.
- **Specific request:** Permit laboratories to initiate PA on behalf of ordering providers and patients using supplied clinical documentation.

**Topic 3: Test requisition forms as documentation**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** TRFs can capture medical necessity information and reduce medical-record submission burden.
- **Specific request:** Allow sufficiently detailed provider-completed TRFs as medical documentation, with safeguards against fraud and support from clinical records when requested.

**Topic 4: Laboratory benefit managers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** LBMs can consolidate payer criteria but also create hidden payer-specific deviations, appeal confusion, and feedback loops.
- **Specific request:** Improve transparency and communication among payers, LBMs, laboratories, and providers regarding criteria, billing requirements, appeals, and claim edits.

**Topic 5: Automation and AI-enabled PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Automation can reduce turnaround time, incomplete submissions, and inconsistent review while preserving program integrity.
- **Specific request:** Automate intake, documentation validation, missing-information flags, payer communication, medical-record review against criteria, and fraud/waste/abuse pattern detection.

**Topic 6: Technology gaps**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA systems often do not align with claims logic, clinical workflows, status visibility, or real-time communication needs.
- **Specific request:** Develop standardized interoperable PA platforms with real-time status, consistent criteria, and alignment between authorization and claims adjudication.

**Topic 7: Workforce impact**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA workload contributes to burnout, staffing costs, and retention problems.
- **Specific request:** Reduce PA administrative burden through automation and process reform.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** GeneDx directly responds to CMS's RFI on laboratory tests and DMEPOS prior authorization, including impacts on care and burden, TRFs, specimen collection timing, LBMs, automation, technology gaps, and workforce effects.
- **S29/S30:** By analogy, the comment supports metrics and technology capable of detecting incomplete submissions, status, utilization trends, and fraud/waste/abuse, but does not address drug API usage metrics directly.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Permit laboratories to initiate PA for lab testing using provider-supplied clinical documentation.
2. Stop requiring PA submission before specimen collection for genomic testing workflows.
3. Recognize that laboratory testing proceeds when analysis begins, not necessarily on collection date.
4. Allow sufficiently detailed provider-completed TRFs to demonstrate medical necessity.
5. Add safeguards to prevent fraudulent TRF use without penalizing established laboratories.
6. Improve transparency into LBM and payer criteria, claim edits, billing requirements, and appeals pathways.
7. Automate PA intake, documentation validation, status communication, and missing-information detection.
8. Use AI-enabled tools to compare medical records to payer criteria and detect unusual utilization patterns.
9. Align authorization systems, claims logic, clinical workflows, and laboratory specimen realities.
10. Reduce PA burden to improve workforce satisfaction and retention.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Provider-only PA workflows, pre-collection PA rules, arbitrary specimen date-of-service policies, LBM opacity, lack of status visibility, and payer/claims misalignment.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed diagnosis, missed testing, loss to follow-up, delayed treatment, access restrictions for rare disease families, patient travel burden, and workforce burnout.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer-specific CPT coding schemes, specimen handling requirements, physician office inability to store specimens, LBM customization, unclear appeals pathways, and inconsistent criteria.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** GeneDx internal data shows a relatively significant number of PAs denied due to submission after collection date; AMA reports 89% of physicians say PA contributes to burnout and physicians handle an average of 39 PAs per week.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA prior authorization burden resource.
- **Examples provided:** Germline genomic testing, blood or buccal samples, NICU and prenatal genomic testing, rare disease patients, specimen stabilization/DNA extraction, payer recommendations to retain specimens in physician offices.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing genomic testing, rare disease families, pediatric/NICU/prenatal patients, ordering providers, laboratories, payers, LBMs, and physician office staff.
- **Economic implications:** Laboratories bear non-payment risk when authorization is denied; provider offices face staffing costs; automation may reduce payer and provider administrative costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** GeneDx sees automation and AI-enabled review as feasible when focused on documentation and administrative components rather than simplistic diagnosis/procedure codes.
- **Patient care impact:** Significant; delayed or missed genomic testing can delay diagnosis, treatment, and family answers.
