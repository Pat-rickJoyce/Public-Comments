- **Author/Organization name:** Oncology Nursing Society (ONS)
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents more than 100,000 oncology nurses nationwide; professional home to more than 35,000 members; established 1975; focused on oncology nursing excellence and cancer care transformation.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ONS broadly supports the proposed rule's goals of modernizing prior authorization, expanding interoperability, and increasing transparency for drug PA, particularly as they affect oncology care. The organization raises a central concern about fragmentation between medical and pharmacy benefit PA pathways for cancer drugs—many therapies exist in multiple formulations that can shift between benefit structures—and urges CMS and ONC to develop a unified interoperability framework. ONS also opposes the proposed removal of formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs, and advocates for a universal 24-hour PA decision timeframe for all cancer drugs regardless of benefit structure.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for Drugs — Medical Benefit**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with Modify
- **Rationale:** ONS supports requiring electronic PA for medical benefit drugs by October 1, 2027, but is concerned the proposed framework does not adequately address drugs that shift between medical and pharmacy benefit based on formulation, route of administration, site of care, or payer benefit design (e.g., infused vs. oral oncology therapies, white bagging, site-of-care restrictions).
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should work with stakeholders to develop a unified interoperability framework providing consolidated access to coverage and documentation requirements across all formulations and benefit structures for a given medication.

**Topic 2: Medical vs. Pharmacy Benefit Fragmentation in Oncology**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cancer therapies frequently exist in both infused (medical benefit) and oral/subcutaneous (pharmacy benefit) formulations. Payer policies on specialty pharmacy distribution, white bagging, and site-of-care restrictions further complicate benefit assignment. No unified standard currently allows clinicians to view PA criteria across both benefit structures in a single workflow, perpetuating administrative burden for oncology nurses.
- **Specific request:** Develop a seamless interoperability framework enabling consolidated access across medical and pharmacy benefit systems; providers and patients should access coverage information through a unified process.

**Topic 3: PA Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Requiring specific denial reasons across all impacted payers starting October 1, 2027 is critical to helping oncology providers and nurses more efficiently address coverage barriers and avoid unnecessary delays.
- **Specific request:** None beyond support for the proposal as written.

**Topic 4: PA Timeframes for Cancer Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify (expand)
- **Rationale:** Cancer treatment is time-sensitive. Delays beyond 24 hours contribute to patient anxiety, disrupt treatment planning, and can delay initiation of medically necessary care. Oncology providers already follow evidence-based clinical pathways.
- **Specific request:** All impacted payers should be required to adhere to a 24-hour PA decision timeframe for all cancer drugs, regardless of whether covered under the medical or pharmacy benefit.

**Topic 5: PA Metrics Reporting and API Usage Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with Modify
- **Rationale:** ONS supports public reporting of PA metrics for drugs by MA organizations, state Medicaid/CHIP FFS, managed care plans, and QHP issuers. However, Part A cancer drugs subject to PA should also be included.
- **Specific request:** Extend required public reporting to include cancer drugs payable under Part A where PA requirements apply in connection with inpatient or bundled oncology services.

**Topic 6: Formulary Information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY (cross-cutting)
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Removing formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs would eliminate an important opportunity to advance integrated, interoperable access to coverage and utilization management information. Oncology nurses rely on formulary data to coordinate timely access to cancer therapies, especially for patients transitioning between payers.
- **Specific request:** Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs; continue working with stakeholders to improve standardization and usability of formulary and utilization management data.

**Topic 7: Step Therapy (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify/Support with conditions
- **Rationale:** Step therapy delays and redundant requirements can cause missed treatment windows, disease progression, and diminished quality of life in cancer patients. Payers should recognize previously completed step therapy unless there is documented clinical rationale to require additional steps; patients should not repeat step therapy solely due to plan transitions.
- **Specific request:** Support seamless interoperability between medical and pharmacy benefit systems for step therapy; require payers to recognize prior step therapy determinations when patients transition; maintain guardrails against automation replacing clinician judgment; evaluate step therapy using patient-centered measures.

**Topic 8: Lab Tests and DMEPOS (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with recommendations
- **Rationale:** Timely access to molecular diagnostics, biomarker testing, and genetic testing is critical to oncology treatment selection. DMEPOS items are essential to supportive cancer care. PA delays create administrative burden for oncology nurses and risk clinical deterioration.
- **Specific request:** Work with stakeholders to streamline PA requirements for oncology-related lab tests and DMEPOS items, reduce redundant documentation, and leverage interoperability tools.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** ONS acknowledges technical challenges in extending PA APIs to drugs under the medical benefit and highlights the added complexity when oncology drugs exist in both medical and pharmacy benefit formulations; urges development of a unified framework.
- **S15:** ONS directly addresses the medical vs. pharmacy benefit distinction, noting that oncology therapies frequently shift between benefit structures based on formulation, route, and payer policy; urges a seamless, cross-benefit solution.
- **S16:** ONS supports requiring specific denial reasons, emphasizing clinical importance for oncology providers in addressing coverage barriers efficiently.
- **S20:** ONS advocates for a universal 24-hour PA decision timeframe for all cancer drugs across both benefit structures, citing time-sensitivity of cancer treatment.
- **S29/S30:** ONS supports collection and reporting of API usage metrics and requests extension to include Part A cancer drug PA data.
- **S43:** ONS supports aligning PA timeframes and urges the 24-hour standard universally for cancer drugs.
- **S66 (step therapy RFI):** ONS provides detailed recommendations on step therapy continuity, payer recognition of prior determinations, guardrails on automation, and patient-centered evaluation metrics.
- **S67/S68 (lab/DMEPOS RFI):** ONS addresses PA burden for oncology-related lab tests, molecular diagnostics, and DMEPOS items; urges streamlining and interoperability improvements.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Develop a unified interoperability framework providing consolidated access to coverage and documentation requirements across all drug formulations and benefit structures.
2. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs; do not remove it as proposed.
3. Require a 24-hour PA decision timeframe for all cancer drugs regardless of benefit structure.
4. Extend required PA metrics reporting to include cancer drugs payable under Part A where PA applies in inpatient or bundled oncology services.
5. Require payers to recognize and honor previously completed step therapy determinations when patients transition between health plans, absent documented clinical rationale for additional steps.
6. Maintain guardrails preventing automation from replacing clinician judgment in complex oncology decisions; evaluate step therapy using patient-centered outcomes.
7. Streamline PA requirements for oncology-related laboratory tests and DMEPOS items, leveraging interoperability tools to reduce redundant documentation.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Fragmentation between medical and pharmacy benefit PA pathways for oncology drugs; proposed removal of formulary data from APIs; lack of a unified workflow for clinicians navigating cross-benefit coverage requirements.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays and patient anxiety from PA wait times; missed treatment windows and disease progression from step therapy redundancy; clinical deterioration and avoidable hospitalizations from DMEPOS PA delays; administrative burden diverting oncology nurses from direct patient care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No current unified standard or mechanism for cross-benefit coverage and PA criteria access; white bagging, specialty pharmacy distribution, and site-of-care restrictions complicate benefit assignments.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Oncology nurses and care teams; patients with cancer; oncology providers; payers (MA organizations, Medicaid/CHIP, QHP issuers); health IT vendors building PA and formulary APIs.
- **Economic implications:** Administrative burden reduction for oncology nurses if interoperability is improved; potential cost savings from reduced avoidable hospitalizations and treatment delays.
- **Technical feasibility:** Primary technical challenge identified is the absence of any current unified workflow spanning medical and pharmacy benefit systems; FHIR adoption not opposed.
- **Patient care impact:** Significant—delays in PA decisions for cancer drugs can disrupt time-sensitive treatment plans; step therapy redundancy after plan transitions can delay medically necessary care.

Dear Administrator Oz and Coordinator Keane:

Oncology nurses are privileged to support people with cancer through their entire cancer experience, from
diagnosis and treatment into survivorship or as they face their end-of-life journey. On behalf of the Oncology
Nursing Society (ONS), which represents more than 100,000 oncology nurses nationwide, we appreciate the
opportunity to comment on the aforementioned proposed rule.
General Comments
ONS supports efforts by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) and the Office of the National
Coordinator for Health IT (ONC) to modernize prior authorization processes, expand interoperability, and
improve transparency. Specifically, we support proposals that would require impacted payers to support
electronic prior authorization and make decisions within shorter, aligned timeframes across CMS programs,
as well as increase transparency for the prior authorization of drugs by requiring denial reasons and reporting
on key metrics. We also support requirements for impacted payers to update health information technology
(health IT) standards, report interoperability API endpoints and usage metrics to CMS, and adopt Health Level
Seven® (HL7®) Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources® (FHIR®) standards and implementation
specifications for prior authorization transactions across HIPAA-covered entities...
