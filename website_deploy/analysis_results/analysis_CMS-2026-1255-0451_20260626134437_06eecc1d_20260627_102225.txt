## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

C-TAC broadly supports all major provisions of the proposed rule, emphasizing the disproportionate harm that PA delays and administrative fragmentation inflict on people with serious illness and their family caregivers. The coalition endorses faster PA decision timeframes, electronic PA for drugs, specific denial reasons, API usage metrics reporting, cross-program coverage scope, and HIPAA/FHIR standardization, framing each through the lens of seriously ill patients' clinical urgency and caregiver burden.

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specific

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Drug PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** For seriously ill patients, time is clinically consequential; delays worsen symptoms, destabilize function, and trigger avoidable ED visits or hospitalizations. Delays also shift substantial burden onto family caregivers.
- **Specific request:** Shorter, more aligned, and more predictable decision timeframes across all programs.

**Topic 2: Electronic PA for Drugs (PA API)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Seriously ill patients rely on multiple providers and medications across settings; any coordination breakdown has immediate consequences. Better electronic workflows reduce avoidable treatment interruptions.
- **Specific request:** Accurate, actionable PA information at point of prescribing.

**Topic 3: Specific Denial Reasons and PA Data in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Vague denials are uniquely harmful for seriously ill patients—every extra day deciphering payer reasoning risks poorly controlled symptoms, caregiver exhaustion, and escalation to ED/hospital.
- **Specific request:** Specific denial reasons required; PA data to flow through patient, provider, and payer-to-payer APIs.

**Topic 4: API Usage Metrics and CMS Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Value of interoperability depends on actual use reducing delays, not technology existence. Oversight identifies plans imposing harmful friction even after nominal compliance.
- **Specific request:** Required reporting of API endpoints and annual usage metrics; focus on access improvements for seriously ill patients.

**Topic 5: Cross-Program Coverage Scope**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Serious illness affects people across all coverage categories; transitions are common; cross-program consistency protects treatment continuity.
- **Specific request:** Apply protections consistently across MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP coverage.

**Topic 6: HIPAA/FHIR Standardization for PA Transactions**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Administrative fragmentation directly interferes with symptom management, treatment sequencing, and preferred care setting for seriously ill patients.
- **Specific request:** Adopt standardized FHIR-based approaches for PA transactions under HIPAA administrative simplification.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20 (PA timeframes for drugs):** Supports shorter, aligned timeframes; emphasizes clinical urgency for seriously ill patients where delays trigger avoidable hospitalizations and caregiver burden.
- **S12 (technical challenges for medical benefit drug PA):** Supports electronic PA extension; seriously ill patients with complex multi-provider care are disproportionately harmed by fragmented authorization.
- **S16-S17 (specific denial reasons and structured codes):** Strongly supports; vague denials uniquely harmful—delays in understanding denial translate to symptom deterioration and care escalation.
- **S29-S30, S34-S35 (API usage metrics):** Supports reporting requirements; value depends on actual real-world use reducing delays, not just nominal technical compliance.
- **S44-S45 (FHIR as HIPAA standard, scope):** Supports FHIR-based HIPAA standardization; administrative fragmentation directly harms seriously ill patients' ability to remain in preferred care settings.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish shorter, more aligned, and predictable PA decision timeframes across all programs.
2. Implement electronic PA for drugs to provide actionable PA information at the point of prescribing.
3. Require specific denial reasons for drug PA denials.
4. Ensure detailed PA data flows through patient, provider, and payer-to-payer APIs.
5. Require impacted payers to report API endpoints and annual usage metrics to CMS.
6. Apply PA protections consistently across MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP coverage.
7. Adopt standardized FHIR-based approaches for PA transactions under HIPAA administrative simplification.
