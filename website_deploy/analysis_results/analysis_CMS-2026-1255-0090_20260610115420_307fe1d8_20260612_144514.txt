# Comment Analysis: CMS-2026-1255-0090

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Dr. Amy Payne, DSW, LCSW.
- **Stakeholder type:** Individual; Advocacy Group perspective.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Doctor of Social Work, licensed clinical social worker, cholangiocarcinoma advocate and legacy caregiver.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to protect patients from prior authorization delays and denials for laboratory testing, particularly oncology and specialty biomarker testing. She asks CMS to require retroactive approvals after specimen collection, prohibit timing-only denials, avoid repeat blood draws, expedite time-sensitive diagnostics, and protect patients from financial liability.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to urgent PA timelines; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by concern over timing-based denials.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P referenced; no CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** None.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratory testing PA timing rules**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Plans may deny testing unless PA is approved by the specimen collection date, even where testing occurs later and administrative review lags.
- **Specific request:** Permit retroactive PA approvals after specimen collection and prohibit denials based solely on paperwork timing.

**Topic 2: Oncology and rare disease diagnostic access**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delays in biomarker and specialty lab testing can delay treatment decisions for lethal cancers and rare diseases.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited PA pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics.

**Topic 3: Patient burden from repeated specimens and costs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Discarding viable specimens can force repeat blood draws and create geographic and financial burdens, especially for rural and low-income patients.
- **Specific request:** Prevent redundant testing when a valid specimen exists and shield patients from surprise out-of-pocket costs when acting in good faith.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Responds to a general RFI topic on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing, asking CMS to align PA requirements with real clinical workflows.
- **S20/S43:** Indirectly relevant because the comment stresses expedited decisions for time-sensitive diagnostics, although it does not address drug PA timeframes specifically.
- **S16:** Indirectly relevant because timing-based denials are criticized as inappropriate where medical necessity is not disputed.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow PA approvals to be processed retroactively after specimen collection.
2. Prohibit denials of medically necessary tests solely because PA paperwork was not complete before specimen collection.
3. Prevent repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.
4. Create expedited pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic testing.
5. Protect patients from surprise costs when they act in good faith.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rigid PA timing requirements for lab testing, discarded specimens, repeated blood draws, delayed cancer treatment decisions.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Worse mortality risk, treatment delays, financial liability, geographic burdens for rural patients, disproportionate harm to low-income populations.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Date-of-service rules tied to specimen collection rather than processing date; administrative review lag.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** The commenter's mother died 79 days after cholangiocarcinoma diagnosis.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Biomarker and specialty labs collected in a provider office and sent elsewhere for processing; denials based on specimen collection date.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Cancer patients, rare disease patients, rural and low-income patients, providers, laboratories, health plans.
- **Economic implications:** Potential patient surprise bills and redundant testing costs; no quantified estimates.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment focuses on policy and workflow rules, not technical standards.
- **Patient care impact:** High; delayed testing can delay time-sensitive oncology treatment decisions and require avoidable repeat procedures.
