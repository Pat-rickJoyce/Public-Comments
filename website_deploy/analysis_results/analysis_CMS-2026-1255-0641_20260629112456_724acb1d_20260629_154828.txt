# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** MAPRx Coalition and undersigned member organizations.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Professional Association coalition.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National coalition of more than 60 beneficiary, caregiver, and healthcare professional organizations focused on Medicare beneficiaries with chronic diseases and disabilities and Part D medication access.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MAPRx supports CMS's modernization of prior authorization but argues that Medicare Part D beneficiaries are left behind. The coalition asks CMS to extend specific denial explanations, public reporting, clinical history portability, electronic clinical documentation, timeframe oversight, and caregiver access protections to Part D sponsors.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PATIENT-ACCESS; Payer-to-Payer API; GENERAL Part D policy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 423.160; 42 CFR 423.568; 42 CFR 423.572.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based APIs; NCPDP SCRIPT; Payer-to-Payer API; Patient Access API; EHR connections.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Specific denial explanations for Part D**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Vague Part D denials such as "medical necessity not met" prevent providers and beneficiaries from correcting documentation gaps or understanding next steps.
- **Specific request:** Require Part D sponsors to provide actionable, plain-language denial reasons to both the provider and beneficiary, including missing criteria, required alternatives, or documentation needs.

**Topic 2: Public reporting for Part D and granular data**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Oppose exclusion; Modify.
- **Rationale:** Internal CMS reporting is not visible to beneficiaries, caregivers, and providers and aggregate reporting masks denial patterns for critical therapies.
- **Specific request:** Extend public reporting to Part D; integrate PA metrics into Medicare Plan Finder; require disaggregation by therapeutic area, denial reason, brand/generic status, UM type, delay length, abandonment, and final therapy received.

**Topic 3: Carry clinical history across plan transitions**
- **Provision ID:** Payer-to-Payer API / GENERAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Beneficiaries switching Part D plans may be forced to repeat step therapy or re-prove medical necessity despite documented prior approvals or failed therapies.
- **Specific request:** Require Part D sponsors to retrieve and apply PA and step therapy history through the Payer-to-Payer API and establish standards for when history may be disregarded.

**Topic 4: End faxing of clinical documentation for Part D**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY / TECHNICAL-FEASIBILITY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** NCPDP SCRIPT handles transaction data but not clinical documentation, leaving providers to fax or manually submit documents.
- **Specific request:** Establish interoperability requirements enabling clinical documentation to flow from provider EHRs to Part D sponsors through FHIR-based APIs while NCPDP handles pharmacy transactions.

**Topic 5: Part D decision timeframe transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Part D has 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes, but extension usage and incomplete-submission findings are not publicly reported.
- **Specific request:** Require public reporting of Part D timeframe compliance, extension usage, and incomplete-submission findings.

**Topic 6: Caregiver access to PA information**
- **Provision ID:** PATIENT-ACCESS / PRIVACY-SECURITY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Many beneficiaries rely on caregivers to track PA status, appeals, and denials, but caregivers lack electronic access pathways.
- **Specific request:** Require prior authorization status and decision information to be accessible to authorized caregivers and personal representatives through the Patient Access API consistent with HIPAA authorization.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** MAPRx supports specific denial reasons and urges extension to Part D with actionable explanations.
- **S17:** Supports denial reasons that identify missing documentation, required alternative therapies, and next steps in plain language.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Addresses public reporting design by calling for standardized, plan-shopping-accessible reporting, including Medicare Plan Finder integration.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports granular public PA metrics and tracking denial rates with timeframe compliance.
- **S43:** Asks CMS to monitor Part D timeframe compliance, extension usage, and incomplete-submission findings.
- **S46/S51:** Suggests FHIR and NCPDP coexistence for Part D, with FHIR carrying clinical documentation and NCPDP handling pharmacy transactions.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy and caregiver access RFIs by urging Payer-to-Payer API use for prior treatment history and authorized caregiver access.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend specific denial-reason requirements to Part D sponsors.
2. Require denial explanations to be actionable and plain-language for beneficiaries.
3. Extend standardized public PA reporting to Part D and integrate metrics into Medicare Plan Finder.
4. Require granular, disaggregated reporting by therapy area, denial reason, UM type, delay, abandonment, and final treatment outcome.
5. Require Part D sponsors to retrieve and honor prior PA and step therapy history through Payer-to-Payer APIs.
6. Track and publicly report continuity of PA history after plan switching.
7. Enable FHIR-based clinical documentation exchange for Part D while retaining NCPDP SCRIPT for pharmacy transactions.
8. Publicly report Part D timeframe compliance, extension usage, and incomplete-submission findings.
9. Permit authorized caregivers and personal representatives to access PA status and decisions via the Patient Access API.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Part D exclusion from protections, vague denials, hidden reporting, repeated step therapy, faxed documentation, unreported timeframe compliance, and lack of caregiver access.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Prescription abandonment, unsafe delays, treatment gaps for chronic diseases, repeated failed therapy, unnecessary administrative burden, and beneficiary confusion.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** NCPDP SCRIPT's limitations for clinical documents; lack of explicit requirement to apply Payer-to-Payer API history; gaps in caregiver authorization workflows.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Coalition membership and scope; examples of chronic disease groups; references to existing Part D regulatory timeframes.
- **Studies or research referenced:** MAPRx references its own documentation of increased Part D step therapy since IRA implementation.
- **Examples provided:** Beneficiaries with diabetes, neurological disorders, heart disease, cancer; multiple sclerosis patient stable on biologic; January 1 plan-switch step therapy reset; providers forced to fax documentation.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Medicare Part D beneficiaries, caregivers, providers, Part D sponsors, PBMs, health IT vendors, and chronic disease advocacy organizations.
- **Economic implications:** Reporting and documentation reforms may reduce wasteful repeat step therapy and administrative rework; Part D plans may incur implementation costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** MAPRx supports hybrid FHIR/NCPDP architecture and believes existing APIs can solve Part D continuity problems if CMS requires use.
- **Patient care impact:** Stronger Part D protections could prevent treatment delays, prescription abandonment, and care disruptions for beneficiaries with serious chronic conditions.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with significant concerns.
- **Tone:** Professional, beneficiary-centered, critical of Part D gaps.
- **Priority level:** High.
