# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Westside Family Healthcare; signed by Chris Fraser, MBA, FACHE, President & CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; Safety-net provider; Federally Qualified Health Center.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Delaware FQHC serving 25,000 community members with family medical, prenatal/women's health, behavioral health, and oral health care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Westside Family Healthcare supports CMS's drug ePA and interoperability proposals because standardized APIs, faster decisions, denial transparency, endpoint reporting, and usage metrics could reduce burden and improve access for medically underserved CHC patients. It requests stronger safeguards: vendor accountability, CHC technical and funding support, benefit-classification transparency, 340B/pharmacy workflow alignment, proactive PA expiration alerts, centralized reporting, automatic approvals for missed deadlines, HIPAA transition support from X12 to FHIR, CDex guardrails, gold-carding, and CMS-ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); 90 FR 37130; references CMS-0062-P, CMS-0053-F, CMS-0057-F, HTI-4, HTI-5, and CMS National Standards Group enforcement discretion dated February 28, 2024.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; CRD, DTR, and PAS v2.2; HL7 Da Vinci IGs; Provider Access API; Patient Access API; Provider Directory API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization API; CRD Coverage Information extension; NCPDP-related pharmacy workflows by implication; X12 278; X12 6020; FHIR/CDex; C-CDA; PDF; FHIR resources; ONC certification criteria.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Interoperability and Safety-Net Implementation Support**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs can reduce duplicative documentation, improve PA visibility, and streamline coordination for medically underserved patients. CHCs rely heavily on vendors and limited IT staff, creating risks if vendor readiness lags payer compliance.
- **Specific request:** Pair requirements with realistic timelines, ONC coordination, readiness validation/testing, vendor accountability, cybersecurity/IT support, technical assistance, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.

**Topic 2: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug ePA can improve point-of-prescribing visibility into formulary requirements, utilization management criteria, and authorization status, supporting medication adherence for CHC patients with chronic disease and behavioral health needs.
- **Specific request:** Finalize drug ePA requirements, but require real-time benefit-classification information through Provider Access APIs and clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies when the same drug may be billed under the other pathway.

**Topic 3: Medical vs Pharmacy Benefit and 340B Operations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify; Modify
- **Rationale:** Clinic-administered drugs and 340B contract pharmacy arrangements require coordination among EHRs, pharmacy management systems, TPAs, contract pharmacies, and payer APIs. Poor integration can cause gaps in care, duplicated work, and errors.
- **Specific request:** CMS should evaluate pharmacy-operation impacts, coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs, and provide targeted guidance, funding, and implementation support for CHCs.

**Topic 4: PA Expiration Alerts and PA Reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Proactive alerts can avoid treatment interruption and retroactive reauthorization burden. Westside cautions that ePA alone may accelerate inefficient PA processes and may increase total PA activity without broader reform.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR alerts, extend gold-carding to prescription drugs, and sunset PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 5: Denial Reasons, Decision Timeframes, Metrics, and Enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** CHC patients experience treatment delays, exacerbations, and medication interruptions when PA decisions are slow or denials are unclear. Public metrics and specific denial reasons can improve transparency and accountability.
- **Specific request:** Finalize proposed timelines and specific denial-reason requirements, publish centralized standardized PA metrics on CMS's website, add operational-burden metrics, strengthen enforcement for delays or inaccurate reporting, and automatically approve requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 6: API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Centralized endpoint information reduces provider and vendor burden, especially for CHCs with small IT teams, and helps avoid disruptions from outdated hard-coded endpoints.
- **Specific request:** Publish payer API endpoint information centrally on CMS's website in machine-readable files; require updates within one week of changes and at least annual verification.

**Topic 7: API Usage Metrics and Formulary Information**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** Program- and plan-level metrics can reveal access gaps; usability and functionality metrics are needed to show whether APIs work in clinical workflows. Removing formulary data from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs would force unreliable manual lookups and could worsen prescribing delays.
- **Specific request:** Add successful request rates, authorization failures, malformed queries, calls not received, structured vs. unstructured responses, workflow impact, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance cost; retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 8: HIPAA PA Standards Transition and CDex Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support; Modify
- **Rationale:** Westside supports moving from X12 to FHIR but warns that CHCs and clearinghouses will need to operate X12 6020 and FHIR/CDex in parallel, creating a real dual-standard burden.
- **Specific request:** Provide transition support for safety-net providers, extend the February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion allowing FHIR PA API as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition, and add CDex guardrails requiring complete initial documentation requests, no more than two solicited attachment requests before a decision, and payer response timeframes after attachment receipt.

**Topic 9: ONC-HHS Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS-0062-P payer deadlines may precede availability of certified provider-facing ePA technology, and HTI-5's proposed certification reductions could undercut supporting infrastructure needed by safety-net providers.
- **Specific request:** Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance, publish a formal harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P, and clarify providers cannot be treated as non-compliant during gaps when certified technology is not yet available.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize standardized API and drug ePA requirements with safety-net implementation support.
2. Clarify that providers are not penalized for vendor, clearinghouse, or EHR failures outside provider control.
3. Coordinate CMS timelines with ONC certification and require readiness validation/testing before enforcement.
4. Establish uniform minimum implementation requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.
5. Provide CHC technical assistance, cybersecurity support, IT workforce support, and health IT funding.
6. Require benefit-classification information via Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension.
7. Clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to an alternate medical/pharmacy benefit pathway.
8. Coordinate with OPA on 340B contract pharmacy and pharmacy operations impacts.
9. Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration alerts.
10. Pair ePA with PA reduction policies, including gold-carding for prescription drugs and PA sunset provisions for high-approval categories.
11. Finalize specific drug denial reasons and proposed decision timeframes.
12. Publish PA metrics centrally on CMS's website and add denial, first-pass approval, data quality, and operational burden measures.
13. Establish automatic approval when payers miss required PA decision deadlines.
14. Publish API endpoint data centrally in machine-readable files.
15. Add API usability and functionality metrics and retain formulary data in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
16. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds and implementation support.
17. Extend FHIR PA API enforcement discretion as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition.
18. Add CDex guardrails limiting iterative attachment requests and setting response timeframes.
19. Issue CMS-ONC joint guidance and publish a harmonization assessment before the final rule.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CHC resource constraints; vendor readiness; cybersecurity exposure; payer inconsistency; dual medical/pharmacy workflows; 340B and pharmacy interoperability; ePA without PA reduction; lack of enforcement; removal of formulary data; X12/FHIR dual-standard burden; CMS-ONC timing mismatch.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, interrupted medication access, worsening chronic disease control, COPD/asthma exacerbations, medication titration restarts, severe side effects after treatment gaps, duplicated work, errors in 340B workflows, unreimbursed staff time, and access disparities for underserved patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC IT staff and budgets, reliance on vendors and third-party support, EHR/PMS interoperability gaps, contract pharmacy and TPA complexity, cybersecurity needs, variable payer rollouts, lack of certified technology at payer compliance dates, and legacy X12 transition burden.
