- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National nonprofit law organization focused on Medicare access, health equity, and quality care; provides education, legal assistance, research, analysis, and individual legal representation for older people and people with disabilities, especially those with longer-term conditions; experience assisting thousands of individuals and families with Medicare coverage and appeal issues annually.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CMA supports requiring impacted payers to include specific denial reasons for drug prior authorization requests but urges CMS to define the requirement more rigorously for both drug and non-drug denials. CMA also responds to the laboratory and DMEPOS RFI by urging CMS to avoid additional pre-access barriers that increase patient and family burden and to improve access pathways for dually eligible beneficiaries needing durable medical equipment.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL/RFI — laboratory tests and DMEPOS; TRANSPARENCY-related aspects of PA communications.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specific beyond the proposed rule docket and file code CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** None. CMA does not discuss FHIR IGs, NCPDP, HL7, X12, or API technical standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Specific denial reasons and appeal usability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** CMA states that generic denials, such as “not medically necessary,” do not identify the criterion not met or the criteria used, undermining patients' and providers' ability to appeal.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require greater detail about what counts as a specific denial reason for both drug and non-drug PA denials.

**Topic 2: Plain language, standardized denial notices, and accessibility**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients and caregivers need clear instructions, timelines, deadlines, next steps, required information, and covered alternatives to take timely action.
- **Specific request:** Require plain-language notices, standardized reconsideration/appeal instructions, language-access and accessibility standards, and standardized denial notice templates consistent with the AMA proposal.

**Topic 3: Patient and family burden from pre-access barriers for DMEPOS/lab services**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Concerned
- **Rationale:** CMA reports that beneficiaries and families already perform significant administrative coordination among prescribing providers and DMEPOS suppliers.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider patient and family burden, including time, stress, and economic burden, before adding pre-access requirements.

**Topic 4: Dually eligible beneficiary access to DME**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** When Medicaid coverage is delayed pending an explicit Medicare denial, suppliers may delay furnishing equipment. This is especially problematic when Medicare and Medicaid coverage standards differ.
- **Specific request:** Establish processes that clearly outline coverage and allow prior approval practices that support delivery of items, trigger Medicare denial where needed, and enable Medicaid payment for dually eligible beneficiaries.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** CMA supports specific denial reason requirements but asks CMS to define specificity more clearly and require explanations of the exact coverage criterion not met.
- **S17:** CMA supports standardized denial notice templates and plain-language, accessible notices with clear appeal instructions and next steps.
- **S66-S82 / Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS RFI:** CMA urges CMS to consider patient and family burden from pre-access processes and to improve DME access for dually eligible individuals where Medicare and Medicaid coverage sequencing causes delays.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize the requirement for specific denial reasons in drug prior authorization decisions.
2. Extend detailed denial-reason expectations to non-drug items and services as well.
3. Require denial notices to identify the coverage criterion not met and the criteria used.
4. Require plain-language denial notices with standardized appeal and reconsideration instructions, timelines, deadlines, and next steps.
5. Require language-access and accessibility standards for denial notices.
6. Adopt or align with standardized denial notice templates, including the AMA proposal.
7. Avoid additional pre-access requirements that impose unnecessary patient and family burden.
8. Create DMEPOS processes that improve access for dually eligible beneficiaries when Medicaid payment depends on a Medicare denial.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Generic denial notices; appeal rights undermined by lack of explanation; language/accessibility barriers; added pre-access burdens; delayed DMEPOS access for dually eligible beneficiaries.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Difficulty appealing denials, delayed access to treatment or equipment, increased patient/family time and stress, economic burden, and supplier reluctance to furnish equipment.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Differences between Medicare and Medicaid coverage standards; need for explicit Medicare denials before Medicaid payment; supplier uncertainty about coverage.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None quantitative.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Plans deny treatment as “not medically necessary” without explaining the unmet criterion; dually eligible beneficiaries needing power wheelchairs face delays because Medicare and Medicaid coverage standards differ and Medicaid as payer of last resort may require a Medicare denial.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Medicare beneficiaries, older adults, people with disabilities, dually eligible individuals, caregivers/family members, providers, DMEPOS suppliers, impacted payers, Medicaid programs.
- **Economic implications:** Patient and family economic burden may increase due to administrative pre-access barriers; suppliers may delay delivery because of coverage uncertainty.
- **Technical feasibility:** CMA's recommendations are process and notice-focused rather than technically complex; feasibility depends on CMS defining notice content, accessibility, and coordination processes.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly positive if adopted because clearer denials would improve appeals and better DMEPOS processes would reduce delays for dually eligible beneficiaries.
