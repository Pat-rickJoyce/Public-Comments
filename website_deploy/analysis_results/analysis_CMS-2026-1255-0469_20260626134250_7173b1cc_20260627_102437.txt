## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Excellus Health Plan, Inc. (James R. Reed, President & CEO), doing business as Excellus BlueCross BlueShield and Univera Healthcare; nonprofit independent licensee of the Blue Cross Blue Shield Association
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Nonprofit health plan serving upstate New York for ~90 years; 4,000+ employees; 1.5 million members across Medicare, Medicaid, employer-sponsored, and individual plans; 250,000+ Medicare Advantage members; nearly 200,000 Medicaid Managed Care/safety net members; 98% provider network penetration in upstate NY.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Excellus Health Plan generally supports CMS-0062-P's direction toward standardized, electronic prior authorization and FHIR interoperability, but has significant concerns about implementation feasibility. Their primary ask is to extend the compliance date by at least 15 months (October 1, 2027 → January 1, 2029, or preferably April 1, 2029), citing cumulative implementation burden, resource constraints, complex multi-vendor dependencies, and overlap with NCPDP Telecommunication Standard F6 transition. They also call for CMS to extend requirements to providers and EHR vendors, not just payers, to ensure end-to-end adoption.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 C.F.R. § 170.215; 91 FR 19890
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR implementation guides (Patient Access, Provider Access, Provider Directory, Payer-to-Payer, Prior Authorization APIs); Da Vinci Plan-Net IG; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit (F&B) Standard; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB) Standard; Da Vinci CRD; NCPDP Telecommunication Standard Version F6

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Mandatory FHIR Implementation Guides**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Making IGs mandatory promotes greater consistency across implementations, reduces ambiguity for payers and technology vendors, and creates clearer expectations for all stakeholders.
- **Specific request:** No modification; however, caution about overlapping version transition periods creating operational complexity.

**Topic 2: NCPDP F&B Standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Enables plan-level formulary and utilization management information to be incorporated into prescribing workflows and surfaced in provider EHR systems.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 3: NCPDP RTPB Standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardizing real-time patient-specific eligibility, coverage, and estimated cost-sharing reduces reliance on fragmented payer-specific portals and proprietary solutions.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 4: Provider Directory API Clarification**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specifying Da Vinci Plan-Net resolves existing implementation ambiguity and promotes consistency across impacted payers.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 5: FHIR API Standards (Version Transitions)**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Overlapping versions of standards that don't integrate well create avoidable operational complexity, increase implementation burden, and delay broader alignment.
- **Specific request:** Avoid lengthy transition periods for older standards once newer standards are adopted; minimize concurrent version overlap.

**Topic 6: Implementation Timeline — Extend to April 1, 2029**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose (as proposed)
- **Rationale:** Technical resources already committed to existing interoperability initiatives; complex multi-vendor dependencies (PBM, specialty drug UM SaaS, oncology UM partner, FHIR platform); contracting alone may take 6-8 months; cumulative burden with NCPDP F6 transition affecting same teams and partners.
- **Specific request:** Extend compliance date by at least 15 months from October 1, 2027 to January 1, 2029; prefers April 1, 2029 as most realistic.

**Topic 7: Provider and Vendor Readiness**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify (expand scope)
- **Rationale:** CMS has imposed significant requirements on health plans, but provider and EHR vendor readiness remains inconsistent; many providers still on legacy systems or lack financial/technical resources; payers may build advanced capabilities without achieving end-to-end electronic exchange.
- **Specific request:** CMS should encourage and support FHIR API adoption by providers and EHR vendors; collect and report API adoption/usage metrics for providers and EHR vendors (not just payers); provide targeted funding, incentives, and technical support.

**Topic 8: Future FHIR Expansion for Eligibility/Benefits**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support (future rulemaking)
- **Rationale:** Broader FHIR-based eligibility and benefits inquiries beyond PA would support more general use cases for confirming coverage and patient financial responsibility.
- **Specific request:** CMS, in coordination with NCVHS, should consider broader future rulemaking to expand FHIR-based APIs for eligibility and benefits inquiries beyond those associated with PA.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Strongly supports requiring the currently recommended FHIR implementation guides across all required APIs; sees mandatory status as reducing ambiguity and promoting consistency.
- **S2/S3:** Advises against lengthy overlapping transition periods; overlapping versions that don't integrate well create operational complexity and delay alignment on updated standards.
- **S21:** Disputes achievability of October 1, 2027 date; recommends extension to January 1, 2029 at minimum, preferably April 1, 2029; cites cumulative burden with NCPDP F6, committed technical resources, multi-vendor contracting timelines of 6-8 months.
- **S22:** Notes cumulative burden with NCPDP Telecommunication Standard Version F6 transition affecting same health plans and PBM partners.
- **S44:** Supports using FHIR CRD within PA workflow; encourages broader future rulemaking for FHIR-based eligibility/benefits inquiries beyond PA.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend the compliance date by at least 15 months from October 1, 2027 to January 1, 2029; prefers April 1, 2029.
2. Avoid lengthy transition periods for older standards once newer standards are adopted; minimize concurrent version overlap.
3. CMS and ONC should encourage and support FHIR API adoption by providers and EHR vendors, not just payers.
4. Collect and report FHIR API adoption and usage metrics for providers and EHR vendors (in addition to payers) to assess uptake and identify gaps.
5. Provide targeted funding, incentives, and technical support programs to accelerate provider and EHR vendor adoption.
6. CMS and NCVHS should consider future rulemaking to expand FHIR-based APIs for eligibility and benefits inquiries beyond PA use cases.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** October 1, 2027 compliance date is not achievable given cumulative implementation burden; parallel NCPDP F6 transition is consuming the same technical resources; multi-vendor contracting timelines add months before development can begin.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Layering requirements on overlapping timelines risks delayed implementation, reduced system quality, and slower adoption; health plans may be forced to build capabilities without corresponding provider/EHR vendor readiness, preventing end-to-end electronic PA.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Complex multi-partner dependencies (PBM, specialty drug UM SaaS, oncology UM partner, FHIR platform, UM recommendation partner); contracting alone may take 6-8 months; technical resources already committed to existing interoperability initiatives with approaching deadlines.
