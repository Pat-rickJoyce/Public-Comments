# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** The AIDS Institute; signed by Rachel Klein, Deputy Executive Director
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Advocates for people with HIV, hepatitis, and chronic conditions; provides patient-experience perspective on prior authorization and treatment continuity.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The AIDS Institute supports the proposed rule as a positive step toward aligning prior authorization standards across medical services and prescription drug benefits. It recommends stronger patient protections: faster PA timelines across QHP and Medicare programs, detailed denial explanations and appeal instructions, appeal timeframes, evergreen approvals for lifelong chronic disease treatment, and prohibition on using prior authorization to route patients into Alternative Funding Programs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY generally; GENERAL/RFI step therapy and patient protections
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; no CFR citations cited in the body.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization APIs; electronic data exchange and automated processes; no specific FHIR IG or NCPDP version discussed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extend electronic PA to prescription drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Aligning prescription drug PA with medical-service PA standards will reduce harmful treatment interruptions and create a uniform expectation for patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize requirements requiring payers to add prescription drugs to electronic prior authorization APIs.

**Topic 2: Faster PA decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients with serious chronic conditions, including HIV and hepatitis, can face treatment interruptions and loss of viral suppression if PA takes too long.
- **Specific request:** Apply Medicaid-style 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes to QHP and Medicare issuers, rather than 7-day standard and 72-hour expedited timeframes.

**Topic 3: Specific denial reasons and appeal instructions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Ambiguous denials waste provider resources and prevent patients and providers from knowing whether to appeal or pursue another treatment.
- **Specific request:** Finalize specific-denial-reason requirements, require detail beyond “not medically necessary,” and include clear instructions for appeal.

**Topic 4: Appeals process timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Denials are often overturned, but appeal processes can extend delays and few denials are appealed because patients lack information or capacity.
- **Specific request:** Establish timelines for issuers to process and respond to PA denial appeals.

**Topic 5: Evergreen approvals for lifelong chronic disease treatment**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / RFI Step Therapy
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Stable patients with HIV, hepatitis, and other chronic diseases face unnecessary adherence risks and administrative burden when they repeatedly reauthorize established treatment.
- **Specific request:** Eliminate PA for the duration of lifelong treatment when patients are stable, or at least significantly restrict recurring PA.

**Topic 6: Alternative Funding Programs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Oppose misuse
- **Rationale:** Plans may exploit PA to deny coverage for specialty drugs and force patients into outside funding channels, creating delays, financial exposure, and lack of appeal rights.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that PA cannot be used to require individuals to source covered services or prescription drugs outside the plan; PA should focus only on medical need.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20 / S43:** Supports 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug PA timeframes for Medicaid and CHIP and asks CMS to extend the same faster standards to QHP and Medicare issuers.
- **S16:** Supports requiring specific denial reasons and asks CMS to require detailed explanations beyond broad “not medically necessary” statements.
- **S17:** Does not request structured codes specifically, but asks for denial responses detailed enough to guide correction, resubmission, or appeal.
- **S66-S82 / Step Therapy and utilization management RFIs:** Discusses overuse of PA, recurring PA for chronic disease treatment, and AFP misuse; recommends patient protections and guardrails.
- **S29-S35 / metrics and transparency, indirectly:** Relies on denial and appeal data to support transparency and guardrails, although it does not propose detailed metrics.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for prescription drugs.
2. Require 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited PA timeframes for QHP and Medicare issuers.
3. Require thorough, specific denial explanations that go beyond “not medically necessary.”
4. Require denial notices to include appeal instructions.
5. Establish timelines for PA denial appeals.
6. Limit overuse of PA and establish guardrails for when PA may be applied.
7. Create evergreen or long-duration approvals for stable lifelong chronic disease treatments.
8. Prohibit use of PA to force patients into Alternative Funding Programs or other out-of-plan sourcing mechanisms.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA is overused and weaponized to delay or deny care; vague denials and slow appeals create harmful treatment interruptions.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment abandonment, viral suppression disruption, disease exacerbation, prolonged hospitalizations, worsened outcomes, financial exposure, drug resistance, painful flare-ups, and patient-provider relationship disruption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of appeal timelines, opaque denial rationales, AFP processes that delay determinations for months, and risk that electronic PA could increase overuse if not paired with guardrails.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 7 in 10 adults with chronic conditions identify PA delays/denials as a major problem; 79% treatment abandonment reported by physicians; patients can wait up to 14 business days for medication PA; over 90% of initial PA requests approved; over 80% of denied PAs reversed on appeal.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF Health Tracking Poll; AMA prior authorization survey; AJMC article on continuity of care; AMA article on appeals; HIV Plus, Aimed Alliance, PLoS One hepatitis C article.
- **Examples provided:** HIV viral suppression risk, hepatitis treatment interruptions, AFPs delaying specialty drug access, recurring PA for stable lifelong treatment.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** People with HIV, hepatitis, and chronic illnesses; physicians and provider staff; QHP and Medicare enrollees; payers; specialty-drug patients affected by AFPs.
- **Economic implications:** PA delays may increase plan costs through worse outcomes and hospitalizations; AFPs create patient financial exposure because outside assistance does not count toward deductibles.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports API-driven automation and data exchange, but stresses that electronic processes must not amplify improper PA use.
- **Patient care impact:** Very high. Timely access to medication is framed as essential to adherence, viral suppression, chronic disease management, and avoidance of disease progression.

Re: Proposed Rule and RFI; Interoperability Standards & Prior Authorization for Drugs (CMS-0062-P) 
 
Dear Dr. Oz:  
We are pleased that the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) has issued the Interoperability 
Standards & Prior Authorization for Drugs proposed rule. Filling a gap left by previously finalized 
interoperability rules, this rule will align standards and transparency requirements across medical 
services and prescription drug benefits creating a more uniform expectation for patients who encounter 
prior authorization during their health care experience. People with serious, chronic conditions like HIV 
and hepatitis rely on consistent and timely access to prescription medications to manage and maintain 
their health; modernizing and streamlining the prior authorization process will alleviate harmful 
treatment interruptions due to delayed approvals. The AIDS Institute believes this rule moves the 
healthcare industry in the right direction to improve patient access, reduce provider burden, and 
decrease system inefficiencies. 
 
Prior authorization approval as a utilization management technique gives health insurance plans the 
opportunity to evaluate whether a given prescription or treatment is medically necessary and is used to 
contain costs by reducing unnecessary, duplicative, or excessive care. However, far too often prior 
authorization serves as a bureaucratic hurdle that creates extra work for both patients and providers 
and interrupts access to appropriate and necessary care without the added benefit it claims.1 As new 
policies and electronic processes are introduced to create a more efficient prior authorization system, 
CMS must ensure it doesn’t lead to over- or improper use, magnifying the burden patients often 
experience now....
