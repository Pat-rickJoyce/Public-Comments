## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Consumers for Quality Care (CQC).
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** CQC describes itself as a coalition of advocates and former policy makers partnering with health advocacy organizations to provide a patient voice in health care debates.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CQC strongly supports the proposed rule's overall approach to making prior authorization evidence-based, transparent, and immediate. It supports expedited drug PA decisions, electronic prior authorization, detailed denial justifications, robust appeals, step therapy transparency, and recognition of step therapy already completed under a prior insurer.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI step therapy; transparency-related API provisions.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR IG, NCPDP, HL7, or CFR standards are named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Overall prior authorization reform**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CQC argues current PA and step therapy policies delay needed medications, treatments, surgeries, and other care, increase costs, and can worsen patient conditions.
- **Specific request:** Continue and finalize reforms that make PA more evidence-based, transparent, immediate, and patient-centered.

**Topic 2: Expedited drug PA timelines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Faster decisions would reduce patient and provider burden and help prevent delayed care.
- **Specific request:** Require 24-hour decisions for all drugs and ensure expedited requirements do not cause unnecessary denials.

**Topic 3: Denial justifications and appeals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients need to understand why care is denied and have a meaningful way to challenge denials.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed justification for all denials and a robust appeals process.

**Topic 4: Electronic prior authorization and status tracking**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING/TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA would let doctors request and track PA status more efficiently and in a timelier manner.
- **Specific request:** Implement electronic PA to streamline information sharing between insurers, providers, and patients.

**Topic 5: Step therapy transparency and continuity across plans**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can force patients to fail on treatments before the prescribed therapy is covered, causing serious health deterioration and hospitalization.
- **Specific request:** Require insurers to disclose step therapy requirements, build exceptions and strong appeals into step therapy systems, and honor completed step therapy when patients change insurers.

**Topic 6: Prior authorization reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CQC supports reporting as part of the rule's transparency and accountability framework.
- **Specific request:** Implement prior authorization decision and reporting requirements, though the comment does not specify the exact metric set.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S21:** CQC supports electronic prior authorization for drugs because it allows doctors to request and track PA status more quickly and efficiently.
- **S16/S17:** The comment asks CMS to require detailed justification for all denials and a robust appeals process.
- **S20/S43:** CQC supports expedited timelines, specifically 24-hour decisions for all drugs, and asks CMS to ensure faster timelines do not produce unnecessary denials.
- **S29/S34/S35:** The comment supports prior authorization reporting generally as part of the rule's transparency framework, without specifying detailed metric fields.
- **S66-S82 (General/RFI topics, including step therapy):** CQC directly responds to the Step Therapy RFI by asking for disclosure of step therapy requirements, exceptions, appeals, and cross-plan recognition of completed step therapy.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize the proposed rule's overall approach to prior authorization reform.
2. Require 24-hour decisions for all drug prior authorization requests.
3. Ensure expedited PA timelines do not lead to unnecessary denials.
4. Require detailed justifications for all denials.
5. Require a robust appeals process.
6. Implement electronic PA so doctors can request and track PA status efficiently.
7. Require insurers to disclose when step therapy is required.
8. Require step therapy exceptions and strong appeals processes.
9. Require insurers to honor completed step therapy when a patient changes plans.
10. Continue streamlining health information sharing between insurers, providers, and patients.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delayed care, step therapy/fail-first policies, unnecessary denials, insufficient transparency, provider burden, and lack of cross-plan recognition of prior step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Hospital stays, increased costs, worsening patient conditions, delayed access to care, serious adverse events, and patients going untreated.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Complicated prior authorization requirements, insurer step therapy policies, and lack of transparency about step therapy requirements.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Pew Research poll finding 73% of Americans say health care affordability is a very big problem; average physicians complete 31 PA requests weekly, taking about two days of physician and staff time; AMA survey of 1,000 physicians found nine in ten reported PA delayed necessary care and more than 25% said delays led to a serious adverse event.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Pew Research poll; American Medical Association survey.
- **Examples provided:** Joel Hechler, diagnosed with Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, experienced a six-week fight with Premera Blue Cross over Remicade due to step therapy and became so ill he was hospitalized.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, providers, insurers, health advocacy organizations, people needing medications or other care, and patients changing insurance plans.
- **Economic implications:** CQC connects PA and step therapy delays to increased costs, hospital stays, rising affordability concerns, and physician/staff time diverted from patient care.
- **Technical feasibility:** CQC supports electronic PA and streamlined information sharing but does not discuss implementation constraints.
- **Patient care impact:** The comment emphasizes that delayed approvals and step therapy can worsen conditions, cause hospitalization, and delay access to recommended treatment.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with requested strengthening.
- **Tone:** Professional, patient-centered, and critical of insurer delay practices.
- **Priority level:** High.
