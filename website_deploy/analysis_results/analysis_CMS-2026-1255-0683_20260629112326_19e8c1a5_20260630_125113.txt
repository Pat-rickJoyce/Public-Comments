# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; signed by Carrie Harney, Executive Director, Public Policy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Other - Pharmaceutical manufacturer / biopharmaceutical company
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Research-driven biopharmaceutical company developing therapies; comments focus on patient access to medications and therapies affected by prior authorization and utilization management.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Boehringer Ingelheim supports CMS's proposed prior authorization and interoperability reforms but recommends expanded transparency into prior authorization, utilization management, appeals, denial rationales, AI-assisted reviews, and patient access outcomes. The company urges CMS to add per-drug and therapy-level reporting, time-to-therapy metrics, Part D reporting, continuity-of-care protections, and step therapy safeguards for stable patients and plan transitions.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY by drug-reporting scope; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS/GENERAL public PA metrics; GENERAL/RFI step therapy and continuity of care.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P. No CFR sections are cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Interoperability/data sharing generally; no specific FHIR IG, NCPDP, HL7, or HIPAA standard is named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Expanded PA reporting and per-drug transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Boehringer argues that patients, providers, plans, and CMS need meaningful visibility into how PA and UM practices operate across plans and affect access.
- **Specific request:** Require reporting and disaggregation of PA denials, coverage determinations, and UM data on a per-drug basis, including denial rates by brand/generic status and therapeutic class.

**Topic 2: Prescription rejection and pharmacy channel reporting**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Initial claims rejections and differences between affiliated and non-affiliated pharmacy channels may reveal access barriers that PA-only metrics miss.
- **Specific request:** Require reporting on the total number and percentage of claims initially rejected for medications and denial rates at affiliated versus non-affiliated pharmacies.

**Topic 3: Timing, format, and accessibility of public data**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Public reporting is only useful if stakeholders can access timely, usable, meaningful data.
- **Specific request:** Clarify expectations for timing, format, and accessibility of publicly reported data.

**Topic 4: Time-to-therapy and treatment abandonment metrics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Administrative processing timelines do not fully capture patient experience or delays in starting therapy.
- **Specific request:** Require metrics for time from prescription submission to initial decision, denial to appeal decision, appeal to therapy initiation, prescription abandonment, and treatment drop-off after PA delays or rejections.

**Topic 5: Include Medicare Part D in reporting scope**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug access barriers in Part D are central to the patient-access concerns raised by the company.
- **Specific request:** Expand PA metric reporting requirements to Medicare Part D and make UM policies publicly available.

**Topic 6: Appeals, overturns, exception requests, denial rationales, and AI oversight**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** OIG findings and provider surveys suggest some UM practices may improperly restrict access and that AI-assisted reviews could affect denial patterns.
- **Specific request:** Report appeals, overturns, exception requests, denial rationales, AI use in initial reviews or denials, and AI-related denials later overturned.

**Topic 7: Continuity-of-care protections**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA and UM requirements can disrupt patients stable on therapy and create unnecessary delays for continuation or maintenance care.
- **Specific request:** Distinguish new therapy initiation from ongoing treatment, streamline or limit PA for stable patients, and extend transition protections to patients changing plans.

**Topic 8: Step therapy safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy may be clinically inappropriate, overly restrictive compared with clinical guidelines, and burdensome when repeated after plan changes.
- **Specific request:** Require clinically appropriate step therapy, timely exception processes, and policies preventing stable patients or those who already satisfied step therapy from repeating protocols solely because they changed plans.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** The company asks CMS to expand transparency into denial rationales so patients, providers, and CMS can understand why requests are denied.
- **S17:** The comment supports more detailed denial information and reporting but does not specifically request structured denial codes.
- **S20:** Boehringer supports stronger accountability around PA timeliness and requests time-to-therapy metrics that capture actual patient access delays.
- **S21:** The commenter supports CMS's drug PA reforms while asking CMS to broaden reporting beyond administrative processing timelines.
- **S28:** The company asks CMS to expand PA reporting into Medicare Part D and make UM policies publicly available.
- **S29:** Boehringer recommends numerous metrics, including per-drug denials, coverage determinations, initial rejections, pharmacy channel denial rates, appeals, overturns, exceptions, abandonment, and treatment drop-off.
- **S34:** The comment asks CMS to clarify timing of public reporting so data are timely and meaningful.
- **S35:** The company supports public availability of detailed, disaggregated metrics rather than high-level aggregate reporting alone.
- **S43:** The comment supports timeliness metrics and continuity protections to reduce therapy interruptions.
- **S66-S82:** The company responds to the step therapy RFI by urging clinically appropriate step therapy, rapid exceptions, and continuity protections during plan transitions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require per-drug reporting of PA denials, coverage determinations, and related UM data.
2. Report denial rates by brand versus generic products and by therapeutic class.
3. Report medication claims initially rejected.
4. Report denial rates at affiliated versus non-affiliated pharmacies.
5. Clarify timing, format, and accessibility requirements for public data.
6. Require time-to-therapy metrics from prescription submission through decision, appeal, and therapy initiation.
7. Report prescription abandonment and treatment drop-off after PA delays, denials, or rejections.
8. Expand PA metric reporting requirements to Medicare Part D.
9. Require public availability of UM policies.
10. Report appeals, overturns, exception requests, and denial rationales.
11. Require transparency when AI is used in initial PA reviews or denials and track AI-related denials later overturned.
12. Strengthen continuity-of-care protections for patients stable on therapy or transitioning between plans.
13. Prevent unnecessary repeated step therapy for patients who are stable or have already satisfied prior step therapy requirements.
14. Require timely step therapy exceptions when step therapy is ineffective or medically inappropriate.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Insufficient PA transparency, lack of per-drug reporting, inadequate patient-experience metrics, Part D exclusion, unclear UM policies, inappropriate denials, AI-assisted denial risks, treatment disruption, and overly restrictive step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed treatment initiation, treatment abandonment, adverse events, increased administrative burden, disrupted continuity of care, and poorer outcomes for patients stable on medication.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited current reporting granularity, lack of standardized public data expectations, appeals/exception opacity, plan transitions, and UM practices that differ from clinical guidelines.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA 2024 survey: 93% of physicians report PA delays care; 82% report PA can lead to treatment abandonment; 29% report PA has resulted in a serious adverse event. 2026 JAMA Health Forum study: among branded prescriptions initially rejected due to PA, 35% were processed within one day, remaining prescriptions had a median six-day processing delay, and 54% were ultimately approved. 2022 HHS OIG report: 13% of MA-denied PA requests met coverage rules and likely should have been approved. AMA 2025 survey: 60% of physicians are concerned AI will increase denial rates; 95% report PA requirements delay necessary care.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA 2024 and 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Surveys; Wang et al. 2026 JAMA Health Forum study; 2022 HHS OIG report on MA denials; Westrich et al. Health Affairs 2025 step therapy/UM study; AGA step therapy access resource; CMS Medicare Part D Benefits Manual.
- **Examples provided:** Branded medication prescriptions rejected due to PA; affiliated versus non-affiliated pharmacy denials; AI-assisted denials; stable patients facing PA for continuation therapy; patients changing plans and being required to repeat step therapy.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing medications and therapies, providers, plans, Medicare Part D beneficiaries, MA beneficiaries, pharmaceutical manufacturers, pharmacies, and CMS oversight staff.
- **Economic implications:** The comment does not provide implementation cost estimates, but argues that PA/UM delays increase administrative burden and may contribute to treatment abandonment and adverse events.
- **Technical feasibility:** Boehringer views expanded reporting and interoperability/data sharing as achievable policy tools, though it asks CMS to clarify reporting timing, format, accessibility, and scope.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment links PA delays, appeals, prescription rejections, step therapy, and plan transitions to delayed therapy initiation, treatment abandonment, adverse events, and disrupted continuity of care.
