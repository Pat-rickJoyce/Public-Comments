## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NHHF supports expanding electronic prior authorization for medications because real-time coverage and request information would streamline physician workflows and improve patient access. The comment asks CMS to enforce shortened timelines, require detailed denial justifications, expand public PA metric reporting, publish annual summaries, and scrutinize step therapy, especially in Medicare Advantage.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization mechanisms generally; no named FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for medications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Real-time coverage and request information would help physicians complete PAs more efficiently.
- **Specific request:** Expand electronic PA mechanisms for medications.

**Topic 2: Shortened PA timelines and denial safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Faster timelines would reduce disruption to essential medication access, but expedited decisions should not produce wrongful denials.
- **Specific request:** Shorten approval timelines to 24 hours for urgent requests and 72 hours for standard requests, monitor health plans, and require detailed denial justifications.

**Topic 3: Public reporting of PA data and metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Detailed metrics would help CMS and the public understand the real impact of prior authorization.
- **Specific request:** Require all payers, including Medicare Part D plans, to publicly report detailed PA metrics on initial denials, step therapy requirements, and differences between brand-name and generic drugs; CMS should analyze and summarize metrics in an annual report.

**Topic 4: Step therapy scrutiny and standardization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify/Oppose in Medicare Advantage
- **Rationale:** Patients should not be forced to try alternatives before receiving physician-prescribed therapy, and patients changing plans should not be surprised by undisclosed step therapy requirements.
- **Specific request:** Prevent Medicare Advantage plans from using step therapy and require other payers to disclose and standardize step therapy practices.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20/S43:** The commenter supports shortened drug PA timelines, specifically 24 hours for urgent requests and 72 hours for standard requests, with oversight to prevent wrongful denials.
- **S16/S17:** The commenter asks CMS to require detailed denial justifications so expedited timelines do not translate into unsupported denials.
- **S29/S34/S35:** The comment recommends public reporting of detailed PA data and metrics and asks CMS to publish annual analyses of those metrics.
- **S66-S82 (General/RFI topics, including step therapy):** The comment asks CMS to scrutinize step therapy, bar Medicare Advantage step therapy, and standardize disclosure by other payers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Expand electronic prior authorization mechanisms for medications.
2. Implement 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard decision timelines.
3. Monitor health plans for wrongful denials under expedited timelines.
4. Require detailed denial justifications.
5. Require all payers, including Medicare Part D plans, to publicly report PA metrics.
6. Include initial denials, step therapy requirements, and brand-name versus generic drug differences in reported metrics.
7. Publish CMS annual reports summarizing PA metrics.
8. Prevent Medicare Advantage plans from using step therapy and require other payers to disclose and standardize step therapy.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
2 
 
CMS's plans to expand electronic prior authorization mechanisms for medications would help physicians 
streamline their process for completing prior authorizations while allowing them to access real-time 
coverage and request information.  
 
We also applaud CMS's proposal to shorten approval timelines to 24 hours for urgent requests and 72 
hours for standard requests. This change will allow patients to receive medications in a timely manner, 
with fewer disruptions to essential care. We urge the agency to monitor health plans and require payers to 
provide detailed denial justifications so that the new expedited requirements do not cause wrongful 
coverage denials.  
 
We also recommend that CMS require all payers -- including Medicare Part D plans -- to publicly report 
detailed prior authorization data and metrics, including information on initial denials, step therapy 
requirements, and differences between brand-name and generic drugs. This will allow CMS to gain a 
more complete picture of the impact of prior authorization. To maximize transparency, CMS ought to 
analyze and summarize these metrics in an annual report. 
 
Finally, we ask CMS to scrutinize plans' use of step therapy. In particular, CMS ought to prevent 
Medicare Advantage plans from using step therapy -- these patients should not be forced to try 
alternatives before insurers cover the therapy that their doctor has prescribed. And the agency must ensure 
that other payers properly disclose and standardize their use of step therapy, such that patients are not 
blindsided when they change plans. 
 
Thank you for the opportunity to comment on these important reforms. We remain grateful that CMS is 
addressing these challenges and crafting meaningful reforms to improve patient care. NHHF is ready to 
help CMS improve coverage for Hispanics in any way possible.  
 
 
Sincerely, 
 
 
Elena Rios, MD, MSPH, MACP
