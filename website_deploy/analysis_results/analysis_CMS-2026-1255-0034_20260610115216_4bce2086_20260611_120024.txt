## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Obesity Medicine Association (OMA); signed by Teresa Fraker, FACHE, RN, CPHQ, CAE, Executive Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** OMA represents physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, and other clinicians specializing in obesity medicine.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

OMA generally supports CMS's interoperability and electronic drug prior authorization proposals, including NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and Real-Time Prescription Benefit requirements. It asks CMS to align drug PA timeframes toward a 24-hour maximum, require drug-specific and therapeutic-category reporting, retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs, improve step therapy data exchange, and clarify how requirements apply to emerging obesity medication coverage models.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically.
- **Standards referenced:** API technology; NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit; Patient Access API; Provider Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Provider Directory API.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Interoperability API standards**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized interoperability requirements promote consistent exchange of PA, formulary, and utilization-management information and reduce varying payer workflows.
- **Specific request:** Finalize clear standardized API requirements for impacted payers.

**Topic 2: Electronic PA for pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Alignment across impacted payers, Part D sponsors, prescribers, dispensers, and federal initiatives can improve transparency and reduce inconsistent processes.
- **Specific request:** Require support for unexpired NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and RTPB standards.

**Topic 3: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Detailed denial communications help physicians understand coverage barriers, resubmit, and appeal when obesity medications are denied.
- **Specific request:** Require specific denial reasons for all drug PA requests regardless of method.

**Topic 4: Decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Inconsistent timeframes across impacted payers can delay access and disrupt continuity of obesity and chronic disease treatment.
- **Specific request:** Continue rulemaking toward a 24-hour maximum prescription drug PA response timeframe across impacted payers, including Medicare Advantage.

**Topic 5: Public PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Aggregate metrics may not reveal intensive utilization management affecting obesity medications.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed, drug-specific and therapeutic-category reporting for categories frequently subject to intensive UM, including obesity medications.

**Topic 6: Formulary information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Oppose/Modify
- **Rationale:** Provider websites and standard communications are insufficient because coverage criteria and preferred product status change frequently.
- **Specific request:** Do not remove formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 7: Step therapy and payer transitions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Duplicative step therapy, prior treatment history gaps, and formulary changes disrupt clinically effective obesity treatment.
- **Specific request:** Develop data exchange tools that recognize prior payer determinations, step therapy history, and UM information across impacted payers.

**Topic 8: Emerging obesity medication models**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** New models may create fragmentation if standards, timeframes, denial reasons, and reporting do not apply consistently.
- **Specific request:** Clarify applicability to the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Model, BALANCE Model, central processors, and Part D plans under those models.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** OMA supports applying NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB pharmacy benefit standards across impacted payers.
- **S16:** OMA supports requiring specific denial reasons for drug PA requests.
- **S19/S22:** OMA supports cross-payer pharmacy standard alignment and sees it as burden-reducing, though it does not provide detailed state readiness or cost estimates.
- **S20/S43:** OMA urges alignment toward a 24-hour maximum response timeframe across impacted payers, including Medicare Advantage.
- **S29/S35:** OMA supports public reporting of drug PA metrics and requests more granular drug-specific and therapeutic-category data.
- **S66-S82:** OMA responds to step therapy and continuity-of-care RFIs by recommending exchange of prior payer determinations, step therapy history, and utilization-management information.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize standardized API requirements for impacted payers.
2. Require support for unexpired NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and RTPB standards.
3. Require specific denial reasons for all drug PA denials.
4. Align prescription drug PA decision timeframes toward a 24-hour maximum across impacted payers.
5. Require drug-specific and therapeutic-category PA reporting, especially for obesity medications.
6. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
7. Develop interoperability tools that preserve prior PA determinations, step therapy history, and UM information across payer transitions.
8. Clarify applicability of PA and interoperability requirements to Medicare GLP-1 Bridge and BALANCE model entities.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Lack of payer alignment, removal of formulary data from APIs, duplicative step therapy, inconsistent obesity medication coverage criteria, and unclear treatment of emerging coverage/payment models.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, disrupted continuity of care, repeated documentation, therapy interruption, forced switching, and administrative burden for physician practices and patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Changing coverage criteria, variable documentation requirements, payer transitions, changing formularies, and fragmented models.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None quantified.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** GLP-1 therapies, dose escalation, continuation therapy, prior treatment attempts, lifestyle program participation, Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Model, BALANCE Model, obesity medications subject to step therapy and changing formularies.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Obesity medicine clinicians, patients with obesity, impacted payers, Part D sponsors, Medicaid/CHIP programs, QHP issuers, prescribers, dispensers, and model participants.
- **Economic implications:** Standardization may reduce administrative burden; inconsistent UM may increase costs through repeated submissions and disrupted care.
- **Technical feasibility:** OMA supports standardized NCPDP and API implementation, with added data exchange for step therapy and formulary information.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong focus on continuity of obesity medication therapy and avoiding delays or interruptions.
