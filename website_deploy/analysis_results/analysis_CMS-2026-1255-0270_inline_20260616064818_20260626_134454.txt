## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to adopt patient-centered prior authorization protections for critical diagnostic testing, especially oncology and other time-sensitive tests. The comment focuses on preventing denials and patient financial harm caused by pending authorizations, redundant specimen collection, and paperwork delays.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to expedited authorization timing, although the comment concerns diagnostic tests rather than drug PA.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Timing-Based Denials for Critical Tests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not lose coverage for a medically necessary test solely because the authorization process was still pending when the specimen was collected.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit insurers from denying critical tests on this timing basis.

**Topic 2: Expedited Review and Patient Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics can affect treatment timelines, and administrative delays should not trigger repeat procedures or surprise bills.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review pathways, prevent redundant blood draws when a valid specimen exists, and ban financial penalties for patients, providers, and labs caused by insurer paperwork delays.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

No specific numbered solicitation is directly addressed. The comment is broadly relevant to general RFI issues involving diagnostic tests/labs and to prior authorization timing and denial-reason policy themes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Stop timing-based denials for critical tests when authorization was pending on the collection date.
2. Prevent repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is already available.
3. Establish mandatory expedited review for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic tests.
4. Bar surprise bills or other financial penalties caused by insurance paperwork delays outside the control of patients, clinicians, or labs.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative timing denials, redundant procedures, delayed cancer care, and surprise financial liability.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Painful repeat blood draws, delayed diagnosis/treatment, and costs shifted to patients, doctors, and laboratories.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Insurance paperwork delays and prior authorization processes that do not accommodate time-sensitive testing.
