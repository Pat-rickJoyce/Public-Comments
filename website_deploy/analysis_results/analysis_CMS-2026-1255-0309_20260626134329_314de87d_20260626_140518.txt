## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AMGA generally supports the proposed rule's extension of prior authorization and interoperability requirements to drugs, but urges CMS to strengthen several provisions. Key recommendations include standardizing a uniform 24-hour PA decision timeframe across all payer types, establishing a default-to-approval policy when payers miss deadlines, extending the Provider Access API to out-of-network providers with a documented care relationship, mandating minimum content standards for denial notices, and creating a Gold Carding framework to exempt high-performing providers from drug PA requirements.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Uniform 24-Hour Standard Decision Timeframe**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The proposed 72-hour standard for QHP issuers on FFEs is inconsistent with the 24-hour standard proposed for Medicaid/CHIP; clinical urgency of a PA request does not vary by payer type; weekend submissions under a 72-hour window can create delays approaching a full business week; providers caring for patients across multiple payer types face unnecessary complexity tracking different standards.
- **Specific request:** Reduce QHP standard timeframe to 24 hours and apply a uniform 24-hour standard across all impacted payers (MA, Medicaid/CHIP MCOs, QHPs); measure all timeframes in continuous hours, not business or calendar days.

**Topic 2: Default-to-Approval for Missed Deadlines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS-reported data show ~92% of MA PA requests approved initially; ~80% of appealed denials overturned; without a consequence for missing deadlines, payers face no structural incentive to comply; burden of chasing unanswered requests falls on providers and patients; delayed chemotherapy, biologic infusions, and maintenance therapies carry measurable clinical consequences.
- **Specific request:** Require any drug PA request not decided within the applicable standard or expedited timeframe be deemed automatically approved.

**Topic 3: Provider Access API — Out-of-Network Providers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL (and general Provider Access API)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Specialty drug patients often receive care from multiple providers across networks; limiting API access to contracted providers creates incomplete medication histories; patients in underserved communities more likely to rely on out-of-network providers due to limited specialist availability, creating a two-tier care coordination system.
- **Specific request:** Extend Provider Access API access to any provider with a documented care relationship, regardless of network status; define "care relationship" broadly to include providers who have furnished services or are actively involved in ongoing care.

**Topic 4: Gold Carding for Drug Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI response on step therapy)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers with demonstrated appropriate prescribing records add limited utilization management value from PA/step therapy requirements; state-level models in West Virginia and Texas demonstrate feasibility of 90% approval-rate threshold triggering PA exemption; particularly applicable in high-PA specialties like oncology, rheumatology, and neurology.
- **Specific request:** Establish mandatory Gold Carding framework exempting providers with >=90% drug PA approval rate over a six-month period for specified drug categories; apply automatically from existing payer data; evaluate additional exemptions for risk-bearing value-based care providers.

**Topic 5: Denial Reason Specificity and Minimum Content Standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify/Strengthen
- **Rationale:** Without minimum content standards, denials may continue to rely on boilerplate language such as "not medically necessary" that provides no actionable guidance; value of the requirement depends entirely on how CMS defines and enforces "specific."
- **Specific request:** Codify minimum content standards for denial notifications requiring: (1) the specific clinical criterion or coverage requirement not met; (2) documentation or clinical information needed for approval; (3) specific plan policy or clinical guideline cited; (4) electronic delivery through the PA API in structured, machine-readable format.

**Topic 6: Value-Based Care Exemptions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI response on event notifications and care coordination)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers in risk-bearing arrangements already have financial incentives to avoid unnecessary services; applying PA requirements in these contexts is duplicative and counterproductive.
- **Specific request:** CMS should collect data on drug PA volume and cost within risk-bearing arrangements and develop a regulatory pathway for exemptions; evaluate targeted exemptions when providers bear financial responsibility for the services at issue.

**Topic 7: Extension to Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI response)
- **Position:** Support/Expand
- **Rationale:** Operational challenges for lab and DMEPOS PA are substantially similar to drug PA; high-volume burden for AMGA members in primary care, oncology, and post-acute care.
- **Specific request:** Extend electronic PA requirements, decision timeframes, and denial transparency standards to lab tests and DMEPOS in future rulemaking.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Recommends minimum content standards for "specific reason" — denial must identify the unmet clinical criterion, documentation needed, and the specific guideline or plan policy supporting the determination.
- **S17:** Requests that denial reasons be communicated in structured, machine-readable format through the Prior Authorization API; should be codified in regulation rather than left to payer discretion.
- **S20:** Argues 24-hour timeframe is achievable and should be the uniform standard across all payers, not just Medicaid/CHIP; 72 hours for QHPs is inconsistent and clinically unjustifiable.
- **S43:** Supports aligning timeframes across all payer types; urges consistent measurement in continuous hours rather than business/calendar days.
- **S66 (RFI - Event Notifications/Care Coordination):** Recommends CMS collect data on drug PA burden in risk-bearing arrangements and develop a pathway for value-based care exemptions.
- **S67/step therapy RFI:** Recommends mandatory Gold Carding framework (90% approval rate threshold for six-month period) with automatic application from payer data; cites West Virginia and Texas state models.
- **S82/Lab-DMEPOS RFI:** Recommends extending electronic PA requirements, decision timeframes, and denial standards to laboratory tests and DMEPOS in future rulemaking.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Reduce QHP standard drug PA decision timeframe from 72 to 24 hours, aligning with the Medicaid/CHIP standard.
2. Apply a uniform 24-hour standard decision timeframe across all impacted payers (MA, Medicaid/CHIP MCOs, QHPs).
3. Measure all PA decision timeframes in continuous hours, not business or calendar days.
4. Require any drug PA request not adjudicated within the applicable timeframe to be automatically deemed approved.
5. Extend the Provider Access API to all providers with a documented care relationship, regardless of network participation status; define "care relationship" broadly.
6. Establish a mandatory Gold Carding framework exempting providers with >=90% PA approval rate over six months for specified drug categories, applied automatically from payer data.
7. Exempt providers in risk-bearing value-based arrangements from step therapy requirements.
8. Codify minimum content standards for drug PA denial notifications in regulation.
9. Require PA denial reasons to be delivered through the PA API in structured, machine-readable format.
10. Collect data on drug PA administrative burden within value-based care arrangements; develop pathway for future exemptions.
11. Extend electronic PA requirements, decision timeframes, and denial standards to laboratory tests and DMEPOS in future rulemaking.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** 72-hour QHP standard is inconsistent with 24-hour Medicaid/CHIP standard and clinically unjustifiable; absence of default-to-approval renders deadlines unenforceable; restricting Provider Access API to in-network providers creates incomplete medication histories and health equity disparities; boilerplate denial language undermines the value of the denial-reason requirement without minimum content standards.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays in chemotherapy, biologic infusions, and discharge medications; care fragmentation for patients with complex conditions; two-tier care coordination system disadvantaging patients in underserved communities; administrative waste when providers must chase unanswered PA requests.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No specific implementation barriers raised — comment focuses on urging stronger requirements rather than objecting to feasibility.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CMS-reported Medicare Advantage data: >92% of PA requests approved initially; >80% of appealed denials ultimately overturned; UnitedHealthcare reported ~92% approval in Medicaid/CHIP and ~95% in Medicare Advantage.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF (2024) Medicare Advantage prior authorization determinations data; Freed, M. et al., "Insurers' Prior Authorization Data Offers Little Insight Into What Gets Approved or Denied," KFF, April 3, 2026.
- **Examples provided:** West Virginia and Texas Gold Carding models (90% approval rate threshold over six months); Thursday specialty drug infusion request example illustrating weekend delay risk under 72-hour standard; oncologist managing chemotherapy needing cross-network medication history.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Physicians and multispecialty medical groups (AMGA members); patients with complex/specialty drug needs; patients in underserved communities relying on out-of-network specialists; providers in value-based care arrangements.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced administrative costs for providers through Gold Carding; reduced re-work from more actionable denial notices; potential reduction in unnecessary PA administrative overhead within risk-bearing arrangements.
- **Technical feasibility:** Comments note existing payer data is sufficient for automatic Gold Carding application; machine-readable denial reasons through PA API described as feasible "where technically feasible."
- **Patient care impact:** Significant — delays in specialty drugs, biologics, and infusion therapies have direct clinical consequences; out-of-network API access affects care coordination for complex patients; uniform 24-hour standard would reduce care gaps for patients across payer types.

1. "A 72-hour window for standard drug requests does not reflect the realities of clinical care." - Context: Arguing for uniform 24-hour standard, noting weekend submission risks can create week-long delays.

2. "The absence of a default-to-approval rule renders the deadline itself unenforceable as a practical matter." - Context: Urging default-to-approval consequence for missed PA deadlines, noting payers face no structural incentive without it.

3. "Restricting data access to in-network providers creates a two-tier system in which patients with the most complex needs and the least access receive the least coordinated care." - Context: Recommending Provider Access API extension to out-of-network providers with care relationship.

4. "The value of this requirement will depend entirely on how CMS defines and enforces the term 'specific.'" - Context: Supporting denial reason requirement but urging codified minimum content standards to prevent continued boilerplate responses.
