# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Tribal Biotechnology & Health Consortium, submitted by Clear Health Pass Holdings, LLC as Authorized Tribal Agent; signed by John Michael Cataldi, CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Government / Other.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** The Consortium comprises federally recognized Tribal Nations and tribally owned enterprises engaged in biotechnology and health-product development. It identifies ECHAGA Corporation of the Pine Ridge Oglala Sioux Tribe and Blue Lake Rancheria Economic Development Corporation and states that its members serve AI/AN beneficiaries through and alongside the IHS/Tribal/Urban Indian delivery system.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Consortium argues that CMS-0062-P has tribal implications because it affects Medicaid, CHIP, Medicare Advantage, QHPs, AI/AN beneficiaries, I/T/U providers, and access to tribally owned FDA-regulated products. It requests formal Government-to-Government consultation before finalization, an anti-discrimination provision for tribally owned and I/T/U-furnished products, disaggregated PA metrics for AI/AN and tribal impacts, and confirmation that the rule satisfies the Indian Health Care Improvement Act and federal trust responsibility.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING/public reporting; REGULATORY-AUTHORITY; HEALTH-EQUITY; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Executive Order 13175; HHS and CMS Tribal Consultation Policies; Indian Health Care Improvement Act, 25 U.S.C. §§ 1601 et seq.; Administrative Procedure Act; treaty obligations. No CFR sections are cited.
- **Standards referenced:** No technical interoperability standards are discussed. The comment refers generally to the proposed prior authorization architecture for drugs and utilization management criteria.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Tribal Consultation Requirement**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; REGULATORY-AUTHORITY.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** The Consortium states the rule has tribal implications because it governs programs with substantial AI/AN enrollment and I/T/U participation and affects access to care through PA criteria, timeframes, and transparency.
- **Specific request:** CMS should state whether formal G2G tribal consultation occurred before publication and, if not, conduct consultation before finalizing the rule.

**Topic 2: Anti-Discrimination in Prior Authorization Criteria**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** The Consortium says PA can be used to deny access to tribally owned, FDA-regulated, Medicare-recognized products through "unproven" or "investigational" determinations inconsistent with FDA status or Medicare recognition.
- **Specific request:** Include an express provision barring PA criteria that disadvantage FDA-cleared or FDA-approved products based on tribal ownership or I/T/U furnishing and barring inconsistent unproven/investigational designations.

**Topic 3: Disaggregated Public Reporting for Tribal Impacts**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; TRANSPARENCY.
- **Position:** Support and expand.
- **Rationale:** The Consortium supports transparency and metrics reporting but argues aggregate data will not reveal impacts on AI/AN beneficiaries, I/T/U providers, or tribally owned products.
- **Specific request:** Require public PA metrics to be disaggregated for AI/AN beneficiaries, I/T/U providers, and tribally owned products.

**Topic 4: Federal Trust Responsibility and IHCIA Compliance**
- **Provision ID:** REGULATORY-AUTHORITY; HEALTH-EQUITY; GENERAL.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** The Consortium asserts CMS must ensure program rules do not impede AI/AN access through the I/T/U system.
- **Specific request:** Confirm the final rule is consistent with the Indian Health Care Improvement Act and federal trust responsibility and does not impose barriers on I/T/U providers or AI/AN beneficiaries.

**Topic 5: Drug PA Architecture for Tribally Owned Biologics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY.
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify.
- **Rationale:** The Consortium states tribally owned biologic products advancing through the FDA Section 351 pathway will fall within the drug PA architecture established by this rule.
- **Specific request:** Account for tribally owned biologics in the rule's design and respond to the comment in the final-rule preamble.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The comment addresses medical benefit drug PA by noting that tribally owned biologics and other products may fall within the drug PA architecture and should not face discriminatory criteria.
- **S14:** The comment applies its concerns broadly to drug prior authorization across impacted programs and benefit types, including access to tribally owned products.
- **S16:** The Consortium asks CMS to prohibit "unproven" or "investigational" determinations inconsistent with FDA status or Medicare coverage, which implicates the specificity and validity of denial reasons.
- **S17:** The comment does not propose specific structured codes but asks that denial/reporting frameworks surface discriminatory or unsupported determinations affecting tribal products.
- **S29:** The Consortium supports metrics reporting and asks for data capable of identifying approval and denial patterns for AI/AN beneficiaries, I/T/U providers, and tribally owned products.
- **S30:** The requested disaggregation raises privacy-sensitive population reporting considerations, though the comment does not propose specific privacy safeguards.
- **S35:** The Consortium supports public reporting and asks CMS to publish disaggregated PA approval and denial metrics to reveal tribal impacts.
- **S66-S82:** The comment is a general equity, tribal consultation, and trust-responsibility response not tied to a specific technical solicitation.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. State on the record whether formal G2G tribal consultation occurred for CMS-0062-P.
2. Conduct formal G2G tribal consultation before finalizing the rule if it has not already occurred.
3. Add an anti-discrimination provision barring PA criteria that disadvantage FDA-regulated, tribally owned, or I/T/U-furnished products.
4. Prohibit unproven/investigational designations inconsistent with FDA status or Medicare coverage.
5. Require disaggregated public reporting of PA approvals and denials for AI/AN beneficiaries, I/T/U providers, and tribally owned products.
6. Confirm consistency with the Indian Health Care Improvement Act and federal trust responsibility.
7. Respond to the comment in the final-rule preamble.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Lack of tribal consultation, discriminatory PA criteria, unsupported investigational designations, aggregate metrics masking tribal impacts, and inconsistency with trust obligations.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Reduced access for AI/AN beneficiaries, barriers for I/T/U providers, denial of tribally owned FDA-regulated products, and impairment of tribal economic self-determination.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Insufficient consultation, lack of disaggregated reporting, payer discretion in coverage criteria, and failure to evaluate the rule against the federal trust responsibility.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** The comment states CMS designated the Consortium's product as "Tribal" on December 15, 2025 and says it was the only such designation among more than 330 products.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Tribally owned FDA-regulated, Medicare-recognized products; I/T/U-furnished products; biologics advancing through the FDA Section 351 biologics-license pathway; payer use of unproven/investigational labels.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** AI/AN beneficiaries, Tribal Nations, tribally owned enterprises, I/T/U providers, impacted payers, CMS, and developers/owners of tribally owned biologics and health products.
- **Economic implications:** The comment ties PA access to tribal economic self-determination and market access for tribally owned biotechnology products.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment does not assess API technical feasibility; it focuses on reporting design, utilization-management criteria, and legal/governance requirements.
- **Patient care impact:** The Consortium argues discriminatory or unsupported PA criteria could block AI/AN beneficiaries' access to care and tribally owned products.
