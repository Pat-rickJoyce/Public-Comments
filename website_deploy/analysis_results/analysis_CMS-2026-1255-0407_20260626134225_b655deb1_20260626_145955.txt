## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A retina specialist supports the rule's direction but argues it repeats a critical omission from the 2024 rule by failing to extend electronic PA reforms to physician-administered drugs under the medical benefit (Part B). Citing evidence that more than 96% of anti-VEGF PA requests are ultimately approved, the commenter urges CMS to extend electronic PA, decision timeframes, gold-carding, and transparent reporting to Part B drugs in the final rule.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0057-F (2024 Interoperability and Prior Authorization final rule); CMS-0062-P (current proposed rule)
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards (noted as not fitting medical-benefit drugs); FHIR (implicitly via electronic PA API references)

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Exclusion of Physician-Administered (Part B) Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Oppose (the exclusion; supports extending coverage)
- **Rationale:** The rule concentrates on pharmacy benefit drugs and leaves out physician-administered drugs, which is where the PA burden and patient harm are concentrated for specialties such as retina, oncology, and rheumatology. The same omission was present in the 2024 rule.
- **Specific request:** Extend electronic PA requirements, real-time benefit lookup, and standardized data exchange to drugs under the medical benefit (Part B). Where NCPDP standards do not fit, direct development of an equivalent standard.

**Topic 2: Decision Timeframes and Transparency for Part B Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support (and extend to Part B drugs)
- **Rationale:** The 72-hour expedited and 7-day standard decision windows, specific denial reasons, and public metric reporting should apply to physician-administered drugs on the same terms as other items and services.
- **Specific request:** Apply all timeframe and transparency requirements equally to Part B physician-administered drugs.

**Topic 3: Prohibition on Repeat Re-Authorization of Stable Chronic Therapy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new policy)
- **Rationale:** Anti-VEGF patients require injections every 4–12 weeks indefinitely; recurring re-authorization creates risk of treatment lapse and permanent vision loss. Each re-auth cycle is a mechanism for harm, not an oversight tool.
- **Specific request:** Once a payer approves a maintenance regimen, prohibit recurring re-authorization absent a material change in clinical circumstances.

**Topic 4: Gold-Carding Pathway**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new policy)
- **Rationale:** When approval rates exceed 90–95% (as they do for anti-VEGF therapy), routine PA imposes cost on everyone to identify a vanishingly small number of inappropriate requests.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to exempt providers and drug-indication pairs with consistently high approval rates from prior authorization.

**Topic 5: Drug-Level and Plan-Level Public Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support and expand
- **Rationale:** Granular, drug-level and plan-level reporting of volumes, approval/denial/appeal rates, and decision times would reveal where PA is adding value versus functioning as friction.
- **Specific request:** Require public reporting broken out by drug and by plan.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12 (technical challenges, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL):** Commenter argues the rule should extend electronic PA to physician-administered drugs; where NCPDP standards are insufficient, CMS should direct development of equivalent standards for medical-benefit drugs.
- **S15 (medical vs. pharmacy distinction):** Argues the medical benefit/pharmacy benefit distinction is the central flaw — the rule reforms pharmacy benefit PA while leaving medical benefit (where burden is greatest in specialty care) unreformed.
- **S16 (what is 'specific reason'):** Supports requiring specific denial reasons and applies this to Part B drugs.
- **S17 (structured codes):** Implicitly supports structured denial information for Part B drugs.
- **S20 (24-hour achievable):** Supports applying 72-hour expedited and 7-day standard timeframes to Part B drugs.
- **S29 (which metrics):** Advocates for drug-level and plan-level granularity in metrics reporting.
- **S35 (publish aggregate):** Supports public reporting of approval/denial/appeal/overturn rates and decision times by drug and plan.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend electronic PA requirements (real-time benefit lookup, standardized data exchange) to physician-administered drugs under the medical benefit (Part B).
2. Where NCPDP standards do not fit medical-benefit drugs, direct development of an equivalent standard.
3. Apply the 72-hour expedited and 7-day standard decision timeframes to physician-administered drugs.
4. Apply specific denial reason and public reporting requirements to physician-administered drugs.
5. Prohibit recurring re-authorization of stable, approved chronic therapy absent a material change in clinical circumstances.
6. Require a gold-carding exemption for providers and drug-indication pairs with approval rates exceeding 90–95%.
7. Require public reporting of PA metrics broken out by drug and by plan.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** The rule reforms pharmacy benefit PA while leaving medical benefit (Part B) drugs — where specialty care PA burden is greatest — unreformed; this repeats the critical omission from the 2024 rule.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Patients with chronic retinal disease risk irreversible vision loss during PA delays; each PA cycle for anti-VEGF involves ~100 minutes of clinic staff time despite >96% ultimate approval rate.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** NCPDP standards may not fit physician-administered drugs; a new or adapted standard would need to be developed.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:**
  - JAMA Ophthalmology (2024): >96% of anti-VEGF PA requests ultimately approved; each consumed median ~100 minutes of clinic staff time.
  - KFF analysis: MA insurers made ~52.8 million PA determinations in 2024; ~7.7% denied; ~11.5% of denials appealed; >80% of appealed denials overturned in whole or in part.
- **Studies or research referenced:** JAMA Ophthalmology (2024) study on anti-VEGF PA; KFF analysis of Medicare Advantage PA determinations (2024).
- **Examples provided:** Anti-VEGF therapy (aflibercept, ranibizumab, faricimab) for retinal diseases; neovascular AMD, diabetic macular edema, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Retina patients (risk of permanent blindness), specialty physicians (oncology, rheumatology, retina), private specialty practices
- **Economic implications:** ~100 minutes clinic staff time per PA request for drugs with >96% approval rates; system-wide administrative waste without meaningful utilization management benefit

- **Patient care impact:** High — treatment delays in chronic retinal disease directly cause irreversible vision loss; anti-VEGF patients require recurring injections and repeated PA cycles amplify harm risk
