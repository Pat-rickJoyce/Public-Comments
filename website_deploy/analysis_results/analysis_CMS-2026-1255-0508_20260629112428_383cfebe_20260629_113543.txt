## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Society of Hematology (ASH) — Robert Negrin, MD, President; Carina Smith, Manager of Health Care Access Policy
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (healthcare provider)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents more than 18,000 clinicians and scientists worldwide committed to studying and treating blood and blood-related diseases, including malignant hematologic disorders (leukemia, lymphoma, multiple myeloma), sickle cell disease, bleeding/clotting disorders, and gene and cell therapies.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ASH supports CMS's proposal to extend electronic prior authorization (ePA) to drugs, agreeing it has the potential to reduce administrative burden and improve patient access. However, ASH cautions that ePA will only shift—not meaningfully reduce—burden unless documentation requirements are standardized across plans, and urges guardrails against unnecessary additional documentation requests, AI overreliance, and requests for expedited pathways for urgent hematologic cases. ASH also opposes step therapy for hematologic conditions and supports improved PA timeline transparency and appeal process reform.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** ASH members still rely on fax, phone, and payer-specific portals; ePA has potential to reduce these burdens and improve timely patient access. However, ePA will only shift burden rather than reduce it unless documentation requirements are standardized across plans and core clinical data elements are consistent.
- **Specific request:** CMS should promote alignment around core clinical data elements for drug PA requests (diagnoses, biomarker/lab results, prior treatment history, therapy failures, contraindications, adverse reactions); establish guardrails to prevent plans from using electronic submission as a basis to request additional documentation beyond what is reasonably necessary.

**Topic 2: AI in Prior Authorization Decision-Making**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** AI may help expedite PA for straightforward, uncomplicated cases with established clinical pathways, but must not replace clinical judgment of providers. Complex hematologic cases, off-label therapies, rare diseases, genomic-driven treatments require human review. Automated systems cannot appropriately address all clinical situations.
- **Specific request:** Ensure changes do not create overreliance on AI decision-making tools; require accessible escalation pathways like peer-to-peer reviews when automated systems cannot appropriately respond to clinical situations.

**Topic 3: Reporting Metrics and PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** ASH supports 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes but notes that certain hematologic conditions (aggressive leukemias, lymphomas, urgent bleeding/clotting, transplant issues, discharge-related needs) require decisions in less than 24 hours. Timeframes should operate 24/7.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to develop electronic pathways with flexibility or exceptions for clearly urgent clinical circumstances; work toward real-time approval/denial decisions; strengthen appeal timeliness requirements; require timely escalation to qualified clinical reviewers; provide transparency regarding appeal outcomes.

**Topic 4: Public Reporting and Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Public reporting of PA metrics (approval rates, denial rates, appeal outcomes, turnaround times) improves accountability and identifies access barriers. Same-day approvals and approvals within 24 hours would be particularly meaningful metrics.
- **Specific request:** Include timeliness measures such as same-day approvals in public reporting metrics.

**Topic 5: Step Therapy (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (generally) / Support (with conditions for improved interoperability)
- **Rationale:** ASH does not support step therapy for hematologic treatments given the complex and sensitive nature of hematologic conditions; prefers its elimination. If step therapy is used, interoperability processes should make it more consistent, with portability of prior treatment history across plans to prevent treatment disruption. Patients should not retry previously ineffective or harmful therapies, and providers must have autonomy to "skip a step."
- **Specific request:** Require consistent documentation requirements across payers; ensure portability of treatment history across plans; preserve physician autonomy to make clinically appropriate decisions including skipping steps when prior treatment history supports it.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12 (PA-DRUG-MEDICAL - technical challenges):** ASH notes continued reliance on fax, phone, and payer-specific portals; urges standardization of clinical data elements as prerequisite for meaningful interoperability.
- **S20 (PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS - 24-hour achievable):** Supports 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes but requests additional flexibility or exceptions for urgent hematologic clinical circumstances that may require less than 24 hours; advocates for real-time decision pathways.
- **S43 (PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS - align timeframes):** Urges 24/7 operation of PA timelines and work toward real-time decisions.
- **S16/S17 (PA-DENIAL-REASONS):** Implicitly addressed through support for transparency and appeal process reform; recommends timely escalation to qualified clinical reviewers.
- **S66-S82 (GENERAL RFIs - step therapy):** Responds to the step therapy RFI opposing step therapy for hematologic conditions generally while supporting improved interoperability for portability of treatment history; emphasizes physician autonomy to skip steps for clinically inappropriate protocols.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Standardize core clinical data elements for drug PA requests across plans (diagnoses, biomarker/lab results, prior treatment history and therapy failures, contraindications, adverse reactions).
2. Establish guardrails to prevent plans from using electronic submission as a basis for requesting additional documentation beyond what is reasonably necessary.
3. Ensure changes do not create overreliance on AI decision-making tools; preserve human clinical review especially for complex, rare, or off-label cases.
4. Require accessible peer-to-peer review escalation pathways when automated systems cannot appropriately address clinical situations.
5. Require PA timelines to operate on a true 24/7 basis.
6. Develop electronic pathways with flexibility or exceptions for urgent clinical circumstances requiring less than 24-hour decisions.
7. Work with plans and EHR vendors toward real-time approval/denial decisions.
8. Strengthen requirements for appeal timeliness and timely escalation to qualified clinical reviewers.
9. Require transparency regarding appeal outcomes in public reporting.
10. Include timeliness measures (same-day approvals, within-24-hour approvals) in public PA metrics reporting.
11. For step therapy: require consistent documentation requirements across payers; ensure portability of prior treatment history across plans; preserve physician autonomy to skip steps based on prior treatment failures or clinical appropriateness.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** ePA for drugs may only shift administrative burden rather than meaningfully reduce it if documentation requirements are not standardized across plans; plans may use electronic submissions as delay tactics by requesting unnecessary additional documentation; AI overreliance may harm complex hematologic patients requiring nuanced clinical judgment.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Unnecessary documentation requests creating care delays and adverse patient outcomes; automated systems inadequate for complex hematologic cases, off-label therapies, rare diseases, and genomic-driven treatments; appeals process creating longest care delays.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer/plan-specific forms, submission requirements, and documentation standards vary; lack of standardization of core clinical data elements across plans; inadequate escalation pathways for urgent cases.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Hematologists and oncologists, patients with hematologic malignancies and non-malignant blood disorders, health plans, EHR vendors, CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Standardized ePA has potential to reduce administrative and financial burden for physicians, practices, and health systems; without standardization, ePA shifts rather than reduces burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** ASH does not directly assess technical feasibility of FHIR or NCPDP standards; focuses on policy outcomes (standardization, guardrails, AI oversight) rather than technical implementation details.
- **Patient care impact:** High concern for hematologic patients requiring urgent treatment decisions; delays in PA for leukemia, lymphoma, bleeding/clotting disorders, transplant care have serious health consequences; step therapy forcing retry of ineffective/toxic therapies creates treatment disruption and clinical harm.
