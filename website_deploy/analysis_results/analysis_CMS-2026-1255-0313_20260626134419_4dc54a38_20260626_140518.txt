## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Family Health Network of Central New York, Inc. (FHN); signed by Kim Osborne, President/CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider (Federally Qualified Health Center)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Licensed Article 28 Diagnostic and Treatment Center and FQHC; providing primary care in Central New York since 1972; 5 freestanding medical centers, 1 dental site, mobile medical unit, 4 school-based health centers in Cortland, Cayuga, and Madison counties; serves 15,000+ patients annually (3% self-pay/uninsured; 56% Medicare/Medicaid; 41% other third-party payers)

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

FHN strongly supports the proposed rule's electronic prior authorization and interoperability goals as critical for medically underserved FQHC patients, while raising extensive safety-net-specific implementation concerns. The comment urges vendor readiness protections, 340B pharmacy workflow support, medical/pharmacy benefit classification clarity, cybersecurity resources, and CDex guardrails. FHN opposes removing drug formulary data from APIs, calls for automatic PA approval upon missed deadlines, requests enforcement discretion extension during the X12-to-FHIR transition, and asks for joint CMS-ONC guidance to address the HTI-5 certification timing gap.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Interoperability — Vendor Readiness and Payer Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHCs rely on limited EHR vendors; vendor-caused compliance gaps are likely without readiness requirements; payer rollout inconsistency will undermine efficiency gains.
- **Specific request:** Clarify providers not penalized for vendor/clearinghouse failures; coordinate timelines with ONC certification; require uniform minimum implementation standards across MCOs and QHP issuers.

**Topic 2: Cybersecurity and IT Resource Concerns**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHC IT teams are small, wear multiple hats; rural CHCs especially lack cybersecurity infrastructure; unfunded costs would harm safety-net providers.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide technical assistance, cybersecurity/IT workforce development, and dedicated health IT funding.

**Topic 3: Medical/Pharmacy Benefit Classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Dual-workflow burden; extended-release buprenorphine example (pharmacy benefit when dispensed, medical benefit when administered in-clinic).
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension); clarify PA approval portability across dual-pathway drugs.

**Topic 4: 340B Pharmacy Workflow Complexity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Over 65% of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy; ePA requires coordination across EHR, PMS, contract pharmacies, TPAs, and payer APIs that are not consistently interoperable.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with OPA for targeted technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding.

**Topic 5: Proactive PA Expiration Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify (additional requirement)
- **Rationale:** Proactive outreach before expiration achieves 87% positive outcomes vs. 25% without.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.

**Topic 6: ePA Without Concurrent PA Reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA alone increased PA volumes and decreased fill rates in peer-reviewed studies; risks accelerating an inefficient process.
- **Specific request:** Pair ePA mandates with Gold Carding and sunset provisions for high-approval-rate PA categories.

**Topic 7: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Proposed 24-hour and 72-hour QHP standards represent meaningful improvement for underserved patients.
- **Specific request:** Add automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 8: Denial Reasons and Public Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons enable efficient corrections; centralized metrics improve accountability.
- **Specific request:** Centralized CMS website publication; expand metrics to include first-pass approval rates, denial rates by service/medication category, and denials overturned due to inadequate notices.

**Topic 9: API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Small CHC IT departments cannot manually search each payer; hard-coded endpoints cause EHR disruptions.
- **Specific request:** Publish as machine-readable files; prioritize safety-net/rural access.

**Topic 10: API Usage Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Disaggregated Medicaid data reveals access gaps; additional metrics needed to evaluate meaningful value.
- **Specific request:** Add successful/failed request rates, structured vs. unstructured data ratios, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance costs.

**Topic 11: Drug Formulary Information Removal**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Formulary lists change rapidly; payer website access not reliable or real-time; CHC providers lack administrative infrastructure for manual lookups.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removing drug formulary from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 12: HIPAA PA Standards — X12 to FHIR Transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Dual-standard period (X12 6020 + FHIR/CDex simultaneously) burdensome for limited CHC IT staff.
- **Specific request:** Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover CMS-0062-P transition period.

**Topic 13: CDex Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CDex creates mechanism for iterative, escalating payer data requests.
- **Specific request:** All documentation in initial CDex request; limit to two attachment requests per PA transaction; maximum payer response timeframes.

**Topic 14: Gold Carding**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** DMEPOS precedent (WISeR model); CHC patients require more frequent PAs; gold carding reduces hundreds of dollars per PA in unreimbursed staff time.
- **Specific request:** Formal payer guidance; extend to all items/services for eligible providers.

**Topic 15: ONC/HTI-5 Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** HTI-5 removal of 34 certification criteria may affect ePA supporting infrastructure; 3-month certification timing gap after payer compliance date.
- **Specific request:** Joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance; formal harmonization assessment before final rule.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify providers won't be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse/EHR failures outside provider control.
2. Coordinate implementation timelines with ONC certification; readiness validation before enforcement.
3. Require uniform minimum implementation standards across MCOs and QHP issuers.
4. Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity/IT workforce development, and health IT funding for CHCs.
5. Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension).
6. Clarify PA approval portability across dual-pathway drugs.
7. Coordinate with OPA for CHC 340B pharmacy workflow technical assistance and funding.
8. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.
9. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction requirements including Gold Carding and sunset provisions.
10. Require automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
11. Publish PA metrics in centralized, standardized CMS website format.
12. Expand reporting to include first-pass approval rates, denial rates by category, and overturned denials.
13. Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files; prioritize safety-net/rural access.
14. Add API usage metrics: successful/failed request rates, structured vs. unstructured data, and API costs.
15. Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
16. Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover CMS-0062-P transition period.
17. CDex guardrails: all documentation in initial request; limit to two attachment requests per PA; maximum payer response timeframes.
18. Formal payer guidance recommending gold carding for all items/services.
19. Publish joint CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before final rule.
20. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vendor readiness gaps; cybersecurity exposure; benefit classification ambiguity; 340B workflow complexity; dual-standard transition burden; HTI-5 timing gap creating 3-month window where payer APIs operational but certified EHRs unavailable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** CHC compliance failures from vendor issues; unfunded cybersecurity costs; ePA without concurrent reform may increase total PA burden; 340B workflow errors; formulary data removal harms prescribing decisions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC IT staff; EHR/PMS interoperability gaps; 340B system complexity; negative sector operating margins; nearly half of CHCs with fewer than 90 days cash.

---

- **Data or statistics cited:** 94% of physicians report PA delays patient care (AMA); average PA wait ~3 days, one-third >1 week; 53 million MA PA determinations in 2024 (KFF); 90%+ CHC patients below 200% FPL; 65%+ CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy; proactive PA expiration outreach achieves 87% positive outcomes vs. 25% without; ePA associated with higher PA volumes and decreased fill rates; nearly half of CHCs <90 days cash.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA PA survey; KFF MA PA 2024; MGMA PA landscape 2025; NACHC 2025 NTTAP HIT Needs Assessment; Lauffenburger JC et al. ## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** FQHCs/CHCs in Central New York; medically underserved patients; 340B program participants; EHR and PMS vendors serving safety-net markets.
- **Economic implications:** Negative sector margins; unfunded costs would be burdensome; gold carding and automatic approval would reduce administrative costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** EHR/PMS gaps, 340B complexity, limited cybersecurity teams, HTI-5 timing gap are significant barriers.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays harm underserved patients; 340B access depends on smooth PA workflows; formulary data removal would harm prescribing decisions.
