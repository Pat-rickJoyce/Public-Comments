**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
This individual comment expresses strong support for CMS's proposed rule on interoperability standards and prior authorization for drugs, emphasizing benefits for patient outcomes, system efficiency, and clinical burnout reduction. The commenter proposes three key enhancements: (1) automatic approval penalties for insurers missing deadlines, (2) enhanced transparency in denial communications with specific actionable guidance, and (3) a transition period for patients switching insurance plans to maintain continuity of previously authorized medications.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**
- Specific sections/provisions addressed: CMS-2026-1255-0001; Medicare Advantage Organizations; Medicaid Managed Care Plans; Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act provisions
- Regulatory citations mentioned: 24-hour and 72-hour deadline requirements for insurer responses; electronic denial notification requirements
- Standards referenced: Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources Application Programming Interface (FHIR API); electronic prior authorization systems; electronic records exchange between insurance plans

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

**Topic: Overall Support for Proposed Rule**
- Position: Strongly Support
- Rationale: Will improve patient outcomes and system efficiency; reduces clinical burnout on providers; modernizes outdated manual processes.
- Specific request: Finalize rule with recommended enhancements.

**Topic: Electronic Prior Authorization Process**
- Position: Strongly Support
- Rationale: Physicians and staff spend countless hours making phone calls and sending faxes for prior authorizations. Electronic standards (FHIR API) decrease clinical burnout and allow more time for patient care.
- Specific request: Mandate electronic standards (FHIR API) for all prior authorization processes.

**Topic: Insurer Deadline Compliance and Penalties**
- Position: Support with Enhancement
- Rationale: Rule sets 24-hour and 72-hour deadlines but does not clearly state consequences if insurers miss deadlines. Without penalties, insurers may not be held accountable for timely responses.
- Specific request: Add penalties for insurers missing deadlines, such as automatic approval of prior authorization requests. This creates financial incentive for timely processing and accountability.

**Topic: Transparency in Denial Communications**
- Position: Support with Significant Enhancement
- Rationale: Prior authorization denial claims are not specific and often leave prescribers guessing what additional information is needed. While rule requires specific and detailed reasons, it should go further to provide actionable guidance. Vague denials delay patient care and access to necessary medications.
- Specific requests: (1) Require denials to clearly state what is missing for approval; (2) If denial is for alternative therapy requirement, specify exactly which medications should be tried first; (3) Provide specific solutions to move forward with prior authorization approval.

**Topic: Continuity of Care During Insurance Transitions**
- Position: Support with Enhancement
- Rationale: Patients commonly transition between insurance companies when switching jobs or transitioning to Medicare. Electronic system enables exchange of active prior authorization data between plans, preventing delays for patients with existing authorizations.
- Specific request: Add transition period (first several months with new insurance company) during which new insurer must approve drugs previously authorized, giving patients time to communicate with doctors and between insurance companies if alternative is required.

**Topic: Insurer Accountability**
- Position: Strongly Support Enhanced Accountability
- Rationale: Insurance companies must be held liable for delays in prior authorization approvals or denials. Current system allows excessive delays without consequences.
- Specific request: Hold insurers liable for taking long periods of time to process prior authorizations; implement penalties or automatic approvals for missed deadlines.

**5. RECOMMENDATIONS**
1. Implement automatic approval penalties for insurers that miss 24-hour or 72-hour deadline requirements
2. Create financial incentives for insurers to process prior authorizations within required timeframes
3. Require denials to clearly state specific missing information needed for approval
4. Mandate that step therapy denials specify exactly which alternative medications must be tried first
5. Require denials to provide specific actionable solutions for moving forward with prior authorization
6. Establish transition period (first several months) when patients switch insurance plans during which new insurer must approve previously authorized drugs
7. Allow transition period to give patients time to communicate with doctors and between insurance companies about potential alternatives
8. Hold insurance companies liable for excessive delays in prior authorization processing
9. Mandate FHIR API electronic standards for all prior authorization processes
10. Require electronic exchange of active prior authorization data between insurance plans during patient transitions

Medicare and Medicaid Programs: Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act; Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs for Medicare Advantage Organizations, Medicaid Managed Care Plans, etc.

I am writing in support of Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Service's (CMS) proposal for Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs. I am a public health graduate student specializing in health policy and management while also working as a pharmacy technician. I believe strongly in supporting this proposed rule as it will improve patient outcomes and improve system efficiency.

By supporting proposed rule CMS-2026-1255-0001, you are not only supporting efficient patient care, but also reducing clinical burnout on the providers. While I am in full support of this proposed rule, there are several recommendations to make to ensure patients will be getting fast and quality healthcare when it comes to their prescription medications.

The inclusion of an electronic process for prior authorizations will speed up the process and also decrease clinical burnout. Physicians and their staff spend countless hours a week making phone calls and sending out faxes to get a prior authorization completed. By mandating electronic standards, Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources Application Programming Interface (FHIR API), this decreases clinical burnout and allows more time for patient care. While the rule sets a strict 24 hour and 72-hour deadlines for insurers to respond to requests, it is not clearly stated what happens if an insurer misses this deadline. By adding in a penalty for missing a deadline will put pressure on insurers to ensure they are denying/accepting prior authorizations in a timely manner. A simple penalty such as an automatic approval can hold insurance companies accountable for making sure they are not missing any deadlines. This also gives a financial incentive for insurance companies to ensure they are keeping up with the requests since there could be more affordable alternatives the drug in the prior authorization.

In addition to adding a penalty to insurers for not meeting approval/denial deadlines, there should be an addition to be as transparent as possible during denial claims. Prior authorization denial claims are not specific and often has prescribers guessing what is additional information is needed...
