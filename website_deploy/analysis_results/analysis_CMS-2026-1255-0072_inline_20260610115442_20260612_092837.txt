## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter opposes prior authorization barriers for routine genetic screening recommended by national organizations, especially pregnancy-related testing. The comment argues that required pre-approval causes delays, repeat appointments, and potential barriers to timely care.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior authorization for routine genetic screening**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Routine genetic screening recommended by national organizations is widespread and time-sensitive, particularly in pregnancy, so prior authorization creates unnecessary delays or barriers.
- **Specific request:** CMS should eliminate PA for routine recommended genetic screening or, if authorization is required, allow blood draw/testing before approval.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Responds to general RFI topics regarding prior authorization barriers and time-sensitive testing access, with a focus on genetic screening and pregnancy-related testing.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Do not require prior authorization for routine genetic screening recommended by national organizations.
2. If prior authorization remains, do not require approval before the patient can have blood drawn.
3. Reduce administrative barriers for time-sensitive pregnancy-related testing.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Prior authorization for routine genetic screening is unnecessary and delays time-sensitive care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays, complete barriers to care, repeat appointments, and added burden on pregnant patients with work, childcare, and other obligations.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Pre-approval requirements before blood draw.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
Prior authorizations for routine genetic screening that is recommended by national organizations is unnecessary and leads to delays or complete barriers to care. General population screening is done on a wide scale and should require special authorization or if authorization is required, it should not have to be approved before a patient can get her blood drawn. Pregnancy related testing is time sensitive and the burden of mothers to have to come for multiple appointments while juggling a job, other children, and other obligations is high without the hassle of needing to come back due to delayed approvals.
