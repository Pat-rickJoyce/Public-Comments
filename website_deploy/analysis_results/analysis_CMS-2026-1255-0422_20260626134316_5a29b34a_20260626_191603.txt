# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** North Olympic Healthcare Network (NOHN); signed by Beau J. Brown.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; safety-net community health center.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** NOHN provides family medicine, obstetric care, pharmacy services, behavioral health, vision, and dental care to more than 19,000 patients in Clallam County and surrounding areas, including diverse and underserved populations.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NOHN supports CMS's interoperability and drug electronic prior authorization proposals because they could reduce administrative burden and improve medication access for community health center patients. The organization urges CMS to pair these requirements with realistic timelines, vendor accountability, cybersecurity and funding support, benefit-pathway clarity, proactive PA expiration alerts, gold-carding and PA reduction, centralized endpoint and metrics publication, HIPAA transition support, and CMS/ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); CMS-0053-F; CMS-0062-P; HTI-4, 90 FR 37130; February 28, 2024 National Standards Group enforcement discretion; WISeR model references. The comment does not cite the proposed PA CFR sections in detail.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; CRD/DTR/PAS HL7 Da Vinci IGs v2.2; CRD Coverage Information extension; Provider Access API; Patient Access API; Provider Directory API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization API; CDex; C-CDA; PDF/text/FHIR resource attachments; X12 278; X12 6020 claims attachments; HIPAA standard transactions; ONC certification criteria.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Interoperability standards and API implementation support**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs and ePA can reduce duplicative documentation and improve PA visibility, but CHCs have limited IT staff, vendor dependence, and cybersecurity constraints.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide realistic timelines, vendor accountability, readiness validation/testing, ONC coordination, cybersecurity support, technical assistance, and funding for safety-net providers.

**Topic 2: Prescription drug ePA and benefit-pathway clarity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with clarification
- **Rationale:** ePA can improve point-of-prescribing access to formulary and PA information, but dual medical/pharmacy benefit workflows are burdensome and sometimes unclear for clinic-administered or specialty drugs.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require benefit classification through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension and clarify whether approval under one benefit pathway applies to the alternate pathway.

**Topic 3: Pharmacy operations and 340B coordination**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CHC pharmacy operations involve entity-owned pharmacies, contract pharmacies, TPAs, EHRs, pharmacy management systems, payer APIs, and 340B accumulation data that may not interoperate in real time.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs and provide technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding support for CHCs.

**Topic 4: PA expiration alerts and PA reduction**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA alone may accelerate an inefficient process and may increase PA volume unless paired with reform; lack of expiration notice causes treatment interruptions.
- **Specific request:** CMS should mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration alerts, extend gold-carding to prescription drugs, and sunset PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 5: Decision timeframes, denial reasons, and automatic approval**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with stronger enforcement
- **Rationale:** Uniform decision timeframes and specific denial reasons help CHCs correct submissions, appeal, and avoid back-and-forth delays; payer delays can interrupt life-sustaining treatment.
- **Specific request:** CMS should finalize aligned timelines, require actionable denial notices, expand reporting, strengthen oversight, and deem PA requests approved if payers miss required deadlines.

**Topic 6: Public API endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CHCs lack capacity to manually locate each payer's API endpoints, and outdated hard-coded endpoints disrupt EHR workflows.
- **Specific request:** CMS should publish payer API endpoint information centrally on its website in machine-readable form, require one-week updates after changes, and prioritize usability for safety-net and rural primary care providers.

**Topic 7: API usage metrics and PA data availability**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Program- and plan-level usage metrics, denial rates, timeframes, appeals, and API functionality indicators would improve accountability and identify access gaps.
- **Specific request:** CMS should report successful and failed API requests, authorization failures, malformed queries, unreceived calls, structured versus unstructured responses, workflow impact, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance costs.

**Topic 8: Formulary information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose removal
- **Rationale:** Payer websites and standard communications do not provide reliable real-time formulary access for CHCs serving patients across many plans.
- **Specific request:** CMS should retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 9: HIPAA PA standards transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with transition support
- **Rationale:** Moving from X12 to FHIR can enhance interoperability, but CHCs and clearinghouses may need to support X12 6020 and FHIR/CDex simultaneously.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide transition support and extend National Standards Group enforcement discretion allowing the FHIR PA API as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition period.

**Topic 10: CDex guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CDex can clarify payer documentation needs but also could enable iterative, escalating requests.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require payers to request all needed documentation initially, limit solicited attachment requests to two per PA transaction, and set maximum payer response timeframes after CDex attachments are received.

**Topic 11: ONC harmonization and certification timing**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS payer deadlines may precede certified provider-facing technology availability, and HTI-5 changes could weaken supporting infrastructure needed by CHCs.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should issue joint implementation guidance and publish a harmonization assessment before the CMS-0062-P final rule; providers should not be penalized for unavailable certified technology.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** NOHN supports mandatory and standardized API/ePA functionality, provided vendor readiness and safety-net implementation support are addressed.
- **S2/S3:** NOHN asks CMS to synchronize IG timelines and coordinate with ONC certification deadlines to avoid payer APIs going live before provider systems are ready.
- **S12:** NOHN identifies operational and technical challenges for drug ePA, including EHR/PMS/API integration, vendor readiness, cybersecurity, and limited CHC IT capacity.
- **S15:** NOHN directly asks CMS to clarify medical versus pharmacy benefit classification and whether one pathway's PA approval applies to the other.
- **S16/S17:** NOHN supports specific, actionable denial reasons for all drug PA denials and reporting on incomplete or unclear denial notices.
- **S18/S37:** NOHN supports ePA and required implementation standards but warns that CHCs need vendor accountability, testing, technical assistance, and funding.
- **S20/S43:** NOHN supports aligned and expedited PA decision timelines and asks CMS to impose automatic approval when required timeframes are missed.
- **S21:** NOHN supports drug ePA timelines but asks CMS to account for CHC readiness, payer rollout consistency, and provider technology availability.
- **S23-S27:** NOHN supports centralized, machine-readable API endpoint publication, 60-day initial reporting, one-week updates after changes, and annual verification.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** NOHN supports granular API usage metrics and recommends additional functionality, usability, error, workflow, and cost metrics; it also supports CMS publication of trends.
- **S44-S55:** NOHN supports FHIR as the direction for HIPAA PA standards but requests X12/FHIR transition support, CDex guardrails, enforcement discretion, and attention to small safety-net provider burden.
- **S60-S65:** NOHN asks CMS and ONC to harmonize standards and certification timing, assess HTI-5 impacts, and prevent certified technology gaps from disadvantaging CHCs.
- **S66-S82:** NOHN raises broader PA reform recommendations, including gold-carding for prescription drugs, sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories, and federal funding support for safety-net providers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize interoperability and drug ePA requirements with realistic timelines and implementation support for CHCs.
2. Hold vendors, clearinghouses, and EHR systems accountable for readiness and clarify providers will not be penalized for failures outside their control.
3. Coordinate timelines with ONC certification and establish readiness validation/testing before enforcement.
4. Provide cybersecurity, IT workforce, technical assistance, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.
5. Require benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension.
6. Clarify PA treatment for drugs covered under both medical and pharmacy benefits.
7. Coordinate with OPA on 340B-related pharmacy implementation issues.
8. Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration alerts.
9. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction, prescription drug gold-carding, and sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories.
10. Finalize faster and aligned PA decision timeframes and require specific, actionable denial reasons.
11. Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
12. Publish PA metrics centrally on CMS's website and expand metrics to include service/medication category, first-pass approval, incomplete or unclear denial notices, data quality, and API functionality.
13. Publish API endpoints in centralized machine-readable files with timely updates.
14. Retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
15. Extend HIPAA enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use during the transition from X12.
16. Add CDex guardrails limiting iterative attachment requests.
17. Publish a CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before the final rule.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CHC resource constraints, vendor readiness, cybersecurity exposure, fragmented payer requirements, dual medical/pharmacy workflows, pharmacy and 340B interoperability gaps, insufficient formulary visibility, PA delays, and certification timing mismatches.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed medications, interrupted treatment, worsened chronic disease, abandoned care, increased administrative burden, noncompliance risk for providers acting in good faith, and disproportionate harm to rural and medically underserved patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Small IT teams, constrained budgets, EHR and third-party vendor dependence, pharmacy software/EHR/CoverMyMeds disconnects, limited interoperability in pharmacy systems, hard-coded endpoint disruptions, payer rollout variation, X12/FHIR dual-standard burden, and EHR certification gaps.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA survey: 94% of physicians report PA delays patient care; patient surveys show average PA wait about three days and nearly one-third wait more than one week. KFF: MA organizations made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024 with more than four million full or partial denials. CHCs serve more than 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, over 6 million uninsured individuals, 20.5 million low-income individuals, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and over 1.5 million homeless individuals; more than 90% of CHC patients have incomes below 200% FPL. NOHN receives about 100 PA requests daily and reports a case requiring over four physician hours, over 20 staff hours, and a three-month delay. Proactive expiration outreach is cited as positive in 87% of cases versus 25% without notification.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA, KFF, MGMA, HRSA UDS, NACHC, Pharmacy Times, Krauss et al., Murthi et al., Vishwanath et al., Lauffenburger et al., Salzbrenner et al., Fierce Healthcare, CMS enforcement discretion guidance, and WISeR model references.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine may fall under pharmacy or medical benefit depending on dispensing/administration; NOHN names GLP-1s, buprenorphine, Adderall, Dexcom supplies, lidocaine patches, 340B contract pharmacy workflows, and an autoimmune lung disease/inflammatory arthritis infusion delayed three months.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs, rural safety-net providers, Medicaid/CHIP and low-income patients, homeless and agricultural worker populations, patients with chronic disease and behavioral health needs, payer organizations, pharmacy teams, 340B contract pharmacies, EHR and PMS vendors, clearinghouses, PBMs, app developers, ONC, OPA, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** NOHN emphasizes unfunded IT, cybersecurity, vendor, API integration, staff training, pharmacy workflow, and PA labor costs; it notes CHCs operate on thin margins and many have limited cash on hand.
- **Technical feasibility:** NOHN supports FHIR/API modernization but views feasibility as dependent on vendor readiness, ONC certification timing, system interoperability, testing, funding, and coordinated standards.
- **Patient care impact:** If implemented with safeguards, the rule could improve medication access, adherence, continuity of care, prescribing decisions, care coordination, and accountability; without safeguards, it could increase burden and delay care for underserved patients.
