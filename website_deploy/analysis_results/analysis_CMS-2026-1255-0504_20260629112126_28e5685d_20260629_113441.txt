---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Point32Health is a regional not-for-profit health plan that raises two focused, practical concerns about the proposed rule. First, the NCPDP compliance date of October 1, 2027 conflicts with a new NCPDP file effective April 14, 2028, requiring plans to implement the same NCPDP standards twice within one year—an unnecessary expense. Second, the rule creates ambiguity for mixed PA requests that span medical services, medical benefit drugs, and pharmacy benefit drugs, and CMS must issue clear orchestration guidance (including how DTR questionnaires should be handled) to enable plans to comply.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Federal Register April 14, 2026 (CMS-0062-P); October 1, 2027 (primary compliance date); April 14, 2028 (new NCPDP file effective date)
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards, FHIR DTR (Documentation Templates and Rules) questionnaires

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Align NCPDP Compliance Dates**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The October 1, 2027 compliance date for NCPDP standards falls less than one year before a new NCPDP file takes effect on April 14, 2028. This creates a situation where organizations must implement the same NCPDP changes twice in under a year, resulting in unnecessary administrative costs and duplication of effort.
- **Specific request:** Delay NCPDP-related compliance date to on or after April 14, 2028 to align with the upcoming NCPDP file update and avoid redundant implementation.

**Topic 2: Clear Guidance on Mixed PA Request Routing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The proposed rule establishes different requirements for medical services, medical benefit drugs (FHIR-based), and pharmacy benefit drugs (NCPDP-based). A single provider PA request may contain a mix of all three, each of which may need to be routed to a different platform. Without clear guidance, health plans cannot know how to comply.
- **Specific request:** CMS should issue clear guidance on how to orchestrate mixed PA requests across different platforms, including specifically whether DTR questionnaires for different benefit types should be combined into one questionnaire or sequenced separately for the provider.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S15:** Directly relevant—commenter identifies a concrete operational problem at the boundary between medical benefit drugs, pharmacy benefit drugs, and medical services in a single PA request, requesting CMS guidance on routing and DTR questionnaire orchestration.
- **S19/S22:** Implicitly responds to NCPDP readiness concerns by identifying a specific standards versioning conflict requiring date alignment.
- **S43:** Related to PA timeframes—identifies the NCPDP double-implementation problem as a timing/implementation concern.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Delay NCPDP-related compliance provisions from October 1, 2027 to on or after April 14, 2028 to align with the upcoming new NCPDP file, preventing organizations from having to implement NCPDP changes twice in under one year.
2. Issue clear guidance specifying how providers and health plans should route mixed PA requests that span medical services, medical benefit drugs, and pharmacy benefit drugs.
3. Clarify specifically whether DTR questionnaires for different benefit types within a single request should be combined into one questionnaire or presented sequentially.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Unnecessary double implementation of NCPDP standards within one year; lack of clarity on orchestrating mixed PA requests across different standards platforms.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Large and unnecessary administrative expenditures for plans; increased healthcare costs from duplicate NCPDP implementation.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Conflicting NCPDP standard effective dates; ambiguity in the rule about how multi-benefit-type PA requests should be handled at the workflow level.

---

- **Studies or research referenced:** Not addressed.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health plans/payers (primarily), providers (DTR questionnaire experience), CMS (implementation coordination).
- **Economic implications:** Duplicate NCPDP implementation constitutes an unnecessary financial expenditure for plans; aligning dates would prevent cost increase passed through to premiums.
- **Technical feasibility:** The double-implementation concern is a real standards versioning conflict that CMS can resolve by adjusting dates; the mixed-request routing question requires policy clarification, not technical invention.
- **Patient care impact:** Not directly addressed; indirect benefit if administrative complexity is resolved and PA workflows function smoothly.
