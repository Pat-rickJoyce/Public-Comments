# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Southern Christian Leadership Conference Global Policy Initiative (SCLC-GPI); signed by Kevin B. Kimble, Esq., Executive Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / civil rights and health equity policy organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SCLC-GPI describes itself as the policy arm of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, with a six-decade history of advancing civil, economic, and social rights for Black Americans and other marginalized communities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SCLC-GPI strongly supports CMS-0062-P as an overdue prior authorization reform with significant health-equity implications. It urges CMS to finalize aggressive electronic PA requirements, require 24-hour decisions for all drugs, strengthen denial justification and reporting metrics, prohibit Medicare Advantage step therapy, and ensure transparency sufficient to expose disparities.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI step therapy; TRANSPARENCY; HEALTH-EQUITY.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P. No specific CFR citations are discussed.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization infrastructure; API usage metrics. The comment does not discuss specific FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards in detail.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA infrastructure for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** SCLC-GPI views paper and fax-based PA as structural barriers that disproportionately burden under-resourced providers such as safety-net hospitals, FQHCs, and independent Black-owned practices.
- **Specific request:** Finalize electronic PA requirements on the most aggressive operationally achievable timeline and invest in technical assistance for smaller providers so benefits do not accrue only to large systems.

**Topic 2: 24-hour decision timeframe for all drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** A 72-hour non-urgent window is inadequate for patients managing serious chronic or acute conditions, especially in communities where delayed access can escalate into emergencies.
- **Specific request:** Require prior authorization decisions within 24 hours for all drugs, not only urgent requests.

**Topic 3: Oversight of expedited decision-making**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Faster timeframes without accountability could lead to systematically higher denial rates.
- **Specific request:** Establish rigorous oversight mechanisms and treat elevated denial rates associated with expedited timelines as a compliance issue.

**Topic 4: Comprehensive plain-language denial justifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with stronger specificity.
- **Rationale:** Generic denials prevent meaningful response or appeal and perpetuate structural inequity.
- **Specific request:** Require comprehensive, specific, plain-language justifications for every denial, including initial denials, provided simultaneously to the prescribing provider and the patient.

**Topic 5: Payer reporting metrics and equity-focused granularity**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support with stronger reporting.
- **Rationale:** API usage alone does not show whether the system works for patients or whether disparities are being reduced.
- **Specific request:** Pair API reporting with denial rates, approval rates on appeal, time from PA request to treatment initiation, annual CMS reporting, plan-level data across all markets, and breakouts by brand/generic, therapeutic area, initial denials, and step therapy requirements.

**Topic 6: Step therapy reform**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI step therapy
- **Position:** Oppose MA step therapy.
- **Rationale:** Requiring patients to fail lower-cost treatments before receiving the physician-selected medication delays effective care and compounds disparities.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit Medicare Advantage step therapy; collect and publicly release detailed data on MA use of PA for Part B drugs; require plans to honor prior step therapy completion across plan changes; require transparent disclosure of step therapy requirements and robust exceptions/appeals.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12, S14, S15, S19, S21, S22, S25, S28, S31 (drug ePA and benefit pathways):** SCLC-GPI supports ePA for drugs across medical and pharmacy benefits and asks for technical assistance for smaller providers.
- **S16-S17 (denial reasons):** Strong response; CMS should require comprehensive, specific, plain-language denial justifications to providers and patients.
- **S20, S43 (drug PA timeframes):** Strong response; CMS should require decisions within 24 hours for all drugs.
- **S29-S30, S34-S35 (API metrics):** Strong response; API metrics should be supplemented with patient/provider outcome metrics and granular public reporting.
- **S66-S82 / step therapy RFI:** Strong response; CMS should prohibit MA step therapy, require cross-plan recognition of completed steps, and improve transparency.
- **Other solicitations:** FHIR IG maturity, HIPAA standards, endpoint reporting, state extension process, FF-SHOP exceptions, Open Payments CMP, and ONC versioning are not substantively addressed.

## 7. POTENTIAL CMS ACTION ITEMS

1. Consider a universal 24-hour drug PA decision standard.
2. Add equity-focused and patient-outcome reporting metrics beyond API usage.
3. Require plan-level, therapeutic-area, brand/generic, initial denial, appeal, and step-therapy reporting.
4. Require denial reasons in plain language to both provider and patient.
5. Provide technical assistance targeted to safety-net and smaller providers.
6. Consider future MA step therapy restrictions and cross-plan recognition of completed step therapy.

## 8. NOTABLE QUOTES / EVIDENCE

- SCLC-GPI frames prior authorization as a mechanism through which systemic healthcare inequity is enforced.
- The commenter states that a three-day window is not an adequate floor for non-urgent drug PA decisions; it is a ceiling that should be lowered.
- The comment emphasizes that specificity in denial reasons is a structural precondition for a functioning appeals process.

## 9. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

SCLC-GPI provides a concise but forceful equity-focused support comment. It should be coded as strong support for finalizing the rule with major strengthening requests on timing, denial explanations, reporting granularity, technical assistance, and step therapy limitations.
