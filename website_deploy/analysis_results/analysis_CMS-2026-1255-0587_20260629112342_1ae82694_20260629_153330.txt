- **Author/Organization name:** Brady J. Buckner, President, Partnership for Innovation and Empowerment (PIE)
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / nonprofit community organization
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** PIE is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit working directly with Black and low-income communities; healthcare access is described as a persistent community issue.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PIE strongly supports CMS-0062-P as a fairness and health equity reform that can modernize PA for drugs and reduce delays for low-income and minority patients. It asks CMS to finalize ePA for medical and pharmacy benefit drugs, require 24-hour decisions for all drugs, strengthen plain-language denial notices, expand meaningful public reporting, and address step therapy harms.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited beyond CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; APIs generally; no named FHIR IG or NCPDP standard cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Current PA relies on phone calls, faxes, and paper forms, disproportionately burdening community health centers and small practices serving low-income and minority patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA across medical and pharmacy benefits, enable real-time coverage information, electronic submission, and status tracking, and provide technical support for smaller safety-net providers.

**Topic 2: Drug PA decision timelines and oversight**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A 72-hour standard timeframe remains too long for people who cannot afford missed doses, work disruptions, health crises, or avoidable emergency care.
- **Specific request:** Require PA decisions within 24 hours for all drugs and monitor whether faster decision rules are being used to accelerate denials.

**Topic 3: Denial justifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen
- **Rationale:** Vague denials leave families and providers without actionable information for appeals or corrections.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed, plain-language justifications for every denial, including initial denials, delivered simultaneously to provider and patient.

**Topic 4: Payer reporting and public transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** API usage alone does not show whether patients receive medications; aggregate reporting can hide disparities.
- **Specific request:** Pair API usage data with patient/provider experience metrics, denial rates, appeal overturn rates, and time from PA request to treatment; publish a useful annual public report; require Medicare Part D plan-level public PA metrics broken out by market, brand/generic, therapeutic area, initial denials, and step therapy.

**Topic 5: Step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose harmful uses / modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy forces patients to fail on cheaper alternatives and compounds disparities.
- **Specific request:** Do not permit MA plans to use step therapy; collect and release data on MA Part B drug PA use; require plans to honor completed step therapy across plan changes; require clear disclosure and navigable appeals/exceptions.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14:** Supports ePA implementation across both benefit channels and asks CMS to support smaller safety-net providers.
- **S16/S17:** Requests detailed, plain-language denial reasons for every denial, including initial denials, delivered to patients and providers.
- **S20/S43:** Requests 24-hour decisions for all drug PA requests, not only urgent cases.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Requests API usage metrics paired with patient- and provider-experience measures, denial/appeal rates, and annual public reporting.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy RFI topics by opposing MA step therapy, supporting data transparency, portability of completed steps, disclosure, and accessible exceptions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for medications across medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Provide real technical support to smaller and safety-net providers.
3. Require PA decisions within 24 hours for all drugs.
4. Monitor denials to ensure faster timelines do not increase unfair denial rates.
5. Require detailed plain-language denial reasons for every denial and send them to both patient and provider.
6. Publish annual public metrics that include denial rates, appeal overturn rates, and time from request to treatment.
7. Require Medicare Part D plan-level public PA metrics with meaningful breakouts.
8. Prohibit MA step therapy or, at minimum, require transparent data and patient protections.
9. Require portability of completed step therapy across plan changes.
10. Require clear disclosure and usable appeals/exceptions for step therapy.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Missed doses, missed work, health crises, emergency visits, denied care, and hidden disparities.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Safety-net providers' limited staff and systems; outdated paper/fax/phone workflows.

experience over and over: their doctor prescribed a medication, and then they waited. And 
waited. And sometimes they just never got it. (source) 
That is why we are writing today to express our strong support for this proposed rule and to offer 
several recommendations for strengthening it. 
I. Electronic Prior Authorization: We Are Fully Behind It 
PIE supports CMS's proposal to require electronic prior authorization (ePA) for medications 
across both the medical and pharmacy benefit. (source) Right now, prior authorization for many 
drugs still runs on phone calls, faxes, and paper forms. That is a problem for any provider, but it 
is a much bigger problem for the community health centers and small practices serving low-
income and minority patients, who simply do not have the staff or systems to fight a slow, 
outdated bureaucracy. 
Giving providers real-time access to coverage information, letting them submit PAs 
electronically, and allowing them to track request status is not just a technology upgrade. It is a 
fairness issue. We urge CMS to finalize this requirement and to provide real technical support 
for smaller, safety-net providers during the rollout.
