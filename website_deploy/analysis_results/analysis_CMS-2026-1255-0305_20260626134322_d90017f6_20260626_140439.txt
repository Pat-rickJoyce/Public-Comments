## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Agadia Systems, a prior authorization technology platform vendor, broadly supports the proposed rule's PA framework across medical and pharmacy benefits, including the benefit-based standards split, FHIR/NCPDP alignments, denial reasons, timeframes, and API metrics reporting. The organization's most significant substantive contribution is identifying a critical gap: the rule imposes requirements on payers but lacks corresponding provider participation requirements, creating a "one-sided" interoperability model that may limit real-world effectiveness. Agadia also provides detailed input on healthcare resiliency, supporting flexibility-based approaches.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Benefit-Based Standards Split (Medical vs. Pharmacy)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The dual-standard approach accurately reflects how payer operations already function. No uniform statutory definition exists for medical vs. pharmacy benefit drugs; payers and vendor systems manage this through configurable workflows.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide non-binding illustrative examples of benefit classification operationalization to support consistent understanding without constraining flexibility.

**Topic 2: FHIR Prior Authorization API for Medical Benefit Drugs (CRD, DTR, PAS)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Logical extension of existing FHIR-based PA framework; aligns with how medical-benefit drug PA is currently operationalized. Vendor platforms normalize data across internal systems.
- **Specific request:** Maintain flexible approach on benefit determination signaling in CRD responses; focus on outcomes rather than internal system design.

**Topic 3: NCPDP Standards for Pharmacy Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Aligning Medicaid/CHIP/QHP with Part D standards promotes consistency. F&B should be understood as a high-level reference source, not real-time feed.
- **Specific request:** Emphasize strong F&B governance practices (effective dates, version control, audit trails) to avoid confusion from treating it as live data.

**Topic 4: Specific Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Clear denial reasons reduce resubmissions and delays; payer PA systems generate denial reasons through structured decision logic that can meet these requirements reliably.
- **Specific request:** Maintain outcome-based approach rather than prescribing format/coding; allow payer flexibility.

**Topic 5: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized timeframes reduce provider confusion. State variability (e.g., Pennsylvania Act 146 vs. HealthChoices 24-hour requirement) illustrates why federal clarity is needed.
- **Specific request:** Issue sub-regulatory guidance clarifying how "complete request" determination interacts with decision clock initiation.

**Topic 6: PA Metrics Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Raw metrics without context can be misleading—approval/denial rates reflect plan design, population complexity, and program requirements.
- **Specific request:** Encourage inclusion of high-level contextual explanations alongside metrics to improve interpretability.

**Topic 7: API Usage Metrics and Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Greater API visibility supports oversight; centralized endpoint registry reduces integration friction and implementation costs.
- **Specific request:** Maintain clarity on payer accountability vs. vendor support roles.

**Topic 8: Provider Participation Gap (Critical Substantive Issue)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** The rule imposes requirements only on payers; providers are permitted but not required to use standardized electronic channels for PA submissions under HIPAA. This creates a one-sided interoperability model where provider adoption has been uneven and manual processes persist.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider: (1) baseline expectations for provider responsiveness; (2) aligning ONC certification criteria for bidirectional PA; (3) incentives for provider adoption; (4) clarifying HIPAA electronic response methods; (5) clarifying PA as appropriate TEFCA Purpose of Use.

**Topic 9: Healthcare Resiliency (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Existing HIPAA Security Rule obligations already cover business continuity; modern resiliency extends to API-based real-time environments.
- **Specific request:** Recognize modern continuity design approaches without prescribing specific solutions; maintain distinct payer vs. vendor accountability.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Maintain benefit-based dual-standard framework; provide non-binding examples of benefit classification.
2. Preserve implementation flexibility for FHIR CRD/DTR/PAS; focus on provider-facing outcomes, not internal payer system design.
3. Clarify F&B as high-level reference source; emphasize governance practices.
4. Maintain outcome-based approach to PA denial reason requirements.
5. Issue sub-regulatory guidance on "complete request" determination and decision clock initiation.
6. Pair public PA metrics reporting with contextual explanations to improve interpretability.
7. Establish baseline provider participation expectations for electronic PA workflows.
8. Align ONC EHR certification criteria to require bidirectional PA workflow support.
9. Explore incentives (not mandates) for provider adoption of automated PA workflows.
10. Clarify HIPAA acceptable electronic response methods to reduce provider practice variability.
11. Clarify that prior authorization is an appropriate Purpose of Use under TEFCA.
12. Support prospective structured clinical data exchange at PA submission.
13. Recognize modern resiliency design patterns without prescribing specific solutions.
14. Maintain distinct payer vs. vendor accountability roles.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Provider participation gap creates one-sided interoperability model; payer investments in electronic PA infrastructure may be underutilized if providers continue manual processes; public PA metrics reporting without context risks misleading oversight audiences.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Unevenly adopted payer-side APIs fail to achieve efficiency gains; repeated manual submissions persist; misinterpreted public metrics may suggest poor performance where benefit design or program requirements are the actual driver.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No HIPAA requirement for providers to use standardized electronic PA channels; PA under "payment/healthcare operations" allowing provider discretion; EHR systems may restrict data sharing under TEFCA; heavy clearinghouse dependencies increase outage risk.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers (impacted by all provisions); health IT vendors/PA platforms; providers (participation gap); patients (access improvements contingent on actual provider adoption).
- **Economic implications:** Centralized endpoint registry reduces integration friction and implementation costs; phased PA metrics reporting timeline allows data validation; provider non-participation limits ROI on payer interoperability investments.
- **Technical feasibility:** Agadia assesses proposed requirements as generally feasible; identifies implementation flexibility as critical to success; raises TEFCA data-sharing restrictions as practical barrier to automated clinical data exchange.
- **Patient care impact:** Positive if provider participation increases; standardized timeframes and denial reasons directly improve patient access. Risk that one-sided model limits actual care access improvements.
