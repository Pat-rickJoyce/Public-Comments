## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Society of Nuclear Medicine and Molecular Imaging (SNMMI)
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SNMMI represents more than 15,000 professionals in nuclear medicine, molecular imaging, and theranostics; it develops practice guidelines, education, research, and advocacy for molecular imaging and therapy.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SNMMI supports modernization of prior authorization but is concerned that extending electronic PA to “all drugs” could sweep in Medicare Part B radiopharmaceuticals and inadvertently expand utilization management for highly time-sensitive diagnostic and therapeutic products. SNMMI urges CMS and ONC to ensure implementation does not delay care, undermine adoption of molecular imaging and theranostics, or create additional barriers for radiopharmaceuticals.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; TRANSPARENCY/GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR, NCPDP, or HL7 implementation guides referenced.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Inclusion of radiopharmaceuticals in “all drugs”**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Radiopharmaceuticals, including diagnostic and therapeutic products, are often time-sensitive and require coordination among manufacturers, radiopharmacies, facilities, and clinicians. Even modest administrative delays can affect staging, treatment planning, missed treatment windows, rescheduling, waste, and costs.
- **Specific request:** Evaluate the impact on radiopharmaceutical products and ensure electronic PA implementation does not expand or intensify barriers to clinically necessary radiopharmaceuticals.

**Topic 2: Avoid expansion of utilization management**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** SNMMI is concerned that requiring PA infrastructure for both services and Part B products could set a precedent for additional PA burdens on prescribing clinicians.
- **Specific request:** Modernize administrative processes without increasing utilization management requirements for time-sensitive Part B drugs.

**Topic 3: Transparency without access barriers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS/GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with guardrails
- **Rationale:** SNMMI supports transparency in PA utilization, approvals, denials, and processing times but warns transparency should not justify new barriers for Part B radiopharmaceuticals.
- **Specific request:** Achieve program integrity and oversight goals without adding administrative barriers to medically necessary radiopharmaceutical products.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Addresses challenges for medical benefit drugs by emphasizing the special timing, handling, and care-coordination needs of radiopharmaceuticals.
- **S15:** Implied response regarding Part B products and services, noting radiopharmaceuticals may be bundled with imaging and diagnostic procedures and should not face compounded burdens.
- **S16/S17:** Supports transparency in approval, denial, and processing metrics, though does not define specific reason-code requirements.
- **S20/S21:** Responds indirectly by stressing that even modest delays can harm diagnostic and therapeutic timelines.
- **S66-S82:** Provides RFI-like input on patient access and the need to avoid new barriers for time-sensitive technologies.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Carefully evaluate how PA requirements affect radiopharmaceuticals and other clinically time-sensitive Part B therapies.
2. Ensure electronic PA requirements do not expand utilization management or create new barriers to radiopharmaceutical access.
3. Preserve timely access to molecular imaging and theranostic technologies.
4. Use transparency and oversight tools without adding administrative barriers to medically necessary products.
5. Engage SNMMI and clinical stakeholders during implementation.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delays in access to diagnostic and therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals, expansion of PA requirements, and added clinician burden.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Missed treatment windows, rescheduled procedures, increased waste, higher costs, delayed diagnosis/treatment, and reduced adoption of precision medicine technologies.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Coordination among manufacturers, radiopharmacies, imaging facilities, and clinicians; short half-lives and timing constraints.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** SNMMI represents more than 15,000 members.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Short half-lives of radiopharmaceuticals; bundled imaging/diagnostic procedures; cancer, neurological, and cardiovascular use cases.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing imaging or therapy, nuclear medicine clinicians, molecular imaging providers, radiopharmacies, manufacturers, facilities, and Part B payers.
- **Economic implications:** Waste and rescheduling costs if products expire or treatment windows are missed.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not the main issue; SNMMI focuses on clinical workflow and access consequences.
- **Patient care impact:** High; delays may affect diagnosis, staging, treatment planning, and outcomes.
