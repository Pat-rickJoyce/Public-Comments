ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
From the payer perspective, prior authorization is an important policy tool to help ensure services are medically necessary, clinically appropriate, and consistent with coverage requirements, while also helping prevent fraud, waste, and abuse. This includes targeted oversight of high-risk durable medical equipment and laboratory services where billing patterns may indicate overutilization, duplicative testing, excessive repeat orders, provision without sufficient documentation of medical necessity, or other potentially fraudulent or wasteful practices. At the same time, consistent with the goals reflected in the Centers for Medicare &amp; Medicaid Services proposed rule CMS-0062-P, the prior authorization process should be as streamlined, timely, transparent, and electronically enabled as possible in order to reduce provider burden and avoid unnecessary delays in care. Prior authorization programs should support clear clinical criteria, prompt determinations for urgent and standard requests, transparent communication of approval and denial rationale, and standardized electronic workflows that improve efficiency while preserving program integrity safeguards....
