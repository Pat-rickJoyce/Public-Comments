## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NPAF broadly supports the proposed rule's drug PA provisions, framing all major components through the lens of financial hardship for patients with complex and chronic conditions. The organization supports shorter PA decision timeframes, PA APIs for drugs, specific denial reasons, API usage metrics, broad payer scope, and FHIR-based HIPAA standardization. NPAF recommends that step therapy history be transferred across payers and that API certification include outcome-based patient financial health metrics.

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None cited explicitly

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Drug PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delays cause missed work, worsening health, avoidable ER visits, and unaffordable out-of-pocket costs for low-income patients. Cross-program alignment removes the burden of navigating fragmented timelines from least-resourced patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the proposed timeframes; integrate Needs Navigation services.

**Topic 2: PA API for Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA reduces administrative friction that disproportionately harms low-income patients; early coverage visibility prevents cash-pay episodes and treatment abandonment.
- **Specific request:** Finalize PA API extension; integrate Needs Navigation alongside electronic PA tools.

**Topic 3: Specific Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Vague denials prevent timely error correction and effective appeals, causing low-income patients to forgo treatment, incur debt, or switch to less appropriate therapies.
- **Specific request:** Finalize requirement for specific, actionable denial rationales accessible through all interoperability APIs.

**Topic 4: API Endpoint Reporting and Usage Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Reporting enables identification of plans imposing disproportionate burdens; plans could appear compliant while maintaining hardship-causing workflows.
- **Specific request:** Maintain robust reporting requirements to enable external oversight.

**Topic 5: Scope of Impacted Payers (including FF-SHOP)**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Financial hardship is not confined to one coverage type; coverage transitions are peak moments of vulnerability.
- **Specific request:** Maintain broad payer scope; expressed concern about Medicaid cuts and work requirements threatening continuity.

**Topic 6: FHIR as HIPAA Standard for PA Transactions**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardization helps safety-net and rural providers serve low-income patients equitably; administrative complexity translates directly to financial harm.
- **Specific request:** Finalize FHIR-based standardization; ensure under-resourced providers are not left behind.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20:** Supports proposed 24-hour expedited drug PA timeframes; financial protection value of faster decisions for low-income patients.
- **S43:** Supports cross-program alignment; fragmented timelines burden least-resourced patients.
- **S12/S15:** Supports PA API extension to medical and pharmacy benefit drugs.
- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons requirement.
- **S23/S24:** Supports API endpoint reporting as accountability mechanism.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports API usage metrics; recommends patient-level financial outcome indicators.
- **S40/S41/S42:** Supports FF-SHOP inclusion; cross-program consistency protects transition-period patients.
- **S44/S45:** Supports FHIR as HIPAA PA standard; reduces interoperability equity gaps.
- **S54:** Recommends API certification assess patient financial outcomes (cash-pay episodes, abandoned prescriptions, debt).
- **S66-S82 (Step therapy RFI):** Recommends requiring plans to honor prior step-therapy steps across payers; track cumulative out-of-pocket costs for Medicaid, CHIP, and low-income populations.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize shorter, cross-program aligned drug PA decision timeframes.
2. Finalize extension of PA API to drugs (medical and pharmacy benefit).
3. Finalize requirement for specific, actionable denial reasons.
4. Maintain robust API endpoint and usage metrics reporting.
5. Apply interoperability protections across all impacted payer types, including FF-SHOP.
6. Finalize FHIR-based HIPAA standardization for PA transactions.
7. Require plans to honor completed step-therapy steps when patients switch payers (Payer-to-Payer API).
8. Track cumulative out-of-pocket costs from step-therapy sequences, especially for Medicaid, CHIP, and low-income MA/QHP populations.
9. Require API testing/certification to assess patient-level financial outcomes.
10. Integrate Needs Navigation models across Medicare programs.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Financial hardship cascades from PA delays; opaque denial processes; fragmented timelines; upcoming Medicaid spending cuts and work requirements.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment abandonment, medical debt, avoidable emergency care, worsening illness, lost wages.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Under-resourced providers struggling with fragmented workflows; risk of technical compliance without patient benefit.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 2024 Gallup poll: 35% of Americans unable to access quality, affordable healthcare. 2025 PAF survey (n=2,510): 82% experienced financial difficulties; 89% say Needs Navigation should be universal; 95% valued it as vital. PAF 2025: 3.4 million reached, $259 million distributed, 186,000+ directly assisted.
- **Studies or research referenced:** PAF Patient Insight Institute national survey (2025); Gallup poll (2024).
- **Examples provided:** Patients discovering medication inaccessibility at pharmacy; inability to afford cash prices during PA delays.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Low-income patients and caregivers; Medicaid, CHIP, MA, and QHP enrollees; safety-net and rural providers.
- **Economic implications:** Faster PA and clearer denials reduce medical debt, avoidable ER use, and cascading financial harm.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not directly assessed.
- **Patient care impact:** Significant—PA delays cause missed work, worsening health, emergency care, and financial destabilization.

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "For patients living with financial hardship, even brief delays in medication access can trigger missed work, worsening health, avoidable emergency care, and out-of-pocket spending they cannot absorb; faster decisions are therefore not only an access protection but a financial protection." - Context: Supporting shorter drug PA timeframes.

2. "Accountability is essential, because interoperability requirements will not meaningfully protect patients if plans can appear compliant on paper while maintaining workflows that continue to delay treatment and shift hidden costs onto patients." - Context: Supporting API reporting requirements.

3. "Administrative complexity is not a neutral systems issue; for patients with financial hardship, it often translates directly into delayed care, extra appointments, unpaid time away from work, transportation expenses, and worsening illness that becomes more expensive to treat." - Context: Supporting FHIR as HIPAA standard.
