# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Partnership to Fight Chronic Disease (PFCD); signed by Candace DeMatteis, JD MPH, Vice President, Policy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Internationally recognized organization of patients, providers, community organizations, business and labor groups, and health policy experts focused on reducing the burden of chronic disease.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PFCD supports the core proposals to expand electronic prior authorization for drugs, provide faster timelines, improve denial transparency, and increase patient/provider access to PA data. It recommends stronger patient protections, including a universal 24-hour drug PA decision standard, detailed denial notices to both providers and patients, stronger API and PA metrics, Part D reporting, and limits on non-clinical step therapy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; PATIENT-ACCESS/API-related GENERAL; GENERAL/RFI step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-2026-1255; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; Patient Access API. No specific FHIR IG, NCPDP, or HL7 standard named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for medications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** PFCD states that ePA can help providers access current drug coverage information, complete PAs more seamlessly, and track status in real time.
- **Specific request:** Implement ePA across medical and pharmacy benefit drugs and continue broader PA reforms so PAs do not block prescribed treatment.

**Topic 2: Universal 24-hour drug PA decision standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients with chronic disease face dangerous delays, and the commenter cites evidence that delayed PA can lead patients to abandon treatment.
- **Specific request:** Require all health plans to issue determinations for all drug PA requests within 24 hours of receipt, regardless of standard or expedited status.

**Topic 3: Denial notices to patients and providers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PFCD argues that patients need plain-language denial explanations to participate in appeals, especially given low appeal rates and high reversal rates.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed denial justifications for all denials, including initial denials, to be sent to both providers and patients in lay terms.

**Topic 4: API and PA reporting metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Raw API usage metrics do not show whether patients experience delays, confusion, or care disruptions.
- **Specific request:** Pair API metrics with patient/provider experience data, denial rates, appeal rates, care delay outcomes, and an annual CMS-published report.

**Topic 5: Medicare Part D reporting**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PFCD argues that excluding Part D from public reporting allows plans to block access without sufficient accountability.
- **Specific request:** Require Part D plans to publicly report PA metrics, including initial denials, resolutions, and step therapy requirements for brand and generic drugs.

**Topic 6: Step therapy protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PFCD contends step therapy should not override provider judgment or patient needs for cost-cutting reasons.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit non-clinical step therapy in Medicare Advantage, require evidence-based clinical guidelines, honor prior step therapy across plans, and require disclosure of step therapy requirements and exceptions/appeals processes.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14/S22/S25:** PFCD supports ePA for drugs across medical and pharmacy benefits but does not provide technical NCPDP-specific feedback.
- **S16:** PFCD requests detailed denial justifications for all denials, including initial denials, and wants notices written in lay terms for patients.
- **S20/S43:** PFCD urges CMS to go beyond the proposal and require all drug PA determinations within 24 hours.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** PFCD recommends pairing API metrics with patient/provider experience measures and annual CMS publication.
- **S66-S82:** PFCD responds to step therapy RFI issues by calling for limits on non-clinical step therapy, honoring prior steps across plans, disclosure, and transparent appeals/exception processes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic prior authorization for medications across medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Require all drug PA determinations within 24 hours of receipt.
3. Strengthen oversight so faster review timelines do not produce inappropriate denials.
4. Require detailed denial explanations to both providers and patients, written in lay terms for patients.
5. Pair API metrics with patient/provider experience data and publish an annual CMS report.
6. Require Medicare Part D public reporting of PA metrics, including denials, resolutions, and step therapy requirements.
7. Prohibit non-clinical step therapy in Medicare Advantage.
8. Require step therapy to be based on current evidence-based clinical guidelines.
9. Require plans to honor previous step therapy across plan transitions.
10. Require clear disclosure of step therapy and transparent exceptions and appeals processes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays for chronic disease medications; inadequate denial transparency; insufficient metrics; lack of Part D reporting; misuse of step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment abandonment, disease progression, complications, preventable hospitalizations, ineffective initial treatments, and poor adherence.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Need for oversight, meaningful metrics, and accountability; lack of patient-facing denial information.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 170 million Americans live with at least one chronic condition; more than 70 million manage multiple chronic diseases; 39% of insured adults with a chronic condition identify PA as the biggest burden; 58% requested PA in the past year; 41% requested it two or more times; 27% waited over a week for an insurance decision; 32% waited over a week for prescribed care; 78% of patients facing significant delays may abandon treatment; only 1 in 10 denied MA PA requests in 2022 were appealed; 83.2% of appeals were partially or fully overturned; 40% of surveyed patients abandoned a step-through plan due to lack of symptom relief; nearly seven in ten medical professionals believe PA led to ineffective initial treatment due to step therapy.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF 2026 survey; AMA 2024 PA survey; KFF 2024 Medicare Advantage denial analysis; Rubin et al. 2017 JMCP; AMA step therapy/prior authorization survey.
- **Examples provided:** Chronic disease patients needing life-sustaining drugs; step therapy in psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and Crohn's disease.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with chronic disease, providers, payers, Medicare Part D plans, Medicare Advantage enrollees, CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Avoidable complications, hospitalizations, and utilization may increase when PA delays block chronic disease treatment; public reporting could discourage access barriers and costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports ePA and API reporting, but focuses less on technical implementation than accountability.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong focus on preventing treatment delays, abandonment, non-adherence, and harm from non-clinical step therapy.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with modifications
- **Tone:** Advocacy-focused, patient-centered, policy-oriented
- **Priority level:** High
