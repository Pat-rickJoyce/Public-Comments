# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Drummond Group, LLC; signed by Sonia Galvan, Senior Director, ONC Health IT Testing and Certification.
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Drummond describes itself as an NVLAP-accredited and ANAB-accredited ONC-Authorized Testing Laboratory and ONC-Authorized Certification Body with expertise in ONC health IT testing and certification, interoperability testing, healthcare security, and compliance assessment.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Drummond supports CMS's use of FHIR-based APIs and expansion of drug prior authorization, but argues CMS must close the gap between standards conformance and operational readiness. It recommends mandatory payer API conformance testing through ONC-authorized infrastructure, multi-party interoperability testing, clear test-kit timelines and version-transition rules, stronger FHIR API security requirements, clarification of FHIR/X12 transition obligations, and formal payer API certification requirements.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFI on cybersecurity, resiliency, testing, and certification.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Sections II.A, II.B, II.C, II.E, II.F, II.H, and II.J; 45 CFR 170.215(a); 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31), (32), and (33); HIPAA Security Rule; HIPAA Administrative Simplification; X12N 278.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; Da Vinci CRD v2.2.1, DTR v2.2.0, PAS v2.2.1; US Core; CARIN Blue Button; Da Vinci PDex; NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit; RTPB; Endpoint Resource; NDH; OAuth 2.0; TLS 1.2 or higher; Inferno; FHIRplace; ONC test kits; X12N 278.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Conformance Testing Is Necessary but Insufficient**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond says laboratory conformance tools can show FHIR syntax and IG compliance but cannot prove that payer systems will reliably deliver notifications, apply plan-specific business logic, or function with provider and pharmacy systems in real workflows.
- **Specific request:** CMS should clarify that conformance testing is necessary but not sufficient and require evidence of multi-party interoperability testing before payers claim Prior Authorization API compliance.

**Topic 2: Payer API Certification Gap**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond argues CMS relies too heavily on self-testing against Inferno and should use the existing ONC-Authorized Testing Laboratory and ONC-Authorized Certification Body ecosystem.
- **Specific request:** Require mandatory conformance testing and eventually mandatory payer API certification through ONC-authorized testing/certification infrastructure, with recertification tied to IG version transitions.

**Topic 3: Multi-Party Interoperability Testing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond says semantic failures often arise only when real payer, provider, EHR, pharmacy, and PA systems interact, including incomplete CRD coverage information, DTR questionnaires that do not match requirements, and PAS pended statuses without proper notification mechanisms.
- **Specific request:** Require structured multi-party testing against live or production-representative systems and full PA lifecycles before compliance claims are finalized.

**Topic 4: Test Kit Timeline and Versioning**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE.
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify.
- **Rationale:** Multiple unexpired versions may create ambiguity for certification and compliance; test kits must be available well before October 1, 2027.
- **Specific request:** Establish a committed timeline for ONC test kit development for CRD v2.2.1, DTR v2.2.0, and PAS v2.2.1; clarify how ONC-ACBs handle simultaneous valid versions and certification against declared versions.

**Topic 5: Medical Benefit vs Pharmacy Benefit Scope**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY.
- **Position:** Clarify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond says using the claims-processing system as the proxy for medical versus pharmacy benefit is operationally reasonable but not a testable standard because the same drug may fall into different categories across payers or plans.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to publish which drugs fall under which standard and document how the distinction and business rules will be communicated and validated with partners.

**Topic 6: NCPDP and Pharmacy Benefit Readiness**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP payers that have not implemented NCPDP SCRIPT PA capabilities may face a tight timeline and PBM integration dependencies.
- **Specific request:** Make NCPDP SCRIPT v2023011 PA notification test procedures clear and available before the compliance date and account for PBM contract/system dependencies.

**Topic 7: FHIR/X12 Transition and HIPAA PA Standards**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS.
- **Position:** Support with clarification.
- **Rationale:** Drummond supports replacing X12N 278 with FHIR but says CMS/HHS must clarify the period between the October 1, 2027 CMS interoperability date and the later HIPAA Administrative Simplification compliance date, when payers may need to support both FHIR and X12N 278.
- **Specific request:** Clarify payer obligations during the transition and support multi-party testing at the scale required for all HIPAA covered entities.

**Topic 8: Security Requirements for FHIR APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PRIVACY-SECURITY; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond says the shift from clearinghouse-mediated X12 transactions to direct FHIR APIs creates new attack surfaces and transfers security obligations to payer API infrastructure, while the HIPAA Security Rule does not mandate penetration testing or vulnerability assessments.
- **Specific request:** Establish minimum FHIR implementation security standards, including OAuth 2.0, TLS 1.2 or higher, API-level access controls, and annual penetration testing.

**Topic 9: Endpoint Registry and API Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS.
- **Position:** Support and clarify.
- **Rationale:** Drummond supports endpoint and API usage reporting but notes certification tooling must validate endpoint resources against declared versions, and static files may create latency for endpoint changes.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate NDH adoption, consider a FHIR-queryable registry, validate endpoint resources against declared versions, and pair API adoption metrics with certification/conformance status.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify that conformance testing is necessary but not sufficient for operational readiness.
2. Require evidence of multi-party interoperability testing before payers claim Prior Authorization API compliance.
3. Establish committed ONC test kit timelines for CRD v2.2.1, DTR v2.2.0, and PAS v2.2.1.
4. Clarify unexpired-version testing and certification rules for ONC-ACBs and ONC-ATLs.
5. Require payers to document and validate prior authorization business rules, including medical versus pharmacy benefit classification.
6. Clarify interim obligations for supporting both FHIR-based PA and X12N 278 during the transition to HIPAA FHIR PA standards.
7. Establish minimum FHIR API security requirements: OAuth 2.0, TLS 1.2 or higher, API access controls, and annual penetration testing.
8. Recognize third-party interoperability testing programs as an essential readiness best practice.
9. Require mandatory payer API conformance testing through ONC-authorized infrastructure before October 1, 2027.
10. Establish payer API certification requirements, surveillance, recertification, remediation windows, and safe harbors.
11. Coordinate CMS and ONC testing so Prior Authorization API certification validates downstream Patient Access and Provider Access API availability.
12. Consider a FHIR-queryable endpoint registry and coordinate a path to NDH adoption.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Certification gap, overreliance on conformance testing, lack of operational testing, unclear version-transition mechanics, medical/pharmacy benefit ambiguity, FHIR/X12 transition uncertainty, and insufficient API security requirements.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Broken production workflows, failed notifications, incorrect PA business logic, delays in drug access, provider support burden, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, fragmented implementations, and repeated API failures after compliance dates.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Test-kit availability, payer and PBM integration, simultaneous unexpired versions, lack of payer certification criteria, production versus sandbox differences, endpoint registry latency, and lack of mandatory security testing.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Drummond estimates payer API certification costs at approximately $10,000-$25,000 per API in the visible RFI response. No broader quantitative cost model is provided.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No external studies are emphasized in the visible text. The comment relies on Drummond's certification, testing, and interoperability experience.
- **Examples provided:** Incomplete CRD coverage information, DTR questionnaires mismatching actual PA requirements, PAS pended statuses without notification mechanisms, specialty drug formulary tiering, step therapy sequencing, quantity limits, therapeutic substitution rules, endpoint version transitions, and clearinghouse-to-direct-API security shifts.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, providers, pharmacies, PBMs, EHR and health IT developers, clearinghouses, ONC-ATLs, ONC-ACBs, CMS, HHS, patients, and all HIPAA covered entities affected by FHIR PA standards.
- **Economic implications:** Certification and testing impose upfront costs, but Drummond argues they reduce support burden, provide regulatory clarity, create safe harbors, and prevent costly post-production failures.
- **Technical feasibility:** Achievable by October 1, 2027 for standards conformance if CMS clarifies test-kit timing, versioning, testing expectations, and certification pathways; operational readiness requires multi-party testing and stronger security validation.
- **Patient care impact:** Reliable certified payer APIs could reduce authorization delays and improve access to medically necessary drugs; unreliable implementations could perpetuate or worsen delays despite formal compliance.
