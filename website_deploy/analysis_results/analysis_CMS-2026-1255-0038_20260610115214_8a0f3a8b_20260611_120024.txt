## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** MCG Health
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Publisher of evidence-based clinical care guidelines and software used by major health plans, over 3,200 hospitals, and state/federal agencies; part of Hearst Health.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MCG supports extending electronic prior authorization to medical benefit drugs and using standardized APIs to expose coverage criteria and documentation requirements. It asks CMS to preserve clinical nuance in machine-readable criteria, clarify medical benefit drug implementation, make documentation requirements explicit, avoid mandatory automatic step therapy portability, support override pathways, clarify timeframe clocks for complete requests, and develop a standardized denial reason taxonomy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API; FHIR-based PA API; NCPDP SCRIPT and related standards; machine-readable clinical criteria; ICD-10; HCPCS/NDC drug codes.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medical benefit drug PA API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Extending the PA API to biologics, oncology agents, gene therapies, and other medical benefit drugs reflects modern specialty medication workflows.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the extension and provide clear technical guidance for representing multi-step medical benefit drug criteria.

**Topic 2: Medical vs pharmacy benefit standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** MCG considers the distinction between NCPDP pharmacy benefit standards and FHIR medical benefit APIs functionally and technically sound.
- **Specific request:** Maintain the distinction while clarifying implementation for medical benefit criteria.

**Topic 3: Machine-readable criteria and clinical nuance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Evidence-based criteria may involve disease severity, biomarkers, prior treatment response, longitudinal response, intolerance, and clinical stability that cannot always be reduced to binary logic.
- **Specific request:** Distinguish structured required elements from clinical judgment elements that may require semi-structured or narrative review.

**Topic 4: Documentation requirements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Explicit documentation requirements reduce provider burden and support faster determinations.
- **Specific request:** Require and standardize enumerated documentation requirements such as labs, prior treatment records, attestations, and diagnosis confirmations.

**Topic 5: Step therapy transparency and portability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy should be evidence-based; automatic portability may omit clinical context, documentation details, and payer criteria differences.
- **Specific request:** Encourage rather than mandate recognition of prior therapy, require documentation standards, and allow payers to request additional information where needed.

**Topic 6: Step therapy overrides**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Clinically appropriate exceptions are needed for contraindications, prior failure/intolerance, and urgency.
- **Specific request:** Require override criteria in the PA API, standardized override categories, and decision timeframes for overrides.

**Topic 7: Decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Complex specialty drug determinations may require review of unstructured records and clinical nuance.
- **Specific request:** Clarify whether clocks start upon receipt of complete requests, define completeness, consider tiered timeframes for complex drugs, and require extension reasons.

**Topic 8: Denial reasons and reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Criterion-level denial language can improve resubmission, appeals, and reporting comparability.
- **Specific request:** Develop a standardized denial reason taxonomy tied to criteria, documentation deficiencies, appeal drivers, and therapeutic classes.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1-S3/S18/S37:** MCG supports structured electronic PA workflows but urges flexibility for criteria not reducible to binary machine logic.
- **S12/S15:** MCG supports the medical benefit drug PA API and comments on how medical benefit drug criteria should be represented.
- **S16/S17:** MCG supports specific denial reasons and recommends a standardized denial reason taxonomy.
- **S20/S43:** MCG supports faster timeframes but asks CMS to define complete requests and consider operational complexity for specialty drugs.
- **S29/S35:** MCG supports public reporting of PA metrics and sees denial taxonomy as improving metric usefulness.
- **S66-S82:** MCG responds to the step therapy RFI by recommending clinical validity, documentation adequacy, override pathways, and caution against mandatory automatic portability.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize medical benefit drug PA API requirements.
2. Provide technical guidance for representing multi-step criteria in APIs.
3. Preserve flexibility for semi-structured or narrative clinical judgment elements.
4. Standardize explicit documentation requirements in PA APIs.
5. Require step therapy override criteria to be surfaced in APIs.
6. Establish standardized override categories and decision timeframes.
7. Encourage, rather than mandate, automatic step therapy portability.
8. Clarify decision timeframe clocks and complete-request requirements.
9. Consider tiered timeframes for complex specialty drugs with disclosed extension reasons.
10. Develop a standardized denial reason taxonomy.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Oversimplified computable criteria, loss of clinical nuance, mandatory portability of step therapy, ambiguous timeframe clocks, and inconsistent denial reasons.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Inappropriate approvals or denials, resubmissions, appeals, adoption of less evidence-based criteria, inability to incorporate emerging evidence, and provider confusion.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Unstructured chart notes, laboratory values, imaging reports, specialty consultation records, variable payer criteria, and incomplete prior treatment histories.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** MCG reviews more than 214,041 references annually, with over 48,100 unique citations; guidelines used by a majority of the largest U.S. health plans and over 3,200 hospitals.
- **Studies or research referenced:** General peer-reviewed literature and evidence-based guideline development process; no named studies.
- **Examples provided:** Biologics, oncology agents, gene therapies, biomarker requirements, diagnosis verification, site-of-care constraints, contraindications, prior treatment failure or intolerance.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, providers, patients, guideline vendors, electronic PA platform vendors, CMS, standards development partners.
- **Economic implications:** Structured criteria and documentation can reduce administrative friction; oversimplification may increase appeals and resubmissions.
- **Technical feasibility:** MCG supports machine-readable criteria but emphasizes current implementation limits for nuanced clinical evidence.
- **Patient care impact:** Better structured and transparent criteria may speed access, but inflexible automation could cause clinically inappropriate decisions.

- **Tone:** Technical, professional, collaborative
