    # Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Pharmacists Association (APhA); signed by Michael Baxter, Vice President, Government Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents pharmacists, student pharmacists, and pharmacy technicians across community pharmacies, hospitals, long-term care, specialty pharmacies, community health centers, physician offices, ambulatory clinics, managed care, hospice, and government settings.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

APhA supports CMS proposals to streamline drug prior authorization through NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drugs, FHIR APIs for medical benefit drugs, denial transparency, shorter/standardized decision timeframes, and public PA metrics. APhA urges CMS to ensure reforms reduce pharmacy burden, provide real-time patient-specific benefit/formulary information, protect continuity of care, expand pharmacists' access to interoperability data, and use pharmacy input in step therapy workflows.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL/RFI Step Therapy; Provider Access API and Payer-to-Payer API related interoperability.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Federal Register page references including FR 19901, 19923, 19932, 19951, and 20013; no CFR citations emphasized.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit; HL7 FHIR; Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS; Provider Access API; Payer-to-Payer API.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB can reduce fax/phone/portal workflows, provide real-time patient-specific information, and reduce rejected claims at the pharmacy counter.
- **Specific request:** Finalize NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit ePA and ensure point-of-prescribing and point-of-dispensing access to formulary, benefit, PA, step therapy, and cost-sharing information.

**Topic 2: FHIR PA API for medical benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Adding medical benefit drugs to the existing PA API requires no changes to existing standards and can streamline provider workflow and reduce barriers.
- **Specific request:** Finalize incorporation of medical benefit drugs into the Prior Authorization API.

**Topic 3: Denial reasons and communication**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons help pharmacists and other providers respond more efficiently, reduce resubmissions, and coordinate care.
- **Specific request:** Denial explanations should include clinical rationale, missing information, and covered alternatives.

**Topic 4: Decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Shorter timelines reduce prescription abandonment and therapy interruption.
- **Specific request:** Keep timelines as short and standardized as possible, including 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited QHP timeframes.

**Topic 5: PA metrics and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Public reporting improves transparency and helps stakeholders assess prior authorization impacts.
- **Specific request:** Finalize annual PA metrics reporting and consider broader burden-reduction policies such as gold carding.

**Topic 6: Pharmacist access to interoperability data**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / Provider Access API
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacists need data to coordinate care and resolve PA issues at dispensing.
- **Specific request:** Provide pharmacists with appropriate Provider Access API data access, establish a timeline for pharmacy interoperability, and use Pharmacy Profiles as a provider directory source.

**Topic 7: Step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI S66-S82
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacists often know when a medication failed or caused adverse effects and can supply real-time information to streamline exceptions.
- **Specific request:** Expand interoperability with pharmacies for real-time pharmacist input and ensure efficient exceptions/overrides for clinically appropriate cases.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports incorporating medical benefit drugs into the PA API.
- **S14:** Supports NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drug PA.
- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons for all drug prior authorization denials.
- **S17:** Recommends denial explanations include clinical rationale, missing information, and covered alternatives; implies support for structured/actionable denial detail.
- **S20/S43:** Supports shorter, standardized decision timeframes across payers.
- **S22:** Supports NCPDP standards as burden-reducing for pharmacists and pharmacy technicians.
- **S25:** Supports RTPB use because it provides patient-specific eligibility, coverage restrictions, alternatives, and estimated cost sharing.
- **S29/S35:** Supports public reporting of PA metrics to promote accountability and transparency.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy RFI by recommending pharmacy interoperability, pharmacist input on prior therapy failures/adverse effects, and efficient exceptions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Reference and align final policies with the 21 Prior Authorization and Utilization Management Reform Principles.
2. Finalize NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB standards for pharmacy benefit drug PA.
3. Ensure real-time, patient-specific formulary, benefit, PA, step therapy, and cost-sharing information at prescribing and dispensing.
4. Finalize expansion of the FHIR Prior Authorization API to medical benefit drugs.
5. Require specific denial explanations with clinical rationale, missing information, and covered alternatives.
6. Standardize and shorten PA decision timelines.
7. Publicly report PA metrics for transparency and accountability.
8. Consider limiting PA volume through outlier-based policies and gold carding.
9. Provide pharmacists with appropriate access to Provider Access API data and establish a pharmacy interoperability timeline.
10. Use Pharmacy Profiles as a provider directory data source for pharmacy professionals.
11. Use interoperability to streamline step therapy attestations, exceptions, and overrides.
12. Incorporate continuity-of-care protections so stable patients are not interrupted by new PA or step therapy requirements.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Pharmacy burden, treatment delays, confusing coverage rules, fax/manual processes, prescription abandonment, lack of pharmacist access to data, step therapy delays.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Patients forgo medications, frustration at pharmacy counter, therapy interruptions, disease progression, unnecessary repetition of step therapy.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Variation across payers, limited pharmacist access to interoperability infrastructure, need for credential verification and provider directory inclusion.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No original quantitative data; quotes Federal Register descriptions of NCPDP and FHIR standards and workflows.
- **Studies or research referenced:** 21 Prior Authorization and Utilization Management Reform Principles workgroup report.
- **Examples provided:** Pharmacy counter claim rejections; fax-based PA responses; pharmacist knowledge of treatment failure/adverse effects; Pharmacy Profiles credential verification.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Pharmacists, pharmacy technicians, patients, prescribers, payers/PBMs, pharmacies, managed care organizations.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced manual PA burden could save staff time; real-time benefit information may reduce claim rejections and prescription abandonment.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports using established NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit and FHIR/Da Vinci for medical benefit; calls for pharmacy interoperability expansion.
- **Patient care impact:** Positive if reforms reduce delays, preserve continuity of therapy, improve PA transparency, and streamline step therapy exceptions.
