- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (representing chain pharmacies/PBMs)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents traditional drug stores, supermarkets, and mass merchants with pharmacies; members operate 40,000+ pharmacies employing 155,000 pharmacists filling 3+ billion prescriptions yearly; 900+ supplier partners; 70+ international members in 21 countries

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NACDS supports the overall goals of CMS-0062-P but argues the proposed rule is too payer/prescriber-centric and fails to fully integrate pharmacy dispensing workflows. Key recommendations include making pharmacy a first-class participant in interoperability infrastructure, retaining both NCPDP F&B and RTPB standards, removing the misapplied PANotification requirement for payers, ensuring denial reasons are actionable at the point of dispensing, requiring a minimum 24-month implementation timeline, and supporting a carefully phased transition to FHIR-based standards that preserves existing NCPDP pharmacy standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Pharmacy Must Be Core to Interoperability Policy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The rule is primarily architected around payers and prescribers; pharmacists operate in a dual role as both dispensers and rendering providers, and pharmacies were excluded from prior federal EHR incentive programs, leaving them with less system support than other care settings.
- **Specific request:** Require that interoperability infrastructure be usable within pharmacy dispensing and clinical documentation systems; support pharmacists in both roles.

**Topic 2: Maintain NCPDP SCRIPT as Foundation for Pharmacy Benefit PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support (with modification)
- **Rationale:** NCPDP SCRIPT is built for medications and pharmacy benefit transactions; it is the correct standard for this domain and ensures consistency.
- **Specific request:** Maintain NCPDP SCRIPT as the foundational standard for electronic PA across pharmacy benefit programs.

**Topic 3: Remove PANotification Requirement for Payers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose (specific sub-proposal)
- **Rationale:** The PANotification transaction is designed for prescriber-to-pharmacist communication, not payer involvement. Requiring payers to support it introduces unnecessary complexity, creates confusion over transaction roles, and could increase duplicate PA activity.
- **Specific request:** CMS should retract the proposal requiring impacted payers to support the PANotification transaction.

**Topic 4: Maintain Both F&B and RTPB Standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support (retain both)
- **Rationale:** F&B provides plan-level formulary information early in the workflow; RTPB provides patient-specific, real-time coverage determination at point of dispensing. They are complementary, not duplicative. Eliminating either shifts burden downstream to pharmacies.
- **Specific request:** Maintain requirements for both NCPDP F&B and RTPB; clarify their complementary roles.

**Topic 5: Prior Authorization Must Be Actionable at Point of Dispensing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Current PA information often indicates requirements without supporting next steps; pharmacists must rely on phone calls, resubmissions, and prescriber coordination.
- **Specific request:** Ensure PA and real-time benefit responses clearly indicate whether PA is required, the appropriate submission pathway, required documentation, and support for efficient PA initiation; enable pharmacist participation; support bidirectional data exchange.

**Topic 6: Address Medical/Pharmacy Benefit Fragmentation**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacists and providers often lack a reliable method to determine coverage pathways; bifurcation creates real-world confusion.
- **Specific request:** Preserve distinct standards while advancing policies that reduce fragmentation through clear routing and consistent benefit category signaling.

**Topic 7: Denial Reasons Must Be Actionable**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Denial information is often inconsistent and unstructured; at point of dispensing, clear and usable denial information is essential for immediate workflow decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require denial reasons to be standardized, structured, and actionable with clear next steps embedded; integrate denial data into real-time benefit tools.

**Topic 8: Implementation Timeline — Minimum 24 Months**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Industry experience shows standard version transitions require at least 24 months; this rule introduces new functionality for many entities. Proposed timeline also creates NCPDP version conflict (2017071 vs. 2023011, January 2028).
- **Specific request:** Minimum 24-month implementation timeline from effective date of the final rule.

**Topic 9: Clarify PA Timeframe Triggers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Without clear definitions of receipt, completeness, and iterative submissions, accountability may shift to downstream actors lacking control over documentation completeness.
- **Specific request:** Define PA clock triggers clearly.

**Topic 10: Phased, Flexible FHIR Transition**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support (with conditions)
- **Rationale:** Pharmacy systems designed around high-volume transaction processing differ from EHR-centric FHIR models; uncoordinated adoption creates parallel systems increasing complexity.
- **Specific request:** Phased implementation timelines; flexibility for limited-infrastructure entities; alignment between FHIR and NCPDP standards.

**Topic 11: Include Pharmacies in Electronic Event Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacies uniquely positioned to support care transitions and medication reconciliation but cannot function without timely patient event information.
- **Specific request:** Include community pharmacies as eligible participants in electronic event notification frameworks.

**Topic 12-15: API Resiliency, Payer API Consistency, Step Therapy Portability, Lab/DMEPOS PA**
- **Provision IDs:** GENERAL, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer API variability burdens pharmacies; system disruptions block medication access; step therapy forces unnecessary retreatment; lab/DMEPOS PA delays harm patients.
- **Specific requests:** API redundancy and fallback mechanisms; consistent standards-based API implementation with centralized endpoint discovery and audit; Payer-to-Payer API for step therapy history portability; standardized PA for lab/DMEPOS.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require interoperability infrastructure to be usable within pharmacy dispensing and clinical documentation systems.
2. Maintain NCPDP SCRIPT as foundational standard for electronic PA across pharmacy benefit programs.
3. Retract the proposal requiring impacted payers to support the PANotification transaction.
4. Maintain requirements for both NCPDP F&B and RTPB standards; clarify their complementary roles.
5. Require PA and real-time benefit responses to be actionable at point of dispensing (clear next steps, submission pathway, required documentation).
6. Enable pharmacist participation in PA workflows; support bidirectional data exchange.
7. Preserve distinct pharmacy and medical benefit standards while reducing fragmentation through clear routing.
8. Require standardized, structured, actionable denial reasons within real-time pharmacy workflows.
9. Integrate denial reason data into real-time benefit tools and PA workflows.
10. Establish a minimum 24-month implementation timeline from effective date.
11. Clarify PA clock triggers: receipt, completeness, and iterative submission handling.
12. Support phased, flexible FHIR transition aligned with existing NCPDP standards.
13. Include community pharmacies as eligible participants in electronic event notification frameworks.
14. Require API reliability and redundancy; establish fallback mechanisms for pharmacy continuity.
15. Require consistent, standards-based payer API implementation; establish centralized endpoint discovery and audit mechanisms.
16. Support data exchange for step therapy history portability via Payer-to-Payer APIs.
17. Standardize PA requirements across lab and DMEPOS; extend electronic PA consistently.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rule architecture too payer/prescriber-centric; pharmacy dispensing workflows not operationalized; misapplied PANotification requirement; risk of increased complexity without burden reduction; insufficient implementation timeline; NCPDP version transition conflict.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Rejected claims at point of dispensing if F&B or RTPB eliminated; increased duplicate PA activity from PANotification payer requirement; therapy abandonment; FHIR/NCPDP parallel systems if transition uncoordinated.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
The National Association of Chain Drug Stores (NACDS) appreciates the opportunity to comment on the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) proposed rule on interoperability standards and prior authorization for drugs (CMS-0062-P). NACDS supports CMS' overall goal to expand electronic prior authorization, advance application programming interface (API)-enabled data exchange, and reduce administrative burden. However, to achieve meaningful improvements in patient access and provider efficiency, the final rule must ensure that interoperability requirements are operationalized in the context of real-world workflows, including within pharmacy systems and dispensing environments where coverage and prior authorization barriers are most acutely encountered.

The policies finalized through this rulemaking will shape how coverage information, prior authorization requirements, and clinical documentation are exchanged across payers, providers, and pharmacies. Accordingly, CMS should prioritize implementation approaches that deliver actionable, real-time information; and ensure consistent, enforceable execution across payers. Absent these refinements, there is a risk that expanded data exchange will increase system complexity without reducing burden or improving patient access...
