# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** SCAN Health Plan
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer / Medicare Advantage health plan
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SCAN identifies itself through its federal government affairs submission and references operational experience with pharmacy benefit real-time prescribing and Medicare Part C interoperability/electronic PA implementation.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SCAN supports CMS's goals to modernize prior authorization, expand electronic data exchange, increase transparency, and reduce administrative burden. It asks CMS to refine the proposal by extending the implementation timeline, encouraging provider adoption of electronic prescribing, adopting a complete-request standard for decision clocks, requiring supporting records where appropriate, and improving provider API communication.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; STAKEHOLDER-COORDINATION.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No specific CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prescribing; electronic PA tools; APIs; Medicare Part C interoperability and electronic prior authorization implementation. Specific FHIR IGs and NCPDP standards are not named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation timeline**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** SCAN says the October 1, 2027 compliance date is insufficient given operational, technical, and workflow changes across medical and pharmacy benefits. It notes that Medicare Part D has distinct workflows, systems, and pharmacy networks.
- **Specific request:** Provide a minimum of 24 months after publication of the final rule for implementation, testing, provider alignment, and education.

**Topic 2: Provider adoption of electronic prescribing and ePA tools**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PROVIDER-BURDEN
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** SCAN currently supports real-time prescribing through electronic prescribing channels but reports variable provider adoption, limiting tool effectiveness.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider incentives to encourage broader provider adoption of electronic prescribing and electronic PA tools.

**Topic 3: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Clear and actionable denial information improves transparency and helps providers submit more complete appeals.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the requirement to provide specific denial reasons as proposed.

**Topic 4: Complete request standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** SCAN argues that many denials occur because submissions are incomplete and that review clocks tied to incomplete requests could increase avoidable denials.
- **Specific request:** Adopt a complete-request standard so decision timeframes begin only after receipt of sufficient clinical information and pause when additional information is requested.

**Topic 5: Supporting documentation and medical records**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Upfront clinical documentation could facilitate faster approvals and reduce administrative back-and-forth.
- **Specific request:** Consider requiring submission of supporting medical records at the time of PA request where appropriate.

**Topic 6: Provider API communication**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; STAKEHOLDER-COORDINATION
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** SCAN reports difficulty obtaining APIs from providers during Part C implementation and states that electronic medical records work only if providers and plans can communicate through APIs.
- **Specific request:** CMS should work with HHS to ensure providers understand the importance of communicating their APIs to Medicare Advantage and Part D plans.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S21:** SCAN responds to implementation and technical challenges for drug PA by recommending at least 24 months after final rule publication.
- **S16:** SCAN supports the requirement to provide specific denial reasons.
- **S20:** SCAN proposes a complete-request standard for PA timeframes so clocks begin when sufficient clinical information is received.
- **S23/S24/S26/S33:** SCAN indirectly addresses API endpoint/reporting issues by emphasizing the need for providers to communicate their APIs to plans.
- **S37:** SCAN highlights implementation challenges arising from provider API availability and adoption variability.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide at least 24 months after publication of the final rule before compliance.
2. Align implementation time with thoughtful testing, provider alignment, and Part D education.
3. Consider incentives to increase provider adoption of electronic prescribing.
4. Finalize the specific denial reason requirement.
5. Adopt a complete-request standard for PA decision timeframes.
6. Pause decision clocks when additional information is requested.
7. Require supporting medical records at the time of PA request where appropriate.
8. Work with HHS to improve provider communication of API information to MA and Part D plans.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Operational complexity, insufficient implementation time, provider adoption gaps, incomplete PA submissions, and lack of provider API communication.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disruption to clinical workflows, higher costs and friction for providers/patients not using electronic tools, unnecessary denials from incomplete submissions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Distinct Part D workflows and networks, variable provider adoption, inadequate API communication, documentation gaps.

SCAN Health Plan (SCAN) appreciates the opportunity to comment on CMS’s proposed rule 
addressing interoperability standards and prior authorization (PA) for drugs. We support CMS’s 
goal to modernize PA, expand electronic data exchange, increase transparency, and reduce 
administrative burden. However, several elements of the proposal would benefit from refinement 
to avoid operational complexity and disruption to clinical workflows. 
 
Implementation Timeline 
 
Implementing the proposed interoperability and PA requirements will require significant 
operational, technical, and workflow changes across both medical and pharmacy benefits. The 
proposed compliance date of October 1, 2027, is not sufficient given the scope of change. SCAN 
recommends CMS provide a minimum of 24 months following publication of the final rule to 
allow for thoughtful implementation, testing, and provider alignment, thereby minimizing 
disruption to patient care. Notably, the final interoperability and electronic prior authorization 
rule for Medicare Part C provided Medicare Advantage organizations with three years to 
implement the new requirements. Because Medicare Part D operates under distinct workflows, 
systems, and pharmacy networks, implementation presents unique challenges that warrant a 
comparable transition period. Providing at least 24 months from publication of the final rule for 
Part D will allow adequate time for the process implementation and education.
