## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The National Council for Mental Wellbeing generally supports the proposed rule's goals of expediting and improving transparency in prior authorization, particularly the PA-DENIAL-REASONS provision. However, the organization raises significant concerns about cybersecurity risks and resource constraints facing mental health and substance use providers—a population historically underinvested in health IT—and urges CMS to provide technical assistance, workforce support, and dedicated funding, and to clarify that providers will not be penalized for vendor failures outside their control.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED (general support for API standardization)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 FR 19890 (April 14, 2026); RIN 0938-AV44; Medicare Part D 6 protected classes policy; HITECH Act of 2009
- **Standards referenced:** Application Programming Interface (API), electronic prior authorization (ePA), HIPAA

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: PA Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Clear denial reasons enable transparent communication, reduce administrative burden, and help patients understand their care decisions, supporting care continuity.
- **Specific request:** Support the October 1, 2027 requirement that Medicaid/CHIP FFS, managed care plans, and QHP issuers provide specific reasons for drug PA denials.

**Topic 2: API Standardization and ePA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Standardized, interoperable, real-time data exchange can reduce duplicative documentation and improve care coordination for medically complex patients. However, mental health and substance use providers depend heavily on a limited number of EHR vendors and third-party technology partners.
- **Specific request:** CMS should clarify that providers will not be penalized for failures attributable to vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems outside provider control; coordinate timelines with ONC certification requirements; establish readiness validation or testing before enforcement begins.

**Topic 3: Cybersecurity Risks for Safety-Net Providers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Concerns
- **Rationale:** The expansion of API-based exchange significantly increases cybersecurity exposure for mental health and substance use providers, which often lack dedicated cybersecurity teams, operate with constrained IT resources, and were historically excluded from HITECH Act funding. Rural providers are particularly vulnerable. Integration with broader health systems also exposes these organizations to third-party security risks.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide technical assistance guidance, cybersecurity and IT workforce development support, and dedicated health IT funding; ensure the final framework reflects operational realities of mental health and substance use providers; support recovery planning tools.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Commenter supports requiring specific denial reasons; emphasizes value for transparent communication, reduced administrative burden, and care continuity for mental health and substance use patients.
- **S37:** Commenter highlights implementation challenges for safety-net providers—specifically mental health and substance use organizations—citing limited IT staff, reliance on third-party vendors, and resource constraints as barriers.
- **S18 (implicit):** Commenter notes that mandatory IG/API requirements place obligations on payers but the corresponding burden on providers and vendor readiness is not addressed; calls for provider protections.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Support the PA-DENIAL-REASONS provision requiring specific reasons when denying drug PA requests.
2. Clarify that providers will not be penalized for compliance failures attributable to vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems outside their control.
3. Coordinate implementation timelines closely with ONC certification requirements.
4. Establish readiness validation or testing before enforcement begins.
5. Provide technical assistance and implementation guidance to mental health and substance use providers.
6. Provide cybersecurity and IT workforce development support and dedicated health IT funding for safety-net providers.
7. Ensure the final implementation framework reflects operational realities and resource constraints of mental health and substance use providers.
8. Invest strategically in cybersecurity resources for mental health and substance use providers, including recovery plan support tools.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Cybersecurity risks and unfunded mandates for mental health and substance use providers; vendor readiness gaps that could cause provider compliance failures through no fault of their own.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Compliance gaps and operational disruption for safety-net providers; increased cybersecurity exposure for organizations with limited IT staff; unfunded costs for cybersecurity compliance, vendor upgrades, API integration, and maintenance.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited financial flexibility; small IT teams wearing multiple hats; historic underinvestment (exclusion from HITECH Act 2009); reliance on third-party vendors; rural providers lacking advanced IT infrastructure; exposure to other organizations' security risks through data sharing.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** National Council represents over 3,200 mental health and substance use treatment organizations serving 10 million+ children, adults, and families.
- **Studies or research referenced:** References to HITECH Act of 2009 and its exclusion of mental health and substance use providers.
- **Examples provided:** Rural community providers lacking dedicated cybersecurity teams; small IT teams maintaining HIPAA compliance with third-party vendor support; data sharing exposures when integrating with hospitals, ERs, and primary care.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Mental health and substance use treatment providers (3,200+ organizations); patients with mental illness and substance use disorders (10M+); rural communities; EHR vendors and third-party technology partners.
- **Economic implications:** Unfunded costs for cybersecurity compliance, vendor upgrades, API integration, and maintenance would fall on resource-constrained safety-net providers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible in principle but constrained by limited IT workforce, vendor readiness gaps, and lack of dedicated funding for this provider population.
- **Patient care impact:** Positive if implemented well—faster PA decisions and clearer denial reasons improve access for mental health and substance use patients. Risk of care disruption if implementation failures occur.

serve across our nation.
An essential safeguard in Part D, the 6 protected classes policy ensures that patients managing complex
health conditions, such as mental illness, are able to access the full spectrum of medications under Part
D. Specifically, the policy says that Medicare must cover "all or substantially all" medications within the
six classes (6PC), which includes antidepressants and antipsychotics.
Under the law, Medicare Part D and MA plans are permitted to manage costs by using utilization management tools like step therapy, and prior authorization within all 6PC classes except HIV. While
legal, these tools can sometimes lead to unfortunate delays for patients to medication access or
destabilization of their health condition(s) due to requirements put in place by the health plans.
The 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule addresses
many of the problems patients face due to utilization management requirements, specifically prior
authorization. We appreciate the administration's continued focus on improving patient access to health
care as reflected in the proposed rule, which would create a "more expeditious, transparent and reliable
process" for prior authorization...
