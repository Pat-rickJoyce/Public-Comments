## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** John Czwartacki, Founder & Chairman, Survivors for Solutions / Access for All
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group; Individual patient advocate
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Patient advocate, public policy veteran, person living with multiple sclerosis for more than 30 years.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports CMS efforts to reduce prior authorization delays and improve transparency, emphasizing that PA and step therapy can delay medically necessary treatment for patients with progressive neurological diseases. He urges CMS to require detailed, clinically grounded denial reasons and to prohibit step therapy where no validated failure or intolerance criteria exist.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior authorization delays and denial transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states PA often delays care already recommended by physicians and that most requests are eventually approved, making delays harmful and inefficient.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed, clinically grounded denial justifications for every rejection, not boilerplate denial language.

**Topic 2: Upfront drug PA transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports interoperability proposals that would show physicians upfront whether a drug requires prior authorization, reducing downstream burden and improving access.
- **Specific request:** Finalize transparency measures that make PA requirements visible before prescribing or dispensing decisions are made.

**Topic 3: Step therapy limits for progressive neurological disease**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues step therapy is dangerous where disease progression is rapid or irreversible and where no validated sequencing or failure criteria exist.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that step therapy is inappropriate for serious progressive neurological conditions without validated failure or intolerance definitions.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** The commenter responds directly by urging detailed, clinically grounded denial justifications for every PA rejection.
- **S17:** The comment does not request structured codes specifically, but supports more actionable denial explanations that physicians and patients can use.
- **S20:** The commenter supports shorter PA timeframes and argues delays are especially harmful for progressive diseases.
- **S66-S82:** The comment addresses broader step therapy and patient access issues, including the need for clinically validated failure criteria before sequencing requirements are imposed.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Reduce prior authorization timeframes further.
2. Require detailed, clinically grounded denial justifications for every PA rejection.
3. Preserve upfront transparency about whether a drug requires prior authorization.
4. Require commercial plans to define failure or intolerance criteria before applying step therapy.
5. Prohibit step therapy in therapeutic areas where no validated failure criteria exist, especially serious progressive neurological conditions.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays, boilerplate denials, payer judgment overriding physician judgment, and step therapy without clinical guardrails.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed treatment, worsening progressive disease, permanent harm, administrative burden, and patients being left without a clear path forward.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Inconsistent payer step therapy protocols and lack of clinically validated failure definitions.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** The commenter states approximately 95 percent of PA requests are eventually approved and that delays have increased over the past four years.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** MS treatment delays; commercial payer step therapy inconsistencies for early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease involving Leqembi and Kisunla; examples naming Florida Blue, Blue Cross Blue Shield of Arizona, and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Rhode Island.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with MS, Alzheimer's disease, and other progressive neurological conditions; physicians; commercial plans; drug manufacturers; patient advocacy organizations.
- **Economic implications:** The commenter frames PA and step therapy as cost-containment tools that can impose clinical and administrative costs on patients and providers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not addressed beyond supporting interoperability tools that disclose PA requirements upfront.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment emphasizes delayed access, disease progression, and irreversible harm.
