## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Melanoma Research Foundation responds to the RFI on prior authorization and laboratory testing, commending CMS for streamlining PA processes while urging specific protections for patients needing diagnostic laboratory tests. MRF highlights that strict pre-collection PA requirements cause specimen discard, redundant blood draws, and treatment delays for cancer patients. They call on CMS to mandate retroactive PA approvals, prohibit timing-based denials, eliminate redundant testing requirements, establish expedited oncology pathways, and protect patients from surprise financial liability.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Retroactive PA Approvals for Laboratory Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker and specialty lab specimens are often collected at the provider's office and sent to a separate lab for processing. The "date of service" is the collection date, not processing date, creating a timing gap that leads to denials even when the test was medically necessary.
- **Specific request:** Mandate that health plans permit prior authorizations to be processed after specimen collection.

**Topic 2: Prohibition on Timing-Based Denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Denying medically necessary tests solely because paperwork was not finalized before the draw date does not reflect clinical reality and harms patients.
- **Specific request:** CMS should prohibit health plans from denying coverage based solely on PA paperwork timing relative to specimen collection.

**Topic 3: Eliminating Redundant Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** When viable specimens are discarded due to administrative delays, patients are forced to undergo repeat blood draws, increasing burden on already-vulnerable cancer patients.
- **Specific request:** Prevent forced repeated blood draws when a valid specimen is already available.

**Topic 4: Expedited Pathways for Oncology Diagnostics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS (analogous for lab)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics require faster turnaround to avoid treatment delays.
- **Specific request:** Establish accelerated PA turnaround times for oncology and time-sensitive diagnostics.

**Topic 5: Patient Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients acting in good faith should not face surprise out-of-pocket costs when PA is denied on technical timing grounds.
- **Specific request:** Insulate patients from surprise financial liability when following provider guidance.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

This comment responds to the RFI in Section III.E rather than to numbered solicitations S1–S82. No direct responses to specific numbered solicitations identified.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Mandate that health plans allow retroactive PA approvals for laboratory tests processed after specimen collection.
2. Prohibit denials of medically necessary tests based solely on timing of PA paperwork relative to specimen collection date.
3. Prevent forced, repeated blood draws when a valid specimen is already available.
4. Establish expedited PA turnaround pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics.
5. Protect patients from surprise out-of-pocket costs when acting in good faith under provider guidance.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA frameworks that require approval strictly before specimen collection are misaligned with clinical reality for laboratory testing, particularly oncology biomarker tests.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Viable specimens discarded; patients forced into repeat blood draws; treatment decisions delayed; disproportionate financial and geographic burden on rural and low-income populations.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative lag in PA reviews; disconnect between specimen collection date ("date of service") and processing date at reference labs.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Cancer patients (especially melanoma), rural and low-income patients, oncology providers, reference laboratories.
- **Economic implications:** Repeat testing costs; surprise patient cost-sharing; administrative overhead for appeals.
- **Technical feasibility:** Recommendations are policy changes to health plan practices, not technical standards changes—feasibility is high.
- **Patient care impact:** Direct and significant; PA timing barriers delay cancer treatment decisions and force unnecessary procedures on vulnerable patients.
