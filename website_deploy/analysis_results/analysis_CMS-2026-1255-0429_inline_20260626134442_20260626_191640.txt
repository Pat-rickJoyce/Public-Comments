- **Author/Organization name:** Scott Underwood
- **Stakeholder type:** Individual
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Private citizen; software architect; former voting member of the HL7 Standards Committee; contributor to HL7 v2.3 ADT, SIU, and ORM messages; designed and operated a high-throughput HL7 integration engine with more than 800 production interfaces across EHRs, laboratories, and e-prescription partners.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports electronic prior authorization for drugs but identifies a narrow technical routing problem in the dual-standard architecture. Because the same drug may be covered under either the medical or pharmacy benefit depending on payer and coverage terms, CMS should require payers to return an authoritative, machine-readable standard and endpoint selection rather than leaving path determination optional.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based Prior Authorization API; Da Vinci PAS IG; CRD; NCPDP SCRIPT; medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit drug PA standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Standard selection for medical vs pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The rule creates two mutually exclusive standard paths, but drug benefit classification depends on payer coverage and can change over time. Optional payer indication leaves providers and EHRs to guess, causing misrouted requests, rejected or dropped transactions, and fallback to phone/fax.
- **Specific request:** Make path determination a required capability rather than an optional convention.

**Topic 2: Authoritative CRD response with standard and endpoint**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** In message-routing systems, the reliable solution is a centralized authoritative lookup rather than having every endpoint re-derive the route.
- **Specific request:** Require the first-stage CRD response to return, in machine-readable form, which standard and endpoint the requester must use for that drug, patient, and payer coverage.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Identifies a technical implementation challenge for medical-benefit drug PA: requests may be routed incorrectly if the requester cannot determine the correct standard.
- **S14:** Identifies the parallel challenge for pharmacy-benefit drug PA, where NCPDP should be used but may not be selected correctly without authoritative payer guidance.
- **S15:** Directly responds to the medical-benefit vs pharmacy-benefit distinction by arguing CMS should require authoritative machine-readable path determination.
- **S24:** Recommends that the endpoint and standard needed for a particular request be made available to the requester, not merely as optional general information.
- **S32/S37:** Relevant to CRD implementation because the commenter proposes using the first-stage CRD response to solve routing and reduce real-world implementation failures.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Treat medical-vs-pharmacy standard selection as a required routing capability.
2. Require payers to provide an authoritative, machine-readable answer identifying the correct standard for a given drug, patient, and coverage context.
3. Require that answer to include the endpoint the requester must use.
4. Use the first-stage CRD response as the mechanism for returning the standard and endpoint.
5. Avoid leaving path selection to optional conventions or individual provider/EHR inference.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Optional path signals will cause misrouting between incompatible FHIR and NCPDP stacks.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Wrong-standard submissions, rejected or silently dropped requests, fallback to fax/phone, increased provider burden, and patient delays.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No statutory or regulatory definition distinguishing medical from pharmacy benefit drugs; same drug may be categorized differently by payer and coverage terms; classification can change over time.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** PAS IG warning that it should not be used for medications covered under a pharmacy benefit where another electronic exchange process such as NCPDP SCRIPT applies; network with many providers transacting with many payers.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Providers, EHR vendors, payers, pharmacy benefit systems, medical benefit systems, and patients affected by misrouted PA requests.
- **Economic implications:** Misrouting would waste implementation effort and push transactions back to manual channels, though no dollar estimate is provided.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter frames the solution as a standard message-routing pattern: centralize the routing answer in an authoritative lookup/CRD response.
- **Patient care impact:** Correct routing should reduce failed electronic PA submissions and avoid delays caused by fallback to phone or fax.
