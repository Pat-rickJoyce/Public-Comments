## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Association of Nurse Practitioners (AANP); signed by Jon Fanning, MS, CAE, CNED, Chief Executive Officer.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association; Healthcare Provider.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents nurse practitioners, described as APRNs prepared at master's or doctoral level. Cites NP prescriptive authority in all states and D.C., nearly one billion patient visits annually, over 243,000 NPs billing Medicare as of 2024, and large NP role in Medicare, Medicaid, rural, and primary care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AANP strongly supports CMS's proposed expansion of electronic prior authorization for drugs and associated interoperability, denial reason, timeframe, NCPDP, RTPB, and reporting provisions because they can reduce burden and improve patient access. It asks CMS to add explicit provider non-discrimination protections for NPs, centralize PA metrics on CMS's website, retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs, and provide technical support and hold-harmless policies for smaller and rural practices.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING/related public reporting; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.205(b); 45 CFR 170.205(c); 89 FR 8758; 89 FR 8897; 91 FR 19901; 91 FR 19904; 91 FR 19923; 91 FR 19927; 91 FR 19932; 91 FR 19933; 91 FR 19941; ACA Section 2706; No Surprises Act Section 108.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR standards; Prior Authorization API; NCPDP SCRIPT Standard Implementation Guide; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB) standard; ONC standards framework; EHR systems.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for Medical Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Manual and payer-specific PA channels delay care and increase burden for NPs and patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize requirements for impacted payers to expand the Prior Authorization API to medical benefit drugs and incorporate coverage and documentation requirements by October 1, 2027.

**Topic 2: NCPDP Standards for Pharmacy Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized NCPDP SCRIPT and RTPB requirements will reduce fragmentation, confusion, and delays across payers.
- **Specific request:** Require Medicaid/CHIP FFS, managed care, CHIP managed care, and QHP issuers on FFEs to support unexpired NCPDP SCRIPT and RTPB standards adopted at 45 CFR 170.205.

**Topic 3: Specific Denial Reasons and Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** NPs often lack information needed to understand and address denials; differing payer timelines are burdensome.
- **Specific request:** Require specific reasons for drug PA denials regardless of submission method and standardize drug PA decision timeframes across impacted payers.

**Topic 4: Public and API Metrics Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** PA metrics are necessary for transparency, accountability, oversight, research, and identifying denial and delay trends.
- **Specific request:** CMS should publish required PA metrics centrally in a standardized public format, with greater granularity including denial rates, extension timeframes, and adjudicated appeals.

**Topic 5: Provider Non-Discrimination**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payers may discriminate against NPs or patients choosing NPs, including in utilization management and potential gold-carding programs.
- **Specific request:** Include language clarifying that payers cannot discriminate against NPs or other clinicians based on licensure in PA procedures.

**Topic 6: Drug Formulary Information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Providers and patients often lack current formulary information through websites or payer communications, and formularies change rapidly.
- **Specific request:** Do not remove drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 7: Technical Support for Smaller and Rural Practices**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Practices vary widely in technological capability; small and rural practices may struggle to implement or use new systems.
- **Specific request:** Provide technical support and hold providers harmless where technical implementation problems occur.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** AANP supports requiring standards and IGs for APIs because common standards reduce payer-by-payer variation and EHR customization.
- **S2/S3:** AANP supports use of unexpired versions of NCPDP SCRIPT and RTPB standards aligned with ONC's update framework.
- **S12:** AANP supports incorporating medical benefit drugs into the Prior Authorization API and says standardization should reduce ambiguity and delays.
- **S14:** AANP supports non-Part D impacted payers supporting NCPDP SCRIPT for pharmacy benefit drug prior authorization.
- **S16:** AANP supports specific denial reasons, emphasizing that providers need to know why a claim was denied and how to address it.
- **S19:** AANP supports state Medicaid/CHIP FFS and managed care implementation of pharmacy benefit drug ePA standards.
- **S20:** AANP supports aligned drug PA decision timeframes across impacted payers.
- **S21:** AANP supports the October 1, 2027 compliance date for electronic drug PA requirements.
- **S22:** AANP acknowledges EHR complexity and cost but expects common standards to reduce customization burden.
- **S25:** AANP supports NCPDP RTPB because real-time benefit information can reduce confusion, delays, and unexpected costs.
- **S29:** AANP supports expanding usage and PA metrics, including denial rates, extension timeframes, and appeals.
- **S34:** AANP supports annual reporting beginning in 2028 but wants centralized CMS publication.
- **S35:** AANP supports public reporting and asks CMS to publish standardized metrics centrally rather than relying on plan websites.
- **S37:** AANP asks CMS to provide technical support and hold-harmless policies for smaller and rural practices facing implementation issues.
- **S66-S82:** AANP generally encourages additional PA reform, including limits on PA and consideration of gold-carding, but with safeguards against NP discrimination.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for all drugs requiring PA under impacted payer coverage.
2. Require medical benefit drugs to be incorporated into the Prior Authorization API by October 1, 2027.
3. Require NCPDP SCRIPT and RTPB support for pharmacy benefit drugs by applicable non-Part D impacted payers.
4. Maintain broad definition of provider to include prescribing and ordering clinicians such as NPs.
5. Add explicit non-discrimination language protecting NPs and other licensed clinicians in PA procedures.
6. Require specific denial reasons for all drug PA denials.
7. Align and standardize drug PA decision timeframes across impacted payers.
8. Publish PA metrics centrally on CMS's website in standardized, comparable format.
9. Expand metrics granularity to include denial rates, extensions, and adjudicated appeals.
10. Retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
11. Provide technical support and hold-harmless treatment for smaller and rural practices.
12. Ensure future gold-carding or PA reduction programs include NPs and avoid licensure discrimination.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Burdensome payer-specific PA processes, opaque denials, inconsistent timeframes, lack of provider non-discrimination protections, insufficient centralized transparency, removal of formulary data, and uneven technical capabilities.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays in medications and treatment, increased provider burden, patient confusion, unexpected costs, discrimination against patients choosing NPs, and rural or small-practice implementation hardship.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Proprietary payer portals, fax/phone workflows, complex EHR systems, rapid formulary changes, limited technology resources in smaller and rural practices, and fragmented plan website reporting.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Over 243,000 NPs billing Medicare as of 2024; about 80% of NPs see Medicare and Medicaid patients; approximately 70% of NP graduates are prepared in primary care; APRN/PA Medicare encounters grew 50% from 2018 to 2023 while primary care physician encounters declined 22%; 57% of Medicare beneficiaries and 66% of rural Medicare patients received a primary care service from an NP or PA.
- **Studies or research referenced:** MedPAC July 2025 data book; MedPAC March 2026 report; AANP publications on quality and cost-effectiveness; research on NP care in rural and low-income areas; GAO and other cited sources.
- **Examples provided:** Providers use fax machines, proprietary payer portals, telephones, and other means when interacting with insurers; providers may not receive denial information or real-time responses.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Nurse practitioners, patients, Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries, rural patients, small and rural practices, payers, EHR vendors, CMS, QHP issuers, Medicaid/CHIP programs.
- **Economic implications:** Standardization may reduce administrative burden and EHR customization costs; lack of support could impose disproportionate costs on small and rural practices.
- **Technical feasibility:** AANP views payer-side standardization as feasible and beneficial but stresses that provider-side capability varies.
- **Patient care impact:** Expected to improve medication access, reduce delays, lower confusion, and support patient-centered care, especially where NPs deliver primary and rural care.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS
