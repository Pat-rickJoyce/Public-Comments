ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am responding to the request for comments on the question, &quot;Would requiring impacted payers to make information available using both standards add value, or create additional burden, specifically related to electronic prior authorization of drugs?&quot; Although requiring impacted payers to make information available using both standards may seem like it creates extra burden as payers may lack the resources, education, or knowledge to provide this information, if given the proper steps from their insurance and/or providers this requirement will add value. Through simply working as a receptionist in the medical field, and experiencing the stress of receiving prior authorization from insurance as a patient, I have seen firsthand from both sides how tedious and prolonged this process can be. For many patients, the inability to receive prior authorization of drugs in a timely manner results in jepardized health. Additionally, this creates extra stress on providers. Providers have a responsibility to care for their patients, and when prior authorizations are not accepted the provider is put into a difficult position to provide resources for the patient to receive the drug either through samples or through contacting insurance and pharmacies. Many patients receive drugs that are medically necessary to either stay alive or in comfortable health. Through working in medical reception, I have seen patients who have received denials for their prior authorizations be admitted to hospitals due to their efforts to prolong the use of their medication instead of taking their prescribed dosages. This is can be life threatening, and cause additional payments for the patient and their insurance providers. The implementation of requiring impacted payers to make information available using both standards in order to streamline electronic prior authorization of drugs would not only relieve the patient&#39;s provider of extra work and stress when prior authorization denials come in, but also relieve the patient and insurance providers of additional financial burdens. 84159<br/>...
