Re: CMS-0062-P, 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule
I am the CTO and co-founder of EasyPA.ai, an AI-powered prior authorization platform serving medical practices. We work daily across EMRs, clearinghouses, and payers to automate prior authorization workflows. The proposals in CMS-0062-P will materially change the infrastructure our customers and partners operate on, so I want to share field perspective on what will work, what will struggle, and what CMS can do to make this rule succeed.
Overall support
EasyPA.ai strongly supports the direction of this rule. Extending electronic prior authorization to drugs, adopting FHIR for HIPAA-covered PA transactions, aligning decision timeframes, and requiring specific reasons for denial are the right calls. They reduce burden on providers, improve patient access, and create the structured data foundation needed for trustworthy automation downstream.
HL7v2 is not going away by October 1, 2027
CMS should plan for a hybrid reality. HL7v2 still moves the majority of clinical messages between EMRs, hospitals, labs, and pharmacies in the United States today. FHIR adoption is real and accelerating, but smaller payers, rural providers, regional plans, and many state Medicaid systems will not have rebuilt their cores in the next 17 months. They will depend on bridge infrastructure that translates between HL7v2 and FHIR in both directions.
We recommend CMS explicitly acknowledge bidirectional HL7v2 to FHIR conversion as a compliant pathway, provided the FHIR endpoint exposed to trading partners conforms to the required implementation guides. Without this, smaller plans and rural providers will face binary outcomes: full rip-and-replace, or fall out of compliance. Neither serves patients.
Specific reason for denial: require structured codes, not free text
The requirement to provide a specific reason for denying drug PA requests is one of the most important provisions in this rule. To make it actually useful for providers and patients, CMS should require denial reasons to be expressed using structured FHIR coded values, not free-text strings. Free text cannot be reliably parsed for appeals, automated resubmission, or population-level analysis. Structured denial reasons unlock all three and create the data exhaust CMS itself will need to evaluate whether this rule is working.
CDex attachments: preserve flexibility, push for structured exchange where possible
Real-world PA submissions still lean heavily on PDFs, scanned documents, and faxed clinical notes. Adopting the Da Vinci CDex IG is the right move because it preserves that flexibility while creating a clean envelope. We support this. We also encourage CMS to push payers to publish, in machine-readable form, exactly which data elements they require for each PA scenario, so providers stop sending entire charts to cover unknown payer requirements.
Response to RFI on payer API implementation
API compliance is uneven today. Many published FHIR endpoints fail in production on issues that basic conformance testing would catch. CMS should require end-to-end testing against published test scripts, with results filed annually alongside the API usage metrics already proposed in this rule. Self-attestation alone has not produced reliable interoperability and will not produce it for drug PA either.
On AI in the prior authorization pipeline
CMS will receive many comments invoking artificial intelligence. The technical reality is that different AI systems do very different things in this workflow. Classification models triage and route. Generative models draft appeals and summarize clinical documentation. These are distinct capabilities with distinct risk profiles. Any future rulemaking on AI in prior authorization should make this distinction explicit and require transparency on which model types are used at each decision point, particularly when AI contributes to a denial.
Closing
The October 1, 2027 compliance date is achievable for payers that begin work now and that can access interoperability infrastructure built on open standards. EasyPA.ai is committed to supporting that transition through purpose-built tooling, including our HL7v2 to FHIR converter available on AWS Marketplace.
Thank you for the opportunity to comment.
