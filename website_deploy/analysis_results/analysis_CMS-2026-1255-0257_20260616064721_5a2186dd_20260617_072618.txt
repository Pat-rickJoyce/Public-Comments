## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Society of Gynecologic Oncology (SGO); signed by Matthew A. Powell, M.D.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SGO represents more than 2,700 gynecologic oncology physicians, advanced practice providers, nurses, and patient advocates. Dr. Powell is the Ira C. and Judith Gall Professor in Obstetrics and Gynecology, Division of Gynecologic Oncology, Washington University School of Medicine.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SGO supports CMS and ONC efforts to streamline prior authorization for drugs and improve interoperability, especially for oncology therapies where delays can affect survival and care outcomes. The commenter recommends making drug and therapy coverage criteria available through payer APIs, grounding ePA decisions in current clinical guidelines, creating exceptions for faster-than-proposed PA decisions when clinically urgent, and integrating step therapy information into ePA while preserving physician override authority.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL/RFI topics on step therapy and treatment history portability.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations mentioned.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization, application programming interfaces, interoperability standards, EHR systems; no specific FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, or HL7 IGs are named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized ePA processes can reduce manual workflow burden, make PA more efficient for patients and providers, and support timely access to necessary drugs and therapies.
- **Specific request:** Finalize policies that include drugs in PA and interoperability requirements and modernize ePA workflows.

**Topic 2: Coverage criteria availability through payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers need access to coverage criteria before submitting PA requests so they know what documentation is needed and can avoid preventable delays.
- **Specific request:** Require drug and therapy coverage criteria to be accessible to providers through plan APIs.

**Topic 3: Clinically grounded PA decisions and exemptions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology treatment success varies by patient, and PA approvals or denials should reflect current clinical criteria, consensus statements, and specialty guidelines.
- **Specific request:** Require plans using ePA for drugs to consult current clinical criteria and consider exempting treatments with proven success or those following recognized standards and guidelines.

**Topic 4: Decision timeframes and real-time determinations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** SGO supports 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited PA timelines, but aggressive gynecologic cancers may require faster decisions for chemotherapy and other life-saving therapies.
- **Specific request:** Create exceptions requiring faster determinations in urgent clinical situations and continue working toward real-time approval or denial decisions.

**Topic 5: Step therapy transparency, documentation, and overrides**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can be administratively burdensome and clinically harmful when it forces repeat use of ineffective, poorly tolerated, or unsuitable therapies.
- **Specific request:** Integrate step therapy protocols with ePA, establish consistent documentation expectations across plans, make step therapy requirements visible at the point of care, improve portability of treatment histories, and preserve physician authority to override or bypass step therapy when clinically necessary.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** SGO addresses technical and workflow challenges for medical-benefit drug PA by supporting standardized ePA and requesting payer API access to drug and therapy coverage criteria.
- **S20:** SGO supports the proposed 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timelines but asks CMS to require faster exceptions for urgent oncology situations and move toward real-time decisions.
- **S16:** The comment does not focus on denial reason wording, but it asks that approval and denial decisions be based on current clinical criteria and specialty guidelines.
- **S66-S82:** SGO responds to the RFI on step therapy by recommending interoperable, point-of-care access to requirements, consistent documentation, treatment history portability, and physician override flexibility.
- **S37:** The comment implicitly addresses implementation challenges by identifying the need for collaboration among CMS, plans, and EHR vendors to reach real-time PA decisions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize drug ePA and interoperability policies to reduce manual PA burden.
2. Require plan APIs to make drug and therapy coverage criteria available to providers before PA submission.
3. Require ePA decisions to use current clinical criteria, consensus statements, and specialty society guidelines.
4. Require plans to consider PA exemptions for treatments with proven success or that follow recognized clinical standards.
5. Maintain proposed 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited PA timelines, but add exceptions for faster determinations in urgent clinical situations.
6. Continue working with plans and EHR vendors toward real-time PA approval or denial decisions.
7. Integrate step therapy protocols with ePA and make requirements visible to patients and providers at the point of care.
8. Standardize step therapy documentation expectations across health plans.
9. Improve portability of treatment histories between plans to prevent inappropriate repeat therapy requirements.
10. Preserve physician authority to override or bypass step therapy when clinically necessary.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Treatment delays for cancer patients, administrative burden, inconsistent plan requirements, lack of coverage criteria visibility, and step therapy protocols that do not reflect clinical appropriateness.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Patient anxiety, delayed care delivery, emotional and physical harm, worse outcomes for aggressive gynecologic cancers, and burdens on patients who travel long distances for specialty care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Need for payer API functionality, EHR vendor and plan collaboration, consistent documentation across plans, and better capture/portability of treatment histories.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** SGO represents more than 2,700 members.
- **Studies or research referenced:** SGO references its repository of gynecologic oncology resources and consensus statements/guidelines at https://www.sgo.org/resources/.
- **Examples provided:** Life-saving chemotherapy and therapies for aggressive gynecologic cancers; patients traveling long distances for specialty care; patients forced to repeat ineffective or poorly tolerated therapies after changing plans.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Gynecologic oncology patients, oncologists, specialty practices, health plans, EHR vendors, and CMS/ONC.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced manual workflow burden may improve provider efficiency; the comment does not quantify costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** SGO views interoperability, payer APIs, EHR integration, and treatment-history portability as necessary but requiring continued stakeholder collaboration.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment stresses that delays in oncology PA can affect survival, treatment continuity, shared decision-making, and patient anxiety.
