## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** HealthHIV
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / national nonprofit
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National nonprofit advancing HIV, viral hepatitis, STI, mpox, and LGBTQ health care through education, technical assistance, advocacy, communications, health services research, and evaluation; comment includes first-person patient experience aging with HIV.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HealthHIV supports more electronic, transparent, and transferable prior authorization processes, including faster ePA, clear denial reasons, and payer-to-payer continuity. It warns that making PA electronic does not make it appropriate for every drug, especially HIV treatment, PrEP, and other time-sensitive chronic medications where any delay can harm treatment continuity.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drugs; FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs for medical benefit drugs; Access APIs and Payer-to-Payer exchange.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with caution
- **Rationale:** FHIR and NCPDP approaches can reduce avoidable delays, but electronic processing does not answer whether PA should be used for HIV treatment, PrEP, or other medications where interruption can undermine care.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA requirements for drugs across pharmacy-benefit and medical-benefit pathways while scrutinizing whether PA is appropriate for time-sensitive chronic medications.

**Topic 2: Continuity across coverage transitions**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** People with HIV may move among Medicaid, Marketplace coverage, Medicare Advantage, Part D, Ryan White HIV/AIDS Program supports, pharmacies, PBMs, and specialty pharmacies; PA history and rationale should not disappear.
- **Specific request:** Use Access APIs and Payer-to-Payer exchange to preserve prior authorization history, documentation, denial rationale, and continuity across payer changes.

**Topic 3: Medical vs pharmacy benefit distinctions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Medications and related therapies may move through different benefit channels, creating confusion over standards, documentation, routing, and timelines.
- **Specific request:** Ensure rules and implementation guidance address benefit channel distinctions for chronic and complex conditions.

**Topic 4: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with monitoring
- **Rationale:** Denial rationales determine whether providers can correct documentation, appeal, identify step therapy, or locate bridge coverage such as Ryan White support.
- **Specific request:** Finalize specific denial-reason requirements and monitor whether reasons are clear, structured, and actionable.

**Topic 5: Real-world burden metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Success should be measured by reduced medication disruptions, not only by API use or completed transactions.
- **Specific request:** Evaluate time to therapy, avoidable denials, appeal resolution, refill disruption, provider and navigator workload, and gaps in access.

**Topic 6: State-based exchange protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / extend
- **Rationale:** Consumers in State-based Exchanges and State-based Exchanges on the Federal Platform may face similar access barriers.
- **Specific request:** Clarify a pathway for those consumers to receive similar protections.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14:** Supports FHIR/NCPDP ePA pathways while highlighting real-world implementation across EHRs, payer systems, specialty pharmacies, PBMs, and pharmacy workflows.
- **S15:** Addresses medical versus pharmacy benefit distinctions and requests clarity because the pathway affects standards, routing, and documentation.
- **S16/S17:** Supports clear, structured, actionable denial reasons.
- **S20/S43:** Supports faster processing and continuity but emphasizes that PA delay may still be clinically harmful for HIV and PrEP.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Requests metrics beyond API volume, including time to therapy, avoidable denials, appeal resolution, refill disruption, workload, and access gaps.
- **S66-S82:** Responds broadly to continuity, step therapy/fail-first implications, and real-world burden for chronic, complex, and time-sensitive conditions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for drugs, including pharmacy-benefit and medical-benefit pathways.
2. Finalize clear, structured, actionable denial-reason requirements.
3. Use Access APIs and payer-to-payer exchange to preserve continuity across Medicaid, Marketplace, MA, Part D, Ryan White, and other payer transitions.
4. Evaluate PA burden through time-to-therapy, avoidable denials, appeal resolution, refill disruption, provider/navigator workload, and gaps in access.
5. Clarify protections for consumers enrolled through State-based Exchanges and State-based Exchanges on the Federal Platform.
6. Consider whether PA should be used at all for HIV treatment, PrEP, and other medications where delay threatens continuity.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA may be inappropriate for HIV treatment and prevention drugs; delays can create clinical risk; benefit channel distinctions and coverage transitions can disrupt continuity; vague denial reasons and step therapy can cause long-tail access problems.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Late treatment starts, delayed refills, viral rebound, immune decompensation, clinical progression, reduced PrEP protection, increased HIV acquisition risk, lost-to-care status, and erosion of trust.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Fragmented payer systems, multiple prescribers and pharmacies, PBM/specialty pharmacy involvement, benefit pathway ambiguity, and lack of visible PA history across coverage transitions.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics cited; relies on clinical context and lived experience.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None named.
- **Examples provided:** Older adults with HIV managing comorbidities and polypharmacy; personal experience challenging PA and quantity-limit decisions while remaining dependent on the same payer/PBM/specialty pharmacy for monthly access; movement among Medicaid, Marketplace, MA, Part D, and Ryan White supports.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** People living and aging with HIV, PrEP users, patients with chronic and complex conditions, providers, navigators, pharmacies, PBMs, specialty pharmacies, payers, and consumers in state-based exchange structures.
- **Economic implications:** Avoidable delays can increase downstream clinical and system costs; bridge coverage and navigator burden may be needed when PA fails.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if APIs exchange history, documentation, denial rationale, and status across systems and if implementation testing includes real workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Potentially positive if ePA preserves continuity and reduces gaps; harmful if electronic systems still produce delays, vague denials, or missing information.
