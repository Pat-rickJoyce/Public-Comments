# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** College of American Pathologists (CAP); signed by Qihui "Jim" Zhai, MD, FCAP, President.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** World's largest organization of board-certified pathologists and leading provider of laboratory accreditation and proficiency testing programs.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CAP argues that prior authorization for laboratory services delays medically necessary testing, disrupts diagnosis and treatment, burdens physicians and laboratories, and can be misused by MA plans and laboratory benefit managers. CAP recommends workflow-aligned reforms including TRF acceptance, post-specimen authorization pathways, public reporting of denial reasons and metrics, LBM oversight, limits on broad utilization management, stronger decision timeframes, and safeguards for automation and AI.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to PA timeframes; API-USAGE-METRICS by implication for reporting and metrics; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED/API workflow concepts by implication for unified API-based ePA.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 422.138(b).
- **Standards referenced:** Test requisition forms; Medicare Part B laboratory date of service policy; Medicare NCDs and LCDs; API-based electronic prior authorization; EHRs; LISs; laboratory ordering systems; AMA Consensus Statement on Improving the Prior Authorization Process; AMA augmented intelligence policy.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratory PA impact on patient care and provider burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Laboratory testing supports diagnosis, treatment selection, monitoring, and care coordination; PA delays can postpone care and create serious adverse events.
- **Specific request:** Align PA requirements with the operational realities of laboratory medicine and reduce unnecessary PA burden.

**Topic 2: TRF acceptance and correction periods**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support and modify.
- **Rationale:** Laboratories often lack full medical records and reasonably rely on TRFs containing test orders, demographics, diagnosis or clinical indication, and medical necessity information.
- **Specific request:** Accept TRFs as valid medical documentation, create correction/amendment periods for incomplete TRFs, prevent subjective completeness denials, and protect laboratories relying in good faith absent fraud or material misrepresentation.

**Topic 3: Date-of-service and post-specimen authorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Denials based on specimen collection timing are procedural and inconsistent with laboratory workflows, especially when specimens are limited, time-sensitive, or irreplaceable.
- **Specific request:** Require MA plans and other payers to accept PA requests submitted after specimen collection or date of service, and maintain retrospective or post-service PA pathways for laboratory services.

**Topic 4: LBM oversight and safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** CAP says LBMs can add proprietary portals, duplicative questionnaires, rigid criteria, payment denials, financial incentives to reduce utilization, and conflicts when affiliated laboratories are involved.
- **Specific request:** Require transparency regarding criteria, evidence, and financial relationships; limit PA to high-cost/esoteric tests; prohibit technical denials unrelated to medical necessity; prohibit designated-lab referrals when other in-network labs are available; require interoperable LBM platforms; and require qualified reviewers.

**Topic 5: Transparency, reporting, and denial notices**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support and modify.
- **Rationale:** Public reporting can reveal inappropriate procedural denials, turnaround delays, appeal outcomes, and LBM performance.
- **Specific request:** Require reporting of denial reasons, DOS/timing denials, procedural grounds, turnaround times, approval/denial rates, appeal outcomes, LBM-related metrics, and denial notice details including reviewer credentials and automated-decision involvement.

**Topic 6: Timeframes and enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Delays in laboratory testing can produce serious patient harm and treatment abandonment.
- **Specific request:** Shorten expedited timeframes to 24 hours and standard timeframes to 48 hours across all payers, with meaningful enforcement for repeated failures.

**Topic 7: Automation and AI safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Automation could speed requirements identification, submission, and tracking, but could also accelerate opaque or inappropriate denials.
- **Specific request:** Require transparent disclosure, consistent implementation across payers, integration into EHR/LIS/lab ordering systems, individualized clinical review, override flexibility, and final determinations by providers at least as qualified as the ordering provider.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons and asks that denial notices identify the coverage criterion applied, insufficient documentation, reviewer credentials/specialty, and automated decision support involvement.
- **S17:** Supports distinguishing denial categories, including medical necessity versus timing, documentation, or procedural grounds.
- **S20:** Urges 24-hour expedited and 48-hour standard PA timeframes across payers for time-sensitive testing contexts.
- **S29:** Recommends public reporting of detailed PA metrics, denial reasons, turnaround times, approval and denial rates, appeals outcomes, and LBM-related metrics.
- **S30:** Addresses transparency and accountability safeguards around automated decision-making and payer systems, though not privacy metrics specifically.
- **S54:** By analogy to testing and automation, supports consistent technology adoption, interoperability, and qualified clinical review rather than opaque automated determinations.
- **S66-S82:** Provides extensive RFI responses on laboratory test PA, TRFs, post-specimen authorization, LBMs, process improvements, automation, technology limits, and workforce impacts.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Accept TRFs as valid documentation of medical necessity when they contain necessary clinical information.
2. Establish correction or amendment periods for incomplete TRFs.
3. Protect laboratories from retrospective denial or recoupment when they rely in good faith on TRFs, absent fraud or material misrepresentation.
4. Require payers to accept PA requests submitted after specimen collection or date of service for laboratory tests.
5. Require retrospective or post-service PA pathways when testing must occur before PA can reasonably be completed.
6. Require public reporting of denial reasons, procedural denials, turnaround times, approval/denial rates, appeals outcomes, and LBM-related metrics.
7. Limit laboratory PA to narrowly defined high-cost or esoteric testing and avoid duplicative PA where NCDs or LCDs already define coverage.
8. Adopt gold-carding or exemption programs for high-approval clinicians and services.
9. Prohibit technical or procedural denials unrelated to medical necessity.
10. Strengthen LBM oversight, transparency, interoperability, conflict safeguards, and qualified reviewer requirements.
11. Shorten expedited PA decisions to 24 hours and standard PA decisions to 48 hours across payers.
12. Require mechanisms to report, track, and resolve payer portal failures and interoperability disruptions.
13. Permit automation only with transparency, interoperability, individualized clinical review, override flexibility, and qualified human determinations.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Laboratory PA delays, specimen timing denials, inconsistent payer requirements, proprietary portals, LBM interference, automated denials, inadequate transparency, and workforce burnout.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed diagnosis, delayed treatment initiation, disrupted care coordination, worsened outcomes, adverse events, abandonment of care, hospitalization, life-threatening complications, permanent harm, nonpayment, and workforce attrition.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Laboratories' limited access to full medical records, dependence on ordering providers, inconsistent payer portals and documentation requirements, incomplete TRFs, limited/time-sensitive specimens, EHR/LIS integration needs, and LBM conflicts.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** HHS OIG found 13% of MA PA denials would have been approved under traditional Medicare and 18% of denied payment requests met Medicare coverage and billing rules; one survey found 40% of physicians have staff working exclusively on PA and physicians/staff spend 13 hours per week on PA; projected shortage of up to 86,000 physicians by 2036; HRSA projects a shortage of 3,540 pathologists by 2036 and 83% national adequacy.
- **Studies or research referenced:** HHS OIG April 2022 report; AMA prior authorization survey; CMS MolDX article; ProPublica reporting on EviCore; AMA Consensus Statement on Improving the Prior Authorization Process; AMA augmented intelligence policy; HRSA workforce projections.
- **Examples provided:** BeaconLBS/UnitedHealthcare workflow reportedly requiring up to 22 steps; pathologists handling time-sensitive specimens with incomplete requisitions; technical denials based on portal or documentation formatting issues.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing diagnostic testing, pathologists, ordering providers, laboratories, MA plans, MACs, non-Medicare payers, LBMs, EHR/LIS vendors, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** PA increases administrative staffing costs, risks nonpayment for performed testing, creates payment uncertainty, and may worsen workforce sustainability.
- **Technical feasibility:** Automation and API-based ePA are feasible if standardized, integrated into EHR/LIS/lab ordering systems, and paired with policy safeguards.
- **Patient care impact:** Delayed or denied laboratory testing can postpone diagnosis and treatment, compromise specimen integrity, and worsen clinical outcomes.
