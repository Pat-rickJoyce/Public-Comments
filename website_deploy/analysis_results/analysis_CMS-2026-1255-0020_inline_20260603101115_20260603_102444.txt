CMS-0062-P — 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule

To Whom It May Concern:

I am writing in strong support of CMS's proposed efforts to improve prior authorization transparency, interoperability, and timeliness under CMS-0062-P. As a practicing psychiatrist and medical leader involved in treating patients with severe and treatment-resistant psychiatric illness, I believe these reforms are critically needed—particularly for advanced psychiatric treatments such as Spravato (esketamine) and Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS).

Patients who qualify for Spravato or TMS often suffer from treatment-resistant depression, severe suicidal ideation, obsessive-compulsive disorder, or other highly impairing psychiatric conditions. These individuals have frequently failed multiple traditional medication trials and may be experiencing substantial functional decline, hospitalization risk, suicidality, inability to work, or profound impairment in daily living. Delays in treatment access can therefore result in serious clinical deterioration, psychiatric hospitalization, emergency department utilization, self-harm risk, and increased healthcare costs.

Current prior authorization processes for advanced psychiatric treatments are frequently burdensome, inconsistent, duplicative, and excessively delayed. Providers are often required to repeatedly submit documentation that already clearly demonstrates medical necessity. These delays create significant administrative burden while placing vulnerable patients at substantial clinical risk.

The patients needing these treatments have already undergone extensive medication trials and psychotherapy before being considered for treatment. Yet providers commonly encounter repetitive authorization requirements, inconsistent payer criteria, and repeated requests for documentation that delay access to care. These barriers are especially concerning given the strong evidence base supporting these treatments as safe and effective treatment for treatment-resistant depression and other psychiatric disorders.

I strongly support CMS proposals that would:

• Require electronic prior authorization capabilities for drugs and medical services
• Establish standardized response timelines for prior authorizations
• Require specific reasons for denials
• Increase public reporting and transparency regarding prior authorization metrics

I also encourage CMS to consider the following additional protections and clarifications for psychiatric treatments such as Spravato and TMS:

Enforce Rapid Review Standards for Serious Psychiatric Conditions
Psychiatric illnesses can rapidly worsen when treatment is delayed. Expedited review standards should explicitly include severe treatment-resistant depression, suicidality, and other serious psychiatric conditions. Delays measured in weeks can have life-threatening consequences.
Reduce Redundant Step Therapy and Documentation Requirements
Many patients seeking Spravato or TMS have already failed multiple evidence-based treatments. Excessive or repetitive step therapy beyond established clinical guidelines unnecessarily delays medically necessary care and prolongs suffering.
Improve Transparency Around Denials
Providers should receive clinically meaningful explanations for denials, including the exact missing criteria, records, or rationale. Generic denial language creates unnecessary appeals, delays, and administrative waste.
Promote Continuity of Care
Patients who are stabilized and responding to Spravato or TMS should not face repeated interruptions due to recurring administrative barriers. Continuity of treatment is especially important in severe psychiatric illness.
Encourage Greater Standardization Across Payers
Significant variability exists among payers regarding psychiatric prior authorization criteria, particularly for TMS. Greater standardization would reduce administrative burden and improve equitable patient access to evidence-based care.
Recognize the Severity of Behavioral Health Conditions
Behavioral health conditions often carry significant morbidity, mortality, disability, and healthcare utilization. Prior authorization systems should account for the time-sensitive and medically serious nature of psychiatric care similarly to other high-risk medical conditions.

CMS's proposed interoperability and prior authorization reforms represent an important step toward improving patient access, reducing administrative waste, and supporting timely evidence-based care. These reforms can meaningfully improve outcomes for patients suffering from severe mental illness while reducing downstream healthcare costs associated with untreated or undertreated psychiatric disease.

Thank you for the opportunity to comment on this important proposed rule.
