ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a physician, neurologist, headache specialist, and a person living with migraine with aura. I agree with recommendations to shorten times for prior authorization determinations and to eliminate as many barriers as possible to step therapy requirements for medications.

Migraine is a chronic neurologic disease that is the number one leading cause of disability for women under 50 and one of the top five leading causes of years lived with disability globally. I have had multiple patients present to me specify when asked how many hours of their lives they have lost to migraine, they will specify years worth of hours and days spent on the couch or in bed, disabled by migraine. Multiple individuals within my practice alone have lost their jobs to migraine.

Prior authorizations cause a significant delay in providing medical care for patients when delayed. These hours, days, weeks and even months of delay can worsen a patient's ability to work and sometimes those instances will make the difference between working to getting fired and losing their jobs. Multiple patients have repeatedly stated that because of step therapy requirements, they have spent months or years battling insurance to get on recommended, targeted medical therapies.

In my practice, prior authorization delays additional cause significant administrative burden on our staff. I agree that faster decisions, less requirements for patients to getting into medical therapies, forced switches based on insurance formularies and not on patients prior clinical responses or medical necessity would vastly improve patients' quality of life and overall medical expenditures.

The American headache society in 2024 recommended cgrp migraine targeted treatment options as preventive first line medical therapies in migraine and for rescue therapies these are immensely important and effective when used as first line therapies. CMS should consider this when deciding on future rules and formulary requirements/precedent moving forward.

Often times, prior authorizations are not clear what they require from an office on how patients can start therapies, or the requirements necessary do not make sense or are contraindicated for patients. Electronic prior authorization status updates should be required as insurances often will not answer requests for feedback or provide clear updates. Overall, patients need expedited access to care that is medically recommended by their providers.
