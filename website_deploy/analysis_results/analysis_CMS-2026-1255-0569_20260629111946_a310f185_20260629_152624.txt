## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Paltown Development Foundation; signed by Director of Operations & Finance / Interim Executive Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** 501(c)(3) nonprofit patient advocacy organization supporting people affected by colorectal cancer and caregivers through online patient communities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Paltown Development Foundation responds to the RFI on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing, urging CMS to protect cancer patients from delays and denials for medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing. The commenter asks CMS to allow retroactive authorization after specimens are collected, prohibit timing-only denials, prevent repeat blood draws when valid specimens exist, require expedited oncology review, protect patients from surprise costs, and require transparency around criteria, denial reasons, and specialty performance.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; TRANSPARENCY.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P referenced; no CFR citations mentioned.
- **Standards referenced:** Colorectal cancer molecular biomarker testing guideline from ASCP/CAP/AMP/ASCO; no FHIR, NCPDP, or API implementation standards discussed directly.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior authorization barriers to oncology laboratory and biomarker testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Biomarker testing determines targeted therapy, immunotherapy, treatment changes, recurrence detection, resistance mutations, and clinical trial eligibility. Delays caused by PA paperwork can lead to wrong therapy, later diagnosis, disease progression, repeat blood draws, and lost treatment opportunities.
- **Specific request:** CMS should keep PA only where genuinely necessary and remove it where it functions as an administrative barrier to standard, guideline-recommended cancer testing.

**Topic 2: Retroactive authorization and timing-based denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Plans that require approval by the specimen collection date can cause valid samples to be delayed, rejected, or discarded, even when the patient, clinician, and lab acted correctly.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to allow retroactive authorization after specimen collection and prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.

**Topic 3: Avoiding repeat blood draws and administrative rework**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Repeat draws impose travel, childcare, work, financial, and physical burdens, especially for rural, low-income, immunocompromised, seriously ill, and cancer patients undergoing treatment.
- **Specific request:** Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.

**Topic 4: Expedited review for oncology diagnostics and biomarker testing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** In aggressive cancers, weeks lost to paperwork can determine whether a patient remains healthy enough to receive targeted therapy.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.

**Topic 5: Patient financial protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Patients should not face surprise costs when delays are administrative and outside their control.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from surprise costs and coverage denials caused by administrative PA delays.

**Topic 6: Transparency and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support/expand.
- **Rationale:** Patients, clinicians, and CMS need visibility into where PA processes fail, especially when denials rely on outdated criteria inconsistent with current science.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to be transparent about decision criteria, denial reasons, performance by specialty, and accountability when policy is outdated.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16/S17:** The comment calls for clear denial reasons and prohibits denial based only on PA timing when medical necessity is documented.
- **S20/S43:** Responds indirectly by requesting expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing where delay can affect treatment decisions and survival.
- **S29/S35:** Recommends transparency and performance reporting by specialty to identify payer barriers, outdated policies, and process failures.
- **S66-S82:** Directly responds to the RFI on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing in Section III.E, emphasizing biomarker/genomic testing protections for colorectal cancer.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.
3. Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.
4. Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.
5. Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative.
6. Require plan transparency around decision criteria, denial reasons, and performance by specialty.
7. Hold plans accountable when denials stem from outdated policies that no longer reflect current medical and scientific evidence.
8. Keep prior authorization only for cases where it is genuinely necessary and eliminate it where it delays guideline-recommended cancer testing.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rigid PA timing rules, outdated payer policies, delayed biomarker results, repeat blood draws, surprise costs, and lack of transparency.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Later diagnoses, delayed cancer care, wrong initial therapy, permanent chemotherapy side effects, missed targeted therapy opportunities, clinical trial ineligibility, disease progression, palliative care, hospitalizations, lost income, family strain, and widened disparities.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Insurer systems that assume outdated one-gene testing; lack of rural clinic staff to fight denials; labs holding tests because of payment risk; payer requirements that authorization be complete on or before specimen collection.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Professional benchmark that 90% of colorectal cancer molecular biomarker results should be reported within ten working days of lab receipt; survey of more than 300 oncology providers found 66% view insurer coverage and PA rules as a moderate/significant barrier to appropriate biomarker testing; 91% rely on clinical guidelines to decide what to order; laboratory tests inform roughly 70% of medical decisions.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Annals of Oncology article on biomarker results delaying treatment decisions; ASCP/CAP/AMP/ASCO colorectal cancer molecular biomarker testing guideline; ACS CAN survey of payer coverage policies for tumor biomarker and pharmacogenomic testing; American Clinical Laboratory Association 2024 PA reform recommendations; Binaytara article on insurance barriers to genomic testing.
- **Examples provided:** Advanced CRC patient denied comprehensive testing by outdated rules; two stage IV CRC patients with different outcomes depending on academic center vs rural clinic PA capacity; BRAF V600E suspected aggressive CRC patient whose approval delay means targeted therapy arrives too late.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Colorectal cancer patients, caregivers, oncology providers, rural clinics, low-income patients, immunocompromised and seriously ill patients, laboratories, health plans, CMS, and patient advocacy organizations.
- **Economic implications:** Delays increase costs through wrong therapy, hospital stays, palliative care, lost income, family strain, repeat testing, and later-stage disease care.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment does not address FHIR/API feasibility; it focuses on policy guardrails that align PA timing with lab workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Very high; biomarker testing can determine treatment selection, targeted therapy eligibility, resistance mutations, trial eligibility, and survival-related treatment timing.
