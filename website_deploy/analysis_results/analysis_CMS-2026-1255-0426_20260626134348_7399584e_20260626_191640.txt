# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Avectic Corporation; signed by Ryan Kamykowski, Chief Executive Officer.
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Avectic describes itself as a Michigan healthcare software company building prior authorization decision systems for provider organizations, beginning with orthopedic spine surgery and provider-administered infusion therapy. It is also convener of the Healthcare Decision Integrity Working Group, which published the draft RCD-1 standard for reproducibility and traceability in coverage decision systems.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Avectic strongly supports the rule's move toward standardized, electronic, transparent prior authorization for drugs. It asks CMS to add requirements for automated or AI-assisted determinations: reproducibility, criterion-level machine-readable reasons, policy version identification, and accountable human review for adverse outcomes.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No specific CFR citation is quoted; the comment references CMS-0057-F and CMS guidance for Medicare Advantage automated tools.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API; RCD-1 Working Draft v0.9; machine-readable criterion-level determination data; electronic prior authorization drug framework.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Reproducibility of automated determinations**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Automated determinations that cannot be re-executed with the same inputs and policy version cannot be meaningfully appealed, audited, or overseen.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to be able to re-execute software-produced or software-assisted PA determinations using the originally recorded inputs and policy version and obtain the identical result.

**Topic 2: Criterion-level denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Denial reasons should be actionable for prescribers and should show which coverage criteria were evaluated, how each criterion was resolved, and which policy provision supported each result.
- **Specific request:** Require adverse or partial determinations through the Prior Authorization API to provide criterion-level, structured, machine-readable reasons.

**Topic 3: Coverage policy versioning**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Without version identifiers and effective dates, providers and auditors cannot determine which rule governed a case or compare cases over time.
- **Specific request:** Require each electronically exposed coverage policy artifact to include a unique version identifier and effective date range, and require each determination to record the policy version applied.

**Topic 4: Human accountability for adverse determinations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Automated tools should assist but not replace required clinical review, and national consistency would reduce state-by-state fragmentation.
- **Specific request:** State that automated processing may not be the sole basis for an adverse drug PA determination and require identification of an accountable, qualified human reviewer.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Avectic responds that a specific reason should operate at the coverage-criterion level, identifying each criterion evaluated, the result, and the governing policy provision.
- **S17:** The commenter supports structured, machine-readable denial information through the Prior Authorization API.
- **S54:** The RCD-1 framework is offered as a testable approach, including a re-execution test protocol for reproducibility.
- **S66-S82:** The comment addresses general automation, AI, transparency, auditability, and accountability issues not tied to a single listed drug-benefit solicitation.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require reproducibility for software-produced or software-assisted PA determinations.
2. Require criterion-level, machine-readable reasons for adverse or partial drug PA determinations.
3. Require unique version identifiers and effective date ranges for electronically exposed coverage policies.
4. Require each determination to record the coverage policy version applied.
5. Prohibit automated processing from serving as the sole basis for adverse drug PA determinations.
6. Require identification of an accountable, qualified human reviewer for each adverse determination.
7. Consider RCD-1 as a free reference framework for reproducibility and traceability requirements.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Electronic PA standardization may move information efficiently without ensuring automated decisions are explainable, reproducible, auditable, or accountable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Unreproducible automated decisions could undermine appeals, audits, oversight, and trust in drug PA decisions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of standardized policy versioning, lack of structured criterion-level denial outputs, and fragmented state requirements for human review.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, prescribers, payers, regulators, vendors, auditors, and human clinical reviewers.
- **Economic implications:** The commenter characterizes the requirements as inexpensive and technology-neutral for conformant systems, because properly designed decision systems already generate the information.
- **Technical feasibility:** The proposals are framed as feasible software evidence requirements rather than architecture mandates.
- **Patient care impact:** More transparent, reproducible, and accountable decisions would support fair appeals, reduce delays caused by opaque denials, and increase trust in automated PA workflows.
