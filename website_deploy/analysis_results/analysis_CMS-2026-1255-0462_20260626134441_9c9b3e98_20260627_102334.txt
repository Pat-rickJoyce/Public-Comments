## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ACLA responds to the RFI on prior authorization for laboratory tests, documenting significant systemic barriers that payers and laboratory benefit managers (LBMs) impose on clinical laboratories, including denial of PA requests submitted after the specimen collection "date of service," refusal to accept PA requests from laboratories, and rejection of test requisition forms (TRFs) as medical necessity documentation. ACLA offers eight specific recommendations to CMS to address these barriers, including prohibiting date-of-service denials, requiring acceptance of laboratory-initiated PA requests and TRFs, mandating granular PA metrics, limiting PA for LCD/NCD-covered tests, ensuring laboratory appeal rights, establishing a payer non-compliance reporting mechanism, and strengthening Organization Determination requirements for plan-directed care.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prohibition on Date-of-Service Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Oppose (current payer practice); recommend new policy
- **Rationale:** The Medicare Part B "date of service" rule (42 C.F.R. § 414.510(a)) was designed for billing attribution (hospital vs. independent lab), not as a PA deadline. MA plans and payers have distorted it to reject PA requests submitted after specimen collection, effectively making PA impossible for labs that receive specimens without pre-authorization. CMS itself stated this practice "is not compliant with the rules in the MA program."
- **Specific request:** CMS prohibit impacted payers from denying coverage based on PA having been sought after date of service; communicate through rulemaking, HPMS memo, and/or Medicare Managed Care Manual; monitor compliance and establish a non-compliance reporting mechanism.

**Topic 2: Right of Laboratories to Submit PA Requests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Support (CMS's stated position) and enforce
- **Rationale:** Some payers (notably those using Carelon as an LBM) allow only ordering clinicians to submit PA requests, even though it is the laboratory's reimbursement at risk.
- **Specific request:** Require all impacted payers to accept PA requests from clinical laboratories or ordering clinicians; make repeated non-compliance subject to corrective action and negative Star Ratings.

**Topic 3: Acceptance of Test Requisition Forms (TRFs)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Recommend new requirement
- **Rationale:** TRFs contain patient demographics, diagnosis codes, tests ordered, clinical indications, and physician attestation — sufficient to demonstrate medical necessity. The Medicare MolDX program already recognizes TRFs as sufficient documentation in more than half of states.
- **Specific request:** CMS clarify that TRFs are valid medical documentation; additional documentation may be demanded only when TRF lacks sufficient clinical information to determine medical necessity.

**Topic 4: Granularity of PA Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with additions
- **Rationale:** Metrics aggregated at the organization level have little practical value; meaningful analysis requires breakdown by provider type of requestor and by denial reason.
- **Specific request:** Report PA metrics by Health Care Provider Taxonomy Level II Classification; add metrics for percentage denied per denial reason, percentage not decided within applicable timeframe, and list of PA-required items covered under LCDs/NCDs.

**Topic 5: Limit PA for LCD/NCD-Covered Laboratory Tests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Recommend new policy
- **Rationale:** MA plans require PA for tests already covered under LCDs/NCDs; more than half of such MA claim denials are overturned on appeal, indicating they are frequently inappropriate.
- **Specific request:** Require MA plans to limit PA for LCD/NCD-covered tests; permit PA only when plans offer more expansive coverage than the applicable LCD or NCD.

**Topic 6: Laboratory Appeal Rights for Administrative Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Recommend new requirement
- **Rationale:** Payers classify denials as "administrative" when PA was sought by the "wrong" party or after the date of service, blocking independent medical necessity review.
- **Specific request:** Ensure laboratories have the option to appeal administrative PA denials on medical necessity grounds.

**Topic 7: Payer Non-Compliance Reporting Mechanism**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Recommend new mechanism
- **Rationale:** Current reporting mechanisms not designed for systematic payer non-compliance; typical appeals process is not feasible when a payer has a systematic problem affecting thousands of claims.
- **Specific request:** CMS establish a "hotline" or publicized reporting process for laboratories, ordering clinicians, and patients to report payer PA non-compliance; develop a resolution process for delayed/denied payments for good-faith services.

**Topic 8: Organization Determination Requirements for Plan-Directed Care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (laboratory RFI)
- **Position:** Recommend strengthening/clarification
- **Rationale:** MA regulations protect enrollees from financial liability for plan-directed care, but do not clarify plans' responsibility to pay laboratories for such care.
- **Specific request:** CMS clarify that ordering provider referrals for covered laboratory services constitute favorable coverage determinations for laboratory reimbursement; classify failure to obtain PA as plan-directed care with plan responsible for payment.

**Topic 9: LBM Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** TRANSPARENCY, GENERAL
- **Position:** Recommend new requirement
- **Rationale:** LBMs (Carelon, Evicore, Avalon) manage PA for major payers but operate with little transparency; coverage policies treated as trade secrets; payers often do not know what they cover.
- **Specific request:** Require MA plans to disclose clearly whether and how LBMs oversee their coverage and utilization management policies, with contact information for the responsible entity.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82 (General RFI — Laboratory Tests):** ACLA responds comprehensively to the laboratory PA RFI: documents workflow incompatibility between traditional PA timing and laboratory specimen collection; details date-of-service denial abuse; explains TRF evidentiary value; describes LBM opacity and shared-savings incentives to deny care; provides member survey data; offers 8 specific policy recommendations.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prohibit impacted payers from denying laboratory PA coverage based on PA having been sought after the "date of service."
2. Communicate this prohibition through rulemaking, HPMS memo, and/or Medicare Managed Care Manual; monitor compliance.
3. Require all impacted payers to accept PA requests from clinical laboratories or ordering clinicians, regardless of lab contract status.
4. Make repeated refusal to accept laboratory-initiated PA requests subject to corrective action and negative Star Ratings.
5. Clarify that TRFs are valid medical documentation; additional documentation demanded only when TRF lacks sufficient clinical information.
6. Require PA metrics broken down by Health Care Provider Taxonomy Level II Classification.
7. Add required metrics: percentage denied per denial reason; percentage not decided within applicable timeframe; list of PA-required items covered under LCDs/NCDs (MA plans).
8. Require MA plans to limit PA for LCD/NCD-covered tests; permit PA only for more expansive coverage than the applicable determination.
9. Ensure laboratories have the option to appeal administrative PA denials on medical necessity grounds.
10. Establish a publicized reporting mechanism ("hotline") for laboratories, clinicians, and patients to report payer PA non-compliance.
11. Develop a clear resolution process for delayed/denied payments for services provided in good faith.
12. Clarify that ordering provider referrals for covered laboratory services constitute favorable coverage determinations for laboratory reimbursement; classify failure to obtain PA as plan-directed care.
13. Require MA plans to disclose whether LBMs manage their PA processes and provide LBM contact information in coverage policies.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Date-of-service PA timing incompatibility with laboratory workflow; refusal to accept laboratory-initiated PA requests; TRF non-acceptance creating documentation barriers; LBM opacity hiding shared-savings incentives to deny care; administrative denial classifications blocking medical necessity appeals; lack of financial protection for laboratories providing plan-directed care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Laboratories absorbing financial losses to provide timely patient care; patient delays in diagnosis and treatment; cancer patients experiencing clinically meaningful harm; laboratory service availability threatened by uncompensated care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Specimen integrity requires testing before PA can be obtained; PA turnaround times (up to 14 days for MA ODs) incompatible with clinical workflow; laboratories lack access to full patient medical records; LBMs treat coverage criteria as trade secrets.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 70% of healthcare decisions depend on laboratory test results (CDC); 40% of total test volume subject to PA for labs with ≤50 tests on menu (ACLA member survey); 24% of claim denials due to PA process; 18% of PA denials overturned on appeal; 15–49% of MA claims for certain cancer-related laboratory tests denied for pre-certification/PA (XiFiN, 2024–2025); >50% of MA LCD/NCD-covered service denials overturned on appeal.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Two OIG reports on MA PA denial practices (OEI-09-18-00260; OEI-09-16-00410); XiFiN revenue cycle management analysis (2024–2025); MolDX Clarification of Order Requirements billing article (May 2024).
- **Examples provided:** Carelon LBM refuses PA requests submitted after date of service; payer self-service portal screenshot (Exhibit A) showing date-of-service denial policy; cancer laboratory test denial rates table.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Clinical laboratories; ordering physicians; Medicare Advantage enrollees; Medicaid managed care enrollees; cancer patients; LBMs (Carelon, Evicore, Avalon).
- **Economic implications:** Laboratories employ multiple full-time PA management staff; many contract third-party PA vendors at millions in cost; absorb financial losses providing uncompensated care; LBM shared-savings contracts create structural incentives for inappropriate denials.
- **Technical feasibility:** No major technical barriers to most recommendations; primarily policy and regulatory changes needed; granular reporting metrics require enhanced data collection from payers.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays in diagnosis, treatment selection, and therapy initiation; meaningful clinical consequences for cancer patients; laboratories currently protecting patients by running tests before PA disputes resolve — a practice that is financially unsustainable.
