# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Sarcoma Foundation of America; signed by Brandi Felser, Chief Executive Officer.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SFA describes itself as the leading national nonprofit dedicated to increasing sarcoma research, improving patient outcomes, and supporting people and families affected by sarcoma. The comment is grounded in rare cancer, oncology biomarker testing, and patient access concerns.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Sarcoma Foundation of America supports CMS efforts to modernize prior authorization but urges patient-centered protections for medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing, especially for sarcoma and other rare cancers. The comment focuses on preventing administrative delays, timing-based denials, repeat specimen collection, and patient financial liability when testing is time-sensitive and clinically necessary.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy for expedited review of time-sensitive oncology diagnostics.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** No FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, HL7 standards, or other technical standards are referenced. The comment references molecular profiling and biomarker testing as clinical services.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior Authorization Barriers for Laboratory and Biomarker Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization delays can postpone diagnostic and biomarker testing needed for diagnosis, treatment planning, disease monitoring, and clinical trial eligibility in rare cancers.
- **Specific request:** CMS should adopt patient-centered standards for laboratory testing and biomarker services.

**Topic 2: Retroactive Authorization and Timing-Based Denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Testing specimens may already have been collected in good faith before authorization is finalized; denying coverage solely due to authorization timing does not reflect clinical workflows.
- **Specific request:** Allow retroactive authorizations when medically necessary specimens have already been collected and prohibit denials based only on whether authorization preceded specimen collection.

**Topic 3: Repeat Testing and Specimen Collection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring repeat blood draws, additional specimen collection, or additional clinic visits imposes financial, logistical, and emotional burdens, especially on rare cancer patients traveling to specialty centers.
- **Specific request:** Prevent unnecessary repeat testing when a valid specimen is already available for analysis.

**Topic 4: Expedited Review for Oncology Diagnostics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker results can determine diagnosis, treatment selection, clinical trial options, and monitoring; delays may limit available treatment pathways.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited review pathways for oncology-related and other time-sensitive diagnostic testing.

**Topic 5: Patient Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not bear unexpected costs when administrative processing delays are outside the control of the patient, physician, or laboratory.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from financial liability caused by administrative delays.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to the General/RFI category, specifically the RFI issue concerning prior authorization and access to laboratory testing. It asks CMS to address timing-based denials, repeat specimen collection, expedited oncology diagnostic review, and patient liability protections for medically necessary lab and biomarker services.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected and testing is medically necessary.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on whether authorization was finalized before specimen collection.
3. Prevent repeat testing or additional specimen collection when a valid specimen is already available.
4. Create expedited review pathways for oncology-related and other time-sensitive diagnostic testing.
5. Protect patients from financial liability when administrative delays are outside the control of patients, clinicians, or laboratories.
6. Ensure prior authorization reforms account for the clinical realities of sarcoma and other rare cancers.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative barriers delay access to critical diagnostic and biomarker testing; prior authorization processes do not align with real-world clinical workflows.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed diagnosis, postponed treatment planning, missed or delayed clinical trial identification, repeat blood draws, rejected specimens, added travel, emotional burden, denied coverage, and unexpected patient costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Authorization timing requirements, lack of retroactive authorization flexibility, administrative processing delays, and policies that require repeat testing despite available valid specimens.

Re: Request for Information on Prior Authorization and Access to Laboratory Testing (CMS-2026-1255)

To Whom It May Concern:

On behalf of the Sarcoma Foundation of America (SFA), we appreciate the opportunity to provide comments regarding the impact of prior authorization requirements on access to medically necessary laboratory testing. As the leading national nonprofit organization dedicated to increasing sarcoma research, improving patient outcomes, and supporting individuals and families affected by sarcoma, SFA is deeply concerned about administrative barriers that delay access to critical diagnostic and biomarker testing.

For patients with sarcoma, and other rare cancers, timely laboratory testing is not simply a matter of convenience-it is essential to diagnosis, treatment planning, disease monitoring, and clinical trial participation. Advances in molecular profiling and biomarker testing have transformed cancer care, particularly in sarcoma, enabling physicians to make more informed decisions and connect patients with targeted therapies and clinical trials that may not otherwise be available.

Unfortunately, prior authorization requirements often fail to reflect real-world clinical workflows. Delays in approval can result in testing being postponed, specimens being rejected, or patients being asked to undergo repeat blood draws or additional specimen collection procedures despite having already provided a valid sample. In some cases, these delays occur even when the patient, physician, and laboratory have acted appropriately and in good faith.
