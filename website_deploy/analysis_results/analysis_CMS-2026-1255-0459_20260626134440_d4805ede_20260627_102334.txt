## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Iowa Primary Care Association (Iowa PCA); signed by Aaron Todd, Chief Executive Officer
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (representing Community Health Centers / safety-net providers)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Iowa PCA supports Iowa's 14 Community Health Centers (CHCs) serving over 263,000 patients annually; 91% of patients below 200% FPL; cites AMA, MGMA, HRSA UDS, and peer-reviewed pharmacy and informatics literature.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Iowa Primary Care Association broadly supports the proposed rule's interoperability and electronic prior authorization (ePA) goals, viewing them as critical to reducing administrative burden and improving timely medication access for the low-income, medically complex patients served by Iowa's CHCs. However, Iowa PCA raises significant concerns about vendor readiness, cybersecurity exposure, the dual-standard transition burden under HIPAA, conflicts between CMS-0062-P and the HTI-5 proposed rule, and the need for targeted implementation support and funding for safety-net providers. Iowa PCA urges CMS to pair ePA technology mandates with concurrent PA reform measures including gold-carding and automatic approval for expired timeframes.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Interoperability Standards — Vendor Readiness and Safety-Net Support**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** CHCs rely on a limited number of EHR vendors and lack internal IT staff to absorb rapid changes; vendor readiness requirements are absent from the proposed rule, creating compliance gaps for good-faith providers.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that providers will not be penalized for failures attributable to vendors/clearinghouses/EHRs; coordinate implementation timelines with ONC certification; establish readiness validation before enforcement; provide technical assistance, cybersecurity support, and health IT funding for CHCs.

**Topic 2: Electronic PA for Drugs — Medical vs. Pharmacy Benefit Clarity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Determining whether a drug falls under medical vs. pharmacy benefit is not always clear at point of care; managing dual workflows increases burden for CHCs.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension) in real time; clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway covers the alternate pathway.

**Topic 3: Electronic PA for Drugs — 340B and Pharmacy Interoperability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Over 65% of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy arrangements. ePA implementation spans EHR, pharmacy management systems, contract pharmacies, TPAs, and payer APIs that are often not interoperable.
- **Specific request:** CMS coordinate with OPA to provide targeted technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding to help CHCs operationalize ePA safely.

**Topic 4: Proactive PA Expiration Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify (add requirement)
- **Rationale:** Proactive outreach before PA expiration achieves positive outcomes in 87% of cases vs. 25% without such notification.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications to providers via FHIR-based alerts.

**Topic 5: ePA Without PA Reform is Insufficient**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Peer-reviewed evidence shows providers using ePA submitted higher PA volumes, spent more total time on PA activities, and experienced slightly decreased medication filling rates.
- **Specific request:** Pair ePA mandates with gold-carding extension to prescription drugs and sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 6: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with additions
- **Rationale:** Delays result in treatment interruption and worsened health outcomes; cited case of antipsychotic PA delay leading to psychiatric hospitalization.
- **Specific request:** Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 7: PA Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons enable more efficient correction, appeal, and resubmission.
- **Specific request:** Require denial notices to explain reason, identify missing information, and provide actionable resubmission guidance.

**Topic 8: API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CHCs have small IT departments; centralized endpoints reduce lookup burden and prevent EHR disruptions from outdated hard-coded endpoints.
- **Specific request:** Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files on CMS website.

**Topic 9: API Usage Metrics — Enhanced Metrics and Formulary Data**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with additions; oppose formulary removal
- **Rationale:** Current proposed metrics insufficient to assess whether APIs deliver meaningful clinical value. Removing formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs leaves CHC providers without real-time coverage data at point of care.
- **Specific request:** Add API functionality/usability metrics; retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 10: HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS — X12 to FHIR Transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** CHCs must support both X12 6020 (claims attachments, May 2028) and FHIR/CDex (PA attachments, 2029) simultaneously, creating dual-standard burden with limited IT staff.
- **Specific request:** Extend the February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover the CMS-0062-P transition period; implement CDex guardrails (no drip-feed; max 2 solicited-attachment requests per PA transaction; maximum payer response timeframes after receiving attachments).

**Topic 11: Gold-Carding for Prescription Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** PA is expensive and time-consuming; CHC pharmacists spend significant uncompensated time; gold-carding already implemented for DMEPOS and WISeR model.
- **Specific request:** CMS issue guidance formally recommending payers implement gold-carding; extend to all items/services for eligible providers.

**Topic 12: ONC/HTI-5 Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** HTI-5's proposed removal of 34 of 60 ONC certification criteria may undercut ePA infrastructure; EHR certification deadlines (Dec 2027–Jan 2028) lag behind payer compliance dates (Oct 1, 2027), creating a gap where payer APIs exist but certified EHR modules do not.
- **Specific request:** Issue joint CMS-ONC guidance ensuring HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P; publish formal harmonization assessment before finalization.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Technical challenges for CHCs implementing PA for medical benefit drugs include EHR vendor readiness gaps and lack of IT staff; requests vendor accountability and CMS technical assistance.
- **S15:** Benefit classification is often unclear at point of care for clinic-administered drugs (e.g., extended-release buprenorphine); requests real-time classification via CRD Coverage Information extension.
- **S19:** Raises state and CHC readiness concerns for pharmacy-benefit drug ePA, particularly around 340B workflows and PMS/EHR interoperability gaps.
- **S20:** Supports the 24-hour expedited drug PA timeframe; urges automatic approval for requests not decided within required timeframes.
- **S16/S17:** Supports specific denial reason requirements; recommends denial notices include actionable resubmission guidance.
- **S23:** Raises cybersecurity concerns associated with expanded API-based exchange for CHCs with limited IT resources.
- **S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports API endpoint reporting; recommends machine-readable publication format; approves proposed timelines.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports usage metrics expansion; recommends additional API functionality/usability metrics; opposes removal of formulary data from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
- **S44/S45/S46/S47:** Supports FHIR as HIPAA PA standard but urges transition support; requests enforcement discretion extension during dual-standard transition period.
- **S50:** Concerned about CDex creating iterative data requests; recommends guardrails (no drip-feed; 2-request maximum; response timeframes).
- **S60/S61/S64/S65:** Concerned about HTI-5 conflicts with CMS-0062-P; urges joint CMS-ONC harmonization guidance; notes temporal gap between payer and EHR certification compliance dates.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify that providers will not be penalized for compliance failures attributable to vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems outside their control.
2. Coordinate CMS-0062-P implementation timelines with ONC certification requirements; establish readiness validation before enforcement begins.
3. Establish uniform minimum implementation requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.
4. Provide CHCs with targeted technical assistance, cybersecurity/IT workforce development support, and dedicated health IT funding.
5. Require payers to expose benefit classification through the Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension) in real time.
6. Clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to the alternate pathway.
7. Coordinate with OPA to provide technical assistance and funding for 340B/pharmacy interoperability challenges.
8. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications to providers via FHIR-based alerts.
9. Pair ePA mandates with PA reform: extend gold-carding to prescription drugs; sunset PA categories with consistently high approval rates.
10. Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
11. Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files on CMS website.
12. Add API functionality/usability metrics (success rates, authorization failures, structured vs. unstructured data ratios, cost of API maintenance).
13. Retain formulary information in the Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
14. Expand public reporting metrics: denial rates by service type/medication category, first-pass approval rates, denials overturned due to unclear notices, data quality metrics.
15. Strengthen enforcement for plans with persistent delays, inaccurate reporting, or inappropriate denial patterns.
16. Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover the CMS-0062-P HIPAA transition period.
17. Implement CDex guardrails: no drip-feed requests, max 2 solicited-attachment requests per PA transaction, maximum payer response timeframes.
18. Issue formal CMS guidance recommending gold-carding by payers, extended to all items and services for eligible providers.
19. Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance ensuring HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P; publish harmonization assessment before final rule.
20. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds to support interoperability implementation.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vendor readiness gaps leaving CHCs exposed; cybersecurity burden on resource-constrained CHCs; dual X12/FHIR standard burden during transition; HTI-5 conflicts creating implementation timing gaps; ePA without PA reform may accelerate rather than reduce administrative burden; removal of formulary data from APIs reduces real-time prescribing support.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** CHCs may face compliance gaps due to vendor failures; CHCs may absorb unfunded implementation costs; 340B workflows disrupted by pharmacy system interoperability gaps; patients may experience PA delays when EHR systems lack certified modules while payer APIs are already required.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited IT staff at CHCs; financial constraints (nearly half with <90 days cash on hand; average operating margin negative); 340B multi-system complexity; EHR/PMS interoperability gaps; up to 3-month window where payer APIs operational but certified EHR modules unavailable.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 94% of physicians report PA delays patient care (AMA); average wait time ~3 days, one-third wait >1 week; MA organizations made ~53 million PA determinations in 2024 with 4+ million denials (KFF); 91% of Iowa CHC patients below 200% FPL; 87% positive outcome rate for proactive PA expiration outreach vs. 25% without (Vishwanath et al., 2021).
- **Studies or research referenced:** Lauffenburger JC et al. (2021), JAMIA; Salzbrenner SG et al. (2022), J Manag Care Spec Pharm; Vishwanath S et al. (2021), Am J Manag Care; Aremu TO et al. (2022), Pharmacy (Basel); Krauss ZJ et al. (2022), Pharmacy (Basel); Murthi S et al. (2024), J Med Syst.
- **Examples provided:** Rural Iowa CHC patient unable to receive routine long-acting antipsychotic due to PA delay, leading to psychiatric hospitalization; extended-release buprenorphine billing under pharmacy vs. medical benefit depending on care setting.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Iowa's 14 CHCs; safety-net providers nationally; low-income and medically complex patients (263,000+ in Iowa); EHR vendors serving CHCs; 340B pharmacies and contract pharmacy partners; state Medicaid programs.
- **Economic implications:** CHCs have thin margins (average operating margin negative; ~half with <90 days cash on hand); unfunded API integration, cybersecurity, and training costs pose operational risk; gold-carding could yield significant savings in pharmacist time.
- **Technical feasibility:** Significant interoperability gaps in 340B environments; EHR certification lag creates 3-month implementation gap; CHCs rely on vendors who may deprioritize safety-net capabilities.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster medication access for patients with chronic conditions, behavioral health, and substance use disorders; risk of disruption during transition if safeguards inadequate; formulary data gaps may lead to suboptimal prescribing.
