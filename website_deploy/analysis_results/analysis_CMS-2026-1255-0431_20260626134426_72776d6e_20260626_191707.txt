## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter strongly supports the proposed rule but argues that its success depends on faithful translation of human-written drug PA policies into computable artifacts. He recommends CMS recognize policy-to-artifact translation as a distinct compliance activity, require semantic fidelity and versioning, and add criteria transparency to reporting and denial-reason guidance.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed by CFR citation; references sections II.A.2, II.A.3, II.A.4, II.B.3, II.C.2.c, and II.C.7 of the proposed rule.
- **Standards referenced:** Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS IGs; FHIR Questionnaire; Clinical Quality Language (CQL); NCPDP Formulary & Benefit (F&B); NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB); ASTP/ONC conformance tooling.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Policy-to-artifact translation for medical benefit drug PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that the main compliance risk is not API engineering but knowledge engineering: converting payer policy prose into FHIR Questionnaire and CQL artifacts that faithfully reproduce written criteria.
- **Specific request:** CMS should acknowledge policy-to-artifact translation as a distinct compliance activity, require semantic faithfulness to published policy, and encourage validated, auditable methods with human verification.

**Topic 2: Semantic conformance for required FHIR IGs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Structural conformance testing can verify required artifacts while missing incorrect, contradictory, unsatisfiable, or undisclosed interpretations of payer criteria.
- **Specific request:** CMS, ASTP/ONC, and tooling developers should validate computable PA artifacts for semantic equivalence with published criteria and internal logical consistency, initially through guidance, pilots, or voluntary certification.

**Topic 3: Versioning and synchronization of PA criteria and indicators**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Human-readable criteria, machine-readable indicators, and adjudication logic may not be synchronized or version-stamped, leaving providers and patients unable to determine which policy governs a request.
- **Specific request:** Require effective dates and version identifiers for PA criteria and PA-requirement indicators and establish a maximum permissible lag after policy changes.

**Topic 4: Criteria transparency and denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Public PA metrics and denial reasons are more useful when the underlying criteria are public, machine-readable, versioned, and internally consistent.
- **Specific request:** Add a public criteria-transparency metric and clarify that specific denial reasons depend on clear, noncontradictory criteria.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Acknowledge policy-to-artifact translation as a distinct compliance activity.
2. Clarify that computable PA criteria must be semantically faithful to published written policies.
3. Encourage validated, auditable extraction and maintenance methods with human verification.
4. Establish expectations for semantic equivalence and logical consistency testing of PA artifacts.
5. Require effective dates and version identifiers for PA criteria and indicators across NCPDP and FHIR channels.
6. Establish a maximum lag between policy changes and machine-readable updates.
7. Add a public criteria-transparency metric covering retrievability, machine readability, and version dating.
8. Explain in denial-reason guidance that specific reasons depend on clear and consistent underlying criteria.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Computable artifacts may diverge from payer policies; structural conformance testing may miss semantic errors; policy updates may not synchronize with machine-readable indicators.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Inconsistent adjudication outcomes, digitized ambiguity, unreliable denial reasons, inability to identify governing policy versions, and wasted implementation investment.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Ambiguous and contradictory policy prose, discretionary criteria, manual policy-by-policy translation, repeated updates after policy revision, and limited semantic validation in current conformance frameworks.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics; the commenter cites observations from developing and testing an automated extraction system across publicly available payer PA policy documents.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Ongoing independent research into extractability, internal consistency, and cross-payer variation; not yet peer-reviewed.
- **Examples provided:** Ambiguous step-therapy logic using "and"/"or" constructs; differing reauthorization intervals between tables and policy body; undefined "adequate trial" durations.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, providers, EHR vendors, PA technology vendors, patients, ASTP/ONC, and CMS oversight teams.
- **Economic implications:** Criteria transparency is described as low-cost and auditable; broader semantic validation could reduce wasted spending on inaccurate digitization.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter believes automated semantic validation methods exist and are improving, but should be paired with human verification.
- **Patient care impact:** Accurate computable criteria can reduce inconsistent approvals and denials and improve reliability of drug access decisions.
