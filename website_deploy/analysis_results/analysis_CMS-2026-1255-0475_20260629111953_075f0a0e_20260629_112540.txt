## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Community Oncology Alliance (COA); signed by Debra Patt, MD, PhD, MBA, FASCO, President, and Ted Okon, MBA, Executive Director
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National nonprofit dedicated to independent community oncology practices and patients with cancer; signatory includes physician oncology leadership.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

COA supports CMS-0062-P as an important step toward improving drug prior authorization, especially for physician-administered oncology drugs, while maintaining that prior authorization is fundamentally harmful in cancer care. COA asks CMS to improve oncology-specific interoperability, strengthen public reporting and metrics, require transparency on AI use in PA decisions, and limit fail-first step therapy and non-medical switching in cancer care.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** The comment references CMS-0062, CMS-0062-P, Section II.A, Section II.B, Section II.C, and Section III.D, but does not cite specific CFR sections.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; APIs; FDA approval; National Comprehensive Cancer Network category recommendations; AHIP prior authorization commitments; AI in PA decisions.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Oncology-Specific Interoperability Data Elements**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology data such as staging, biomarker data, treatment intent, and outcomes are often incomplete, poorly normalized, inconsistently structured, or fragmented across settings and vendors.
- **Specific request:** Support standards and implementation approaches that reliably exchange key oncology care data elements for prior authorization and broader treatment planning.

**Topic 2: Electronic Prior Authorization for Medical-Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Physician-administered oncology drugs are increasingly subject to PA, creating a significant gap if medical-benefit drugs are not included in electronic workflows.
- **Specific request:** Finalize expansion of electronic prior authorization for drugs, including medical-benefit oncology drugs.

**Topic 3: Drug Denial Reasons and Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specific reasons for denials and improved communications/timeframes can help patient access, reduce provider burden, and align requirements across payer types.
- **Specific request:** Require Medicaid, CHIP, and FFE QHPs to provide specific denial reasons for all drugs and improve drug and non-drug PA decision timeframes.

**Topic 4: Public Prior Authorization Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Total counts and percentages can mask PA trends, and Part B-only reporting leaves gaps for oncology drugs and services.
- **Specific request:** Require MA plans to report Part B and Part D drug PA metrics in the same format; stratify metrics at item or service level; include counts as well as percentages; add metrics on reduced PA scope, staff available to assist members, and whether AI was used.

**Topic 5: Step Therapy and Non-Medical Switching in Cancer Care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy and non-medical switching can interfere with physician-patient decision-making and timely cancer treatment.
- **Specific request:** Ban fail-first step therapy and non-medical switching for FDA-approved, NCCN Category-recommended cancer drugs, and require clear exception processes, potentially including gold carding.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports API/interoperability standards generally, but asks CMS to ensure oncology-specific data elements are reliably structured and exchanged.
- **S12:** Supports expanding electronic PA for drugs under the medical benefit and identifies oncology medical-benefit drugs as a key use case.
- **S16:** Supports requiring specific denial reasons for all drugs across Medicaid, CHIP, and FFE QHPs.
- **S17:** Does not specifically discuss structured denial codes, but supports clearer denial communication.
- **S20:** Supports improved decision timeframes for drug and non-drug prior authorizations because timely treatment initiation is critical in cancer care.
- **S28:** Recommends MA plans publicly report PA metrics for both Part B and Part D drugs in the same format to align transparency across oncology drugs.
- **S29:** Recommends item- or service-level PA metrics, numeric counts as well as percentages, AI-use reporting, and additional metrics aligned with AHIP commitments.
- **S35:** Supports public reporting of more granular PA metrics to help patients make informed decisions and improve CMS awareness of patient and provider experience.
- **S43:** Supports alignment of decision timeframe requirements across payer types, including Medicaid, CHIP, QHPs, MA, and Part D.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to a general RFI on step therapy by recommending limits on fail-first protocols, a ban in specified cancer-drug circumstances, and clear exceptions including possible gold carding.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Promote interoperability standards that exchange oncology staging, biomarker, treatment intent, and outcomes data reliably.
2. Finalize electronic prior authorization for drugs, including medical-benefit oncology drugs.
3. Require specific denial reasons for all drugs across Medicaid, CHIP, and FFE QHPs.
4. Improve and align PA decision timeframes across payer types.
5. Require MA plans to publicly report PA metrics for both Part B and Part D drugs in the same format.
6. Stratify PA metrics at the item or service level and report numeric counts in addition to percentages.
7. Add public metrics on reductions in claims subject to PA and staff dedicated to helping members after PA determinations.
8. Require public reporting of whether AI was used in PA decisions.
9. Ban fail-first step therapy and non-medical switching for FDA-approved, NCCN Category-recommended cancer drugs.
10. Establish clear exception processes for step therapy and non-medical switching, including possible gold carding.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Prior authorization burden, patient harm from treatment delays, inadequate oncology data exchange, insufficient transparency in PA metrics, AI-driven denials, and step therapy in cancer care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed lifesaving cancer treatment, adverse clinical outcomes, increased administrative burden on community oncology practices, masked PA trends, and interference with physician-recommended treatment.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Poor data quality, fragmented systems, inconsistent FHIR implementation, access restrictions, and limited practice resources.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA survey findings that 84% of physicians reported increased prescription-drug PA over five years; 82% reported the same for medical services; 94% reported PA negatively affects outcomes; more than one in four reported PA led to a serious adverse event; a 2026 COA survey found 96.6% of independent community oncology practice respondents observed cancer patients struggling due to insurer step therapy.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA prior authorization surveys, ACS CAN cost-of-cancer report, Journal of Clinical Pathways article on oncology medical-benefit payer management, AHIP statements, NAIC AI/ML survey, COA survey and gold carding issue brief.
- **Examples provided:** Oncology staging, biomarkers, treatment intent, outcomes, physician-administered oncology drugs, AI use in PA decisions, fail-first step therapy, non-medical switching, and gold carding.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with cancer, community oncology practices, oncologists, MA plans, Medicaid and CHIP plans, QHP issuers, payers using AI, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** PA imposes administrative costs on practices and may drive higher downstream costs through treatment delays and adverse events; clearer standards and reporting may improve accountability.
- **Technical feasibility:** COA supports FHIR and API-based interoperability but stresses that real-world implementation remains inconsistent and oncology-specific data elements need better structure.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment focuses on timely cancer treatment, avoiding adverse outcomes, and preserving physician-patient treatment decisions.
