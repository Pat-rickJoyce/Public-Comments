## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

LADA, a national patient advocacy organization, responds to CMS's RFI on step therapy, urging comprehensive reform of step therapy ("fail-first") protocols. The organization argues that insurer-mandated step therapy overrides physician clinical judgment, harms patients with complex autoimmune and other serious diseases, and demands that CMS require a standardized, technology-enabled exceptions process with transparency and continuity-of-coverage protections.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Step Therapy Exceptions Process**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Current step therapy lacks a standardized, medically reasonable exceptions process. Patients with complex conditions like lupus require individualized treatment decisions, not cost-driven formulary sequences.
- **Specific request:** Require all MA orgs, Medicaid/CHIP FFS and managed care, and QHP issuers to offer an exceptions process with 24-hour (emergency) to 72-hour (standard) response times and five defined exception categories: prior failure/expected ineffectiveness, delayed treatment causing irreversible harm, drug causing patient harm, drug preventing work/daily activities, or patient stable on current medication.

**Topic 2: Prohibition on Repeating Step Therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring patients to restart step therapy upon plan change (new year, formulary change, new payer) causes significant harm, increased utilization, and higher costs.
- **Specific request:** Additional rulemaking to ban payers from forcing patients to repeat steps already completed, with portability standards covering fewer prior steps, different timelines, and different drugs within the same therapeutic class.

**Topic 3: Standardization Across Payers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** No standardized approach exists; each payer uses proprietary forms and varying documentation requirements, creating burden for providers and patients.
- **Specific request:** Standard form and instructions across all payers; same documentation requirements; same criteria for exception approval; required inclusion of clinical rationale in denials including alternative coverage and appeal rights.

**Topic 4: Technology Platform for Step Therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Technology is critical to creating a streamlined, standardized exceptions process accessible to both patients and providers.
- **Specific request:** CMS-created technology platform with standardized forms, documentation, and criteria; plain-language patient-specific UM information; annual public reporting on step therapy exception requests, approvals, denials, and appeals.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82 (General/RFIs):** Specifically responds to step therapy RFI. Commenter argues step therapy reforms are urgently needed and provides detailed policy recommendations including exceptions processes, portability, standardization, and technology solutions.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require all MA orgs, Medicaid/CHIP, and QHP issuers to offer a standardized step therapy exceptions process with 24-72 hour response times.
2. Require exceptions in five defined circumstances (prior failure, irreversible harm, drug harm, functional impairment, stability on current medication).
3. Base step therapy protocols on independent clinical practice guidelines or peer-reviewed data, not cost alone.
4. Ban payers from requiring patients to repeat step therapy steps already completed upon plan transition.
5. Establish continuity standards for new payers recognizing prior payer step therapy history across different timelines, steps, and same-class drugs.
6. Standardize the exceptions process: uniform forms, documentation requirements, and approval criteria across all payers.
7. Require denial notices to include clinical rationale, covered alternatives, and appeal rights.
8. Create a CMS technology platform for standardized step therapy information and exceptions requests.
9. Require payers to provide plain-language, patient-specific UM, cost-sharing, and step therapy protocol information.
10. Require annual public reporting on step therapy exception volumes, results, and appeal outcomes.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Step therapy overrides physician clinical judgment; protocols are cost-driven, not clinically individualized; patients forced to suffer on ineffective treatments before accessing appropriate care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disease progression; increased healthcare utilization and costs; physical, emotional, and financial distress; disruption of continuity of care; denial of physician's right to make individualized treatment decisions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of standardization across payers; burdensome and non-uniform documentation requirements; use of outdated processes (e.g., phone calls).
