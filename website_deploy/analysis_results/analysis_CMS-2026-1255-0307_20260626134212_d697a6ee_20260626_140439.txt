## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Washington State Health Care Authority supports the rule's goals of electronic PA modernization but raises urgent concerns about the October 1, 2027 compliance deadline—requesting extension to January 1, 2030—citing state budget cycles, separate pharmacy POS systems not yet updated, and competing IT priorities. The agency also requests specificity on denial reason requirements, clarification on the 24-hour timeframe (should apply after all information is received, not from initial request), and guidance on FHIR/NCPDP coordination and statewide HIE orchestration models. HCA emphasizes the risk of "compliance on paper, failure in practice" if only technical endpoint availability is measured rather than real operational adoption.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation Timeline**
- **Provision ID:** STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS
- **Position:** Oppose (current date); Modify
- **Rationale:** Three reasons make October 1, 2027 infeasible: drugs excluded from 2024 rule require new physician-administered drug integration; separate pharmacy POS systems have not yet begun updates and face competing deadlines (NCPDP F6 by August 2027); state funds not available until FY beginning July 1, 2027.
- **Specific request:** Extend compliance deadline to no earlier than January 1, 2030 (consistent with three-year period allowed for 2024 rule).

**Topic 2: PA Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** HCA already provides denial reasons but the proposed rule lacks specificity about what is required—unclear whether denial reasons must point to each unmet criterion or whether a standard code set (like NCPDP rejection codes) will be used.
- **Specific request:** CMS must specify level of detail required and whether a standardized code set will be used before states can scope implementation costs.

**Topic 3: 24-Hour PA Decision Timeframe**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Section 1927(d)(5)(A) SSA 24-hour response has been interpreted as not requiring a final determination. Requiring final decisions within 24 hours of initial request—without time for additional information—will generate unnecessary denials, harm Medicaid enrollee access, require weekend/holiday staffing, increase hearings, and raise program costs.
- **Specific request:** Final rule should require final determination within 24 hours of receiving all information necessary to decide.

**Topic 4: Medical vs. Pharmacy Benefit Drug Metrics Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Unclear whether medical-benefit drugs should be reported with non-drug items (2024 rule) or with pharmacy-benefit drugs, or separately.
- **Specific request:** Clarify reporting structure; standardize metric calculations across state Medicaid programs.

**Topic 5: FHIR/NCPDP Standards Applicability**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Some drugs billed under both benefits depending on place of service (specialty pharmacy at home vs. provider office). Proposed rule states FHIR applies to dental/professional/institutional but not retail pharmacy, creating ambiguity.
- **Specific request:** Final rule must clarify whether standards apply based on the actual drug or the claim type.

**Topic 6: Provider Adoption Gap**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Building payer-side APIs creates capacity but does not ensure provider adoption; prior initiatives have left capacity unused.
- **Specific request:** Support adoption entities analogous to HITECH-era Regional Extension Centers.

**Topic 7: Operational Adoption vs. Technical Compliance**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Risk of "compliance on paper, failure in practice"—states may achieve technical endpoint compliance while providers continue using portals and manual workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require operational metrics (transaction volume, success rates, error rates, endpoint uptime, response latency, fallback rates) rather than attestation of endpoint availability.

**Topic 8: FHIR/NCPDP Coordination and HIE Orchestration**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Dual FHIR/NCPDP framework risks fragmented workflows; statewide HIE entities could serve as orchestration intermediaries but federal guidance and APD funding clarity is needed.
- **Specific request:** Provide FHIR/NCPDP coordination guidance; explicitly support optional statewide/HIE orchestration models; clarify APD funding.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend compliance deadline to no earlier than January 1, 2030.
2. Specify denial reason requirements (criterion-specific or standard code set) before states must scope implementation.
3. Require final PA determination within 24 hours of receiving all information necessary—not from initial request.
4. Clarify whether medical-benefit drug metrics combine with non-drug items or pharmacy drugs, or are reported separately.
5. Standardize metric calculations across all state Medicaid programs for comparability.
6. Clarify whether FHIR/NCPDP standards apply based on the drug or claim type/place of service.
7. Support HITECH-era Regional Extension Center-type entities to drive provider adoption.
8. Require operational metrics (transaction volume, success rates, error rates, uptime, latency, fallback rates).
9. Provide clearer guidance on FHIR/NCPDP coordination to minimize workflow fragmentation.
10. Explicitly support optional statewide/HIE orchestration models; clarify APD and federal funding.
11. Provide phased implementation guidance and statewide governance model support.
12. Encourage standardization of IG versions, endpoint structures, and workflows across MCOs within a state.
13. Encourage consent management approaches that minimize provider workflow disruption.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** October 2027 deadline infeasible given budget cycles and POS readiness; 24-hour timeframe from submission will cause unnecessary denials; lack of denial reason specificity prevents scoping; risk of technical compliance without operational adoption.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Unnecessary PA denials under rigid 24-hour rule; increased Medicaid hearings; weekend/holiday staffing requirements; program cost increases; provider adoption stagnation; fragmented workflows.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** State funds not available until July 1, 2027; pharmacy POS systems with competing deadlines (NCPDP F6 by August 2027); physician-administered drug integration is new work; MCO variation in IG versions/endpoints; absent HIE orchestration guidance.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** NCPDP F6 transition deadline: August 14, 2027; twelve-digit NDC implementation: March 7, 2033; state funds available for CMS-0062-P no earlier than July 1, 2027.
- **Studies or research referenced:** CMS-0057-F (2024 rule); Section 1927(d)(5)(A) Social Security Act.
- **Examples provided:** Washington HCA's current PA practice (receipt confirmation within 1 business day; additional info request by end of next business day; final determination within 1 business day of receiving additional info); specialty pharmacy drugs billed under pharmacy benefit when administered at home but medical benefit when in provider office.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** State Medicaid FFS programs; Medicaid MCOs/CHIP; Medicaid enrollees; pharmacy POS system vendors; statewide HIE entities.
- **Economic implications:** October 2027 deadline infeasible given state budget cycles; implementation costs unquantifiable until denial reason specifications clarified; rigid 24-hour rule will increase administrative hearing and staffing costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Significant concerns—POS systems not yet begun updates; physician-administered drug integration is new work; MCO variation creates state-level complexity requiring governance frameworks.
- **Patient care impact:** Rigid 24-hour timeframe risks causing unnecessary denials and reduced Medicaid enrollee access; properly implemented, the rule would improve PA speed and transparency.
