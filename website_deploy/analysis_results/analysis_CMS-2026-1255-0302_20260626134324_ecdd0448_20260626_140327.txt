# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Ohio Association of Community Health Centers (OACHC); signed by Julie DiRossi-King, CEO & President.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; Professional Association; Safety-net provider association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** OACHC supports 61 FQHCs and FQHC Look-Alikes serving more than one million Ohioans through 600+ sites in 76 of Ohio's 88 counties. It describes CHCs as nonprofit providers serving medically underserved populations with integrated care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

OACHC strongly supports CMS's interoperability and electronic prior authorization goals, including drug ePA, API endpoint reporting, API-based PA information, clearer denial reasons, and aligned timelines. It urges CMS to pair these requirements with safety-net implementation support, vendor accountability, cybersecurity and IT funding, medical/pharmacy benefit transparency, formulary data retention, meaningful API usability metrics, PA reduction reforms such as gold-carding, CDex guardrails, and CMS-ONC standards harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); references to CMS-0062-P, CMS-0053-F, HTI-4, HTI-5, 90 FR 37130, and National Standards Group enforcement discretion dated February 28, 2024.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; CRD/DTR/PAS HL7 Da Vinci IGs v2.2; CRD Coverage Information extension; Provider Access API; Patient Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization API; CDex; C-CDA; PDF/text/FHIR attachment formats; X12 278; X12 6020; ONC certification criteria; machine-readable API endpoint files.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Interoperability and ePA for Safety-Net Providers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs could reduce duplicative documentation, improve PA visibility, and streamline care coordination for medically underserved patients, but CHCs have limited staffing, IT capacity, and financial flexibility.
- **Specific request:** Pair final requirements with realistic timelines, vendor readiness requirements, readiness validation/testing, implementation guidance, cybersecurity support, IT workforce support, and dedicated funding.

**Topic 2: Vendor Accountability and Provider Protection**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify.
- **Rationale:** CHCs rely on EHR vendors, clearinghouses, and third-party technology partners. Provider-facing technology may not be certified or ready when payer APIs become mandatory.
- **Specific request:** Clarify providers will not be penalized for failures caused by vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems outside provider control; coordinate CMS implementation with ONC certification timelines.

**Topic 3: Cybersecurity and Resource Support**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Expanded API exchange increases cybersecurity exposure for CHCs that lack large health-system IT resources.
- **Specific request:** Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity and IT workforce support, and dedicated funding so CHCs do not absorb unfunded costs for API integration, vendor upgrades, and ongoing maintenance.

**Topic 4: Prescription Drug ePA and Benefit Classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** ePA for every drug requiring PA can improve medication access, but providers need real-time clarity on whether a drug is under the medical or pharmacy benefit and whether approvals transfer across pathways.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension and clarify whether one pathway's PA approval applies to alternate benefit pathways.

**Topic 5: 340B and Pharmacy Workflow Implementation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** CHC pharmacy workflows involve entity-owned pharmacies, contract pharmacies, TPAs, EHRs, pharmacy management systems, payer APIs, claims, 340B accumulation, eligibility, and documentation data that may not exchange in real time.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs and provide targeted assistance, guidance, and funding for CHCs operationalizing prescription drug ePA.

**Topic 6: PA Expiration Alerts and PA Reduction**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Proactive expiration notices can prevent treatment interruptions, but ePA alone may increase PA volume or accelerate inefficient workflows.
- **Specific request:** Mandate FHIR-based PA expiration alerts, extend gold-carding to prescription drugs, and create sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 7: Timelines, Denials, and Automatic Approval**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Medically underserved patients can be harmed by delayed medication access, unclear denials, and payer missed deadlines.
- **Specific request:** Maintain 24-hour covered outpatient drug timelines, 7-day standard and 72-hour expedited item/service PA timelines, QHP 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug timelines, require actionable denial reasons, and establish automatic approval if required timeframes are missed.

**Topic 8: Public Reporting and API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Centralized CMS reporting improves accountability, reduces provider burden, and supports oversight better than fragmented plan websites.
- **Specific request:** Publish PA metrics and API endpoints centrally on CMS's website in standardized, machine-readable formats, update endpoints within one week of changes, verify annually, and include additional metrics on first-pass approvals, service/medication categories, incomplete/inaccurate notices, data quality, API success/failure, malformed queries, authorization failures, workflow impact, and API maintenance costs.

**Topic 9: Retaining Formulary Information**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Oppose removal.
- **Rationale:** Payer websites are not reliable real-time sources, PBM exclusions change rapidly, and CHCs lack administrative capacity for manual formulary lookups across payers.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 10: X12 to FHIR Transition and CDex Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify.
- **Rationale:** CHCs and clearinghouses may need to support X12 6020 for claims attachments and FHIR/CDex for PA attachments simultaneously, creating a dual-standard burden. CDex can also enable iterative payer documentation demands.
- **Specific request:** Provide transition support, extend enforcement discretion allowing FHIR PA API as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition, require all documentation in the initial CDex request, and limit solicited attachment requests to two per PA transaction before a decision.

**Topic 11: ONC-CMS Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify.
- **Rationale:** HTI-5 certification changes and HTI-4 ePA certification timelines may create a gap where payer APIs are live before certified CHC EHR modules are available.
- **Specific request:** Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance, ensure HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P, and publish a formal CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S2/S3:** OACHC supports required CRD/DTR/PAS-based ePA but asks for synchronized IG timelines, readiness validation, and alignment with ONC certification.
- **S12:** The comment identifies prescription drug ePA technical challenges, including vendor readiness, EHR/PMS integration, pharmacy workflows, 340B data, cybersecurity, and CHC resource constraints.
- **S15:** OACHC asks CMS to clarify medical versus pharmacy benefit classification and whether approvals under one benefit pathway apply to the other.
- **S16/S17:** OACHC supports specific denial reasons and asks that denials identify missing or insufficient information and provide actionable resubmission or appeal guidance.
- **S20/S21/S43:** OACHC supports proposed drug PA timelines, including 24-hour covered outpatient drug decisions and QHP 72-hour standard/24-hour expedited drug decisions, and seeks automatic approval if deadlines are missed.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** OACHC supports payer API endpoint reporting to CMS, publication on CMS's website, machine-readable files, updates within one week of changes, and annual verification.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** OACHC supports expanded API and PA metrics and recommends additional functionality, usability, error, data quality, workflow, and cost metrics.
- **S37:** The comment gives extensive implementation concerns for CHCs, including limited IT capacity, vendor dependence, cybersecurity, payer inconsistency, and certification timing gaps.
- **S44/S46/S47/S50/S54:** OACHC supports movement from X12 toward FHIR but requests dual-standard transition support, enforcement discretion, and testing/certification alignment.
- **S60/S61/S62/S63/S64/S65:** OACHC urges CMS-ONC harmonization, joint guidance, and assessment of how HTI-5 and HTI-4 certification timelines affect CMS-0062-P readiness.
- **S66-S82 (General RFIs):** OACHC recommends FHIR-based PA expiration alerts, gold-carding for prescription drugs, PA category sunsetting, and safeguards against iterative CDex attachment requests.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA and interoperability requirements with realistic timelines and implementation safeguards for CHCs.
2. Establish vendor readiness and accountability expectations, and protect providers from penalties for vendor, clearinghouse, or EHR failures outside provider control.
3. Coordinate CMS timelines with ONC certification and require readiness validation before enforcement.
4. Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity support, IT workforce development, and dedicated funding for safety-net providers.
5. Require benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using CRD Coverage Information.
6. Clarify whether PA approvals transfer between medical and pharmacy benefit pathways.
7. Coordinate with OPA on 340B-related ePA implementation and support CHC pharmacy workflows.
8. Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration alerts.
9. Pair ePA with PA reduction policies such as prescription drug gold-carding and sunset rules for high-approval PA categories.
10. Maintain proposed PA timelines, require actionable denial reasons, and adopt automatic approval for missed deadlines.
11. Publish PA metrics and API endpoint data centrally in standardized, machine-readable CMS formats.
12. Add metrics for first-pass approvals, denial categories, data quality, API failures, usability, workflow impact, and API cost.
13. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
14. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds and transition support.
15. Extend enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute during transition.
16. Add CDex guardrails requiring initial complete documentation requests and limiting solicited attachments to two per PA transaction.
17. Issue joint CMS-ONC guidance and publish a harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Safety-net implementation burden, vendor readiness, cybersecurity exposure, fragmented payer workflows, medical/pharmacy benefit ambiguity, 340B workflow complexity, missed deadlines, insufficient metrics, loss of formulary data, dual X12/FHIR standards, CDex over-requesting, and ONC-CMS timing gaps.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment interruptions, delayed medications, preventable complications, administrative strain, inaccurate claims processing, duplicated work, cybersecurity cost burdens, EHR disruptions, and safety-net resource diversion away from patient care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC IT staff, small cybersecurity teams, constrained budgets, vendor dependencies, rural infrastructure, EHR/PMS interoperability gaps, terminology and value-set misalignment, contract pharmacy/TPA complexity, payer rollout variation, and certification deadlines that lag payer compliance.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** OACHC supports 61 FQHCs/FQHC Look-Alikes serving over one million Ohioans at 600+ sites in 76 counties. CHCs serve more than 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, 6 million uninsured people, 20.5 million low-income people, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and 1.5 million homeless individuals. More than 90% of CHC patients have incomes below 200% FPL. AMA survey: 94% of physicians reported PA delays care. KFF: MA organizations made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024 with more than four million full or partial denials. OACHC cites CHC chronic disease volumes, 340B usage by more than 65% of CHCs, and nearly half of CHCs operating with fewer than 90 days cash on hand.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA prior authorization survey; KFF MA PA determinations; MGMA burden reporting; NACHC/CHC population data; pharmacy interoperability literature; studies on pharmacy access to EHR data; evidence on proactive outreach before PA expiration; peer-reviewed evidence that ePA without reform may increase PA volumes and time spent; gold-carding/DMEPOS and WISeR examples.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine may be billed under pharmacy benefit when dispensed by a pharmacy or medical benefit when administered in clinic; CHCs may struggle when PA determinations, dispensing, eligibility, documentation, and 340B data sit across separate systems.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs/FQHCs, medically underserved patients, Medicaid beneficiaries, uninsured patients, rural patients, agricultural workers, homeless individuals, CHC IT and clinical staff, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, pharmacies, contract pharmacies, TPAs, Medicaid MCOs, QHP issuers, CMS, ONC, and OPA.
- **Economic implications:** Without support, CHCs face unreimbursed PA labor, vendor upgrade costs, cybersecurity costs, workflow redesign costs, and financial strain; successful reform could reduce administrative costs and prevent avoidable hospitalizations and complications.
- **Technical feasibility:** OACHC supports FHIR-based APIs and ePA but views success as dependent on vendor readiness, certified provider technology, harmonized standards, data quality, machine-readable endpoint publication, and implementation testing.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong implementation could speed medication access, improve adherence, preserve continuity, reduce treatment interruptions, and support care coordination for underserved patients.
