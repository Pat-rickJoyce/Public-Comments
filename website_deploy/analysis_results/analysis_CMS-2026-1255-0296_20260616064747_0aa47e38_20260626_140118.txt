## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Georgia Life Sciences comments on CMS's RFI regarding prior authorization and access to laboratory testing. The organization supports modernization and transparency but urges CMS to prevent prior authorization rules from delaying medically necessary laboratory testing, especially biomarker testing and other advanced diagnostics central to precision medicine.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to expedited review principles; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy to timing-based denials; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED by general reference to electronic prior authorization and interoperability tools.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations are cited. The comment references Section III.E of CMS-0062-P, the RFI for Prior Authorization and Access to Laboratory Testing.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization and interoperability tools are referenced generally; no specific FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, HL7 standards, or CFR standards are named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Timing-Based Denials for Laboratory Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that denying coverage because PA was not finalized before or on the specimen collection date penalizes patients, providers, and laboratories for timing issues outside their control.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit timing-based denials for medically necessary laboratory testing when the patient, provider, and laboratory acted in good faith.

**Topic 2: Retroactive Authorization and Valid Specimens**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A valid specimen may already be available and suitable for testing, so requiring repeat collection creates avoidable burden and delay.
- **Specific request:** Allow retroactive authorization after specimen collection when the test is medically necessary and supported by clinical documentation, and prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws or repeat specimen collection.

**Topic 3: Expedited Review for Time-Sensitive Diagnostics**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker testing, rare disease diagnostics, recurrence monitoring, medication management, and clinical trial eligibility testing can be central to urgent treatment decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review pathways for time-sensitive diagnostics, particularly oncology-related biomarker testing and rare disease diagnostics.

**Topic 4: Documentation Simplification and Workflow Alignment**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Inconsistent payer requirements, duplicative documentation, resubmissions, appeals, and coordination of repeat testing divert provider and laboratory staff from patient care.
- **Specific request:** Standardize and simplify documentation requirements, including acceptance of test requisition forms and ordering provider records to support medical necessity.

**Topic 5: Automation and Laboratory Benefit Managers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports electronic PA and interoperability but cautions that automation should not simply digitize existing barriers. The role of laboratory benefit managers may either improve coordination or create additional administrative barriers.
- **Specific request:** Design electronic PA tools to reduce burden, support real-time decision-making where possible, account for laboratory workflows, and monitor the impact of laboratory benefit managers.

**Topic 6: Patient Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not bear financial liability when administrative timing or payer processing delays are beyond their control.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from surprise bills or out-of-pocket liability related to PA timing or payer processing problems.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** The comment directly responds to the general RFI topic on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing. It recommends prohibiting timing-based denials, allowing retroactive authorization, preventing repeat specimen collection, requiring expedited review, simplifying documentation, protecting patients financially, ensuring automation improves access, and monitoring laboratory benefit managers.
- **S20/S43:** The comment does not address drug PA timeframes directly, but it supports expedited review principles for time-sensitive diagnostics where delays can affect treatment decisions.
- **S16:** The comment does not discuss denial reason content directly, but it objects to timing-based denials as an inappropriate basis for denying medically necessary laboratory testing.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prohibit denials based solely on PA not being finalized by the specimen collection date.
2. Permit retroactive authorization after specimen collection when medical necessity is documented.
3. Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws and repeat specimen collection when a valid specimen is available.
4. Require expedited review for time-sensitive diagnostics, including biomarker testing and rare disease diagnostics.
5. Standardize and simplify clinical documentation requirements.
6. Protect patients from surprise bills or out-of-pocket liability caused by administrative timing or payer processing delays.
7. Ensure electronic PA and interoperability tools reduce burden and account for laboratory workflows.
8. Monitor whether laboratory benefit managers improve coordination or create additional barriers.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative timelines that do not align with real-world laboratory workflows, inconsistent payer requirements, duplicative documentation, resubmissions, appeals, and laboratory benefit manager practices.
