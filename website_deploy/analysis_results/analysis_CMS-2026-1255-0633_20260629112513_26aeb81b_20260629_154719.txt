# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Cabarrus Rowan Community Health Centers, Inc. (CRCHC); signed by Don Holloman.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider / Community Health Center / Safety-net Provider.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Established June 14, 2006; operates six brick-and-mortar sites across four counties, two special-populations clinics for migrant and homeless individuals, two mobile medical/dental buses, and a school-based care program. In 2025, served 19,381 unique patients across 60,779 encounters, with 44% uninsured.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CRCHC supports CMS's interoperability and drug ePA proposals because they could reduce administrative burden and improve access for medically underserved patients, but it stresses that implementation must account for safety-net providers' limited IT, cybersecurity, vendor, and financial capacity. It recommends vendor accountability, no provider penalties for vendor failures, payer alignment, cybersecurity and funding support, benefit-pathway transparency, 340B/pharmacy workflow coordination, proactive PA expiration alerts, stronger PA reduction policies including gold-carding, centralized metrics and endpoint reporting, FHIR transition support, CDex guardrails, and CMS-ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0053-F; 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); 90 FR 37130 (Aug. 4, 2025); CMS February 28, 2024 National Standards Group enforcement discretion.
- **Standards referenced:** APIs; FHIR; CRD/DTR/PAS HL7 Da Vinci IGs v2.2; Provider Access API; Patient Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization API; CRD Coverage Information extension; X12 6020 claims attachments; FHIR/CDex PA attachments; X12 278; C-CDA, PDF, text, and FHIR resource attachments; HTI-4 and HTI-5 ONC certification rules.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API interoperability and safety-net implementation support**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Standardized real-time exchange can reduce duplicative documentation, improve visibility into PA requirements, and streamline care coordination, but CHCs face vendor dependence, limited IT staff, and fragmented workflows.
- **Specific request:** Clarify providers will not be penalized for failures attributable to vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems; coordinate timelines with ONC certification; require readiness validation/testing; create uniform implementation requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.

**Topic 2: Cybersecurity and funding support for CHCs**
- **Provision ID:** PRIVACY-SECURITY / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Broader API exchange increases cybersecurity exposure for resource-constrained CHCs that lack dedicated cybersecurity officers and rely on third parties.
- **Specific request:** Provide technical assistance, implementation guidance, cybersecurity and IT workforce development support, and dedicated health IT funding opportunities.

**Topic 3: Electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug ePA can improve point-of-prescribing visibility and reduce manual PA burden for medications used in diabetes, hypertension, behavioral health, HIV, OUD, and serious mental illness.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA for drugs but pair it with implementation support, benefit-pathway transparency, and PA reduction reforms.

**Topic 4: Medical vs pharmacy benefit classification**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Drugs such as Sublocade, long-acting injectable antipsychotics, and Cabenuva may be billed under pharmacy or medical benefits depending on payer and care setting.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension; clarify whether PA approvals under one pathway apply to alternate payment pathways.

**Topic 5: 340B, pharmacy operations, and interoperability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; TECHNICAL-FEASIBILITY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CHC pharmacy operations depend on EHRs, pharmacy management systems, TPAs, contract pharmacies, and payer APIs that may not exchange real-time structured information, risking missed 340B accumulation and delays.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs to provide technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding support for CHCs.

**Topic 6: PA expiration alerts and PA reduction reforms**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA alone may accelerate inefficient processes and not reduce total burden; proactive notifications and reduced PA volume would protect continuity.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts; extend gold-carding to prescription drugs; create sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 7: Communications, denial reasons, timeframes, and automatic approval**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Denial reasons and aligned timeframes help CHCs correct submissions, appeal, and avoid delays; payer deadline misses should not harm patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize specific denial reasons regardless of submission method; maintain aligned timeframes; publish PA metrics centrally; expand metrics; create automatic approval when payers miss required timeframes.

**Topic 8: API endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Centralized endpoint information reduces CHC IT burden and helps avoid outdated hard-coded endpoints.
- **Specific request:** Publish endpoint information on CMS's website as machine-readable files; require updates within one week and annual verification; prioritize ease of access for rural and safety-net primary care providers.

**Topic 9: API usage metrics and PA data through APIs**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; PATIENT-ACCESS; PROVIDER-ACCESS; PAYER-TO-PAYER
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Detailed PA information in APIs supports accountability and continuity for populations that move frequently. Metrics should measure usability and operational value, not just availability.
- **Specific request:** Make PA request/decision information for all drugs available through Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs beginning October 1, 2027; retain information for at least one year; report managed care metrics by program and plan; add API functionality/usability metrics including success rates, authorization failures, malformed queries, unreceived calls, structured/unstructured data, workflow impact, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance cost.

**Topic 10: Retain formulary information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose / Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer websites and standard communications are not sufficiently reliable or real-time for CHC prescribing across multiple payers.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 11: HIPAA standards transition from X12 to FHIR**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** CRCHC supports moving from X12 to FHIR but warns CHCs will face dual-standard burdens during transition.
- **Specific request:** Provide transition support; extend CMS's February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition period.

**Topic 12: CDex guardrails and gold-carding**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** CDex can support PA attachments but may enable iterative payer data requests. Gold-carding can reduce unreimbursed CHC staff burden.
- **Specific request:** Require all documentation in the initial CDex request, limit solicited-attachment requests to two per PA transaction before decision, set maximum payer response timeframes after attachments, formally recommend gold-card initiatives, and extend privileges to all items and services for eligible providers.

**Topic 13: CMS-ONC harmonization and HTI-5 concerns**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS-0062-P payer compliance by October 1, 2027 may precede certified provider-facing technology availability under HTI-4, and HTI-5 deregulation could remove supporting capabilities CHCs need.
- **Specific request:** Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance, conduct a formal CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P, and ensure providers are not deemed non-compliant for unavailable certified technology.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S2/S3:** Supports mandatory CRD/DTR/PAS standards but requests synchronized IG timelines, ONC alignment, and readiness validation.
- **S12/S15/S21:** Supports ePA for drugs but asks for benefit classification through APIs, clarification of dual medical/pharmacy benefit approvals, and implementation support.
- **S16/S17:** Supports specific drug PA denial reasons and asks for actionable information identifying missing or insufficient content.
- **S20/S43:** Supports aligned decision timeframes and requests automatic approval when payers fail to meet deadlines.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports centralized API endpoint reporting, one-week updates, annual verification, and machine-readable CMS publication.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports granular API usage metrics and recommends additional functionality, usability, and workflow-impact measures.
- **S37:** Identifies implementation challenges for CHCs, EHR vendors, cybersecurity, 340B operations, and payer variation.
- **S44-S55:** Supports FHIR transition from X12 but requests enforcement discretion, transition support, CDex guardrails, and realistic certification/timeline alignment.
- **S60-S65:** Requests CMS-ONC harmonization and assessment of HTI-5 impacts on CMS-0062-P implementation.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to broader PA reform issues including proactive PA expiration alerts, gold-carding, sunset provisions, and step-therapy-related PA burden.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize API and drug ePA proposals with safety-net implementation support.
2. Clarify that providers will not be penalized for vendor, clearinghouse, or EHR failures outside provider control.
3. Coordinate CMS timelines with ONC certification and require readiness validation before enforcement.
4. Establish uniform implementation requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.
5. Provide cybersecurity technical assistance, workforce support, and funding for CHCs.
6. Require benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using CRD Coverage Information extension.
7. Clarify cross-pathway PA validity where drugs can be covered under both medical and pharmacy benefits.
8. Coordinate with OPA on 340B and pharmacy workflow implementation assistance.
9. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.
10. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction requirements, including gold-carding for prescription drugs and sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories.
11. Finalize specific denial reason requirements for drug PA denials regardless of submission method.
12. Publish PA metrics centrally in a standardized, publicly accessible CMS format.
13. Add granular metrics including denial rates by medication/service category, first-pass approval rates, denials overturned due to inadequate notices, and data completeness/quality.
14. Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
15. Publish API endpoints as machine-readable files on CMS's website and maintain timely updates.
16. Make drug PA information available through Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs by October 1, 2027 and retain it at least one year.
17. Report Medicaid/CHIP managed care Patient Access API usage by program and plan.
18. Add API functionality/usability metrics and workflow-impact measures.
19. Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
20. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds and implementation support.
21. Extend CMS enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API as X12 278 substitute during the CMS-0062-P transition.
22. Adopt CDex guardrails limiting iterative attachment requests.
23. Issue guidance recommending payer gold-card initiatives across all items and services for eligible providers.
24. Issue joint CMS-ONC guidance and publish a harmonization assessment before final rule.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Safety-net readiness, vendor dependency, cybersecurity exposure, fragmented payer requirements, dual medical/pharmacy benefit ambiguity, 340B workflow gaps, ePA without PA reduction, payer delays, poor metric granularity, X12/FHIR transition burden, and CMS-ONC timeline misalignment.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Medication delays, missed HIV injection appointments, psychiatric decompensation, viral rebound, relapse, avoidable worsening conditions, data/security risk, missed 340B accumulation events, duplicative work, claim errors, and unreimbursed staff costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No native EHR PA submission; reliance on fax, Availity, and payer portals; limited CHC IT staff; lack of cybersecurity officer; non-integrated pharmacy and EHR systems; contract pharmacy/TPA complexity; vendor priorities; certified technology not available by payer deadlines.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CRCHC served 19,381 patients and 60,779 encounters in 2025; 44% uninsured; 94% of physicians report PA delays care; average PA wait about three days and nearly one-third wait over a week; MA organizations made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024 with over four million full/partial denials; CHCs serve more than 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, over 6 million uninsured people, 20.5 million low-income people, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and over 1.5 million homeless individuals; over 90% of CHC patients have incomes below 200% FPL; CRCHC has 2,573 diabetic patients, 4,005 patients with hypertension, 551 with heart disease; more than 65% of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy arrangements; CRCHC has 750+ contract pharmacy arrangements; proactive outreach before PA expiration has positive outcomes in 87% of cases vs 25% without notification; 26% of CRCHC patients are Medicaid; nearly half of CHCs have fewer than 90 days cash on hand; one in four CHCs report negative 5% margins.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA, KFF, MGMA, HRSA UDS, NACHC, Weitzman Institute, Pharmacy Times, Krauss et al., Murthi et al., Vishwanath et al., Lauffenburger et al., Salzbrenner et al., CMS guidance, WISeR model materials, 90 FR 37130.
- **Examples provided:** HIV patient missing scheduled injection after fax PA delay; Sublocade, LAI antipsychotics, Cabenuva, Aristada, Perseris, Uzedy, Invega Sustenna; 340B TPA/EHR/pharmacy management system disconnects.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs, safety-net providers, uninsured and Medicaid patients, rural patients, migrant and homeless populations, providers, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, TPAs, pharmacies, PBMs, Medicaid MCOs, QHP issuers, CMS, ONC, OPA.
- **Economic implications:** Significant unfunded IT/cybersecurity/vendor costs; unreimbursed PA staff burden; possible federal matching funds needed; CHC cash-flow strain; risk of claim/340B errors and payer delays shifting costs to CHCs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS coordinates with ONC, vendors, and safety-net providers; requires certified modules, FHIR/API readiness, CDex guardrails, endpoint/reporting standardization, and cybersecurity support.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly positive if implemented with support; risk of treatment interruptions and access barriers if implementation is fragmented or underfunded.
