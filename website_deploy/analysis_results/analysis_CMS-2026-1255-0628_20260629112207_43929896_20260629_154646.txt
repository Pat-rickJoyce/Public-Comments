# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Christian John Lillis, Chief Executive Officer & Co-Founder, Peggy Lillis Foundation.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / patient advocacy nonprofit.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** The Peggy Lillis Foundation works on C. diff awareness, education, advocacy, and policy.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Peggy Lillis Foundation strongly supports CMS-0062-P because faster, standardized electronic PA workflows could improve access to life-saving C. diff treatments and reduce provider/pharmacy/patient burden. The comment also urges CMS to add substantive guardrails so drug PA and step therapy criteria are clinically appropriate, evidence-based, non-discriminatory, and do not delay medically necessary treatment.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY; GENERAL/RFI on step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P generally; no CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** Standardized electronic workflows; no named technical standards beyond the proposed rule's general ePA framework.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Support for electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Standardized electronic workflows and movement away from fax, portal, and phone PA can shorten decision times and reduce burden for providers, pharmacies, and patients.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the proposal to streamline drug PA through electronic exchange and standardized workflows.

**Topic 2: Timely access to C. diff therapies**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PATIENT-ACCESS
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Delayed PA can prevent timely access to antibiotics and live biotherapeutic products for recurrent C. diff, with potentially serious clinical consequences.
- **Specific request:** Maintain shorter decision timeframes across CMS programs.

**Topic 3: Denial transparency and public reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons and public reporting of denials, appeals, and other data improve accountability and help stakeholders understand PA performance.
- **Specific request:** Finalize payer obligations to provide specific denial reasons and publicly report PA metrics.

**Topic 4: Substantive guardrails for clinical appropriateness and step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs; PATIENT-ACCESS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Electronic and faster PA may still deny or delay clinically necessary care if criteria are not clinically appropriate or evidence-based; C. diff patients may be required to fail vancomycin before fidaxomicin despite clinical need.
- **Specific request:** Consider substantive guardrails for PA denials and step therapy to ensure licensed clinicians' medical advice is not inappropriately denied or delayed.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20/S43:** Supports shorter drug PA decision timeframes because timely access to C. diff treatment can be life-saving.
- **S16/S17:** Supports requiring specific denial reasons to providers.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Supports public reporting of denials, appeals outcomes, and other PA information.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the step therapy RFI by urging guardrails for clinically appropriate, evidence-based step therapy and exceptions where required first-line therapy may be harmful or ineffective.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize CMS-0062-P's drug ePA proposals.
2. Maintain shorter decision timeframes for drug PA across CMS programs.
3. Require payers to provide specific denial reasons to providers.
4. Require public reporting of claims denials, appeal outcomes, and other PA information.
5. Establish substantive guardrails so PA and step therapy criteria are clinically appropriate, evidence-based, and non-discriminatory.
6. Protect access to C. diff treatments including fidaxomicin and FDA-approved live biotherapeutic products such as Vowst and Rebyota.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA and step therapy may delay life-saving C. diff treatment; procedural improvements may not ensure clinically reasonable criteria.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed treatment, greater program expense, repeated C. diff recurrence, prolonged suffering, payer denials despite clinician medical necessity.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Fax/portal/phone workflows, nontransparent denial reasons, step therapy requirements based on inflexible or poor evidence.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** C. diff caused nearly 500,000 infections and 29,000 deaths in one year; infections recur in approximately 26% of patients.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal citations in text beyond clinical references to C. diff therapies and FDA-approved LBPs.
- **Examples provided:** Fidaxomicin denials; step therapy requiring vancomycin first; barriers to Vowst and Rebyota until multiple recurrences.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** C. diff patients, prescribers, pharmacies, payers, CMS programs, patient advocates.

- **Technical feasibility:** Supports standardized electronic PA as feasible and burden-reducing, but emphasizes policy guardrails beyond technology.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong patient-access focus; faster and more clinically appropriate PA can prevent harm from delayed therapy.

The Peggy Lillis Foundation is a nonprofit organization with a mission to build a nationwide C. 
diff (Clostridioides difficile) awareness movement by educating the public, empowering 
advocates, and shaping policy. C. diff is a spore-forming bacteria that can cause life-threatening 
infections, often affecting adults in hospitals or in long-term care settings. In one year, C. diff 
caused nearly 500,000 infections and 29,000 deaths, and C. diff infections recur in approximately 
26% of patients. Treatment options include antibiotics such as vancomycin and fidaxomicin. For 
patients with recurrent C. diff, FDA-approved live biotherapeutic products (LBPs) such as Vowst 
(fecal microbiota spores, live-brpk) and Rebyota (fecal microbiota, live-jslm) are used to prevent 
further recurrences. Antibiotic and LBP therapies represent distinct, clinically important 
categories of treatments and preventives that are increasingly subject to prior authorization 
barriers. 
 
The Peggy Lillis Foundation strongly supports CMS’s proposed rule CMS-0062-P. Our interest 
in CMS-0062-P arises from our shared goal with CMS to streamline the prior authorization 
process for providers, pharmacies, and patients and accelerate payers’ ability to make clinically 
informed decisions. Particularly for patients suffering from C. diff infections, shorter decision 
timeframes for prior authorizations across CMS programs could improve timely access to 
life-saving drug treatments. The move toward standardized electronic workflows and away from
