## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Colorado Department of Health Care Policy & Financing (HCPF); signed by Jim Leonard, PharmD, Deputy Director, Pharmacy Office.
- **Stakeholder type:** Government
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Colorado single state agency for Health First Colorado (Medicaid) and Child Health Plan Plus (CHIP); pharmacy office leadership.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HCPF is concerned that CMS's proposed implementation timeline and drug prior authorization response requirements may be unrealistic for state Medicaid/CHIP programs and could unintentionally slow access to medications. It requests a three-year implementation timeframe, a later or separate effective date with enforcement discretion for IT changes, and clarification that a 24-hour pharmacy PA response may request additional information while a final decision may occur within 72 hours when needed for informed review.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE/GENERAL; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS indirectly.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; proposed rule pages 19935-19938 referenced by page range; no CFR section cited.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards; National Drug Code (NDC) transition to 12-digit format; pharmacy prior authorization response/decision process.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation feasibility**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** States are facing concurrent IT and policy transformations, including WFTC-related Medicaid changes and future NDC format changes.
- **Specific request:** Replace the proposed timeline with a more realistic three-year implementation timeframe.

**Topic 2: Competing state system priorities**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Colorado must implement community engagement requirements by January 1, 2027, six-month renewals, vendor coordination, member communications, and systems changes.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide guidance and technical assistance and account for state implementation capacity.

**Topic 3: 24-hour response vs decision**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** A final decision within 24 hours may force denials when additional clinical information could support approval.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that a 24-hour response may request more information, with a 72-hour decision timeframe when needed for informed pharmacist and medical review.

**Topic 4: Effective date and enforcement discretion**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** October 1, 2027 may be infeasible for this portion of the rule given IT systems adjustments.
- **Specific request:** Consider a later separate effective date and enforcement discretion.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S19:** HCPF addresses state readiness and explains that concurrent state/federal requirements and IT changes make the proposed timeline unrealistic.
- **S20:** HCPF directly responds to the 24-hour Medicaid/CHIP drug PA timeframe, requesting clarification between a response and final decision.
- **S22:** HCPF references NCPDP and NDC-related system work as implementation burdens, though it does not provide a cost estimate.
- **S36/S38:** HCPF's request for a three-year timeframe, separate effective date, and enforcement discretion relates to state extension/timing criteria.
- **S43:** HCPF recommends 72 hours for pharmacy benefit PAR decisions, aligned with other benefits when additional review is needed.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Adopt a three-year implementation timeframe for affected state systems.
2. Account for concurrent WFTC-related Medicaid eligibility and renewal system changes.
3. Provide CMS guidance and technical assistance for implementation.
4. Clarify that 24 hours may mean a response, not necessarily a final decision.
5. Allow a 72-hour decision timeframe when additional clinical information or review is needed.
6. Consider a later, separate effective date for drug PA timeline requirements.
7. Exercise enforcement discretion during necessary IT systems adjustments.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Unrealistic timeline, concurrent statutory IT work, NCPDP/NDC transitions, ambiguity between response and decision, increased denials, and staffing constraints.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Slower medication access, unnecessary denials, delayed care, administrative bottlenecks, and member disruption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** State IT changes, vendor coordination, member outreach, community engagement implementation, six-month renewals, future 12-digit NDC conversion, and need for CMS guidance.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** FDA-required 12-digit NDC transition effective March 7, 2033; community engagement requirements by January 1, 2027; current Colorado practice responds within 24 hours.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Requests requiring additional information before approval; staffing impact if forced final decisions increase denials.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Colorado Medicaid and CHIP members, HCPF, state vendors, providers/requesters, pharmacists/medical reviewers, CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Major state IT and vendor coordination costs implied; increased denials would require additional staffing and administrative handling.
- **Technical feasibility:** HCPF argues the proposed October 1, 2027 date is infeasible given concurrent systems changes.
- **Patient care impact:** Final decisions forced too quickly may cause avoidable denials and delayed appropriate therapy.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Concerned; requests modification and clarification
- **Tone:** Professional, operational, cautionary
- **Priority level:** High

1. "CMS's proposed implementation timeline is unrealistic." - Context: State feasibility concern.

3. "If a decision is required within 24 hours, the number of denials will increase." - Context: Warning about unintended consequences.
4. "A 24 hour response time is preferred but that a 72 hour decision time frame is allowed when needed." - Context: Requested clarification.
