# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Texas Association of Community Health Centers (TACHC); signed by Jana Eubank, CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents 79 Texas federally qualified health centers/community health centers operating more than 700 clinic sites in 131 counties and serving 1.9 million Texans, including Medicaid, uninsured, low-income, agricultural worker, and homeless populations.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

TACHC broadly supports CMS's interoperability and electronic prior authorization proposals because they could reduce administrative burden and improve access for medically underserved patients served by community health centers. It urges CMS to pair the mandates with realistic timelines, vendor accountability, cybersecurity and implementation support, centralized reporting, benefit-classification transparency, CDex guardrails, gold-carding, and CMS-ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); 90 FR 37130; CMS-0053-F; CMS-0062-P. The comment also references the proposed CMS-0062-P interoperability and PA framework generally.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; HL7 Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS implementation guides; CRD Coverage Information extension; Provider Access API; Patient Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Prior Authorization API; CDex; C-CDA; X12 278; X12 6020; ONC certification criteria; HTI-4; HTI-5.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Interoperability API implementation support**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CHCs support standardized, real-time exchange but have limited IT staff, constrained budgets, vendor dependence, and cybersecurity exposure. Without aligned vendor readiness and testing, API requirements may create operational disruption for safety-net providers.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate timelines with ONC certification, validate readiness before enforcement, clarify providers will not be penalized for vendor or clearinghouse failures outside their control, and provide technical assistance, cybersecurity support, workforce development support, and funding.

**Topic 2: Drug electronic prior authorization and benefit classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Prescription drug ePA could improve medication access, adherence, and point-of-prescribing visibility, but dual medical/pharmacy benefit workflows are difficult for CHCs when coverage pathway is unclear.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension and clarify whether a PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to an alternate pathway.

**Topic 3: Pharmacy operations and 340B workflows**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug ePA implementation will cross EHRs, pharmacy management systems, contract pharmacies, TPAs, payer APIs, eligibility, claims, dispensing, and 340B accumulation systems that may not exchange complete structured data in real time.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs and provide targeted guidance, technical assistance, and funding for CHCs and 340B-related pharmacy operations.

**Topic 4: PA expiration notifications and prior authorization reduction**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA alone may digitize a burdensome process without reducing PA volume; proactive expiration alerts can prevent medication interruptions and retroactive reauthorization work.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications through FHIR-based alerts, extend gold-carding to prescription drugs, and create sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 5: Denial reasons, timeframes, and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Clear denial explanations and faster decisions are important for CHC patients who face barriers to access and continuity of care. Public metrics can improve transparency but should capture real-world burden.
- **Specific request:** Require actionable denial information, publish PA metrics centrally on CMS's website, add metrics such as denial rates by service or medication category and first-pass approval rates, enforce against persistent delays or inappropriate denials, and establish automatic approval when required timeframes are missed.

**Topic 6: API endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Centralized endpoint information would reduce provider, EHR vendor, and app developer burden and prevent care delays from outdated or hard-coded endpoints.
- **Specific request:** Publish payer API endpoint information on CMS's website in centralized, machine-readable files; maintain one-week update requirements and annual verification; prioritize ease of access for safety-net and rural primary care providers.

**Topic 7: API usage metrics and formulary information**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** More granular metrics can identify access gaps and payer accountability issues, but CMS should also measure whether APIs function in real clinical workflows. Removing formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs would harm real-time prescribing decisions.
- **Specific request:** Report metrics by program and plan for Medicaid/CHIP managed care; add metrics on successful and failed API requests, authorization failures, malformed queries, unreceived calls, structured versus unstructured responses, calls per query, queries per user, workflow impact, and API maintenance cost; retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 8: HIPAA PA standards transition, CDex, and gold-carding**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** TACHC supports moving from X12 toward FHIR but warns that CHCs will face a dual-standard transition involving X12 6020 and FHIR/CDex. CDex may also enable repeated escalating documentation requests unless bounded.
- **Specific request:** Extend CMS's February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion for use of a FHIR PA API as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition; require payers to specify all documentation in the initial CDex request, limit solicited attachments to two per PA transaction, establish payer response timeframes after receiving attachments, and recommend broad gold-carding initiatives.

**Topic 9: ONC standards and certification alignment**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS payer compliance by October 1, 2027 may precede certified provider-facing ePA technology deadlines in December 2027 and January 2028, creating a gap for CHCs. HTI-5 certification reductions may also weaken supporting infrastructure.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should issue joint implementation guidance, ensure HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P, publish a CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before the final rule, and confirm providers are not non-compliant when certified technology is unavailable.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Coordinate CMS API and PA timelines with ONC certification schedules and validate readiness before enforcement.
2. Clarify that providers are not penalized for vendor, clearinghouse, EHR, or unavailable certified technology failures outside their control.
3. Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity support, workforce development support, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.
4. Require payer disclosure of drug benefit classification through Provider Access APIs using CRD Coverage Information.
5. Clarify whether PA approvals under one drug benefit pathway apply to alternate medical or pharmacy payment pathways.
6. Coordinate with OPA on 340B and pharmacy workflow impacts and provide targeted CHC implementation guidance.
7. Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration notifications to providers.
8. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction reforms, including prescription drug gold-carding and sunset provisions.
9. Require actionable denial notices and automatic approval when required decision timeframes are missed.
10. Publish PA metrics and API endpoint information centrally on CMS's website in standardized, machine-readable formats.
11. Expand API usage metrics to measure functionality, errors, usability, data structure, workflow impact, and maintenance cost.
12. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
13. Extend enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute through the transition period.
14. Add CDex guardrails limiting iterative attachment requests and requiring timely payer decisions after attachments.
15. Publish joint CMS-ONC guidance and a harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vendor readiness, CHC IT and cybersecurity capacity, dual medical/pharmacy benefit workflows, lack of benefit-pathway clarity, fragmented pharmacy and 340B systems, ePA digitizing rather than reducing burden, removal of formulary information from APIs, dual X12/FHIR transition burden, and misalignment with ONC certification timing.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed care, medication interruptions, inappropriate denials, duplicative documentation, higher administrative costs, care coordination disruption, cybersecurity risk, provider confusion, and unreimbursed staff burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC budgets and IT staff, reliance on EHR vendors and third-party technology partners, PMS/EHR interoperability gaps, rural and safety-net resource constraints, changing formularies, hard-coded endpoints, certification timeline gaps, and multiple concurrent standards.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 79 Texas FQHCs, more than 700 sites in 131 counties, 1.9 million Texas patients, more than 500,000 Medicaid patients, over 630,000 uninsured patients, 94 percent of physicians reporting PA delays care, average PA wait time of about three days, nearly one-third waiting more than one week, nearly 53 million MA PA determinations in 2024, CHC national patient population statistics, chronic disease counts for Texas CHCs, over 65 percent of CHCs using 340B contract pharmacy arrangements, and financial strain indicators including fewer than 90 days cash on hand for nearly half of CHCs.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA prior authorization survey, KFF Medicare Advantage PA determinations, MGMA PA burden report, HRSA UDS data, NACHC reports, medication adherence research, pharmacy interoperability literature, EHR access studies, CMS enforcement discretion guidance, WISeR model materials, and HTI-4/HTI-5 regulatory materials.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine may be billed under pharmacy benefit when dispensed by a pharmacy or medical benefit when administered in-clinic; CHC patients who are homeless or agricultural workers may need PA information retained because they move frequently and may not return for months; outdated hard-coded API endpoints can disrupt EHR workflows.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Community health centers, FQHC patients, Medicaid and CHIP patients, uninsured and low-income patients, rural communities, EHR vendors, pharmacy management system vendors, contract pharmacies, TPAs, payers, CMS, ONC, and OPA.
- **Economic implications:** CHCs may face unreimbursed costs for technology upgrades, cybersecurity, training, workflow redesign, pharmacy coordination, and dual-standard support; federal matching funds and dedicated support could reduce safety-net burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS aligns implementation with ONC certification, requires testing, ensures vendor accountability, publishes machine-readable endpoint data, retains necessary formulary data, and supports EHR/PMS/API integration.
- **Patient care impact:** The proposals could improve medication adherence, reduce delays, support continuity of care, and improve transparency, but poor implementation could interrupt treatment and shift costs onto safety-net providers and patients.
