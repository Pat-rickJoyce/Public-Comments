# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** CareDx Inc.; signed by Kimberly Linthicum, VP, Government Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor; Clinical Laboratory Provider; Other healthcare company
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** CLIA-licensed, CAP-accredited laboratory services for transplant recipients; precision medicine company focused on transplant diagnostics including AlloMap and AlloSure.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CareDx responds to the RFI on laboratory testing and prior authorization from the perspective of transplant rejection monitoring. It urges CMS to reduce or eliminate PA barriers for longitudinal non-invasive molecular transplant monitoring, standardize LBM criteria and timelines, improve transparency for LBM-driven coverage changes, and at minimum require one annual authorization for longitudinal biomarker monitoring pathways.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI laboratory tests and DMEPOS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; TRANSPARENCY; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P RFI on laboratory tests and DMEPOS; no CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** No FHIR/NCPDP standards central to the comment; focuses on laboratory benefit managers (LBMs), prior authorization processes, and medical necessity documentation.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: LBM transparency and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CareDx says payer use and switching of LBMs creates disjointed, non-transparent coverage and PA processes. LBMs and payer customers may each direct the lab back to the other, delaying resolution and creating abrupt coverage changes.
- **Specific request:** CMS should standardize clinical criteria, documentation requirements, and decision timelines for LBMs used by payers under CMS authority; payers should remain accountable for LBM policies and decisions.

**Topic 2: Coverage policy changes by LBMs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CareDx reports that changes between LBMs can convert a service from covered to non-covered or experimental/investigational without explanation, disrupting patients undergoing longitudinal monitoring.
- **Specific request:** Require negative coverage changes to be publicly posted with evidence-based rationale, an appeal pathway, and a meaningful transition period.

**Topic 3: Prior authorization for non-invasive molecular transplant monitoring**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Oppose PA / Modify if PA retained
- **Rationale:** CareDx argues non-invasive molecular testing often replaces or informs invasive biopsies, which generally do not require PA, and repeated authorizations for recurring monitoring create duplicative burden and payment uncertainty.
- **Specific request:** Remove PA for non-invasive molecular transplant monitoring tests; if not possible, treat organ rejection monitoring as a care pathway with one annual authorization covering all testing needed that year.

**Topic 4: Longitudinal biomarker monitoring beyond transplant**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Similar recurring testing occurs in oncology minimal residual disease (MRD) monitoring and other advanced biomarker pathways. Repeated PA submissions do not improve clinical decision-making.
- **Specific request:** Apply the single annual authorization/care pathway approach to longitudinal biomarker monitoring services ordered multiple times per year.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82 (Laboratory tests/DMEPOS RFI):** CareDx responds directly to questions about LBMs' impact, process improvement opportunities, and how PA can be made more effective at reducing burden and improving outcomes.
- **S16/S17:** The comment is relevant to denial reasons and transparent rationale for negative coverage determinations, especially when services move to non-covered or experimental status.
- **S20/S43:** CareDx addresses decision timelines for LBMs and argues that a 3-day authorization window can be unreasonable compared with a 7-14 day operational expectation.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Eliminate prior authorization for non-invasive molecular transplant monitoring tests where testing replaces or reduces invasive procedures.
2. If PA remains, create care pathways that limit PA to one annual approval per patient for longitudinal biomarker monitoring.
3. Standardize LBM clinical criteria, documentation requirements, and decision timelines for payers under CMS authority.
4. Require public reporting of negative LBM/payer coverage policy changes with evidence-based rationale.
5. Require appeal rights and meaningful transition periods when coverage status changes.
6. Ensure payers remain accountable for policies implemented through contracted LBMs.
7. Use post-payment review and test requisition forms to substantiate medical necessity and program integrity.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** LBM opacity, abrupt coverage changes, repeated PA for longitudinal monitoring, annual quantity restrictions, short authorization windows, payment uncertainty.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed transplant monitoring, unnecessary biopsies or invasive procedures, delayed detection of rejection, unnecessary administrative burden, reduced predictability for clean claims.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** LBM/payer accountability gaps, inconsistent coverage criteria, lack of transparency into policy disputes and authorization requirements.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Medicare coverage dates for CareDx services in 2006 and 2017; examples of professional guideline monitoring frequencies (monthly in first year and quarterly thereafter for heart/lung; monthly for first four months then quarterly for kidney in some recommendations).
- **Studies or research referenced:** Mentions publication of evidence supporting innovation but does not cite specific studies in the comment text.
- **Examples provided:** One LBM covers post-transplant surveillance testing only one unit per year; another example cites a 3-day authorization timeline where 7-14 days would be more reasonable.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Transplant patients, transplant clinicians, clinical laboratories, LBMs, payers, oncology patients needing MRD monitoring.
- **Economic implications:** Non-invasive monitoring may reduce costs by avoiding biopsies, inpatient rejection treatment, graft failure, return to dialysis, retransplantation, and redundant administrative work.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment emphasizes policy/process reform more than technology standards.
- **Patient care impact:** High; timely monitoring can protect transplanted organs, guide treatment, avoid invasive biopsies, and support personalized care.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive of modernization but critical of current LBM/PA barriers.
- **Tone:** Professional, evidence-oriented, operational, patient-centered.
- **Priority level:** High.
