# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Rural Health Association (NRHA); signed by Alan Morgan, Chief Executive Officer.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Non-profit membership organization with more than 21,000 members representing rural hospitals, critical access hospitals, long-term care providers, clinicians, nurses, and patients.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NRHA broadly supports CMS's proposed prior authorization and interoperability changes because rural patients are disproportionately covered by impacted payers and rural providers face significant PA burden. NRHA asks CMS to strengthen denial transparency, align and shorten decision timeframes, support electronic PA for all drugs, improve public reporting, make metrics easier for rural beneficiaries to access, and use high denial rates as a trigger for oversight.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING by publication implications; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 431.80(a)(2), 438.210(c)(3), 457.732(a)(2); 45 CFR 156.223(a)(2) appears intended though the text includes a typographical "4 C.F.R." citation.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit Standard (RTPB); electronic prior authorization.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for medical benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized ePA will make PA easier for rural patients and providers to navigate.
- **Specific request:** Finalize requirements for impacted payers to support electronic PA for drugs covered under a medical benefit.

**Topic 2: Electronic PA and RTPB for pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA for pharmacy benefit drugs and RTPB can streamline access, reduce administrative burden, and provide real-time information on PA requirements and out-of-pocket costs.
- **Specific request:** Finalize NCPDP SCRIPT and RTPB requirements for impacted payers.

**Topic 3: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Generic reasons such as lack of medical necessity do not give rural providers or patients enough information to appeal or resubmit successfully.
- **Specific request:** Put stronger specificity requirements in regulatory text, require granular information tied to documentation or criteria, and treat partial approvals such as shortened treatment duration as denials requiring explanation.

**Topic 4: Decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Aligned timeframes reduce administrative complexity and improve predictability for rural providers serving patients across payer types.
- **Specific request:** Apply non-drug PA timeframes to QHPs, align non-covered outpatient drug timeframes with related items and services, shorten CHIP FFS drug PA decisions to 24 hours, and consider a 24-hour drug PA timeframe for all payers in future rulemaking.

**Topic 5: Public PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Numeric counts and expanded metrics will give a fuller picture than percentages alone, but rural enrollees may not benefit if data are scattered or only online.
- **Specific request:** Require drug PA metrics, include numeric counts and appeal/extension outcomes, aggregate data on CMS's website, provide non-online formats such as opt-in annual mailed reports, and investigate very high denial rates.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports incorporating medical-benefit drugs into ePA because it standardizes PA for rural patients and providers.
- **S14:** Supports pharmacy-benefit ePA requirements for impacted non-Part D payers using NCPDP standards.
- **S16:** Requests stronger regulatory text defining the specificity needed for denial reasons and rejects generic medical-necessity explanations.
- **S17:** Supports more granular denial information, including documentation-specific explanations, though it does not expressly address structured codes.
- **S20:** Supports 24-hour CHIP FFS drug PA decisions and urges CMS to consider 24-hour drug PA timeframes for all payers in future rulemaking.
- **S21:** Supports aligned 7-day standard and 72-hour urgent timeframes for non-covered outpatient drugs when bundled with related services.
- **S25:** Supports NCPDP RTPB as a real-time exchange mechanism to show PA requirements and patient out-of-pocket costs.
- **S29:** Supports expanded PA metrics, including numeric counts and outcomes after appeal or review extension.
- **S33:** Asks CMS to house payer-reported PA metrics in one aggregated CMS location for comparison.
- **S35:** Supports public reporting and asks CMS to make data accessible beyond payer websites, including mailed reports for some enrollees.
- **S40-S42:** Supports applying aligned QHP timeframes because it reduces administrative variation for rural providers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for drugs covered under medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Finalize NCPDP RTPB requirements for impacted payers other than MA organizations already subject to Part D timing.
3. Strengthen denial-reason specificity in regulatory text.
4. Require payers to explain partial denials, including approvals for shorter durations than requested.
5. Align QHP PA decision timeframes with other impacted payers.
6. Align non-covered outpatient drug timeframes with related non-drug items and services.
7. Apply the 24-hour drug PA timeframe more broadly in future rulemaking.
8. Require numeric counts and expanded PA metrics for non-drug and drug PA.
9. Publish PA metrics in one CMS-hosted location and offer non-online formats for rural enrollees.
10. Investigate payers with very high denial rates, such as rates above 85% for an item or service.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rural administrative burden, patient confusion, insufficient denial specificity, inconsistent timeframes, limited accessibility of online-only metrics, and high denial rates.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed care, damaged patient-provider relationships when patients blame rural providers for payer denials, reduced quality, and inequitable access for older rural adults with lower technology literacy.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Rural provider burden, patient literacy and technology barriers, payer variability, and lack of centralized comparable reporting.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Medicaid and CHIP cover approximately one-fifth of rural adults and almost half of rural children; NRHA has more than 21,000 members; cited AHA report that 81% of rural clinicians report quality reductions due to insurer requirements.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AHA report on Medicare Advantage impact on rural hospitals; press reports and statements on insurer PA reforms.
- **Examples provided:** Denial reason of lack of medical necessity; payer approving treatment but denying requested duration; online metrics inaccessible to older rural adults.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Rural patients, rural providers, rural hospitals, critical access hospitals, QHP enrollees, Medicaid/CHIP beneficiaries, impacted payers, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced administrative burden may help resource-constrained rural providers; oversight of high denial rates may reduce avoidable care delays.
- **Technical feasibility:** NRHA supports standards-based ePA, NCPDP SCRIPT, and RTPB as feasible tools to streamline workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Expected to improve medication access, transparency, patient trust, and timeliness of care for rural residents.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Tone:** Professional, collaborative, rural-access focused
- **Priority level:** High
