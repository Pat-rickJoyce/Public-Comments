## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

UW Health strongly supports the proposed rule's direction toward electronic prior authorization, standardized data exchange, and accelerated decision timeframes, viewing it as a significant step forward for patients and providers. The organization raises concerns about persistent cross-payer variability in data formatting and response structures that will undermine the rule's effectiveness even with common standards, and recommends CMS push for greater payer standardization of denial codes and step therapy definitions.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P
- **Standards referenced:** Implementation guides (IGs) referenced generically; FHIR-based prior authorization systems

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Payer Data Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Even with common IGs, payers impose inconsistent formatting requirements (e.g., "Suite" vs. "Ste."), apply different validation rules for the same information, and generate inconsistent denial/rejection responses for identical situations. This variability is granular enough to impede full automation and makes it hard for health systems to anticipate outcomes.
- **Specific request:** CMS should encourage standardized denial reason codes and response structures across payers; promote greater technical interoperability requirements to ensure consistent processing of similar requests across plans.

**Topic 2: Prior Authorization Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Current turnaround times can reach 21 days; academic medical centers serving complex populations experience treatment postponement, care coordination complications, and increased administrative burden from delayed determinations; 24-hour standard for drug-related requests represents meaningful improvement.
- **Specific request:** Supports proposed timelines as proposed; no modification requested.

**Topic 3: Step Therapy Standardization (RFI Response)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Providers face substantial burden recreating documentation about prior therapies despite availability of claims history; key terms are undefined — what constitutes "tried," what duration is required, what level of clinical unresponsiveness qualifies, and how prior payer determinations should be honored at coverage transitions.
- **Specific request:** CMS should establish standardized definitions for step therapy criteria and explore mechanisms to allow prior step therapy determinations and historical treatment data to follow the patient across payers through interoperable electronic exchange.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Supports requiring specific denial reasons; recommends standardized denial reason codes and response structures across payers to maximize benefit.
- **S17:** Implicitly supports structured/standardized responses; emphasizes consistency across payers, not just within individual payer systems.
- **S20:** Supports proposed 24-hour timeframe for drug-related requests as meaningful improvement over current delays of up to 21 days.
- **S43:** Supports shorter standardized timelines; notes benefit for encouraging greater automation and operational efficiency.
- **Step therapy RFI:** Recommends standardized definitions for step therapy criteria (what constitutes "tried and failed"); supports interoperable portability of prior step therapy determinations across payers and coverage transitions.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Encourage standardized denial reason codes and response structures across payers to ensure consistent and actionable denial notices.
2. Require payers to process similar requests consistently across plans and systems to reduce operational variability.
3. Develop standardized definitions for step therapy criteria, including what constitutes "tried," required duration, and level of documented unresponsiveness.
4. Establish mechanisms for prior step therapy determinations and historical treatment data to follow patients across payers through interoperable electronic exchange.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Cross-payer variability in data formatting and response logic will persist and undermine administrative simplification goals even after IGs are adopted; inconsistent payer validation rules make automation difficult and outcomes unpredictable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Continued administrative burden from payer-specific formatting; treatment delays of up to 21 days at academic medical centers serving complex patients; repeated manual documentation burden for step therapy despite available claims history.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer-specific formatting requirements ("Suite" vs. "Ste."); inconsistent validation rules generating different rejection responses for identical submissions.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Current PA turnaround times can reach up to 21 days; UW Health serves 867,000+ patients annually with 2,000+ physicians.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** Formatting inconsistency example ("Suite" vs. "Ste." for address fields); patient needing to manually attest to prior therapy trials despite existing claims history.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Academic medical centers and integrated health systems; medically complex patient populations; providers managing PA across multiple payer systems.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced administrative costs from automation if payer standardization is achieved; current multi-payer formatting variability imposes ongoing overhead that common IGs alone won't resolve.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports adoption of electronic PA; notes full automation is impeded by payer-specific variability even with common standards.
- **Patient care impact:** Current delays (up to 21 days) postpone treatment for complex patients; 24-hour standard would meaningfully accelerate access.
