## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter submits a detailed product specification and intellectual property declaration for the COB-PA Integrity Gate (CPIG), a proposed FHIR-native prior authorization module that would validate Medicare Secondary Payer/coordination-of-benefits payer order before clinical criteria review begins. The comment recommends that CMS recognize and incorporate a payer-order validation precondition, episode-level MSP tagging, and recovery risk scoring into the electronic PA framework created by CMS-0057-F and extended by CMS-2026-1255.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR Part 411; 42 CFR 411.104; 42 CFR 411.108; 42 CFR 411.40 through 411.52; 42 CFR 411.162; CMS-0057-F; CMS-0062-P; CMS-2026-1255.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR R4, Clinical Quality Language (CQL), Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS implementation guides, FHIR PlanDefinition, Patient Access API, Provider Access API, X12, NCPDP 837.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Add coordination-of-benefits validation before PA clinical review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues current PA systems assume the receiving payer is legally primary, creating MSP recovery risk, provider rework, and patient disruption when the assumption is wrong.
- **Specific request:** CMS should incorporate or recognize CPIG-style payer-order confirmation as a mandatory precondition before clinical PA criteria are evaluated.

**Topic 2: Use FHIR PlanDefinition and CQL for a mandatory gate**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The comment states FHIR PlanDefinition precondition logic can make payer-order validation structural rather than advisory, using the same FHIR ecosystem CMS is requiring for prior authorization APIs.
- **Specific request:** CMS should support FHIR-native CQL precondition logic for MSP payer-order validation within authorization workflows.

**Topic 3: Extend PA API policy beyond drug PA to episode coordination**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The specification ties CPIG to CMS-0062-P's expansion of electronic PA to drugs but focuses on all authorization episodes where payer-order errors can affect payment responsibility.
- **Specific request:** CMS should clarify how drug PA API requirements interact with coordination-of-benefits and MSP validation for medical episodes and provider-administered drugs.

**Topic 4: HIPAA and industry-wide PA standards implications**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The comment presents CPIG as an open, FHIR-native standard that could operate across covered entities if FHIR prior authorization transactions become HIPAA standards.
- **Specific request:** CMS/HHS should consider payer-order validation and episode-level COB tagging as part of the broader FHIR prior authorization standards framework.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** The comment implicitly supports making relevant FHIR implementation structures mandatory by proposing a FHIR PlanDefinition/CQL precondition gate rather than a voluntary advisory check.
- **S12:** The comment identifies a technical challenge in current PA workflows: existing PA vendors and Da Vinci guides do not validate MSP payer order before clinical evaluation.
- **S15:** The comment does not directly address medical versus pharmacy benefit distinction, but its proposed coordination gate is most developed for medical authorization episodes and provider-administered care.
- **S44:** The comment supports FHIR as an appropriate technical substrate for prior authorization standards, specifically citing FHIR R4, PlanDefinition, CQL, and Da Vinci IGs.
- **S50:** The comment argues that FHIR PlanDefinition and CQL have become mature enough under the CMS-0057-F framework to support a COB precondition gate.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds generally to broader care coordination RFIs by proposing episode-level MSP scenario tagging and change logs that persist across an authorization episode.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Add Medicare Secondary Payer payer-order validation before clinical PA criteria evaluation.
2. Implement the validation as a FHIR PlanDefinition/CQL precondition gate.
3. Use Patient Access and Provider Access APIs to query active coverage, payer identity, and related COB indicators.
4. Assign persistent episode-level MSP scenario tags and maintain a COB change log.
5. Generate a recovery risk score at the point of authorization and route high-risk cases for COB/compliance review.
6. Recognize CPIG or a CPIG-like open standard as a gap-filling module within CMS's electronic prior authorization framework.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Historical separation of COB and UM departments, separate vendor stacks, PA vendors lacking COB competency, payment integrity vendors operating downstream at claims, and absence of standardized FHIR-native MSP logic.

SECTION 1: WHAT IS CPIG 
1.1 The One-Paragraph Answer 
The COB-PA Integrity Gate (CPIG) is Module 2 of Episode Intelligence Authorization (EIA). It is the first prior 
authorization standard to embed Medicare Secondary Payer payer-order validation as a mandatory FHIR-native 
precondition gate before clinical criteria evaluation begins in any authorization episode. Before a single clinical 
criterion is evaluated, CPIG asks and answers a question no existing PA vendor asks: is the payer receiving this 
authorization request the legally correct primary payer under federal law for this patient at this moment? If the 
answer is uncertain, CPIG holds the clinical gate closed until it is confirmed. If the answer is no, CPIG routes the 
episode to the correct payer. If the answer is yes, CPIG documents the confirmation, tags the episode, generates 
a recovery risk score, and releases the clinical evaluation. The clinical gate and the coordination gate, which have 
never been integrated in the history of prior authorization, are integrated in CPIG.
