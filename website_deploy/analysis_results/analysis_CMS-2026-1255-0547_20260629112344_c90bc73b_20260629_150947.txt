# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Osteopathic Association and 16 additional osteopathic physician organizations
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** The undersigned organizations represent more than 207,000 osteopathic physicians (DOs) and medical students. Osteopathic physicians are described as trained in a patient-centered, whole-person approach to care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The osteopathic physician organizations strongly support CMS's proposal to extend electronic prior authorization requirements to drugs and require standards-based APIs that support PA within physicians' EMR workflows. They ask CMS to strengthen the rule by requiring specialist review and evidence-based criteria for denials, more granular PA metrics by specialty/therapeutic area/drug/service, shorter non-drug PA timeframes, real-time decision capability, limits on low-value PA/step therapy, and gold card relief for high-performing physicians.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Standards-based APIs; implementation guides; EMR workflows. No specific IG names are identified in the comment.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Standards-Based APIs for Electronic Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Physicians continue to face interoperability challenges exchanging information with health plans, and electronic standards can allow PA to be performed for drugs and services within physicians' EMR workflows.
- **Specific request:** Finalize proposals requiring each impacted payer to implement and maintain standards-based APIs supporting critical PA functions.

**Topic 2: Denial Reasons and Specialist Review**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Denial reasons alone may not reduce inappropriate denials unless adverse decisions are clinically grounded and reviewed by relevant experts.
- **Specific request:** Finalize denial-reason provisions, require potential adverse decisions to be reviewed by relevant specialists before denial, and require PA requirements to be based on publicly available evidence or clinical guidelines.

**Topic 3: Prior Authorization Metrics Granularity**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Aggregate issuer- or contract-level metrics have limited value for beneficiaries shopping for plans and providers contracting with payers.
- **Specific request:** Require PA metrics reporting by specialty, therapeutic area, drug, or individual service to make plan performance transparent and identify areas where PA may be inappropriate.

**Topic 4: Prior Authorization Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Shorter timeframes reduce unnecessary care delays and support timely access to needed care.
- **Specific request:** Keep proposed drug timeframes of 72 hours standard and 24 hours urgent where applicable, shorten non-drug item/service timeframes to 72 hours standard and 24 hours urgent, and consider requiring plans to be capable of real-time PA decisions.

**Topic 5: Comprehensive PA and Step Therapy Reform**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA reforms alone will not eliminate low-value PA policies or step therapy requirements that create burden with limited patient safety or utilization benefit.
- **Specific request:** Limit PA and step therapy to drugs/services truly of concern for patient safety; require specialty experts to develop evidence-based PA policies; require specialist peer review; and create carefully designed gold card programs for physicians with strong documentation and PA approval records.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1 / S18:** Supports requiring implementation guides and standards-based APIs, emphasizing that required standards are needed for seamless data sharing and PA within EMR workflows.
- **S12 / S21:** Supports extending PA requirements to drugs and views the proposal as responsive to stakeholder concerns about drug PA barriers.
- **S16-S17:** Supports denial-reason requirements and recommends additional protections: specialist review before adverse decisions and publicly available evidence-based criteria.
- **S29-S35:** Supports annual PA metrics but requests more granular reporting by specialty, therapeutic area, drug, or service rather than issuer/contract-level aggregates.
- **S43:** Requests non-drug PA decision timeframes be shortened to 72 hours standard and 24 hours urgent, and asks CMS to consider real-time decision capability.
- **S66-S82:** Responds generally to broader PA/step therapy reform by recommending limits on low-value PA and step therapy, specialty-developed criteria, and gold card programs.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize standards-based API requirements for impacted payers to support critical PA functions.
2. Finalize denial-reason communication provisions for denied drug PA requests.
3. Require potential adverse decisions to be reviewed by relevant specialists before denials are issued.
4. Require PA requirements for items and services to be based on publicly available evidence or clinical guidelines.
5. Require PA metrics reporting by specialty, therapeutic area, drug, or individual service.
6. Shorten non-drug PA timeframes to 72 hours for standard requests and 24 hours for urgent requests.
7. Consider requiring plans to be capable of issuing real-time PA decisions.
8. Limit PA and step therapy to drugs and services truly of concern for patient safety.
9. Prevent low-value PA policies that create burden with limited patient-safety or appropriate-utilization benefit.
10. Ensure PA policies are developed by relevant specialty experts using publicly available evidence-based criteria.
11. Require adverse decisions or peer reviews to be conducted by relevant specialists.
12. Establish carefully designed gold card programs for physicians with strong documentation and PA approval records.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Physician administrative burden, care delays, serious adverse events, insufficiently granular transparency, inappropriate denials, low-value PA/step therapy, and inadequate non-drug timeframes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed care, serious adverse events, reduced physician time with patients, diminished trust in plan performance, and unnecessary administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Persistent interoperability challenges with health plans despite existing API requirements; lack of specificity in aggregate metrics; payer PA policies not tied to evidence or specialty expertise.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** The organizations represent more than 207,000 osteopathic physicians and medical students.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** Medicare Advantage already requires relevant specialist review for PA denials for items and services; gold card programs are cited as a model for reducing burden for physicians with strong approval/documentation records.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Osteopathic physicians and medical students, patients, physician practices, impacted payers, health plans, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Reducing low-value PA, improving real-time decisioning, and adopting gold card programs could reduce physician practice time and cost burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment supports standards-based APIs and EMR workflow integration but does not provide detailed technical implementation analysis.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment links PA delays to serious adverse events and urges faster decisions and clinically appropriate denial review.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with modifications
- **Tone:** Professional, supportive, reform-oriented
- **Priority level:** High
