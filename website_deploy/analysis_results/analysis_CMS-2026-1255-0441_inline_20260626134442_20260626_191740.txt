## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Michelle DeAngelo, The Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group; Healthcare Provider/Professional
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Patient advocate; healthcare professional with 19 years of experience; daughter of a person who died from cholangiocarcinoma in 2003; representative of The Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to strengthen prior authorization reforms so medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing is not delayed, denied, or invalidated by administrative deadlines. The comment focuses on cancer care, especially cholangiocarcinoma and other serious time-sensitive conditions, and argues that authorization policies should reflect current evidence, real-world lab workflows, and expedited decision-making needs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; indirectly relevant to GENERAL/RFIs S66-S82 concerning broader prior authorization domains such as laboratory testing, step therapy, and care continuity.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 FR 19890; Document No. 2026-07205; Section III.E of CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Timely Access to Cancer Laboratory and Biomarker Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that biomarker and specialty laboratory testing inform treatment selection, treatment modification, disease monitoring, and clinical trial eligibility. Delays can cause patients to lose critical treatment time.
- **Specific request:** CMS should strengthen prior authorization reforms to prevent delays and denials of medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing for patients with serious, time-sensitive conditions.

**Topic 2: Outdated Criteria and Step Therapy Restrictions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that coverage and authorization policies should not rely on assumptions about age or family history that may no longer match current evidence, especially as rare cancers are increasingly diagnosed in younger adults.
- **Specific request:** CMS should clarify that cancer patients must have timely access to medically necessary testing without delays, step therapy requirements, or denials based on outdated eligibility criteria.

**Topic 3: Specimen Collection Deadlines and Laboratory Workflows**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization policies tied to specimen collection dates can cause valid specimens to be delayed, rejected, or discarded even when patients, clinicians, and laboratories acted appropriately.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require review standards that reflect real-world laboratory workflows and should prevent insurer processing delays or clerical deadlines from overriding medical necessity.

**Topic 4: Patient Financial and Equity Impacts**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeat testing and delays can create out-of-network charges, lost wages, travel expenses, and childcare costs, with greater impact on rural, low-income, medically underserved, immunocompromised, and severely ill patients.
- **Specific request:** CMS should adopt safeguards that protect patients from avoidable repeat testing and preserve timely access to cancer care.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

No specific numbered solicitation is explicitly answered. The comment is most relevant to GENERAL/RFI solicitations S66-S82, particularly those concerning broader prior authorization use cases, step therapy, laboratory/DME-related access, and real-world workflow barriers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Strengthen prior authorization reforms to prevent delays and denials of medically necessary laboratory and biomarker testing.
2. Clarify that cancer patients must receive timely access to medically necessary testing without step therapy restrictions or outdated eligibility criteria.
3. Prohibit administrative timing rules that cause valid specimens to be rejected, discarded, or repeated when clinical parties acted appropriately.
4. Require expedited review standards for serious, time-sensitive conditions.
5. Align prior authorization requirements with real-world laboratory workflows and documented medical necessity.
6. Protect patients from repeat blood draws, missed treatment opportunities, and avoidable financial burdens.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delayed or denied access to cancer laboratory and biomarker testing; outdated eligibility criteria; specimen rejection tied to authorization timing; administrative deadlines overriding medical need.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed treatment, delayed therapy changes, missed clinical trial eligibility, discarded specimens, repeat blood draws, out-of-network charges, lost wages, travel costs, childcare costs, and avoidable clinical risk.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Insurer processing delays; prior authorization rules tied to specimen collection date; rigid administrative timelines that do not match lab workflows.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Recent real-world research found biomarker testing rates among patients with advanced cancer remained only 35% despite guideline recommendations.
- **Studies or research referenced:** The commenter refers generally to recent real-world research on biomarker testing rates but does not provide a specific citation.
- **Examples provided:** Cholangiocarcinoma; rare cancers increasingly diagnosed in younger adults; valid specimens discarded due to authorization timing; repeat blood draws; financial burdens from repeat testing.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Cancer patients, rare cancer patients, rural and low-income patients, medically underserved patients, immunocompromised and severely ill patients, clinicians, laboratories, and health plans.
