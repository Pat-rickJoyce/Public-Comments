## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Auth57 Labs (Automatic Delight LLC)
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Operates a multi-payer prior authorization evidence layer across Medicaid jurisdictions, Medicare, and commercial carriers; exposes FHIR Da Vinci CRD/DTR/PAS, public API, and consumer tools; identifies itself as an HL7/Da Vinci implementer.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Auth57 Labs strongly supports extending electronic prior authorization to drugs and supports use of FHIR CRD/DTR/PAS for medical-benefit drugs. The commenter asks CMS to preserve public referenceability, harmonize FHIR and NCPDP drug paths, require machine-readable metrics and denial/appeal data, add effective dates and change logs, and phase implementation with support for small practices.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT as reflected in PA-DRUG-MEDICAL/PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P and CMS-0057-F are mentioned, but no CFR citations are cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS, NCPDP, HCPCS Level II, RxNorm.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: FHIR Path for Medical-Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Using CRD/DTR/PAS for physician-administered drugs aligns the drug proposal with CMS-0057-F and permits a common standards stack for procedures and Part B drugs.
- **Specific request:** Require code-to-criteria mappings for HCPCS-coded medical drugs to remain publicly referenceable and not blocked by proprietary code licensing.

**Topic 2: Harmonization of Medical and Pharmacy Drug Paths**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Drugs may be billable under either the medical or pharmacy benefit depending on site of care, so separate FHIR and NCPDP paths could create gaps, duplicated burden, or non-comparable decision data.
- **Specific request:** Require deterministic benefit/path routing and a shared minimum data set across FHIR and NCPDP transactions.

**Topic 3: Machine-Readable Drug Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Aggregate or brand-name-only reports and free-text PDFs are not comparable or usable at scale.
- **Specific request:** Require public, machine-readable metrics at drug-class and where feasible drug level, keyed to RxNorm for pharmacy drugs and HCPCS for medical drugs.

**Topic 4: Denial Reasons and Appeal Pathways**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients often do not appeal denials despite high reversal rates because appeal instructions are not clear or actionable at the point of denial.
- **Specific request:** Include specific denial reason plus appeal path, deadline, and forum in a machine-readable PAS response.

**Topic 5: Effective Dates and Change History**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA requirements change frequently and sometimes silently, causing downstream tools and users to rely on stale requirements.
- **Specific request:** Require the drug PA requirements interface to expose machine-readable effective dates and structured change history for each requirement.

**Topic 6: Phased Timeline and Small Practice Support**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Small and independent practices may lack ePA-capable systems and could be disadvantaged by implementation.
- **Specific request:** Adopt a firm but phased rollout and provide a no-cost conformant reference path, such as a hosted DTR questionnaire renderer.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports making the Da Vinci CRD/DTR/PAS FHIR stack required for medical-benefit drugs rather than merely recommended.
- **S12:** Addresses technical implementation by urging public, code-based criteria mappings for HCPCS-coded medical drugs.
- **S15:** Directly addresses the medical versus pharmacy benefit distinction and asks CMS to require deterministic routing and shared data across FHIR and NCPDP paths.

- **S17:** Supports structured, machine-readable denial and appeal data in the PAS response.

- **S22:** Indirectly addresses implementation burden for pharmacy and medical paths by requesting shared data and harmonization.

- **S30:** Not directly addressed.
- **S37:** Addresses implementation challenges involving stale PA requirements, version currency, and small-practice readiness.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Keep FHIR medical-drug requirements publicly referenceable through public HCPCS-based code-to-criteria mappings.
2. Harmonize FHIR and NCPDP drug PA pathways with deterministic routing and a shared minimum data set.
3. Require machine-readable public drug PA metrics at drug-class and, where feasible, drug granularity.
4. Key pharmacy metrics to RxNorm and medical-benefit drug metrics to HCPCS.
5. Include denial reason, appeal path, deadline, and forum in machine-readable PAS responses.
6. Require machine-readable effective dates and structured change logs for PA requirements.
7. Use a phased implementation timeline with a no-cost conformant reference path for small and independent practices.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Fragmentation between FHIR and NCPDP paths, lack of machine-readable public data, stale PA requirements, and small-practice readiness.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Coverage gaps, duplicated provider burden, patient abandonment of successful appeals, use of outdated PA requirements, and widening disparity between large systems and small practices.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Proprietary code licensing, non-machine-readable PDFs, frequent payer requirement changes, and lack of ePA-capable systems in small practices.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** The commenter states that roughly four in five PA denials are overturned on appeal, while only about one in ten are appealed.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Infused biologics may fall under either benefit depending on site of care; a national payer's PA lists are observed to change monthly without machine-readable notice.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, prescribers, small and independent practices, payers, technology vendors, researchers, and oversight bodies.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced burden and better reuse of standards may lower costs, while unsupported implementation could strain small practices.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter presents the recommendations as feasible because it already works with FHIR, public APIs, standard code systems, and payer PA disclosures.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved appeal visibility, current PA requirements, and reduced fragmentation could reduce delays and prevent avoidable denials.

Public Comment on CMS-0062-P (Drug Electronic Prior Authorization 
Expansion)
To:   Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services
Re:   CMS-0062-P - Proposed expansion of electronic prior authorization to 
drugs
From: Auth57 Labs (Automatic Delight LLC) - hello@auth57labs.com
Date: June 14, 2026
Auth57 Labs operates a multi-payer prior-authorization evidence layer 
spanning all 51 Medicaid jurisdictions, Medicare, and major commercial 
carriers, exposed through FHIR (Da Vinci CRD/DTR/PAS), a public API, and 
consumer-facing decision tools. We are an HL7/Da Vinci implementer. In the 
course of normalizing payer disclosures and ingesting payers' own 
published prior-authorization (PA) requirement documents, we have a 
granular, cross-payer view of how PA actually behaves in the field - and 
we write in strong support of extending electronic prior authorization 
(ePA) to drugs, with six recommendations to make the rule work for 
patients, prescribers, and small practices.
1.
Keep the FHIR medical-drug path publicly referenceable.
We support requiring the CRD/DTR/PAS FHIR stack for medical-benefit 
(physician-administered) drugs, identified by HCPCS Level II codes. This 
aligns the drug rule with CMS-0057-F and lets one standards stack serve 
both procedures and Part B drugs. Because HCPCS Level II codes are public 
domain, this path can be implemented and surfaced transparently. CMS 
should preserve this by requiring code-to-criteria mappings to remain 
publicly referenceable - proprietary code-licensing must not become a 
barrier between a patient and the rule that requires this transaction to 
exist for their benefit.
2.
Harmonize the medical (FHIR) and pharmacy (NCPDP) paths.
The proposal routes pharmacy-benefit drugs through NCPDP and medical-
benefit drugs through FHIR. Many drugs are billable under either benefit 
depending on site of care (infused biologics are the clearest case). We 
urge CMS to require deterministic benefit/path routing and a shared 
minimum data set across the two standards, so that a drug billable either 
way cannot fall into a coverage gap or double the provider's burden, and 
so that approval, denial, and decision-timeframe data remain comparable 
across paths. Without explicit harmonization, the benefit split will 
reproduce the fragmentation the rule is meant to reduce....
