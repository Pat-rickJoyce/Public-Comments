# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Patients Rising; signed by Terry Wilcox, Co-Founder & CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Patients Rising describes itself as a national patient advocacy organization with more than ten years working with patients and caregivers across serious and chronic illnesses, including oncology, rare disease, autoimmune, neurological, and immunological conditions.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Patients Rising supports the proposed rule's direction and urges CMS to prioritize patient time, treatment access, and denial transparency. It recommends electronic PA across medical and pharmacy benefits, 24-hour drug determinations across markets, patient-facing status and denial information, public plan-level metrics, limits on step therapy, and reforms to laboratory-test prior authorization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No specific CFR sections cited. The comment references CMS-0062-P, Docket CMS-2026-1255, and the April 14, 2026 Federal Register publication.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; Prior Authorization API concepts; plan-level reporting metrics. No specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is discussed by name.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA across medical and pharmacy benefits**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Real-time coverage-rule checks, request submission, and status tracking can reduce treatment abandonment and delays.
- **Specific request:** Require electronic PA across both benefits and ensure patients, not only providers, can see request status and outcomes.

**Topic 2: Faster drug determinations with oversight**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients experience PA delay as days or weeks without treatment, but faster deadlines must not create faster automatic denials.
- **Specific request:** Require 24-hour determinations across all markets and pair expedited timelines with CMS oversight against inappropriate denials.

**Topic 3: Plain-language denial transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients cannot appeal denials they do not understand, and low appeal rates suggest many patients absorb denials without challenging them.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed, plain-language denial justifications for every denial, including initial denials, delivered to both provider and patient simultaneously.

**Topic 4: Public prior authorization metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Interface-use metrics do not show whether patients get care; denial rates, appeals, outcomes, and turnaround times are more meaningful for patients.
- **Specific request:** Require public, plan-level PA metrics, including Part D plans, broken out by brand/generic, therapeutic area, initial denials, step therapy, and patient-experience data in an annual CMS report.

**Topic 5: Step therapy limits**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose expansion / Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can force patients to fail ineffective treatment and lose ground, especially when switching plans.
- **Specific request:** Do not permit MA expansion of step therapy for Part B drugs; collect and publish data; honor completed steps across plans; disclose step therapy openly; maintain strong exceptions and appeals.

**Topic 6: Laboratory testing prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Timing-based denials and added laboratory benefit manager layers can delay diagnosis and targeted treatment, especially in oncology and rare disease.
- **Specific request:** End timing-based lab-test denials, allow test requisition forms as medical-necessity documentation, examine laboratory benefit managers, and reform lab PA with urgency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14:** Supports electronic PA across both medical and pharmacy benefit drugs, though the comment does not focus on implementation mechanics.
- **S16:** Calls for detailed, plain-language denial justifications for every denial, including initial denials, delivered to patients and providers.
- **S20/S43:** Supports 24-hour determinations for all drugs across markets and warns that expedited timelines need oversight to avoid auto-denials.
- **S29:** Recommends metrics beyond API usage, including denial rates, appeal outcomes, turnaround times, step therapy, and patient-experience data.
- **S34/S35:** Recommends annual public CMS reporting and public plan-level metrics, including Part D plans.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on step therapy and laboratory testing, recommending restrictions, transparency, carryover of completed steps, and lab PA reforms.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require electronic prior authorization across medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Give patients access to PA request status and outcomes affecting their care.
3. Require 24-hour drug PA determinations across all markets.
4. Pair faster timelines with CMS oversight to prevent inappropriate auto-denials.
5. Require detailed, plain-language denial justifications for every denial, delivered to both patient and provider.
6. Publish plan-level PA metrics, including Part D, by brand/generic, therapeutic area, initial denials, step therapy, appeals, turnaround times, and patient experience.
7. Do not expand MA step therapy for Part B drugs; require transparency, data reporting, completed-step carryover, and robust exceptions/appeals.
8. End timing-based laboratory-test denials and allow test requisitions as medical-necessity documentation.
9. Examine laboratory benefit managers and pursue lab PA reform with the same urgency as drug PA reform.
10. Treat qualitative and distributional patient burdens as part of the rule's justification.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays, denials, low appeal rates, opaque denial reasons, step therapy, lab testing delays, and patient burdens not captured in cost tables.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment abandonment, delayed diagnoses, repeat testing, worse outcomes, serious adverse events, financial/job burdens on families, and loss of dignity and peace of mind.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of patient-facing status visibility, insufficient metrics, hidden step therapy requirements, timing-based lab denials, laboratory benefit manager involvement, and weak exceptions/appeals transparency.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 2024 MA data: nearly 53 million PA determinations, 4.1 million denials, 7.7 percent denial rate, 11.5 percent appeal rate, and 80.7 percent partial or full overturn rate among appealed denials. AMA survey: 95 percent of physicians said PA delays care, 79 percent said patients abandon treatment, 92 percent said PA negatively affects outcomes, and 26 percent reported serious adverse events.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF analysis of 2024 Medicare Advantage PA determinations; American Medical Association 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey.
- **Examples provided:** Patient needing biomarker testing whose test was denied because authorization was pending when blood was drawn, requiring a second blood draw and delaying treatment selection.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with serious and chronic illnesses, caregivers, providers, payers, Part D plans, laboratories, laboratory benefit managers, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** The comment emphasizes non-spreadsheet burdens such as unpaid time off, repeat visits, repeat draws, and emotional costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment assumes electronic PA and reporting can support patient-facing status, denial explanations, and plan-level metrics.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly emphasizes that faster, more transparent PA can reduce abandonment, delays, repeated testing, and poor outcomes.
