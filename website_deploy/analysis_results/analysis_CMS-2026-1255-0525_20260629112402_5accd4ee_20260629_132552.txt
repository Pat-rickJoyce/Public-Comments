# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Borinquen Health Care Center, Inc.; signed by Paul Carl Velez, President and CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Federally Qualified Health Center serving medically underserved Miami-Dade County communities since 1972; Level 3 NCQA Patient-Centered Medical Home; accredited by AAAHC and CARF; provides primary care, dental, behavioral health, HIV, pharmacy, podiatry, OB/GYN, and related services.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Borinquen strongly supports CMS's interoperability and drug ePA goals because CHC patients are harmed by prior authorization delays and fragmented payer workflows. It requests realistic timelines, vendor accountability, safety-net funding, cybersecurity support, medical/pharmacy benefit clarity, 340B-aware implementation guidance, proactive PA expiration alerts, automatic approval for missed deadlines, centralized machine-readable endpoint publication, richer API usage metrics, formulary data retention, transition support from X12 to FHIR, CDex guardrails, gold-carding, and CMS-ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0053-F; 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); HTI-4, 90 FR 37130; HTI-5 proposed rule; National Standards Group enforcement discretion dated February 28, 2024.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; HL7 Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS v2.2; CDex; X12 278; X12 6020 claims attachments; NCPDP standards generally through pharmacy ePA context; Provider Access, Patient Access, Payer-to-Payer, PA, and Provider Directory APIs; ONC certification criteria.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API and interoperability implementation for CHCs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs can reduce duplicative documentation and improve PA transparency, but CHCs have small IT teams, limited financial flexibility, dependence on vendors, and substantial cybersecurity exposure.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with ONC, validate readiness before enforcement, set uniform minimum requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHPs, clarify providers will not be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse/EHR failures outside their control, and provide technical assistance, workforce support, cybersecurity support, and dedicated health IT funding.

**Topic 2: Electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHC patients depend on uninterrupted medication access for diabetes, hypertension, behavioral health, HIV, PrEP, antiretroviral therapy, and substance use disorder treatment. However, dual medical/pharmacy workflows and 340B operations make implementation complex.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension; clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to the alternate pathway; coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs on 340B implementation; provide funding and technical assistance.

**Topic 3: PA expiration alerts and broader PA reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA can streamline workflows but may not reduce burden if PA volumes remain high or if reauthorization lapses interrupt treatment.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration notifications and pair ePA mandates with PA reduction tools, including gold-carding for prescription drugs and sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories.

**Topic 4: Communication, denials, and timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Medically underserved patients face worsened outcomes when PA delays interrupt medications or referrals. Specific denial reasons help CHCs correct submissions, appeal, and resubmit without repeated back-and-forth.
- **Specific request:** Maintain 24-hour drug PA timeframes, 7-day standard item/service timeframes, and 72-hour expedited timeframes; require actionable denial reasons; publish PA metrics centrally; add granular burden metrics; and automatically approve PA requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 5: API endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHC staff now must locate payer endpoint information separately across multiple payer websites, which diverts scarce IT resources and delays integration.
- **Specific request:** Publish endpoint information centrally on CMS's website as machine-readable files; retain the proposed reporting timing, one-week change updates, and annual verification; prioritize ease of access for safety-net and rural providers.

**Topic 6: API usage metrics and PA information retention**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Program- and plan-level reporting will help identify gaps for Medicaid managed care patients, and richer metrics are needed to evaluate whether APIs actually work in clinical workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require plan/program disaggregation, denial rates, extension timeframes, adjudicated appeals, successful API request rates, rejected requests, authorization failures, malformed queries, calls not received, structured vs. unstructured responses, workflow impact, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance costs.

**Topic 7: Drug formulary information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose removal / Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer websites are not reliable real-time sources for formulary status, PBMs can change exclusions rapidly, and CHC providers lack staff capacity for manual lookups across payer sites.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 8: Federal matching funds and safety-net support**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CHCs operate on thin margins, incur training and implementation costs, and often have limited cash reserves.
- **Specific request:** Prioritize safety-net providers for available Medicaid/CHIP federal matching funds or related support for training, implementation, staff, clinical training, patient education, and interoperability access.

**Topic 9: HIPAA PA standards and X12-to-FHIR transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Moving from X12 to FHIR can improve interoperability, but CHCs and clearinghouses may need to support X12 6020 and FHIR/CDex simultaneously, creating a dual-standard burden.
- **Specific request:** Provide transition support for safety-net providers and extend the February 28, 2024 National Standards Group enforcement discretion allowing FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute during the CMS-0062-P transition period.

**Topic 10: CDex, gold-carding, and ONC harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CDex can support PA attachments but could enable iterative payer data requests. CMS-0062-P timelines may precede certified provider-facing technology availability under HTI-4/HTI-5.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to specify all documentation in the initial CDex request, limit solicited attachments to two requests before a decision, set response timeframes after attachments, formally recommend broad gold-carding, issue joint CMS-ONC guidance, and publish a harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S18:** Supports mandatory interoperability standards in concept but requests implementation safeguards, readiness validation, and vendor accountability to avoid indirect provider burden.
- **S2/S3:** Requests synchronized IG and certification timelines and CMS-ONC harmonization so payer deadlines do not precede certified provider-facing technology.
- **S12:** Identifies implementation challenges for drug ePA, including vendor readiness, CHC IT capacity, cybersecurity exposure, EHR/PMS integration, 340B workflows, and payer inconsistency.
- **S14/S19/S22/S25:** Supports pharmacy benefit ePA but emphasizes operational complexity for CHCs, 340B contract pharmacies, PMS/EHR interoperability, and NCPDP/pharmacy ecosystem readiness.
- **S15:** Responds directly by requesting benefit classification data through Provider Access APIs and clarification on drugs covered under both medical and pharmacy benefits.
- **S16:** Supports specific drug PA denial reasons that identify why a request was denied, missing or insufficient information, and actionable resubmission or appeal information.
- **S17:** Requests denial reporting that captures incomplete, inaccurate, or unclear denial notices and supports more structured accountability.
- **S20:** Supports 24-hour decisions for covered outpatient drugs and more consistent expedited timelines.
- **S21:** Supports October 1, 2027 drug PA implementation if paired with vendor readiness, testing, funding, cybersecurity support, and provider protections.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports centralized CMS publication of payer API endpoints, machine-readable files, one-week updates after changes, annual verification, and easy access for safety-net providers.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports expanded API usage metrics and recommends metrics on API reliability, authorization failures, malformed queries, structured data, workflow impact, query volume, and maintenance costs.
- **S40/S41/S42:** Addresses QHP timelines indirectly by supporting QHP drug PA notice requirements and urging safety-net implementation support.
- **S43:** Supports alignment of PA timeframes across programs and automatic approvals if payers miss deadlines.
- **S44/S46/S47/S48/S49/S50/S51/S53/S54/S55:** Supports moving from X12 to FHIR but requests transition support, enforcement discretion, CDex guardrails, and recognition of small safety-net provider burden.
- **S60/S61/S62/S64/S65:** Requests CMS-ONC harmonization, HTI-5 alignment, readiness assessment, and avoidance of certification gaps that would undermine ePA.
- **S66-S82:** Addresses broader implementation, cybersecurity, telehealth, AI, and patient access implications for CHCs, though not in detail for each RFI.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify providers will not be penalized for vendor, clearinghouse, or EHR failures outside their control.
2. Coordinate CMS timelines with ONC certification and require readiness validation before enforcement.
3. Establish uniform minimum implementation requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.
4. Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity support, IT workforce support, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.
5. Require benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs using CRD Coverage Information.
6. Clarify whether a PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to alternate medical/pharmacy payment pathways.
7. Coordinate with OPA on 340B-related implementation and support.
8. Mandate proactive FHIR-based PA expiration alerts.
9. Pair ePA with PA reduction requirements, including gold-carding and sunset provisions for high-approval categories.
10. Require actionable drug PA denial reasons and centralized PA metric publication.
11. Add granular PA reporting for denial rates by service type or medication category, first-pass approval, overturned denials from defective notices, and data quality.
12. Automatically approve PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
13. Publish API endpoints centrally as machine-readable files and require timely updates.
14. Expand API usage metrics to include functionality, usability, reliability, and cost measures.
15. Retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
16. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds or implementation support.
17. Extend NSG enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute during transition.
18. Add CDex guardrails: all documentation in the initial request, no drip-feed requests, two-request maximum before decision, and payer response timeframes.
19. Recommend broad payer gold-carding for ePA and extend privileges across all items and services for eligible providers.
20. Publish a CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before finalizing CMS-0062-P.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Safety-net readiness, vendor accountability, cybersecurity exposure, dual medical/pharmacy workflows, 340B operational complexity, inadequate PA reform, unclear denial information, payer deadline noncompliance, endpoint discovery burden, removal of formulary data, dual X12/FHIR standards, CDex drip-feed requests, certification timing gaps, and unfunded implementation costs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Medication interruptions, chronic disease deterioration, relapse risk for behavioral health/substance use disorder patients, delayed referrals and testing, increased CHC staff burden, inappropriate denials, abandoned treatment, cybersecurity risks, reduced prescribing quality, and inequitable impact on low-income, Medicaid, uninsured, homeless, agricultural worker, Hispanic, and Caribbean patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Small IT teams, limited budgets, vendor dependence, cybersecurity staffing gaps, PMS/EHR interoperability limits, terminology and value-set misalignment, multiple payer websites, hard-coded endpoint disruptions, limited certified module availability, and thin CHC operating margins.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA survey finding 94 percent of physicians report PA delays patient care; average PA wait about three days, with nearly one-third waiting longer than one week; MA organizations made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024, including more than four million full or partial denials; CHCs serve more than 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, over 6 million uninsured individuals, 20.5 million low-income individuals, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and over 1.5 million homeless individuals; more than 90 percent of CHC patients have incomes below 200 percent FPL; CHCs serve 3.5 million patients with diabetes, 6.2 million with hypertension, 10 million overweight or obese patients, and 3.2 million with depression or mood disorders; more than 65 percent of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy arrangements; proactive outreach before PA expiration achieves positive outcomes in 87 percent of cases versus 25 percent without notification; nearly half of CHCs operate with fewer than 90 days cash on hand and one in four report operating margins of negative five percent.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA, MGMA, CHC demographic data, pharmacy interoperability stakeholder evidence, studies on pharmacy access to EHR data, and peer-reviewed evidence that ePA can increase PA volumes and time without broader PA reform.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine billed under pharmacy or medical benefit depending on dispensing/administration; Borinquen medications such as HIV antiretrovirals, PrEP, injectable buprenorphine, insulin, and diabetes medications; Borinquen's 340B pharmacy, home delivery, e-prescribing, refill, and MTM services; CHC staff managing PAs across primary care referrals, behavioral health, specialty medications, and imaging.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs/FQHCs, Borinquen patients, low-income and underserved communities, Medicaid and uninsured patients, CHC IT staff, providers, pharmacists, contract pharmacies, TPAs, EHR and PMS vendors, payers, CMS, ONC, OPA, clearinghouses, app developers, and safety-net providers.
- **Economic implications:** Implementation requires vendor upgrades, IT integration, cybersecurity investment, training, clinical workflow redesign, patient education, and staff time that CHCs may not be able to absorb without funding or matching support.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS aligns certification timelines, validates readiness, standardizes payer implementation, provides machine-readable endpoints, maintains formulary data, supports 340B/EHR/PMS integration, and supplies CHC technical assistance.
- **Patient care impact:** The comment predicts improved medication adherence, fewer treatment interruptions, better chronic disease management, faster referrals, fewer avoidable hospitalizations, and more equitable access if implementation is supported and payer accountability is strong.
