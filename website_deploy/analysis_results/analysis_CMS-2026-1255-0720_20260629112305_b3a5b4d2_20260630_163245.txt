## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Bradley Shogren, Founder, Iota Intel LLC
- **Stakeholder type:** Individual / Technology and compliance professional
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Nearly two decades of experience in regulatory affairs, healthcare operations, and compliance at a major health insurance and healthcare services organization; writes in individual capacity and identifies personal experience as a long-term cancer survivor and caregiver.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bradley Shogren supports CMS's objective to make drug prior authorization faster, more transparent, and interoperable, but urges the agency to ensure the rule improves patient outcomes rather than merely digitizing existing processes. He recommends actionable and machine-readable denial reasons, outcome-oriented API and PA metrics, conformance testing and sandboxes, production oversight, accountability for automation, reductions in PA volume, continuity protections, and disciplined standards governance.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL (payer API implementation, automation accountability, PA volume reduction, gold-carding, continuity of care)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Docket No. CMS-2026-1255; section II.C.2; section II.F.2.c; section III.C; 2024 CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization final rule CMS-0057-F; no CFR sections cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Applicable electronic transactions; FHIR-based APIs; implementation guides; code sets; federally adopted health IT standards; ONC health information technology framework; HL7 State of FHIR survey; NCPDP is not named directly.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Drug PA modernization must serve patient outcomes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports faster, more transparent, interoperable drug PA and recognizes legitimate utilization management purposes, but warns against simply migrating existing friction into electronic form.
- **Specific request:** Finalize principal transparency and interoperability provisions while strengthening measures that ensure timely, understandable, clinically actionable results for patients and providers.

**Topic 2: Actionable and machine-readable denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Generic denial phrases or unexplained proprietary codes do not help providers or patients correct issues, resubmit, or appeal.
- **Specific request:** Require denial responses to identify the precise unmet criterion, policy version or effective date, denial category, missing information, covered alternatives or step therapy requirements, and appeal/resubmission instructions in both human-readable and structured machine-readable form.

**Topic 3: Patient-facing adverse decision explanations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients often forgo potentially successful appeals when the basis for denial and challenge process are unclear.
- **Specific request:** Ensure patients, not only prescribers, receive clear explanations of adverse decisions and appeal rights.

**Topic 4: Outcome-oriented API and PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Counts of participating providers, request volume, and API request percentages measure adoption but not reliability, burden reduction, or real-world completion.
- **Specific request:** Add operational metrics including transaction completion, errors, timeouts, failed connections, manual workarounds, supplemental information requests, elapsed time to decision and medication access, denial categories, resubmissions, appeals, and overturns.

**Topic 5: Granular drug PA reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Aggregate plan-level figures can hide variation by drug category, benefit type, and delegated decision-maker.
- **Specific request:** Disaggregate key drug PA metrics by therapeutic category, Part B versus Part D status, and whether the initial determination was made by the plan, PBM, or another delegated entity.

**Topic 6: Conformance testing, sandboxes, and production oversight**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Real-world API reliability requires more than initial availability; testing must cover realistic workflows and material changes.
- **Specific request:** Require standardized conformance testing, public and CMS-visible testing results, maintained sandboxes, renewed testing after material changes or IG updates, production monitoring, endpoint availability information, outage reporting, policy-version change transparency, and practical fallbacks that preserve original submission time.

**Topic 7: Accountability for automated tools**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** As electronic PA uses automation for intake, documentation review, and decision support, payers must remain accountable for accuracy, consistency, and clinical appropriateness.
- **Specific request:** Require auditable records of criteria, policy version, data, and logic when automation materially contributes to an adverse determination, and require meaningful human review when clinical judgment or disputed facts are involved.

**Topic 8: Reducing PA volume and preserving continuity**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Processing fewer unnecessary requests can reduce burden more directly than processing all requests faster.
- **Specific request:** Encourage drug PA reductions, periodic removal of PA for therapies with established clinical use and high approval rates, streamlined pathways for prescribers with strong approval histories, gold-carding, and continuity protections for patients stable on therapy or changing plans.

**Topic 9: Standards governance and version alignment**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Frequent or unpredictable changes to implementation guides, code sets, and technical requirements create moving-target compliance burdens.
- **Specific request:** Pair current-version compliance with predictable update schedules, clear versioning, reasonable backward compatibility, sufficient implementation runway, harmonized versions across impacted payers, and close CMS/ONC coordination.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify that generic denial phrases, unexplained proprietary codes, and vague statements do not satisfy the specific-reason requirement.
2. Require denial explanations to include precise unmet criteria, policy version, denial category, missing information, alternatives, step therapy requirements, and appeal/resubmission instructions.
3. Provide denial information in human-readable language and structured machine-readable fields.
4. Ensure patients receive clear adverse decision explanations and appeal rights.
5. Supplement API adoption metrics with end-to-end operational, reliability, burden-reduction, timing, denial, appeal, and overturn metrics.
6. Disaggregate key drug PA metrics by therapeutic category, Part B versus Part D status, and reviewing entity.
7. Clarify how supplemental information requests affect decision timeframes.
8. Require standardized conformance testing, maintained sandbox environments, retesting after material changes, and production monitoring.
9. Provide fallbacks when APIs fail while preserving original submission time.
10. Preserve payer accountability and auditable records when automated tools contribute to adverse determinations.
11. Encourage PA volume reductions, gold-carding, streamlined prescriber pathways, and continuity protections.
12. Manage standards evolution through predictable schedules, version harmonization, backward compatibility, and CMS/ONC coordination.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vague denials, adoption metrics that do not measure outcomes, aggregate reporting that hides variation, unreliable APIs, lack of testing and production oversight, automation without accountability, excessive PA volume, continuity disruptions, and unstable standards governance.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Patients forgoing appeals, avoidable administrative friction, delayed medication access, manual workarounds, uneven adoption, rushed transitions, and reduced trust in electronic PA.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Legacy payer systems, incomplete electronic PA adoption, investment needs, evolving implementation guides and code sets, standards sunset timelines, and divergent medical/pharmacy PA frameworks.

- **Studies or research referenced:** CMS proposed rule; HHS press release on industry PA pledge; AHIP/BCBSA report; CMS Prior Authorization API FAQs; CMS HPMS memorandum on service-level data collection; AHA summary; UnitedHealthcare and Cigna PA reform announcements; HL7/Firely State of FHIR survey.
- **Examples provided:** Examples of actionable denial content; operational API metrics; disaggregation by therapeutic category and reviewing entity; Saudi Arabia, Israel, and India as international interoperability examples; gold-carding and stable-therapy continuity protections.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients in MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and Marketplace coverage; prescribers; providers; payers; PBMs and delegated entities; health IT developers; CMS and ONC; patients with serious or complex conditions.
