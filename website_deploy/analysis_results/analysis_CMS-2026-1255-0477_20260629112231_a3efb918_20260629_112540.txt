## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Podiatric Medical Association (APMA); signed by Patrick DeHeer, DPM, President
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents the vast majority of the nation's 15,000 practicing doctors of podiatric medicine.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

APMA generally opposes expansion of prior authorization but supports CMS's effort to extend electronic prior authorization and transparency requirements to drugs where PA remains necessary. The commenter asks CMS to preserve clinical expertise in review, adopt short drug PA decision timeframes, expand meaningful public reporting, require specific denial reasons, support small-practice ePA adoption, improve cyber resiliency, and streamline DMEPOS prior authorization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; RIN 0938-AV44; no CFR sections are cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization, interoperability standards, MIPS Electronic Prior Authorization Measure, DMEPOS workflows, WISeR model; no specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: ePA for Drugs with Specialty-Sensitive Implementation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Podiatric physicians prescribe medications and therapies that prevent complications from diabetes, vascular disease, infections, chronic wounds, and lower-extremity conditions, and PA delays can harm outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Extend ePA capabilities and transparency requirements to drugs while ensuring implementation does not increase burden on clinicians.

**Topic 2: Clinical Expertise in PA Review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Electronic workflows are only effective if coverage determinations are clinically appropriate.
- **Specific request:** Encourage payers to ensure medical necessity reviews are conducted by physicians or licensed professionals with relevant expertise, including podiatrists for lower-extremity care.

**Topic 3: Drug PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Drug therapy can be time-sensitive for infections, diabetic complications, post-operative conditions, chronic wounds, and lower-extremity conditions.
- **Specific request:** Finalize standard drug PA decisions within 48 hours and expedited decisions within 24 hours.

**Topic 4: Transparency and Public Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Public reporting promotes accountability and can reveal inappropriate utilization management practices.
- **Specific request:** Require standardized, accessible reporting of approval/denial rates, average response times, overturned denials, extended review periods, drug-specific impacts, and specialty-specific utilization patterns where feasible.

**Topic 5: Gold Carding and Reduction of Unnecessary PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers with histories of evidence-based prescribing and high PA approval rates should not repeatedly obtain approval for routinely authorized treatments.
- **Specific request:** Explore gold carding through future rulemaking and demonstration programs.

**Topic 6: Denial Reasons and Payer Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Detailed denial explanations help clinicians understand requirements, resubmit efficiently, and pursue appeals.
- **Specific request:** Finalize specific denial reasons and evaluate mechanisms to hold payers accountable for meaningful explanations, response deadlines, accurate coverage requirements, and appropriate PA programs.

**Topic 7: Provider Education, MIPS Flexibility, and Small Practice Adoption**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Successful ePA implementation requires education, technical assistance, and flexibility because practices depend on payer and health IT readiness.
- **Specific request:** Engage specialty societies, health IT developers, and payers; focus on small practices; keep MIPS ePA reporting flexible; avoid holding clinicians accountable for payer technology limitations.

**Topic 8: Resiliency, Cybersecurity, and DMEPOS RFIs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cyber incidents and system outages can disrupt claims, reimbursement, PA, prescriptions, DMEPOS, and patient care, particularly for small practices and patients at risk of limb-threatening complications.
- **Specific request:** Promote continuity systems, contingency protocols, backup access, transaction continuity, outage communication, cybersecurity education, relief from penalties during outages, coordination across federal agencies and vendors, DMEPOS-specific ePA workflows, standardized DMEPOS documentation, expedited review for limb preservation/wound care/fall prevention, exemptions for routinely approved DMEPOS, and caution on WISeR model expansion.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports ePA for drugs where PA is necessary and highlights podiatric drug therapies tied to diabetes, vascular disease, infection, wounds, and lower-extremity care.
- **S16:** Strongly supports requiring specific reasons for denial of drug PA requests.
- **S17:** Supports meaningful denial explanations for resubmission and appeals, though it does not specify structured denial code sets.
- **S20:** Supports 48-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug PA decision timeframes.
- **S21:** Supports ePA implementation but emphasizes provider education, technical assistance, and small-practice readiness.
- **S29:** Recommends public PA metrics including approval/denial rates, response times, overturned denials, extended review periods, drug-specific impacts, and specialty-specific utilization patterns.
- **S35:** Supports public reporting to improve accountability for policymakers, providers, and patients.
- **S37:** Addresses implementation challenges involving small practices, payer readiness, health IT capabilities, MIPS burden, and specialty workflows.
- **S43:** Supports shorter clinically appropriate decision timeframes for drug-related PA.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on healthcare resiliency and DMEPOS, including the Change Healthcare cyberattack, contingency planning, DMEPOS ePA, gold carding, and WISeR model concerns.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend ePA capabilities and transparency requirements to drugs where PA remains necessary.
2. Ensure medical necessity reviews use physicians or licensed clinicians with relevant specialty expertise.
3. Finalize 48-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug PA decision timeframes.
4. Publicly report standardized metrics on approvals, denials, response times, overturned denials, extended review periods, drug-specific impacts, and specialty patterns.
5. Develop gold carding programs for providers with high approval rates and evidence-based prescribing.
6. Require specific and meaningful denial reasons for drug PA requests.
7. Provide provider education, technical assistance, and specialty society engagement for ePA adoption.
8. Give special attention to small physician practices and maintain MIPS flexibility.
9. Do not hold clinicians accountable for payer technology limitations or incomplete interoperability implementation.
10. Improve resiliency for claims, payment, prior authorization, pharmacy transactions, and clinical information access during outages.
11. Provide cybersecurity education and protections for practices affected by third-party infrastructure failures.
12. Expand DMEPOS-specific ePA workflows and standardized documentation.
13. Limit repeated PA for ongoing effective DMEPOS treatment and establish expedited review for limb preservation, wound care, infection management, and fall prevention.
14. Consider PA exemptions and gold carding for routinely approved DMEPOS items.
15. Do not use the WISeR model for DMEPOS PA additions or improvements until current issues are resolved.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA expansion, delayed drug therapy, lack of clinical expertise in reviews, insufficient transparency, small-practice burden, payer/health IT readiness, cyber resiliency, DMEPOS delays, and WISeR model limitations.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disease progression, emergency department use, hospitalization, poorer outcomes, diabetic foot ulcer progression, infection, avoidable amputation, fall risk, impaired mobility, cash-flow harm from outages, and delayed reimbursement.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer technology limitations, incomplete interoperability, small practice resource constraints, inconsistent DMEPOS documentation, duplicative submissions, third-party clearinghouse/vendor failures, and WISeR automation limits.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** APMA represents most of the nation's 15,000 practicing podiatric physicians.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Infections, diabetic complications, post-operative conditions, chronic wounds, therapeutic shoes, custom orthotics, walking boots, offloading devices, ankle-foot orthoses, compression products, wound care supplies, Change Healthcare 2024 cyberattack, and WISeR model issues with skin substitute applications.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Podiatric physicians, small and independent physician practices, patients with diabetes, chronic wounds, infections and lower-extremity conditions, payers, health IT vendors, clearinghouses, DMEPOS suppliers, policymakers, and beneficiaries.
- **Economic implications:** PA and cyber outages can create administrative costs and cash-flow harm for small practices; streamlined ePA, gold carding, and fewer unnecessary DMEPOS PAs may reduce costs and delays.
- **Technical feasibility:** APMA supports ePA but stresses dependence on payer and health IT readiness, resilient infrastructure, standardized documentation, and workflow-specific implementation.
- **Patient care impact:** High; delays can lead to disease progression, hospitalization, limb-threatening complications, mobility loss, and avoidable amputation.
