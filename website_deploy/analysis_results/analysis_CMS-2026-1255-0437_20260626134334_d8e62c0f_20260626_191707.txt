## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to impose mandatory prior authorization reforms because current processes can delay clinically urgent renal therapies. He supports expedited review pathways, limits on timing-based denials and redundant testing, and protections against patient/provider liability when payer administrative delay causes costs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed
- **Standards referenced:** KDIGO 2025 Guideline for ADPKD; KDIGO 2021 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Glomerular Diseases. No FHIR or NCPDP standards are referenced.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Expedited review for time-sensitive drug therapies**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that for progressive renal conditions, intervention speed can determine kidney function and organ survival.
- **Specific request:** Establish firm accelerated review timelines for time-sensitive therapies such as tolvaptan for PKD and rituximab for glomerular diseases, aligned with KDIGO recommendations.

**Topic 2: Clinical criteria versus administrative denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter says current PA policies can override evidence-based clinical standards and delay or deny standard-of-care therapy.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials based on authorization timing when medically necessary treatment was ordered while PA was pending.

**Topic 3: Redundant testing and administrative failure**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Redundant documentation or testing burdens patients when valid clinical data already exist, and payer-side administrative delay can shift costs to patients and providers.
- **Specific request:** Eliminate forced repetitive blood draws when valid data are available and protect patients/providers from financial liability caused by insurance-side administrative delays.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** The comment is relevant to denial specificity because it objects to timing-based denials and denials that override recognized clinical standards.
- **S20:** The commenter supports accelerated decision timelines for urgent drug PA, especially where delayed therapy can cause irreversible clinical harm.
- **S43:** The commenter asks CMS to align expedited PA pathways with clinical urgency and KDIGO guideline-based treatment needs.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prohibit coverage denials for medically necessary tests or treatments solely because authorization was pending at the time of order.
2. Prevent redundant blood draws or tests when valid clinical data are already available.
3. Establish mandatory expedited review pathways for time-sensitive therapies such as tolvaptan and rituximab.
4. Align expedited PA timelines with KDIGO clinical recommendations for renal disease.
5. Protect patients and providers from financial liability for costs caused by payer administrative delays.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays for urgent kidney disease therapies; administrative policies overriding clinical guidelines; redundant testing; financial exposure from payer delay.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Irreversible renal decline, catastrophic flare-ups, physical incapacitation, more toxic and complex treatment, and preventable suffering.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative review loops, pending authorization timing rules, and payer policies that do not track evidence-based clinical pathways.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No aggregate statistics.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KDIGO 2025 ADPKD guideline and KDIGO 2021 Glomerular Diseases guideline.
- **Examples provided:** A 27-year-old patient with biopsy-proven Minimal Change Disease whose rituximab PA was delayed, leading to massive rapid-onset nephrotic syndrome and a more toxic treatment course.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with renal disease, nephrologists and other providers, payers, and health systems.
- **Economic implications:** The commenter does not quantify costs but notes potential financial liability for patients and providers caused by payer-side delay.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not addressed in FHIR/NCPDP terms; the requested reforms are policy and workflow mandates.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment presents delayed PA as a direct threat to kidney function, organ survival, and avoidable morbidity.

To the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services,
I am writing as a nephrologist specializing in polycystic kidney disease (PKD) and
glomerulonephritis. My clinical practice at The Rogosin Institute is dedicated to
managing complex, progressive renal conditions where the speed of therapeutic
intervention is the primary determinant of long-term kidney function and organ survival. I
am submitting these comments to urge CMS to enact mandatory reforms to the prior
authorization (PA) processes that currently create dangerous barriers to care.
In the management of renal disease, we are guided by evidence-based international
standards, specifically the KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes)
Clinical Practice Guidelines. These guidelines establish clear criteria for the use of
high-impact therapies such as tolvaptan, which the KDIGO 2025 Guideline for ADPKD
recommends for adults with rapidly progressive disease to slow kidney function decline...
