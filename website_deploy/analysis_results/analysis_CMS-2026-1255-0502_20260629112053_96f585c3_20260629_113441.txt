## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Health IT End-Users Alliance
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Coalition of health information professionals, physicians, hospitals, and front-line healthcare providers/organizations that use health IT; contact via AHIMA (American Health Information Management Association)

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Health IT End-Users Alliance supports CMS' goals to improve prior authorization and reduce burden on end-users, including the extension to drugs and the proposed transition from X12 to FHIR standards as the HIPAA standard for PA. However, the Alliance raises significant concerns about mandating FHIR standards and IGs that are not yet sufficiently mature, the lack of a real-world testing roadmap, and the 24-month implementation timeline being too tight. They urge phased compliance deadlines, continuation of X12 as a fallback during transition, federal technical assistance, and meaningful end-user engagement before enforcement.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE (cross-cutting), API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P (proposed rule); CMS-0057-F (2024 final rule)
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR, X12 278 transaction standard, FHIR implementation guides (IGs), Da Vinci IGs (implied), EHR systems

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Support for PA Extension to Drugs and Process Improvements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Drugs have long been a major source of PA frustration and burden; extending electronic PA requirements is welcomed.
- **Specific request:** Align metrics reporting, timeframes, and transparency requirements across all CMS-regulated and impacted payers.

**Topic 2: Support for FHIR as HIPAA Standard for PA (with conditions)**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** The healthcare industry has long needed a solution for the lack of an attachment standard for the X12 278; FHIR fills this gap. However, compliance should not be enforced until IGs are demonstrably mature.
- **Specific request:** Extend compliance deadlines; maintain X12 278 availability as fallback during transition; require a clear roadmap for IG maturation and real-world testing before enforcement.

**Topic 3: FHIR IG Maturity and Real-World Testing**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** IGs are not yet sufficiently mature. Without robust real-world testing, mandating them risks workarounds, failed implementations, excessive burden, wasted resources, and patient confusion.
- **Specific request:** CMS must establish a clear real-world testing roadmap and reporting mechanism to demonstrate IG maturity before compliance is enforced. Federal support for testing is needed.

**Topic 4: Implementation Timeline**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS (cross-cutting)
- **Position:** Oppose / Modify
- **Rationale:** The 24-month timeline is insufficient: EHR vendors need ~12 months to build and release updates after final rule publication, leaving insufficient time for healthcare organizations to configure, train, test, and coordinate with payers.
- **Specific request:** Phased compliance timeline or extended implementation period, with technical assistance and real-world testing support before enforcement.

**Topic 5: API Endpoints and Event Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Both API endpoint reporting and event notifications will require extensive input from the technology development and end-user communities.
- **Specific request:** CMS should conduct extensive public convenings and stakeholder input processes to develop guardrails that ensure API endpoint accuracy without disrupting existing value-based care event notification workflows.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S37:** Commenter raises concerns about requiring IGs before they are mature enough; calls for real-world testing evidence before mandating adoption. Existing experience with standards that were adopted before sufficient testing shows risk of burdensome workarounds.
- **S44:** Supports FHIR as the appropriate standard for PA transactions; welcomes resolution of the long-standing gap in the X12 278 attachment standard.
- **S46:** Supports FHIR replacing X12 but requests X12 278 remain available as a fallback until FHIR ecosystem proves out in real-world settings.
- **S47:** The 24-month compliance timeline is too short given EHR vendor build time (~12 months) plus organizational configuration, training, and testing requirements. Urges phased or extended timeline.
- **S50:** FHIR IGs are not yet sufficiently mature; a clear roadmap for real-world testing and maturation reporting is needed before compliance is enforced.
- **S53:** Supports phased compliance approach rather than a single hard deadline.
- **S23/S24:** Supports stakeholder engagement and public convenings around API endpoint reporting; emphasizes need to avoid disruption to existing value-based care event notification workflows.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend compliance deadlines for FHIR standards and IGs until real-world testing demonstrates sufficient maturity.
2. Maintain X12 278 as a fallback data exchange method during the FHIR transition period.
3. Establish a clear real-world testing roadmap and reporting mechanism to demonstrate when FHIR IGs are ready for enforcement.
4. Implement a phased compliance timeline (or finalize a longer timeline) for the 24-month requirement.
5. Provide federal resources, technical assistance, and flexibilities—especially for under-resourced organizations—to support implementation.
6. Engage end-users, developers, and payers in extensive public convenings around API endpoint reporting and event notification proposals before finalizing policy.
7. Align metrics reporting, decision timeframes, and transparency requirements across all CMS-regulated and impacted payers.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** FHIR IGs mandated before demonstrating real-world maturity; insufficient implementation timeline; lack of federal support for testing and end-user adoption.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Workarounds by healthcare organizations, wasted money on failed implementations, standards that don't achieve policy goals when deployed, excessive end-user burden, patient confusion.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** EHR vendor build/release cycle (~12 months), organizational configuration and training, workflow redesign, payer coordination—all compressing into the 24-month window.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** EHR vendor software build and release can take at least 12 months after final rule publication.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Alliance's Real-world Testing Consensus Statement (referenced as Appendix to the letter, though not reproduced in the extracted text).
- **Examples provided:** X12 278 attachment standard gap as historical precedent for why FHIR fills a real need; description of healthcare organization implementation sequence (configure, test, validate, train, coordinate, monitor).

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health IT end-users (health information professionals, physicians, hospitals, front-line providers), EHR vendors, impacted payers, under-resourced healthcare organizations.
- **Economic implications:** Risk of wasted money on failed implementations if IGs are immature; costs to EHR vendors and providers to implement within a compressed timeline.
- **Technical feasibility:** Raises material concerns—IGs are not yet sufficiently mature; 24-month timeline is inadequate given the multi-step implementation sequence required.
- **Patient care impact:** Potential patient confusion from immature technology; if done well, electronic PA for drugs will reduce burden and delays in medication access.
