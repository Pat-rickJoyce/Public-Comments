# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Susan G. Komen; signed by Molly Guthrie, Vice President, Policy & Advocacy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National breast cancer nonprofit advocating for patients, research, access to care, direct patient support, and information; cites advocacy for people diagnosed with and dying from breast cancer in 2026.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Susan G. Komen generally supports CMS efforts to modernize and standardize electronic prior authorization for prescription drugs, especially because PA delays can harm breast cancer patients. The organization asks CMS to reduce dual-standard burdens, require faster and more durable PA approvals, increase transparent reporting, and further address step therapy, lab tests, and DMEPOS.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY-related GENERAL; GENERAL/RFIs on step therapy, laboratory tests, and DMEPOS.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations mentioned directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for medical and pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Breast cancer treatment often involves drugs under both medical and pharmacy benefits, and sometimes the same drug can be classified differently based on benefit design. Using two standards could preserve burden for patients and providers.
- **Specific request:** Ensure patients and providers are not left with duplicative paperwork or hurdles that delay care.

**Topic 2: PA decision timeframes and approval duration**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Delays in oncology treatment can worsen outcomes, and repeated PA renewals for long-term treatment can create gaps in therapy.
- **Specific request:** Require all prescription drug PA responses within 24 hours; require PA approvals to remain valid for the duration of treatment or set limits on repeat PA; require approvals to transfer between payers during transitions.

**Topic 3: PA metrics and transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Reporting request volumes and outcomes can reveal the prevalence and consequences of PA, but aggregate data may obscure effects on cancer patients or people with advanced disease.
- **Specific request:** Require disaggregated PA metrics and standardized website display so information is findable and readable.

**Topic 4: Step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can conflict with personalized metastatic cancer treatment and can force patients to fail therapies despite time-sensitive disease progression.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit step therapy for individuals with metastatic cancer diagnoses and consider further rulemaking.

**Topic 5: Laboratory tests and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Support further action.
- **Rationale:** Lab testing is central to breast cancer diagnosis and treatment planning, and PA delays can require repeat specimens, delay diagnosis, and add avoidable costs.
- **Specific request:** Continue reducing PA burdens and delays for laboratory testing and DMEPOS.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S15:** Komen addresses the medical versus pharmacy benefit distinction, warning that two standards may burden providers and patients because breast cancer drugs can span both benefit categories.
- **S20/S43:** Komen responds to drug PA timeframes by urging a 24-hour response in all cases and alignment across QHP, Medicaid, and CHIP standards.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Komen supports PA metrics reporting and asks for disaggregated data and standardized public display to reveal population-specific impacts.
- **S66-S82:** Komen responds to RFIs on step therapy, laboratory tests, and DMEPOS by urging reduced UM burden, more transparency, and a prohibition on step therapy for metastatic cancer.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for drugs covered under both medical and pharmacy benefits, while minimizing dual-standard workflow burden.
2. Require prescription drug PA responses within 24 hours for all request types.
3. Require PA approvals to remain valid for the duration of treatment or limit repeat PA frequency and timing.
4. Require PA approvals to transfer between payers during transitions to prevent treatment gaps.
5. Require disaggregated PA metrics and standardized, readable insurer website reporting.
6. Prohibit step therapy for people with metastatic cancer diagnoses.
7. Continue rulemaking or policy work to reduce PA delays for laboratory testing and DMEPOS.
8. Monitor PA implementation, outcomes, inappropriate use, and erroneous denials.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays, repeat PA for long-term cancer therapies, dual medical/pharmacy benefit workflows, insufficient transparency, and step therapy for metastatic cancer.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed diagnosis or treatment, avoidable treatment gaps, increased mortality risk, added patient/provider burden, and higher system costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Distinguishing medical versus pharmacy benefit drugs, displaying meaningful metrics, and integrating PA workflows without preserving paperwork burdens.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Komen cites estimated 324,600 U.S. breast cancer diagnoses and 42,670 deaths in 2026; states that nearly all breast cancer deaths are due to metastatic breast cancer.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Journal of Oncology Practice 2017 study on prior authorization for medications in a breast oncology practice; studies showing a four-week metastatic cancer treatment delay is associated with increased mortality.
- **Examples provided:** Breast cancer treatment may include drugs under both benefits, multiple concurrent drugs, long-term metastatic breast cancer treatment, and immediate lab orders where PA delays can require a second specimen.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Breast cancer patients and survivors, people with metastatic or advanced cancer, oncology providers, payers, QHP enrollees, Medicaid and CHIP beneficiaries, laboratories, and DMEPOS suppliers.
- **Economic implications:** PA may increase complexity and costs despite cost-control intent; repeat specimens and delayed care can create unnecessary downstream costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Komen does not dispute electronic PA feasibility but flags workflow complexity from multiple standards and benefit classifications.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster and more durable PA approvals could reduce treatment gaps, while step therapy and lab PA delays can directly harm oncology outcomes.
