## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Capital District Physicians' Health Plan, Inc. (CDPHP) (Barry J. Thornton, CEO); physician-founded, not-for-profit health plan
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Not-for-profit health plan serving upstate New York for 40+ years; 56,000+ Medicare Advantage members in Capital Region; 4.5-star CMS ratings for both MA PPO and HMO plans in 2026 (among highest in NY and nationally).

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CDPHP supports the overall direction of CMS-0062-P and endorses mandatory FHIR IGs, NCPDP F&B, RTPB, and Provider Directory API clarification, but requests extending the compliance date by at least 15 months (to January 1, 2029, preferably April 1, 2029). The comment is substantively near-identical to the Excellus Health Plan submission (CMS-2026-1255-0469), sharing the same language, vendor dependency examples, recommended dates, and NCPDP F6 burden argument, indicating coordinated template use across upstate New York health plans. They also call for provider and EHR vendor readiness support and future rulemaking to expand FHIR for eligibility/benefits inquiries.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 C.F.R. § 170.215; 91 FR 19890
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR implementation guides (Patient Access, Provider Access, Provider Directory, Payer-to-Payer, Prior Authorization APIs); Da Vinci Plan-Net IG; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit (F&B) Standard; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB) Standard; Da Vinci CRD; NCPDP Telecommunication Standard Version F6

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Mandatory FHIR Implementation Guides**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Making IGs mandatory promotes greater consistency across implementations, reduces ambiguity for payers and technology vendors, and creates clearer expectations.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 2: NCPDP F&B Standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Enables plan-level formulary and utilization management information to be incorporated into prescribing workflows and surfaced in provider EHR systems.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 3: NCPDP RTPB Standard**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardizing real-time patient-specific eligibility, coverage, and cost-sharing reduces reliance on fragmented payer-specific portals and proprietary solutions.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 4: Provider Directory API Clarification**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specifying Da Vinci Plan-Net resolves existing implementation ambiguity and promotes consistency across impacted payers.
- **Specific request:** No modification.

**Topic 5: FHIR API Standards (Version Transitions)**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Overlapping versions that don't integrate well create avoidable operational complexity, increase implementation burden, and delay alignment on updated standards.
- **Specific request:** Avoid lengthy transition periods for older standards once newer standards are adopted.

**Topic 6: Implementation Timeline — Extend to April 1, 2029**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose (as proposed)
- **Rationale:** Technical resources already committed; complex multi-vendor dependencies with contracting timelines of 6-8 months; cumulative burden with NCPDP F6 transition affecting same teams and partners.
- **Specific request:** Extend compliance date by at least 15 months to January 1, 2029; prefers April 1, 2029 as most realistic.

**Topic 7: Provider and Vendor Readiness**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify (expand scope)
- **Rationale:** Provider and EHR vendor readiness remains inconsistent; payers may build capabilities without achieving end-to-end electronic exchange.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should encourage provider/vendor adoption; collect API adoption metrics for providers and EHR vendors; provide targeted funding, incentives, and technical support.

**Topic 8: Future FHIR Expansion for Eligibility/Benefits**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support (future rulemaking)
- **Rationale:** Broader FHIR-based eligibility/benefits inquiries beyond PA would support general coverage confirmation and patient financial responsibility use cases.
- **Specific request:** CMS, in coordination with NCVHS, should consider future rulemaking to expand FHIR-based APIs for eligibility and benefits inquiries beyond PA.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Strongly supports requiring the currently recommended FHIR implementation guides; sees mandatory status as reducing ambiguity and promoting consistency.
- **S2/S3:** Advises against lengthy overlapping transition periods; overlapping non-integrating versions create operational complexity.
- **S21:** Disputes achievability of October 1, 2027 date; recommends extension to January 1, 2029 at minimum, preferably April 1, 2029; cites cumulative NCPDP F6 burden, committed resources, multi-vendor contracting (6-8 months).
- **S22:** Notes cumulative burden with NCPDP Telecommunication Standard Version F6 transition affecting same health plans and PBM partners.
- **S44:** Supports using FHIR CRD within PA workflow; encourages broader future rulemaking for FHIR-based eligibility/benefits inquiries beyond PA.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Extend compliance date by at least 15 months from October 1, 2027 to January 1, 2029; prefers April 1, 2029.
2. Avoid lengthy transition periods for older standards once newer standards are adopted.
3. CMS and ONC should encourage and support FHIR API adoption by providers and EHR vendors, not just payers.
4. Collect and report FHIR API adoption and usage metrics for providers and EHR vendors.
5. Provide targeted funding, incentives, and technical support programs for provider and EHR vendor adoption.
6. CMS and NCVHS should consider future rulemaking to expand FHIR-based APIs for eligibility and benefits inquiries beyond PA use cases.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** October 1, 2027 compliance date not achievable; NCPDP F6 consuming same technical resources; multi-vendor contracting timelines.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Layering requirements on overlapping timelines risks delayed implementation, reduced system quality, and slower adoption; health plans may build without corresponding provider/vendor readiness, preventing end-to-end electronic PA.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-vendor dependencies (PBM, specialty drug UM SaaS, oncology UM partner, FHIR platform, UM recommendation partner); contracting alone may take 6-8 months; resources already committed to existing initiatives.
