## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Philips responds to the RFI questions in CMS-0062-P regarding prior authorization for DMEPOS items and laboratory tests, drawing on its dual perspective as both a health technology company and a DME supplier. Philips supports automation, interoperability, and standardized electronic documentation to reduce PA burden, but cautions that technology alone cannot address all cases and that clinical review mechanisms must be preserved for complex patients.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL (RFI responses on DMEPOS and lab tests); PA-DRUG-MEDICAL (tangentially, regarding medical benefit coverage determinations); FHIR-IG-REQUIRED (implicitly, supporting electronic interoperability)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-484 form (Certificate of Medical Necessity for Oxygen, discontinued January 1, 2023)
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic health records (EHR), ePrescribing platforms; standardized electronic documentation; interoperable data exchange (no specific FHIR IGs cited)

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Impact of DMEPOS PA on Patient Care and Provider Burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (existing PA processes)
- **Rationale:** Variation in PA requirements across payers creates unnecessary administrative complexity, inconsistent beneficiary access, and operational inefficiency. DME suppliers contract with many payers, each with different documentation requirements, forcing multiple inconsistent workflows for similar equipment.
- **Specific request:** CMS should explore greater alignment among PA determinations, claims adjudication, and post-payment review so that pre-authorized services are not later denied.

**Topic 2: Standardized Electronic Documentation for DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized clinical data elements submitted through EHRs and ePrescribing would strengthen program integrity, improve payment accuracy, reduce fraud/waste/abuse, and enable more consistent coverage determinations.
- **Specific request:** Advance use of standardized electronic documentation requirements for DME medical necessity; preserve benefits of the CMS-484 CMN framework while modernizing to electronic exchange.

**Topic 3: Automation of PA Processes for DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Automated identification of authorization requirements, real-time documentation validation, electronic clinical data exchange, and status visibility would reduce processing delays, minimize avoidable denials, and accelerate access to medically necessary equipment.
- **Specific request:** Advance automation and interoperability for PA and coverage review; provide real-time status updates for providers, suppliers, and patients.

**Topic 4: Limitations of Technology for Complex Patients**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Technology alone cannot fully address all PA challenges; patients with complex conditions, multiple comorbidities, or unique clinical needs require individualized clinical assessment that automated processes may not support.
- **Specific request:** Preserve appropriate mechanisms for clinical review and exception-based decision-making; adopt a balanced approach combining standardized automated workflows with targeted clinical oversight.

**Topic 5: Workforce Impact of PA Requirements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Concern
- **Rationale:** PA requirements contribute to employee burnout, turnover, and workforce retention challenges; physician practices spend an average of 16.4 hours/week on PA activities (AMA); some practices employ staff exclusively for PA.
- **Specific request:** Reduce administrative complexity through automation, standardization, and alignment of PA determinations with claims adjudication.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

Philips responds specifically to the RFI questions embedded in the proposed rule about DMEPOS and laboratory tests:

- **S66 (general RFI — DMEPOS PA impact on patient care):** PA requirements create delays in medically necessary care for DME; inconsistent payer requirements force suppliers to juggle multiple workflows, increasing denials and delays for beneficiaries with serious respiratory conditions.
- **S67/S68 (general RFI — process improvement opportunities):** Standardized electronic documentation, automation, interoperability, and alignment between PA determinations and claims adjudication are key opportunities. Post-payment audits questioning previously authorized services create duplicative burden; CMS should align these processes.
- **S69/S70 (general RFI — automation of DMEPOS PA):** Automation would reduce processing delays, minimize avoidable denials, reduce rework, and free staff for patient care. Real-time status updates would benefit all stakeholders.
- **S71/S72 (general RFI — technology gaps for lab/DMEPOS):** Standardization gaps across payers persist even with electronic tools. Automation must be balanced with clinical review mechanisms for complex patients; a hybrid approach is needed.
- **S73 (general RFI — workforce impact):** PA imposes significant workforce burden including burnout, turnover, and retention challenges; AMA data shows 16.4 hours/week per practice and ~1/3 of practices employ dedicated PA staff.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Advance standardized electronic documentation requirements for DME medical necessity via EHRs and ePrescribing platforms.
2. Align PA determinations with claims adjudication and post-payment review to prevent later denial of pre-authorized services.
3. Implement automated identification of authorization requirements, real-time documentation validation, and electronic clinical data submission for DMEPOS.
4. Provide real-time status tracking for PA requests across providers, suppliers, and patients.
5. Preserve mechanisms for clinical review and exception-based decision-making for complex patients.
6. Adopt a balanced approach combining standardized automated workflows with targeted clinical oversight.
7. Reduce administrative complexity through interoperability and standardization to address workforce burnout and retention challenges.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Lack of standardization across payers; misalignment between PA determinations, claims adjudication, and post-payment review; risk that automation could create barriers for complex patients.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays in medically necessary DME lead to worsening symptoms, disease progression, avoidable hospitalizations, and reduced quality of life; workforce burnout and retention challenges from administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer variation in coverage policies and documentation requirements cannot be solved by technology alone; standardization of criteria across payers is a prerequisite.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA finding that physician practices spend an average of 16.4 hours per week on PA activities; approximately one-third of practices employ staff working exclusively on PA.
- **Studies or research referenced:** American Medical Association (unspecified survey on PA burden).
- **Examples provided:** CMS-484 Certificate of Medical Necessity for Oxygen (discontinued Jan 1, 2023) as model for structured documentation; specific DMEPOS items: supplemental oxygen, PAP therapy devices, ventilators, mobility equipment, enteral nutrition.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** DME suppliers, providers, Medicare beneficiaries (especially elderly and those with complex chronic conditions), payers, administrative/authorization staff
- **Economic implications:** Significant administrative costs from duplicative reviews, appeals, and dedicated PA staff; potential savings from streamlined processes and alignment between PA and claims adjudication
- **Technical feasibility:** Philips views technology as ready to advance automation; flags need for payer standardization as prerequisite; notes technology cannot fully replace clinical judgment for complex cases
- **Patient care impact:** High — delays in DMEPOS access cause worsening symptoms, hospitalizations, and diminished independence for Medicare beneficiaries; automation would materially improve access timeliness
