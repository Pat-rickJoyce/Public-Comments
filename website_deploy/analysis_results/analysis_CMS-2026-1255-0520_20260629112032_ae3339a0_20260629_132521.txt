- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Patient advocacy organization supporting women navigating cancer treatment; works with patients and providers affected by prior authorization and coverage delays.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tigerlily Foundation strongly supports CMS efforts to improve interoperability, prior authorization transparency, and timely access to prescribed treatments. The comment emphasizes that cancer patients can suffer direct clinical harm from PA denials, appeals, and administrative delays, and it urges CMS to ensure care decisions are driven by patients and providers rather than paperwork.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, PA-DENIAL-REASONS, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED (general interoperability support).
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** None specifically; general references to interoperability, data sharing, and health information systems.

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Reduce Prior Authorization Barriers for Cancer Patients**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Cancer treatment can be time-sensitive and sequenced; administrative denials and appeals can cause missed treatment windows, stress, and disease progression risk.
- **Specific request:** CMS should advance reforms that reduce unnecessary PA barriers and support timely access to medically appropriate treatments prescribed by clinicians.

**Topic 2: Timely PA Decisions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Even delays of weeks may have clinical consequences for cancer patients, especially when therapy is intended to prevent progression or recurrence.
- **Specific request:** CMS should promote timely PA decisions and prevent prolonged appeals from functioning as practical denials of care.

**Topic 3: Transparency and Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The commenter links denial practices, repeated documentation, and unclear administrative processes to patient harm and provider burden.
- **Specific request:** CMS should improve transparency and standardization in PA processes so patients and providers do not face avoidable red tape and uncertainty.

**Topic 4: Interoperability and Data Sharing**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Poor communication between health information systems forces providers to resubmit already documented information and prevents insurers from quickly accessing clinical context.
- **Specific request:** CMS should continue improving interoperability and data sharing to speed approvals and reduce administrative backlogs.

- **Relevant to S66-S82:** The comment provides general patient-centered input on interoperability, PA reform, and administrative burden.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Reduce unnecessary prior authorization barriers for prescribed cancer treatments.
2. Ensure PA decisions and appeals occur quickly enough to avoid treatment disruption.
3. Improve interoperability so payers receive needed clinical context without repeated provider submissions.
4. Increase transparency and standardization in denial and appeals processes.
5. Center CMS policy on patient access and clinician-directed care rather than administrative persistence.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Coverage denials, delayed approvals, burdensome appeals, fragmented interoperability, and administrative processes that operate as barriers to medically appropriate cancer treatment.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Missed treatment windows, stress and uncertainty, treatment abandonment or postponement, disease progression, worse outcomes, and provider time diverted from patient care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Inefficient health information exchange, repeated documentation requests, administrative backlogs, and insurer access gaps to clinical context.

- **Studies or research referenced:** IQVIA nationwide commercial claims analysis.
- **Examples provided:** Kathryn Jackson, a Tigerlily ANGEL advocate with metastatic breast cancer, described delayed access as denied access; Paris Smith experienced nearly three months of delay after insurance denied oncologist-prescribed cancer treatment and ultimately accessed treatment through the manufacturer.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
Dear Administrator Oz and CMS Leadership,
Tigerlily Foundation appreciates CMS's continued leadership in advancing interoperability and reforming 
prior authorization practices to reduce unnecessary barriers to care. As an organization dedicated to 
supporting women navigating cancer treatment, Tigerlily Foundation works closely with patients and 
providers who routinely encounter delays and disruptions caused by administrative barriers. These 
obstacles often arise at critical points in care, when timely access to treatment is essential.
Recent data highlights the urgency of this issue. A new IQVIA analysis found that 70 percent of 
commercially insured patients were initially denied coverage for at least one prescribed brand medicine 
in 2024, meaning millions of Americans experienced delays or barriers to treatments their providers 
determined were appropriate for their care.
The IQVIA analysis of nationwide commercial claims data shows that these denials are no longer isolated 
events. Two trends stand out. First, deny first practices have intensified, with initial rejection rates for 
new brand prescriptions increasing by 13 percentage points between 2021 and 2025. Second, nearly 
one in four new brand prescription claims in 2024 remained unapproved even after a full year, despite 
repeated appeals and administrative review....
