## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Society of General Internal Medicine (SGIM), signed by Mark D. Schwartz, MD, FACP
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (medical specialty society of 3,300+ general internal medicine physicians)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** SGIM members deliver high-quality clinical care for adults with multiple chronic diseases; routinely encounter PA and step therapy administrative barriers in practice

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SGIM supports CMS's efforts to modernize prior authorization through electronic processes, response time standards, and increased transparency but argues the proposals do not go far enough. The organization calls for real-time PA determinations, drug-level metrics reporting integrated into Medicare Advantage Star Ratings, indefinite validity for most approved prior authorizations, and evidence-based guardrails for step therapy with point-of-care visibility into plan requirements.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, API-USAGE-METRICS, GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not specifically cited
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic health records; real-time benefit tools; electronic prior authorization; Medicare Advantage Star Ratings

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic Prior Authorization — Point-of-Care Information**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Electronic transmission alone does not reduce burden if clinicians lack point-of-care information about PA requirements. Plans already provide drug-specific cost-sharing information through EHRs, so PA requirement information is feasibly deliverable at the same point.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to make available at the point of care: (1) whether PA is required; (2) applicable step therapy requirements; (3) alternative medications approved without PA; (4) clinical criteria for approval; and (5) clear guidance on next steps when denied.

**Topic 2: Prior Authorization Response Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify (strengthen)
- **Rationale:** The proposed 24/72-hour timeframes do not adequately account for delays beyond the health plan review period—clinical staff must review determinations, communicate with patients, submit appeals, and coordinate alternatives. Small practices with <1 FTE dedicated to PAs cannot absorb even modest additional steps.
- **Specific request:** Require real-time PA determinations for straightforward requests via ePA. For complex requests, require 24-hour (not 72-hour) response with transparent process navigation and rapid peer-to-peer access. Continue moving toward real-time adjudication as capabilities mature.

**Topic 3: Drug-Level Metrics Reporting and Star Ratings Integration**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify (strengthen beyond aggregate)
- **Rationale:** Aggregate statistics mask significant barriers affecting individual therapies or therapeutic classes. Incorporating PA performance into MA Star Ratings would create financial incentives for improvement.
- **Specific request:** Require publicly reported drug-level PA metrics (7 categories). Incorporate PA performance into Medicare Advantage Star Ratings.

**Topic 4: Reduce Reauthorization Requirements for Ongoing Therapy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Repeated reauthorizations for maintenance therapy generate substantial burden with limited clinical value. Patients who have demonstrated successful, safe use should not repeatedly navigate the same approval process.
- **Specific request:** Most approved prior authorizations should be valid indefinitely. For short-term therapies only, require a simplified single-statement reauthorization confirming ongoing need.

**Topic 5: Step Therapy Reform (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support reform
- **Rationale:** Clinicians discover step therapy requirements only after prescribing and denial. Annual formulary changes disrupt stable chronic disease management. Many protocols are not aligned with clinical evidence—requiring older, riskier drugs before newer, safer alternatives (e.g., full-agonist opioids before buprenorphine). Each plan's unique, opaque requirements create confusion and inequitable access.
- **Specific request:** Require step therapy to be based on current clinical evidence, nationally recognized guidelines, and patient safety; require regular updating. Surface patient-specific step therapy requirements in EHRs at point of prescribing. Explore greater cross-plan consistency for common chronic conditions.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20 (24-hour expedited timeframe achievable):** SGIM supports expedited timeframes but argues 24/72-hour standards are insufficient; real-time determinations should be the goal for straightforward requests; for complex requests, 24 hours should be the standard.
- **S43 (align timeframes):** SGIM urges CMS to continue moving toward real-time adjudication in future rulemaking as ePA capabilities mature.
- **S29 (which metrics to collect):** SGIM requests drug-level PA metrics covering 7 specific categories rather than aggregate alone.
- **S35 (publish aggregate data):** SGIM urges drug-level public reporting and incorporation of PA performance into MA Star Ratings.
- **S66-S82 (General/RFIs):** SGIM responds to step therapy RFI requesting evidence-based guardrails, point-of-care step therapy visibility, greater cross-plan consistency, and opioid step therapy reform (buprenorphine access).

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require plans to make PA requirement information available at the point of care in EHRs (5 specific data elements).
2. Require real-time PA determinations for straightforward requests via ePA.
3. Require 24-hour (not 72-hour) response for complex requests with transparent process navigation and peer-to-peer access.
4. Publicly report drug-level PA metrics (7 specific measures identified).
5. Incorporate PA performance into Medicare Advantage Star Ratings.
6. Approve most prior authorizations with indefinite validity; simplify reauthorization for short-term therapies to a single provider attestation.
7. Require step therapy protocols to be based on current clinical evidence, nationally recognized guidelines, and patient safety considerations.
8. Surface patient-specific step therapy requirements in EHRs at point of prescribing.
9. Require regular review and updating of step therapy protocols.
10. Explore greater cross-plan consistency for common chronic conditions (diabetes, heart failure, COPD, CAD, hypertension).
11. Prohibit step therapy requiring full-agonist opioids before buprenorphine for chronic pain patients.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** ePA without point-of-care PA information provides limited burden relief; 72-hour standard timeframes do not address total delay from prescription to patient receipt; aggregate metrics mask drug-specific barriers; step therapy protocols are frequently not evidence-based.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment interruptions even with minor delays; practices with <1 FTE for PAs cannot handle complex workflows; patient frustration from repeated reauthorizations; patient safety harms from requiring older, riskier drugs in step therapy sequences.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Each plan's unique step therapy requirements create confusion; annual formulary changes disrupt stable regimens mid-year.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** SGIM has 3,300+ member physicians; some practices have less than one full-time staff person dedicated to PAs.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Not specifically cited by name.
- **Examples provided:** Opioid crisis/buprenorphine access as example of harmful step therapy requirement; diabetes medications with hypoglycemia risk required before newer safer agents; older adults' susceptibility to medication side effects; VA and international health system formulary models as evidence-based alternative.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** General internists, patients with multiple chronic diseases, Medicare Advantage beneficiaries, small physician practices, patients with chronic pain, older adults, patients with diabetes/heart failure/COPD
- **Economic implications:** Substantial administrative burden cost for practices with limited PA staff; MA Star Rating financial incentives could drive plan behavior change; reduced reauthorization burden for maintenance therapies
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible—plans already provide drug cost-sharing data through EHRs at point of care; same infrastructure could support PA information delivery
- **Patient care impact:** High—treatment delays due to inadequate PA information and complex workflows; patient safety risks from evidence-inconsistent step therapy requirements

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "Electronic transmission alone will not substantially reduce burden if clinicians continue to lack information about the specific requirements necessary for approval." - Context: Arguing for point-of-care PA information alongside ePA.

2. "As prior authorization requirements proliferate, practices, which may have only one full time staff person or less dedicated to prior authorizations, do not have the capacity to respond." - Context: Highlighting practice-level burden from delayed PA responses.

3. 4. "SGIM recommends that the majority of prior authorizations that were approved once, be valid indefinitely." - Context: Recommending indefinite PA validity for maintenance therapies.

## 12. THEMES

- [x] **PROVIDER-BURDEN** - Core concern: ePA without point-of-care information, 72-hour delays, step therapy discovery post-denial, and repeated reauthorizations all impose substantial burden on practices; small practices with <1 FTE for PAs especially affected.
- [x] **PATIENT-ACCESS** - Treatment interruptions from delayed determinations, reauthorization disruptions, and step therapy barriers delay clinically appropriate care and cause patient frustration.
- [x] **TRANSPARENCY** - Drug-level (not aggregate) PA metrics needed; point-of-care step therapy and PA requirement visibility essential; each plan's unique opaque requirements create inequitable access.
- [x] **EXISTING-WORKFLOWS** - Real-time benefit tools already exist for cost-sharing; same infrastructure should surface PA requirements and step therapy information at point of prescribing.

- [x] **HEALTH-EQUITY** - Plan-unique step therapy requirements create inequitable access; older adults especially vulnerable to evidence-inconsistent drug sequences; chronic condition patients disrupted by annual formulary changes.
