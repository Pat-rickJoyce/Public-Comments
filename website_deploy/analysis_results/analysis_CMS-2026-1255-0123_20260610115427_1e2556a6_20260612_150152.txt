**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

EVRESA submits this operational impact statement to explain how CMS-0062-P may affect independent community pharmacies that lack chain-pharmacy IT infrastructure, internal compliance systems, and regulatory affairs staff. The comment supports EVRESA's broader governance proof recommendation by arguing that electronic PA standardization will interact with cumulative audit burden, pharmacist-physician coordination, 340B compliance, high-volume drug automation, and pharmacy revenue recovery.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0057-F; HIPAA privacy requirements; state pharmacy practice acts; HRSA 340B compliance obligations; DEA controlled substance regulations; PBM contracts; NCPDP standards.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards; standardized electronic prior authorization environment; governance proof infrastructure; cryptographic verification and attested evidence packages.

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

**Topic 1: Independent Community Pharmacy Implementation Reality**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Independent pharmacies are underrepresented in federal interoperability comments and often lack the IT, compliance, and regulatory staff resources available to chain pharmacies.
- **Specific request:** CMS should recognize these operational realities in sub-regulatory implementation guidance.

**Topic 2: Cumulative Audit Defense Burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Independent pharmacies face PBM, state board, DEA, HRSA 340B, and Medicaid program integrity audits; each requires record location, documentation gathering, clinical context reconstruction, response drafting, and tracking.
- **Specific request:** Recognize governance proof infrastructure as a way to turn audit response from retrospective reconstruction into queries against attested evidence packages.

**Topic 3: PBM Audit Defense**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Standardized denial reasons improve transparency but do not by themselves reduce later PBM audit burden over pharmacy actions taken after denials.
- **Specific request:** Encourage infrastructure that captures pharmacist clinical reasoning, patient counseling, and appeal-pursuit decisions in audit-ready form.

**Topic 4: Pharmacist-Physician Coordination**
- **Provision ID:** PROVIDER-BURDEN-related GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacists often act as operational coordinators between patients, prescribers, and PBMs, especially for peer-to-peer reviews and appeals; compressed timeframes will intensify this work.
- **Specific request:** Recognize structured coordination workflows that document requests, physician determinations, supporting evidence, timing, and ties to the PA record.

**Topic 5: 340B Audit Defense**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** 340B eligibility depends on patient, prescriber, drug, and Medicaid duplicate-discount criteria; incomplete or fragmented documentation makes later audits difficult and costly.
- **Specific request:** Recognize governance proof as an overlay above pharmacy management systems, 340B TPAs, payer claims systems, and Medicaid systems to create attested packages at dispensing.

**Topic 6: High-Volume Drug Categories and Automation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** GLP-1 volume growth illustrates how automated utilization management can create operational pressure and opaque denial processes for pharmacies.
- **Specific request:** Consider governance proof infrastructure so volume growth and payer automation do not create proportional documentation burden for pharmacists.

**Topic 7: Appeal Outcomes and Pharmacy Revenue**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Successful appeals recover patient access and pharmacy revenue; failed appeals can mean lost dispensing revenue, alternative dispensing, refund, or absorbed cost.
- **Specific request:** Recognize that better documentation, coordination, and appeal timing can support independent pharmacy sustainability.

**5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES**

- **S16/S17:** Supports specific denial reasons but emphasizes that structured reasons must connect to later audit defense, appeal documentation, and pharmacist workflow evidence.
- **S20/S21:** Notes compressed PA timeframes may improve timeliness but will compress pharmacist-physician coordination into shorter windows without reducing complexity.
- **S22:** Identifies operational cost and resource pressures on independent pharmacies, especially where they lack centralized audit and compliance infrastructure.
- **S25:** Discusses high-volume drug categories and automated utilization management in pharmacy workflows, using GLP-1 medications as an example.
- **S48/S49:** Addresses cost and small-entity-like impact by explaining that independent pharmacies face resource constraints and uncompensated coordination burdens.
- **S51:** Explains challenges for entities outside large payer/provider systems, including pharmacies operating in a multi-authority compliance environment.
- **S66-S82:** Relates to broader RFIs by addressing resiliency, payer API implementation effects, and operational realities under standardized electronic workflows.

**6. RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. Recognize independent community pharmacy operational realities in CMS-0062-P implementation guidance.
2. Consider cumulative audit defense burden across PBM, state board, DEA, HRSA 340B, and Medicaid program integrity audits.
3. Encourage governance proof infrastructure that creates attested evidence packages at the time of dispensing or PA action.
4. Recognize pharmacist-physician coordination as a structured workflow needing documentation, measurement, and potential support.
5. Account for 340B documentation and audit-defense needs in standardized electronic PA implementation.
6. Consider how high-volume drug categories and payer automation affect pharmacist documentation and appeal burden.
7. Recognize appeal-driven revenue recovery as a financial sustainability issue for independent community pharmacies.

**7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS**

- **Primary concerns:** Independent pharmacies may experience standardized electronic PA as an added audit and coordination pressure unless governance, documentation, and workflow support are recognized.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Multi-hour audit reconstruction work, PBM chargebacks or claim reversals, contract termination risk, uncompensated coordination, lost dispensing revenue, patient therapy delays, and increased documentation burden from high-volume automated denials.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of chain-level IT infrastructure, limited staff, no centralized audit response teams, fragmented 340B evidence, opaque automated utilization management, and overlapping compliance regimes.

**8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE**

- **Data or statistics cited:** Independent pharmacies may receive twenty or more audit-related communications per calendar year; GLP-1 medications experienced substantial volume growth through 2025.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Stakeholder reporting around CMS-0057-F implementation through 2025 and trade association public statements/comment filings for independent community pharmacies.
- **Examples provided:** PBM audits leading to chargebacks; pharmacist coordinating peer-to-peer review; HRSA 340B audit reconstruction; GLP-1 automated denial and appeal workflows.

**9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT**

- **Affected stakeholders:** Independent community pharmacies, pharmacists, patients, prescribing physicians, PBMs, state boards of pharmacy, HRSA, DEA, Medicaid program integrity contractors, 340B-covered entities, and payers.
- **Economic implications:** Appeal outcomes directly affect pharmacy revenue stability; audit defense consumes pharmacist labor; PBM audit findings can produce chargebacks, reversals, or contract risks.
- **Technical feasibility:** EVRESA proposes network-level governance proof service arrangements that operate above existing pharmacy, 340B, claims, and PA systems.
- **Patient care impact:** Better coordination and appeal documentation may reduce medication access delays, while compressed timeframes without workflow support may shift more burden onto pharmacists.
