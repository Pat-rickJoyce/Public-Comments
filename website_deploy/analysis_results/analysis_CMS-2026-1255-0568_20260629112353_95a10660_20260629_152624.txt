## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Tennessee Hospital Association (THA), signed by Rodney R. Adams, Sr. Vice President, Finance & Reimbursement.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider representative.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents more than 160 Tennessee healthcare facility members, including hospitals and health systems.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

THA strongly supports CMS's proposal to extend interoperability and electronic prior authorization requirements to drugs, especially drugs covered under the medical benefit. THA emphasizes that implementation must account for hospital operational readiness, rural and small provider constraints, technology transition costs, testing needs, and the risk that reporting or standards transitions could create new burdens if not phased and coordinated.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0057-F referenced; no CFR citations mentioned.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based interoperability standards; standardized APIs; existing ANSI transactions; electronic prior authorization; API endpoint and usage metrics.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extending electronic prior authorization to drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Tennessee hospitals experience administrative burden and patient care delays from fragmented PA processes for drugs under medical and pharmacy benefits. Extending CMS-0057-F's framework to drugs would create a more unified and predictable authorization process.
- **Specific request:** Finalize drug ePA requirements using standardized APIs, especially for physician-administered drugs, infusion therapies, oncology treatments, biologics, and other medically necessary therapies.

**Topic 2: Coverage and documentation requirements in the PA API**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Providers spend substantial time identifying payer-specific requirements that are not easily accessible or consistently communicated.
- **Specific request:** Incorporate coverage rules and documentation requirements directly into the Prior Authorization API so providers can view requirements and status earlier in the process.

**Topic 3: Implementation timeline and transition flexibility**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS (by implication) / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Hospitals, especially rural hospitals and smaller community providers, face workforce shortages, financial pressures, and technology implementation challenges. Transitioning from existing ANSI transactions to updated electronic standards requires coordination among hospitals, health systems, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, and payers.
- **Specific request:** Maintain flexibility, consider phased implementation, allow a temporary transition period where current transaction standards remain available alongside new standards, and provide sufficient testing before deadlines.

**Topic 4: API endpoint and usage metric reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with caution.
- **Rationale:** Reporting endpoint and usage metrics can help CMS evaluate adoption, efficiency, burden reduction, and communication improvements, but overly broad requirements may create new administrative burdens.
- **Specific request:** Keep reporting requirements targeted and balanced so they do not unintentionally burden providers and payers.

**Topic 5: Reducing manual, fax, and portal workflows**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Manual, fax-based, and portal-based PA workflows create inefficiencies and divert clinical staff from patient care.
- **Specific request:** Use standardized electronic workflows to reduce manual processes and increase operational efficiency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S18/S37:** Supports requiring standardized API-based ePA, while emphasizing operational readiness, phased implementation, testing, and provider constraints.
- **S12:** Identifies technical and operational challenges for hospitals, including technology development, workflow redesign, staff education, and coordination with EHR vendors, clearinghouses, and payers.
- **S21:** Supports drug ePA but asks CMS to consider implementation timelines and readiness, especially for rural/small providers.
- **S23/S29/S34/S35:** Supports API endpoint and usage metric reporting, provided reporting is targeted and balanced to avoid new administrative burden.
- **S44/S46/S47/S53/S54:** Although not directly framed as HIPAA standards, THA addresses transition from existing ANSI transactions to new standards and recommends coexistence/temporary transition, phasing, and testing.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize extension of interoperability and prior authorization requirements to drugs.
2. Require impacted payers to support ePA using standardized APIs.
3. Incorporate coverage rules and documentation requirements directly into the Prior Authorization API.
4. Recognize operational and financial challenges for rural hospitals and smaller community providers.
5. Provide adequate time for technology development, workflow redesign, staff education, and coordination with payer partners.
6. Allow a temporary transition period where current transaction standards remain available alongside new standards.
7. Consider phased implementation where appropriate.
8. Ensure sufficient testing opportunities before full implementation deadlines.
9. Require API endpoint and usage metrics but keep reporting targeted and balanced.
10. Continue collaboration with providers, hospitals, health plans, vendors, and other stakeholders.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Operational readiness, financial and workforce constraints, transition from existing ANSI transactions, insufficient testing, and reporting burden.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disrupted continuity of care, delayed treatment initiation, provider burden, patient confusion, and implementation disruption for rural and smaller providers.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Workforce shortages, financial pressure, technology implementation challenges, EHR vendor coordination, clearinghouse coordination, payer coordination, workflow redesign, and staff education needs.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** THA represents more than 160 healthcare facility members. No additional quantitative data provided.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Tennessee hospitals and health systems, rural hospitals, smaller community providers, patients needing time-sensitive therapies, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, payers, providers, and clinical staff.
- **Economic implications:** Implementation will require technology development, testing, workflow redesign, staff education, and coordination; financial pressures are significant for rural and smaller providers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible with phased implementation, temporary coexistence of current and new standards, and pre-deadline testing.
- **Patient care impact:** Standardized workflows and clearer documentation requirements could improve continuity of care, reduce confusion, and speed access for specialty, oncology, infusion, biologic, and other high-cost therapies.
