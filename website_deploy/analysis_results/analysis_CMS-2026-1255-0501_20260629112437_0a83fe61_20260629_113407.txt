## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Academy of Managed Care Pharmacy (AMCP), signed by Geni Tunstall, JD, Associate Vice President, Regulatory Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (managed care pharmacy practitioners)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** AMCP represents nearly 8,000 pharmacists, physicians, nurses, and other practitioners managing medication therapies for 270 million Americans across health plans, PBM firms, and government programs.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AMCP broadly supports the proposed rule's direction toward modernizing PA through ePA infrastructure and standardized reporting, but urges a 24-month implementation period and phased rollout to avoid system errors and patient access disruptions. AMCP also seeks refinements to the PA metrics reporting framework to preserve clinical integrity, allow pause of timeliness clocks during incomplete information periods, and clarify implementation across diverse program types.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, API-USAGE-METRICS, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS, GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specific (general reference to CMS-0062-P)
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, NCPDP standards (SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, RTPB), electronic health records (EHR), real-time benefit tools (RTBT)

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation Timeline**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The proposed October 1, 2027 timeline does not reflect the scope of required changes: modifying PA platforms, integrating new data exchange, establishing reporting and audit processes, updating workflows, coordinating with PBMs and technology vendors, and conducting user acceptance testing — all across MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP lines of business with distinct operational requirements. Insufficient time risks system errors, incomplete data exchange, and patient access disruptions.
- **Specific request:** Adopt a 24-month implementation period following publication of the final rule; use a phased approach with an initial technical implementation/testing period before reporting requirements go live.

**Topic 2: Standardized PA Metrics and Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Aggregate approval and denial rates across all drugs may obscure clinically meaningful variation by therapeutic class, disease severity, and evidence standards; risk being misinterpreted as indicators of inappropriate utilization management. Operational complexity of aggregating and validating data across internal systems and vendor platforms without clearly defined data elements is significant.
- **Specific request:** Permit stratification of metrics by therapeutic category, drug type, or indication; clarify whether plans may provide contextual information to aid interpretation; ensure reported data are not used for compliance/enforcement in isolation without considering clinical appropriateness.

**Topic 3: ePA Workflow and Timeliness Standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** AMCP supports FHIR as a meaningful modernization step, but timeliness standards must account for clinical complexity and incomplete information scenarios; provider adoption of ePA and RTBT remains low.
- **Specific request:** Allow plans to pause or exclude time periods during which the payer is awaiting sufficient information to complete clinical review; ensure timeliness standards are operationally feasible and account for clinical complexity; encourage greater provider adoption of ePA and RTBT.

**Topic 4: Scope Across Programs (Medicaid, CHIP, QHP)**
- **Provision ID:** STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** The proposal does not fully address operational differences among market types, particularly in Medicaid where state-specific policies and infrastructure vary significantly; plans operating across programs must harmonize workflows across distinct regulatory environments.
- **Specific request:** Provide additional guidance on implementation in state-administered Medicaid; consider flexibility or alternative compliance pathways for states and plans with differing technological maturity; clarify interaction with existing program-specific PA requirements.

**Topic 5: Data Validation, Auditing, and Clinical Standards**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The rule lacks explicit guidance on data validation, auditing, and standardized data definitions, raising concerns about consistency and comparability; no guidance on incorporating real-world evidence or evolving clinical standards into PA decision-making.
- **Specific request:** Address gaps in data validation guidance and clarify how clinical standards and real-world evidence may be incorporated into the ePA framework.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** AMCP flags technical challenges in ePA workflow implementation, particularly timeliness standards that must account for incomplete information and clinical complexity across diverse program types.
- **S18:** AMCP implicitly supports mandatory FHIR IGs as a modernization step but urges sufficient implementation time and phased rollout to avoid rushed deployment.
- **S20:** AMCP requests that timeliness clock be pausable when payer is awaiting sufficient clinical information; compressed timelines without this flexibility risk inappropriate denials or approvals.
- **S21:** AMCP states the proposed timeline is inadequate; 24 months from final rule publication is needed.
- **S29:** AMCP recommends stratification of metrics by therapeutic category, drug type, or indication; contextual information should be permissible; data should not be used for enforcement in isolation.
- **S34:** AMCP supports standardized reporting but urges phased implementation: technical readiness before reporting requirements go live.
- **S36/S38:** AMCP supports providing additional guidance and flexibility for state-administered Medicaid programs, noting significant infrastructure variation.
- **S37:** AMCP cites complexity of coordinating across MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP lines of business with distinct operational requirements as a key implementation challenge.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Adopt a 24-month implementation period following publication of the final rule.
2. Use a phased implementation strategy: initial period for technical implementation and testing, followed by operational reporting requirements.
3. Permit stratification of PA metrics by therapeutic category, drug type, or indication.
4. Clarify whether plans may provide contextual information alongside reported metrics.
5. Ensure reported metrics data are not used in isolation for compliance or enforcement without consideration of clinical appropriateness.
6. Allow plans to pause the timeliness clock while awaiting sufficient information needed for clinical review.
7. Ensure timeliness standards are operationally feasible and account for clinical complexity.
8. Encourage greater provider adoption of ePA and RTBT.
9. Provide additional guidance on implementation in state-administered Medicaid programs.
10. Consider flexibility or alternative compliance pathways for states and plans with differing technological maturity.
11. Clarify how the rule interacts with existing program-specific PA requirements.
12. Address gaps in data validation, auditing, and standardized data definitions.
13. Clarify how real-world evidence and evolving clinical standards may be incorporated into ePA decision-making.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Insufficient implementation timeline; aggregate metrics may misrepresent clinically appropriate utilization management; timeliness clocks that don't pause for incomplete information may increase inappropriate denials; Medicaid state variation not adequately addressed.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** System errors and incomplete data exchange from rushed implementation; disruption to patient access to medications; incentivized less rigorous utilization management from poorly interpreted metrics; increased costs from undermined clinical appropriateness.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Coordination complexity across MA, Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP with distinct operational requirements; need to modify PA platforms, integrate data exchange, establish reporting/audit processes, conduct user acceptance testing; low provider adoption of ePA requiring incentivization.

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health plans, PBMs, pharmacists and managed care practitioners, prescribing providers, EHR vendors, pharmacies, patients

- **Technical feasibility:** AMCP supports FHIR modernization but highlights the scope of required system changes; notes that provider ePA adoption remains a gap
- **Patient care impact:** Insufficient implementation time risks patient access disruptions; timeliness standards without information-pause provisions may generate incomplete-information denials
