## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Partnership to Protect Coverage patient and consumer organizations, including Cystic Fibrosis Foundation contact and listed national disease/patient advocacy organizations
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Coalition represents millions of patients and consumers with serious, acute, and chronic conditions, including individuals relying on ACA patient protections. Member organizations include disease-specific and consumer advocacy groups.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The coalition strongly supports extending electronic prior authorization standards to prescription drugs and FF-SHOP plans but urges CMS to add stronger patient protections, faster timeframes, continuity-of-care rules, detailed denial explanations, and meaningful public reporting. The comment emphasizes that electronic modernization must not simply accelerate existing utilization management practices; it must reduce delays, prevent inappropriate denials, protect stable patients from repeated PA/step therapy, and make PA data usable to patients, providers, researchers, and policymakers.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY; GENERAL/RFI laboratory tests, DMEPOS, step therapy, provider API adoption.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** APIs; Provider Access API; prior authorization APIs; electronic PA; e-prescribing and EHR adoption as implementation analogies.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extend electronic PA to prescription drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Prescription drugs are essential for chronic conditions, and excluding them from ePA standards forces patients and providers to manage multiple systems.
- **Specific request:** Finalize drug ePA extension; require real-time benefit pathway identification and consistency in terminology, documentation, and workflows across medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit drugs.

**Topic 2: Automation safeguards and clinical review**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Automation should improve timeliness and appropriateness, not simply speed inappropriate denials or replace clinical review.
- **Specific request:** Require qualified clinician review for complex denials and monitor denial/overturn rates.

**Topic 3: Continuity of care and repeat PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL/step therapy
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Stable patients on long-term medications face recurring or surprise PA requirements that can cause refill gaps and avoidable emergency care.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit unnecessary reauthorization for stable long-term therapies; require approvals to last for duration of treatment; prevent mid-course PA without continuity protections; honor existing approvals and step therapy exceptions during plan transitions.

**Topic 4: FF-SHOP extension**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** FF-SHOP insurers already offer individual market QHPs on the FFM, so burden is minimal and patient benefit is substantial.
- **Specific request:** Finalize extension to FF-SHOP and continue promoting consistent PA protections across coverage markets.

**Topic 5: Faster QHP timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients with chronic illness are harmed by delays, and automation should permit faster processing.
- **Specific request:** Require QHP standard requests to be resolved within 72 hours and expedited requests within 24 hours for non-drug items/services; require 24-hour responses for all QHP prescription drug PA requests; limit extensions to 168 hours/7 days and require notice and basis for extension.

**Topic 6: Public API and PA reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Disaggregated and public data are necessary to identify disparities and evaluate patient impacts.
- **Specific request:** Publicly post API usage data at least annually; disaggregate by provider type, plan, size, geography, service type, diagnosis, income, disability, gender, race/ethnicity, and age; standardize posting location, file type, layout, terminology, and consider a centralized dashboard.

**Topic 7: Denial explanations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Generic denial reasons do not allow patients or providers to correct, appeal, or identify covered alternatives.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed, plain-language denial notices identifying missing documents, coverage policy/clinical criteria, covered alternatives, appeal steps, timelines, and accessibility/language access.

**Topic 8: Laboratory tests, DMEPOS, and provider API adoption**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA delays for lab testing can delay diagnosis/treatment; DMEPOS denials can undermine independence; provider API adoption remains limited.
- **Specific request:** Ensure timely updates to providers and labs regardless of network status; require clear medical-necessity standards and appeals for DMEPOS; educate and support small/medium providers through pilots and guidance.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14/S15:** Supports extending ePA standards to drugs under both medical and pharmacy benefits and requests real-time benefit pathway identification.
- **S16/S17:** Requests more specific denial determinations, plain-language notices, missing-document detail, clinical criteria, alternatives, and appeal instructions.
- **S20/S43:** Urges 24-hour responses for all QHP prescription drug PA requests and shorter non-drug timeframes of 72 hours standard/24 hours expedited.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Supports API usage metric collection but urges public annual posting and disaggregation to identify access disparities.
- **S40/S41/S42:** Strongly supports applying ePA standards to FF-SHOP insurers and states burden should be minimal.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on laboratory tests, DMEPOS, step therapy, provider adoption, and patient-centered reporting.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize extension of electronic PA standards to drugs under medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Require real-time benefit pathway identification and consistent terminology/documentation across benefit types.
3. Require qualified clinical review for complex denials and monitor denial/overturn rates.
4. Prohibit unnecessary repeat PA for stable long-term therapies and require approvals for the duration of treatment.
5. Honor existing approvals and prior step-therapy exceptions during plan transitions.
6. Finalize FF-SHOP extension.
7. Shorten QHP non-drug PA timeframes to 72 hours standard and 24 hours expedited.
8. Require QHP prescription drug PA responses within 24 hours in all cases.
9. Treat missed decision deadlines as deemed denials triggering appeal rights.
10. Publicly post API usage data annually and disaggregate PA and API data across patient, provider, plan, geography, and service categories.
11. Standardize reporting location, format, layout, terminology, and consider a centralized public dashboard.
12. Strengthen denial notices with specific clinical criteria, missing documents, alternatives, appeal steps, and accessibility/language access.
13. Improve PA protections for laboratory tests and DMEPOS and support provider API adoption through pilots, education, and guidance.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays, improper denials, repeat PA for stable patients, surprise PA at refill, insufficient QHP timeframes, opaque reporting, vague denial reasons, and low provider API adoption.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment abandonment, gaps in care, exacerbation of symptoms, emergency department visits, delayed lab diagnoses, DMEPOS access barriers, and unequal patient access.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Inconsistent website posting, nonstandard data formats, insufficient provider funding/expertise, vendor/payer coordination challenges, and limited adoption of Provider Access APIs.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Cites KFF polling that prior authorizations rank as the public's biggest burden; cites WEDI survey showing 90% of medical plans started API implementation by January 1, 2027, while only 33% of providers indicated likely Provider Access API implementation; cites literature on e-prescribing and EHR adoption.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF prior authorization polling; KFF analysis of insurer PA data; WEDI interoperability and PA survey; Health Affairs e-prescribing adoption study; JMIR EHR adoption review; CMS announcement on ePA early adopters.
- **Examples provided:** Stable patients facing yearly/surprise PA at refill; patients switching plans and restarting PA/step therapy; DMEPOS denials for medical necessity; lab test delays affecting diagnosis and treatment planning.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with chronic, serious, acute, rare, and disabling conditions; clinicians; providers; laboratories; QHP issuers; FF-SHOP enrollees; plans; researchers; policymakers; small and medium practices.
- **Economic implications:** Repeat PA and treatment gaps waste resources; provider API adoption may require funding, education, and vendor support.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supportive, but adoption depends on provider readiness, standardization, and meaningful implementation assistance.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment emphasizes that PA delays can cause treatment gaps, emergency visits, delayed diagnosis, and disrupted chronic disease management.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with strong modifications
- **Tone:** Patient-centered; policy-focused; constructive
- **Priority level:** High
