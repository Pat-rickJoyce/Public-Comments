**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

EVRESA responds to RFI III.A on electronic event notifications by arguing that prior authorization-triggered notifications should include governance controls for provenance, authority, delivery integrity, recipient acknowledgement, and evidence continuity. The comment does not ask CMS to mandate a specific notification platform, but recommends that future guidance recognize governance proof infrastructure as a layer above PA transactions and event notifications.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING/API-USAGE-METRICS only indirectly through notification and workflow integrity.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; 2020 CMS Interoperability and Patient Access final rule, 85 FR 25510; ONC HTI-1, 89 FR 1192.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic event notifications; standardized electronic PA workflows; governance proof infrastructure; tamper-evident integrity; notification provenance attestation.

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

**Topic 1: Event Notifications as Governance Triggers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Event notifications initiate downstream care coordination, medication reconciliation, follow-up outreach, and value-based care actions; their accuracy, timing, and authority basis affect whether downstream action is appropriate.
- **Specific request:** Recognize that event notifications connected to PA workflows may need authority and evidence attestation.

**Topic 2: Notification Provenance**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization decisions can trigger notifications to prescribers, pharmacies, patients, primary care providers, and care coordinators. Recipients may act based on the notification and need to know the triggering decision was authorized.
- **Specific request:** Encourage governance-layer controls that allow recipients to verify the provenance of triggering PA events.

**Topic 3: Notification Integrity Across Multi-Stakeholder Workflows**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Care coordination breaks down if different stakeholders receive different content, if some do not receive notifications, or if deadlines and responsibilities are not communicated consistently.
- **Specific request:** Encourage tamper-evident notification integrity attestation confirming content, delivery, recipients, acknowledgements, and actions.

**Topic 4: Prior Authorization Decisions as Notification Triggers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Approvals, denials, requests for additional information, and exceptions are triggering events for downstream care coordination.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate recognition of decision provenance, notification provenance, and care coordination evidence packages.

**Topic 5: Care Coordination Evidence Packages**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Notifications alone do not create effective care coordination unless recipient, authority basis, next action, deadline, and responsibility are clear and auditable.
- **Specific request:** Encourage evidence packages that travel with workflows and accumulate attestations at handoff points.

**Topic 6: Independent Pharmacy Notification Asymmetry**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Entities with internal infrastructure can integrate notifications, while independent pharmacies may receive notifications without practical ability to structure the resulting work.
- **Specific request:** Recognize network-level governance proof infrastructure accessible to entities without proprietary compliance systems.

**5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES**

- **S66-S82:** This filing is a direct response to RFI III.A on electronic event notifications for value-based care and care coordination. EVRESA recommends that CMS consider notification provenance, authority attestation, delivery integrity, care coordination handoff documentation, and evidence continuity for PA-related event notifications.
- **S16/S17:** Indirectly addresses denial reason implementation by emphasizing that denial notifications should preserve decision provenance, consistent content, deadlines, and downstream action responsibilities.
- **S23/S30:** Indirectly addresses security/privacy by proposing governance-layer controls, tamper-evident records, and provenance attestation for notifications.
- **S29/S35:** Indirectly relates to metrics and value-based care attribution by proposing coordinated evidence packages that support quality measurement and payment adjustment decisions.

**6. RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. Recognize in future event-notification or implementation guidance that PA-related notifications may benefit from governance-layer controls.
2. Encourage documentation of notification provenance, decision authority, delivery integrity, recipient acknowledgement where appropriate, and continuity with downstream care coordination actions.
3. Treat PA approvals, denials, requests for information, and exceptions as potential notification triggers requiring auditable handoffs.
4. Encourage care coordination evidence packages that include triggering decision, notification provenance, next action, deadline, responsible party, and subsequent attestations.
5. Consider the needs of independent community pharmacies that lack internal infrastructure to integrate and act on notifications.
6. Avoid mandating a specific vendor, platform, or notification technology.

**7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS**

- **Primary concerns:** Notifications without provenance, integrity, and handoff documentation may create downstream action burdens without reliable evidence or accountability.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Incorrect or inconsistent downstream actions, missed appeal deadlines, failed care coordination, unreliable value-based care attribution, and practical disadvantage for independent pharmacies.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-stakeholder workflows, fragmented notification delivery, differing notification content, lack of recipient acknowledgement, unclear authority basis, and limited infrastructure among smaller entities.

**8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE**

- **Data or statistics cited:** None.
- **Studies or research referenced:** 2020 CMS Interoperability and Patient Access final rule; ONC HTI-1; EVRESA's foundational CMS-0062-P comment.
- **Examples provided:** Denial reason delivery to prescriber/pharmacy/patient/care coordinator; additional information requests; appeal deadline notification; peer-to-peer review request delivery; pharmacist appeal initiation; care manager scheduling follow-up; provider therapy adjustment.

**9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT**

- **Affected stakeholders:** Prescribing providers, dispensing pharmacies, patients/beneficiaries, primary care providers, care coordinators, pharmacists, value-based care entities, payers, and technology vendors.
- **Economic implications:** More auditable handoffs may support value-based care quality measurement, payment adjustment, and outcome attribution; missed or inconsistent notifications could create administrative waste and appeal losses.
- **Technical feasibility:** EVRESA describes governance proof infrastructure as an overlay that can provide tamper-evident notification records and coordinated evidence packages without replacing existing event notification frameworks.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved notification integrity and handoff documentation could reduce missed follow-ups, missed appeals, and care coordination gaps after PA decisions.
