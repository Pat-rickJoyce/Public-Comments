# Comment Analysis: CMS-2026-1255-0089

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Brian Nyquist, MPH, Chief Executive Officer, National Infusion Center Association (NICA).
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Nonprofit representing non-hospital, community-based infusion centers; CEO has MPH credential.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NICA supports CMS's proposal to add medical benefit drugs to the Prior Authorization API and supports greater PA transparency, drug PA metrics, and API access to PA information. It asks CMS and ONC to reduce fragmentation between medical and pharmacy benefit workflows, align drug PA timeframes toward a 24-hour maximum, retain formulary information in APIs, and improve step therapy continuity and exceptions.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API; Patient Access API; Provider Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Provider Directory API generally. No specific FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medical benefit drug electronic PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA for medical benefit drugs can improve access to provider-administered medications.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the October 1, 2027 requirement while improving coordination across benefit types.

**Topic 2: Medical and pharmacy benefit fragmentation**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Infused and injectable therapies may fall under either benefit depending on formulation, site-of-care policy, specialty pharmacy requirements, or payer design, forcing providers into separate systems.
- **Specific request:** Continue developing seamless interoperability between medical and pharmacy benefit PA systems.

**Topic 3: Denial reasons, metrics, and drug-specific reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons and public metrics would help infusion providers identify and address coverage barriers.
- **Specific request:** Include drug-specific public reporting, not only broad aggregate drug metrics.

**Topic 4: PA decision timeframes and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Delays disrupt treatment scheduling and can interrupt medically necessary therapies.
- **Specific request:** Align toward a 24-hour maximum response timeframe for prescription drug PA requests across impacted payers, including Medicare Advantage, and enforce compliance with CMPs.

**Topic 5: API content and formulary information**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Clinically meaningful PA information supports care coordination, while removing formulary information would impair coverage verification for infused and injectable therapies.
- **Specific request:** Include coverage criteria, documentation requirements, step therapy protocols, approval duration, reauthorization rules, and retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 6: Step therapy continuity**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy requirements can delay initiation or continuation of provider-administered drugs, especially during payer transitions.
- **Specific request:** Develop data exchange tools to recognize prior approvals, step therapy history, and utilization management determinations, with robust exceptions and appeals.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports adding medical benefit drugs to the Prior Authorization API but highlights cross-benefit workflow challenges for infused and injectable therapies.
- **S15:** Directly addresses medical vs pharmacy benefit distinctions, warning that therapies can move between benefit categories and cause fragmented workflows.
- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons for drug PA requests.
- **S20/S43:** Urges a 24-hour maximum drug PA response timeframe across impacted payers and benefit categories.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Supports public PA metrics and asks that reporting include drug-specific information useful to providers and patients.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to general RFI issues around step therapy, payer transitions, continuity of therapy, appeals, and interoperability-enabled utilization management history.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for medical benefit drugs beginning October 1, 2027.
2. Improve interoperability between medical and pharmacy benefit PA workflows.
3. Require specific denial reasons and drug-specific public PA metrics.
4. Align all impacted payers toward a 24-hour maximum drug PA response timeframe.
5. Enforce PA timeframe compliance, including CMPs where appropriate.
6. Include clinically meaningful PA details in Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
7. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
8. Support clinician-matched peer-to-peer review and longer approval continuity.
9. Build interoperability tools for step therapy history, prior approvals, and exceptions.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Benefit bifurcation, fragmented utilization management, PA delays, loss of formulary information, insufficient step therapy continuity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, disrupted infusion scheduling, avoidable interruptions in therapy, increased provider administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Separate medical and pharmacy benefit systems, payer transitions, differing benefit designs, specialty pharmacy requirements.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Infused and injectable therapies covered under either medical or pharmacy benefit; patients arriving for scheduled infusion without resolved authorization; payer transitions causing repeat utilization management.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Infusion providers, prescribers, patients receiving provider-administered medications, impacted payers, CMS/ONC.
- **Economic implications:** NICA frames community infusion centers as efficient, cost-effective alternatives to hospital settings but does not quantify cost impacts.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports API expansion but stresses need for richer data content and cross-benefit coordination.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved PA interoperability could reduce treatment delays and preserve continuity of therapy for complex, rare, and chronic diseases.
