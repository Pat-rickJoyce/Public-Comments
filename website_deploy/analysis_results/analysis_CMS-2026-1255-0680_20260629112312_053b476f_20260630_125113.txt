# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Medicare Rights Center; signed by Lindsey Copeland, Federal Policy Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National nonprofit serving people with Medicare, family caregivers, and professionals; comments informed by decades helping Medicare beneficiaries access prescription drugs and navigate appeals.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Medicare Rights Center supports CMS efforts to reduce prior authorization burden, improve transparency, and extend protections for drug-related prior authorization, but urges CMS to broaden the rule to include Part D and supplemental benefits. The commenter emphasizes specific denial notices, stronger public reporting, privacy protections for apps, oversight and enforcement, and limits on step therapy and other access barriers.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; PATIENT-ACCESS-related GENERAL/RFI issues; TRANSPARENCY-related GENERAL issues.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** RIN 0938-AV44; CMS-0062-P; 89 Fed. Reg. 8758, including page 8897. No CFR sections are cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic API access is referenced generally; no specific FHIR IG, NCPDP, HL7, or HIPAA standard is discussed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Broad applicability of prior authorization protections**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Medicare Rights argues that prior authorization safeguards should apply wherever prior authorization or overlapping payer responsibility can delay care, including Original Medicare contexts and MA supplemental benefits.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that PA rules and protections apply broadly to supplemental benefits, including medications and non-medication services/items, with electronic API access, notice and appeal rights, and timeframes.

**Topic 2: Specific denial reasons and standardized notices**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports requiring specific denial reasons but states generic medical-necessity explanations are inadequate and prevent enrollees and providers from correcting requests or appealing effectively.
- **Specific request:** Establish standardized denial notice templates for all payers, including MA and Part D sponsors, covering drug and non-drug denials; require clinical, coverage, and procedural information, including criteria relied on, alternatives, next steps, and deadlines.

**Topic 3: Oversight and consequences for noncompliant denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Medicare Rights cites OIG findings that many MA denial letters contained incomplete or incorrect information and states similar problems continue.
- **Specific request:** Conduct regular audits of plan communication requirements and apply meaningful penalties, including invalidating noncompliant denials.

**Topic 4: Public reporting of prior authorization metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports public reporting but argues existing contract-level reporting is too difficult to find and too aggregated to help enrollees, advocates, researchers, or oversight bodies identify problematic utilization management patterns.
- **Specific request:** Require MA and Part D plans to report at plan level, include disability and demographic data, disaggregate by treatment, service, or drug class, and link results prominently on plan websites.

**Topic 5: Include Part D in transparency requirements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter objects that Part D is excluded from public reporting even though prescription drug access is central to the rule's patient-access goals.
- **Specific request:** Include Part D in public prior authorization reporting requirements.

**Topic 6: Patient privacy and app data use**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Increased app use may expose sensitive health information to secondary commercial use.
- **Specific request:** Prevent companies from packaging or selling sensitive medical data without express user permission.

**Topic 7: Step therapy for stable patients**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Oppose/Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring patients stable on a medication to repeat step therapy interferes with patient-provider decisions, delays care, discourages treatment, and may increase long-term costs.
- **Specific request:** Discourage step therapy generally and, at minimum, curtail step therapy when a patient is stable on a treatment plan.

**Topic 8: Laboratory tests and DMEPOS prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization for DMEPOS and other services can impose substantial administrative burden on beneficiaries and families, especially when multiple providers or Medicare/Medicaid coordination are involved.
- **Specific request:** Consider patient and family burden, improve coordination for dually eligible individuals, and ensure prior approval processes reduce rather than add delays.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The comment indirectly responds by urging broad application of electronic prior authorization protections for medical-benefit and supplemental-benefit items, including API access, notices, appeals, and timeframes.
- **S15:** The comment addresses boundary issues by asking CMS to clarify that protections apply where overlapping coverage or supplemental benefits make payer responsibility confusing.
- **S16:** The commenter supports specific denial reasons and asks CMS to define required denial content in a way that identifies the basis for denial, the policy or criterion used, alternatives, next steps, and deadlines.
- **S17:** The commenter supports more structured denial content, including specific information tied to step therapy or insufficient documentation, though it does not expressly discuss structured codes.
- **S29:** The commenter recommends more complete public reporting, including plan-level data, demographic data, and treatment/service/drug-class detail, although its focus is prior authorization metrics rather than API operational metrics.
- **S30:** The commenter raises privacy concerns about app-mediated access and asks CMS to prevent secondary sale or packaging of sensitive medical data without express permission.
- **S34:** The comment does not propose a specific frequency but urges CMS to strengthen public reporting so data are timely, usable, and visible on plan websites.
- **S35:** The commenter supports public availability but argues aggregated reporting is insufficient; it asks for plan-level and disaggregated reporting.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to general RFI topics on step therapy, laboratory tests, and DMEPOS by emphasizing patient access, beneficiary burden, and care coordination.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Include Part D in public prior authorization reporting requirements.
2. Require plan-level reporting for MA and Part D plans rather than only contract-level reporting.
3. Require demographic data, including disability status, and disaggregation by treatment, service, or drug class.
4. Require public reporting links to be prominent and clear on plan websites.
5. Standardize denial notice templates for drug and non-drug denials across payers, including MA and Part D sponsors.
6. Require denial notices to identify the denial basis, applicable policy or criterion, approvable alternatives, corrective actions, and deadlines.
7. Audit plan compliance with communication requirements and impose penalties, including invalidating noncompliant denials.
8. Clarify that supplemental benefits are subject to PA protections, including electronic API access, notices, appeals, and timeframes.
9. Protect app users from unauthorized commercial use or sale of sensitive medical data.
10. Discourage step therapy generally and curtail it when a patient is stable on treatment.
11. Account for patient and family administrative burden in lab and DMEPOS prior authorization policies.
12. Improve DME access coordination for dually eligible individuals.
13. End the WISeR model because the commenter views it as inconsistent with CMS access goals.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Part D exclusion from reporting; vague denial notices; insufficiently disaggregated PA metrics; inadequate oversight; app privacy risks; step therapy disruptions; DMEPOS and dual-eligible coordination barriers; WISeR model access barriers.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed care, inability to appeal effectively, discriminatory or unfair utilization management, treatment disruption, patient/family stress, higher long-term costs, and unauthorized use of sensitive medical data.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Confusing supplemental benefit appeals, buried plan reporting, contract-level aggregation, overlapping MA/Medicaid responsibility, incomplete denial communication, and coordination burdens among providers, suppliers, plans, and beneficiaries.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** OIG finding that 45% of MA plans sent denial letters with incomplete or incorrect information in 2015.
- **Studies or research referenced:** OIG report on MA denials; KFF analysis of insurer prior authorization data; Medicare Rights prior comments and Medicare Watch analysis; Health Affairs article on MA supplemental benefits; studies on step therapy and access restrictions; Washington Post reporting on WISeR; Medicare Interactive DME advocacy guide.
- **Examples provided:** Step therapy for stable patients; power wheelchairs for dually eligible individuals where Medicare and Medicaid coverage standards differ; generic denial messages stating lack of medical necessity; public reports buried on plan websites.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Medicare beneficiaries, dually eligible individuals, MA and Part D enrollees, family caregivers, providers, DMEPOS suppliers, MA organizations, Part D sponsors, advocates, researchers, and app users.
- **Economic implications:** The commenter argues step therapy and poor coordination may create long-term individual, plan, and program costs despite short-term plan savings; enhanced reporting and notices may impose payer compliance obligations but improve oversight and decision-making.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment does not challenge technical feasibility of APIs; it focuses on policy design, data completeness, usability, privacy, and oversight.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong patient-access focus; the commenter argues clearer notices, broader protections, reporting, and limits on step therapy would reduce delays and improve access to medications, services, and DME.
