# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Breakthrough T1D, formerly JDRF
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Leading global type 1 diabetes research and advocacy organization; describes T1D clinical burden, population estimates, and reliance on insulin, CGMs, pumps, and automated insulin delivery systems.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Breakthrough T1D supports CMS's electronic prior authorization modernization but argues the reforms must also address whether PA is clinically appropriate. The comment urges CMS to reduce or eliminate PA for evidence-based standard-of-care T1D therapies and related devices/supplies, ensure FHIR-based API protections apply across pharmacy and medical benefits, create streamlined or automatic approvals where clinical information supports routine therapies, and strengthen accountability for decision timelines.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR; Prior Authorization APIs; electronic prior authorization; EHR integration.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA modernization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** ePA for prescription drugs and devices can improve timeliness, transparency, and quality of information shared among providers, beneficiaries, and payers.
- **Specific request:** Finalize and implement electronic PA reforms while measuring success by fewer beneficiary delays and disruptions, not just digitized processing.

**Topic 2: Appropriate use of prior authorization for standard-of-care T1D therapies**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Insulin, CGMs, insulin pumps, and related technologies are continuous, life-sustaining, standard-of-care therapies for T1D, and PA often functions as a barrier rather than a program integrity tool.
- **Specific request:** Reduce or eliminate PA requirements for broadly accepted, evidence-based standard-of-care chronic condition therapies such as T1D treatments.

**Topic 3: FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs can integrate PA requests, documentation requirements, coverage criteria, and determinations into clinical systems and reduce manual, duplicative, fax-based workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to implement PA APIs using HL7 FHIR and design them for actual access improvements.

**Topic 4: Coverage of devices, supplies, and technologies tied to drug therapy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** For T1D, insulin access is linked to CGMs, insulin pumps, and supplies; excluding related items from ePA protections could undermine the rule.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that ePA requirements and protections apply to devices, supplies, and technologies needed to deliver, administer, or monitor therapies, regardless of pharmacy or medical benefit classification.

**Topic 5: Streamlined or automatic approvals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeated PA for renewal of insulin, CGMs, or pumps for patients with established T1D creates burden and risk without clear integrity value.
- **Specific request:** Establish streamlined pathways, automatic approvals, or exemptions for therapies routinely approved and supported by standards of care when sufficient clinical and diagnostic information is available.

**Topic 6: Timeline accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Even compliant review windows can interrupt chronic disease therapy; patients should not bear administrative delay risk.
- **Specific request:** Evaluate automatic approval when payers fail to respond within established timelines, especially for urgent or ongoing therapies.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs for drugs and emphasizes integration with EHRs and clinical workflows.
- **S15:** Requests clarity that protections apply across pharmacy and medical benefit classification for drugs and related devices/supplies used in T1D care.
- **S20:** Encourages stronger accountability for PA response timelines and automatic approval when plans miss deadlines for urgent or ongoing therapies.
- **S21:** Supports the API direction but emphasizes that implementation must produce fewer therapy disruptions and meaningful access improvements.
- **S37:** Discusses implementation challenges from fragmented payer requirements, documentation standards, and submission pathways.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to broader questions about appropriate PA use, chronic disease management, and whether technology alone addresses access barriers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA reforms for prescription drugs and associated therapies.
2. Ensure the goal is clinically appropriate PA use, not merely faster PA processing.
3. Reduce or eliminate PA for evidence-based standard-of-care chronic disease therapies such as insulin, CGMs, insulin pumps, and supplies.
4. Require FHIR-based PA APIs that integrate with EHRs and clinical systems.
5. Clarify that devices, supplies, and monitoring technologies needed for drug delivery or administration are covered by ePA protections regardless of benefit category.
6. Create automatic approval, streamlined review, or exemption pathways for routinely approved standard-of-care therapies when sufficient information is available.
7. Strengthen accountability for PA response timelines.
8. Consider automatic approval when payers fail to respond within required review windows, particularly for urgent or ongoing therapies.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Inappropriate PA for standard-of-care T1D therapies, delays in life-sustaining insulin or device access, fragmentation of payer requirements, repeated PA renewals, and digitizing administrative complexity without reducing it.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, therapy gaps, worsening outcomes, provider burden, and beneficiary risk.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Varying payer documentation standards, submission pathways, multiple provider/pharmacy/supplier interactions, manual and fax-based workflows, and benefit-category fragmentation.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Nearly 2 million Americans live with T1D, including nearly 600,000 Medicare enrollees and 300,000 Medicaid enrollees; T1D is associated with an 11-year shorter life expectancy and global average of 24 life-years lost; mortality rates are 3 to 18 times higher than the general population; fear of hypoglycemia affects up to 30% of adults with T1D.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Smith et al. 2024 on the U.S. T1D population; Starr et al. 2025 on breakthrough therapies and life-years lost; Grulovic et al. 2022 on diabetes-related distress; American Diabetes Association Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026.
- **Examples provided:** PA to verify a person "still has T1D"; repeated PA renewals for CGM supplies, insulin pumps, and insulin despite longstanding use and established necessity.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** People with T1D, caregivers, endocrinology providers, pharmacies, suppliers, payers, Medicare and Medicaid programs, and technology vendors.
- **Economic implications:** Streamlined or exempted PA could reduce provider administrative costs and avoid costs from preventable complications, while preserving PA for appropriate program integrity uses.
- **Technical feasibility:** FHIR-based APIs are feasible but must integrate with clinical systems and apply consistently to related devices and supplies.
- **Patient care impact:** Reducing unnecessary PA and strengthening timelines could prevent therapy interruptions, acute complications, and administrative barriers to essential care.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with significant modifications
- **Tone:** Professional, clinical, and patient-access focused
- **Priority level:** High
