## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter supports electronic prior authorization for drugs but argues the proposed dual-standard model creates a practical routing problem between FHIR-based medical benefit drug PA and NCPDP pharmacy benefit drug PA. He recommends CMS require payers to return an authoritative, machine-readable indication of the correct standard and endpoint for each drug, patient, and payer coverage context.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based Prior Authorization API; Da Vinci CRD; Da Vinci PAS IG; NCPDP SCRIPT; HL7 v2.3 ADT, SIU, ORM.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Authoritative routing between medical and pharmacy benefit standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The same drug may be covered under a medical or pharmacy benefit depending on payer terms, and the commenter states that leaving the payer's routing signal optional will cause incorrect submissions, rejections, dropped requests, and fallback to phone or fax.
- **Specific request:** Require the first-stage CRD response to return, in machine-readable form, which standard and endpoint the provider must use for the specific drug, patient, and payer coverage.

**Topic 2: Implementation of the dual-standard architecture**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The PAS IG warns against use for pharmacy-benefit medication PA when NCPDP exchange applies, but providers and EHRs lack a reliable way to determine the correct path without an authoritative payer response.
- **Specific request:** Make path determination a required capability rather than an optional convention.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The commenter identifies a technical challenge for medical benefit drug PA: the system must reliably determine whether FHIR or NCPDP applies before submission.
- **S15:** The comment directly addresses the medical versus pharmacy benefit distinction and argues that payer-specific benefit classification must be returned as a machine-readable routing answer.
- **S37:** The commenter describes an implementation challenge for required FHIR IG use: optional or undefined routing signals could undermine interoperability and drive workflows back to manual channels.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require payers to provide an authoritative, machine-readable routing decision for drug PA standard selection.
2. Include the required standard and endpoint in the first-stage CRD response.
3. Treat medical-versus-pharmacy path determination as a required implementation capability, not an optional convention.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Misrouting between FHIR and NCPDP standards; optional payer signaling; inability of requester systems to detect routing errors.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Wrong-standard submissions, rejected or silently dropped requests, fallback to fax or phone, added burden for providers, and delayed patient access.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer-specific and changing benefit classifications; lack of a statutory or regulatory definition separating medical and pharmacy benefit drugs.

- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter frames the solution as a standard message-routing pattern: centralize the authoritative decision in a payer response.
- **Patient care impact:** Incorrect routing could delay medication authorization and access.
