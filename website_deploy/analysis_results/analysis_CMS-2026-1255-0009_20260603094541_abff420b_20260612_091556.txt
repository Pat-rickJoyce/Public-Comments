# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** The ALS Association; signed by Richard D. Brennan Jr., M.A., Vice President, Federal Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents people living with ALS, families, caregivers, and clinicians; policy advocacy focused on ALS care, equipment, medications, and services.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The ALS Association supports CMS's effort to modernize prior authorization and interoperability, especially where electronic prior authorization can reduce delay and administrative burden. It asks CMS to strengthen the rule with patient-protective requirements for serious progressive diseases, including default expedited review, durable approvals, clear denial reasons, specialty clinical review, AI guardrails, stratified public reporting, payer-to-payer transfer of PA decisions, and prescription drug PA visibility through APIs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PATIENT-ACCESS via GENERAL; payer-to-payer via GENERAL; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR sections cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based APIs; electronic prior authorization; patient access APIs; payer-to-payer data exchange; real-time prior authorization; no specific IG names cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Default expedited PA for ALS and serious progressive conditions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ALS progresses quickly, and PA delays can cause preventable hospitalizations, loss of respiratory or mobility function, emergency care, and delayed access to life-sustaining equipment.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to treat PA requests for serious progressive conditions such as ALS as expedited by default and reduce decision windows toward real-time or near real-time decisions where clinically possible.

**Topic 2: Reauthorization and duration of approvals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeated PA for irreversible conditions adds burden and can interrupt access to medically necessary equipment and supplies.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited renewal processes, keep authorizations valid for the duration of treatment when clinically appropriate, and prohibit automated systems from shortening approval duration or increasing reauthorization frequency.

**Topic 3: Denial transparency and appeals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Vague denial language and correctable documentation issues disproportionately harm medically frail patients who may struggle to appeal.
- **Specific request:** Define "specific reason," prohibit vague denial language, require plans to seek missing information before denial, provide plain-language culturally and linguistically appropriate patient notices, and streamline expedited appeals.

**Topic 4: Prescription drug PA integration and APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug PA information available at the point of care would reduce delays, improve coordination, and support patients with complex medication needs and specialty pharmacy use.
- **Specific request:** Require prescription drug PA information to be integrated into Patient Access APIs and provider point-of-care workflows, including specialty pharmacies and home delivery.

**Topic 5: AI and automated decision-making safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Rare disease patients may be underrepresented in training data, and automated tools may undervalue anticipatory care or generate denials without meaningful clinical review.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit fully automated denials for life-sustaining equipment and require human clinical review by clinicians with relevant specialty expertise for AI-assisted determinations affecting access.

**Topic 6: Public reporting and equity oversight**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Stratified metrics can reveal disparities in denial rates, decision times, overturned denials, and denial rationales for people with disabilities or complex conditions.
- **Specific request:** Require comprehensive, timely, accessible public reporting stratified by service type, diagnosis, provider type, race and ethnicity, and age.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports modernization of PA for drugs and services but emphasizes technical implementation must reduce delay for medically complex patients rather than introduce new barriers.
- **S15:** Addresses the importance of including prescription drug PA across settings, including specialty pharmacies and home delivery, so benefit distinctions do not disrupt access.
- **S16:** Requests an enforceable definition of a specific denial reason and rejects vague language such as incomplete claim or failure to establish medical necessity.
- **S17:** Supports actionable denial explanations and patient-facing notice, though it does not specifically endorse structured denial codes.
- **S20:** Argues expedited timeframes should be default for ALS and similar progressive conditions and should move toward real-time or near real-time review when clinically possible.
- **S21:** Supports implementation if it delivers timely electronic PA for medically complex patients and avoids additional administrative burden.
- **S29:** Recommends PA metrics include service type, diagnosis, provider type, race and ethnicity, age, time to decision, overturned denials, and denial rationale.
- **S30:** Implies privacy-sensitive stratification should support oversight and equity, but does not discuss specific privacy protections.
- **S34:** Requests timely public reporting in accessible, user-friendly formats.
- **S35:** Supports public reporting for accountability and enforcement, especially to identify disparities.
- **S66-S82:** Provides general RFI-style recommendations on utilization management reform, gold-carding, AI safeguards, payer-to-payer PA transfer, and patient-centered access.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Treat PA requests for ALS and other serious progressive conditions as expedited by default.
2. Require expedited renewal and treatment-duration approvals for clinically appropriate recurring items and services.
3. Permit bundled PA requests for coordinated care and expedite the full bundle when one component qualifies.
4. Define specific denial reasons, prohibit vague denial language, and require plain-language patient notices.
5. Require specialty clinical expertise in PA determinations for neurodegenerative and rare disease care.
6. Integrate prescription drug PA information into Patient Access APIs and point-of-care workflows.
7. Require new payers to review and consider active prior authorizations from prior payers.
8. Prohibit fully automated denials for life-sustaining equipment and require human clinical review for AI-assisted determinations.
9. Publicly report stratified PA metrics and appeal outcomes in accessible formats.
10. Expand equitable gold-carding and reduce duplicative utilization management for established providers.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delays, inappropriate denials, opaque utilization management, repeated reauthorization, lack of specialty expertise, and insufficient AI safeguards.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Preventable hospitalizations, loss of respiratory or mobility function, delayed ventilators or communication devices, emergency care, treatment gaps during payer transitions, inequitable outcomes for rare disease patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative burden, fragmented pharmacy benefit workflows, specialty pharmacy integration, poor transparency, insufficient reviewer expertise, and algorithmic bias or opacity.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Nearly all MA enrollees are in plans requiring PA; ALS care in the United States is estimated around or above $1 billion annually.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Office of Inspector General findings that MA plans denied requests that met Medicare coverage requirements and sometimes denied for correctable documentation issues.
- **Examples provided:** Ventilators, advanced power wheelchairs, speech-generating devices, specialty pharmacy drugs, bundled coordinated ALS care, and anticipatory ventilatory support.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** People living with ALS and other serious progressive conditions, caregivers, clinicians, MA plans, Medicaid managed care, CHIP, QHPs, pharmacies, specialty pharmacies, and payers.
- **Economic implications:** Avoiding PA delays may reduce hospitalizations, emergency care, duplicative reviews, caregiver burden, and downstream costs associated with ALS.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter supports interoperable ePA and FHIR-based APIs but expects implementation to integrate with EHRs, patient access tools, pharmacies, and payer-to-payer exchange.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly positive if CMS adds expedited, transparent, clinically informed processes; harmful if automation or vague denials delay medically necessary care.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Tone:** Professional, patient-centered, collaborative, and urgent
