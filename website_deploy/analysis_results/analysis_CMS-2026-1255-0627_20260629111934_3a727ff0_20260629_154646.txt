# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Paul A. Salles, President & CEO, Louisiana Hospital Association (LHA).
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** LHA represents more than 150 hospitals and health systems in Louisiana.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

LHA supports extending CMS prior authorization interoperability requirements to drugs and to small-group FF-SHOP QHP issuers, emphasizing the burden of payer-specific workflows and the need for standardization. LHA also supports shorter decision timeframes, public PA metrics, and endpoint/usage transparency, but urges CMS to conduct demonstrations and delay implementation until standards are fully tested.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** HIPAA administrative simplification authority; no detailed CFR citations beyond the proposed rule reference.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, Coverage Requirements Discovery (CRD) IG, Prior Authorization Support (PAS) IG, MedicationRequest resource, HCPCS J-codes, APIs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extension of ePA standards to drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Variation in PA submission processes creates multiple workflows, administrative costs, reprocessing, appeals, and burnout. Existing CRD/PAS functionality can support medical benefit drugs.
- **Specific request:** Finalize requirements to extend standardized electronic PA and electronic data exchange reforms to drugs.

**Topic 2: Expansion of impacted payers, including FF-SHOP**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Including additional payer types increases standardization across plans and encourages provider adoption.
- **Specific request:** Finalize expansion to MA, Medicaid/CHIP FFS and managed care, QHPs, and FF-SHOP small-group QHPs.

**Topic 3: Shorter PA decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with stronger standards.
- **Rationale:** PA directly affects patient care and should be expedited when FHIR transactions provide required information electronically.
- **Specific request:** Apply 72-hour standard and 24-hour urgent timeframes across all medical services when FHIR technology is used, not just drugs.

**Topic 4: Updated interoperability standards and testing**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Updated standards can support federal alignment, but implementation must be fully developed and tested with provider participation.
- **Specific request:** Conduct demonstrations and delay the implementation date until viability and readiness are validated.

**Topic 5: Public reporting and accountability**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** PA metrics promote transparency and can guide oversight; plan-by-plan posting alone is not useful if buried on individual websites.
- **Specific request:** CMS should collect and publicly post PA metrics and use the data for oversight and enforcement.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S18/S37:** Supports mandatory electronic PA standards for drugs but urges demonstrations and delayed implementation after testing.
- **S12/S15/S21:** Supports extending medical benefit drug PA into the FHIR framework and believes existing CRD/PAS resources support this, but asks for testing before rollout.
- **S20/S43:** Supports shorter PA timeframes and asks CMS to apply 72-hour/24-hour timeframes more broadly across medical services when FHIR is used.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Strongly supports PA metrics reporting, CMS collection, public availability, and oversight use.
- **S40-S42:** Supports adding small-group QHP issuers on FF-SHOP to the requirements.
- **S44-S56/S60-S65:** Supports updated interoperability standards and HIPAA alignment but requests demonstrations and a delayed implementation date.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize extension of electronic PA standards to drugs.
2. Finalize expansion of impacted payers to include FF-SHOP small-group QHPs.
3. Require 72-hour standard and 24-hour urgent responses for all medical services when FHIR transactions are used.
4. Conduct demonstrations and delay implementation until standards are fully tested with provider participation.
5. Require CMS-centralized collection and publication of PA metrics.
6. Use public PA metrics to guide oversight and enforcement.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Payer-specific workflow variation, provider burnout, patient delays, insufficient testing before implementation, metrics buried on plan websites.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Dangerous treatment delays, clinician burnout, waste, denials from inadvertent errors, administrative cost.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multiple payer processes, differing medical vs drug PA workflows, provider testing/readiness, lack of centralized reporting.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** LHA represents more than 150 hospitals and health systems; no formal statistics beyond describing proposed decision timeframes.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** PA variation across plans causing errors and appeals; medical benefit drugs using MedicationRequest and J-codes within CRD/PAS capability.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Hospitals, clinicians, staff, patients, payers, utilization review entities, QHP issuers, small group employers/enrollees.
- **Economic implications:** Standardization may reduce provider administrative costs and health system waste; testing/delay may prevent costly failed implementation.
- **Technical feasibility:** LHA views the technology as promising but requiring demonstrations and provider testing before broad mandatory use.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster PA decisions and standardized electronic workflows can reduce delays and interruptions in drug therapy.

- **Tone:** Professional, provider-centered, supportive.
