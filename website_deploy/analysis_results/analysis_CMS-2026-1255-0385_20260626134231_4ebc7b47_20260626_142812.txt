## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DDNC strongly urges CMS to establish rigorous federal safeguards protecting patients from the harmful consequences of step therapy protocols, arguing that technological improvements alone are insufficient. The comment focuses on the RFI regarding step therapy and calls for standardized exception processes across all CMS-administered programs, mandatory continuity of care protections (including recognition of prior step therapy completion across plan changes and benefit categories), and technology-enabled transparent reporting. DDNC supports ePA interoperability but frames it as necessary but not sufficient without substantive step therapy guardrails.

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR § 423.578 (Part D step therapy exception process); Medicaid, CHIP, and ACA exchange programs administered by CMS
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization (ePA); interoperability standards generally; no specific FHIR IGs cited

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Step Therapy Leads to Negative Patient Outcomes**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (step therapy as currently practiced)
- **Rationale:** Step therapy overrides physician-prescribed treatment plans and forces patients to cycle through alternative medications regardless of clinical need. For digestive disease patients, delays in accessing biologic and advanced therapies cause irreversible disease progression, directly contradict clinical practice guidelines (AGA recommends early use of high-efficacy therapies for IBD), increase system costs through ER visits and hospitalizations, and impose unsustainable physician administrative burden. Biologic interruptions also create immunogenicity risks (anti-drug antibody formation), permanently reducing future therapy effectiveness.
- **Specific request:** CMS should establish rigorous federal step therapy safeguards; technology improvements (ePA) are necessary but insufficient alone.

**Topic 2: Standardize Step Therapy Exception Process**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL, PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify (create new uniform standard)
- **Rationale:** Many CMS-administered programs (Medicaid, CHIP, ACA exchanges) do not offer the standard exception process of 42 CFR § 423.578, even though these populations are among the most vulnerable. AMA estimates physicians spend 12 hours/week on PA/step therapy administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Require all plans to establish a standard process for step therapy exemption requests; respond within 72 hours (standard) and 24 hours (medical emergency). Automatic exemptions when: patient tried and failed insurer-preferred drugs; delayed treatment will cause irreversible harm; required drug will cause harm; required drug will prevent work or activities of daily living; patient is stable on current medication.

**Topic 3: Technology Platform for Step Therapy Exceptions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Technology is a critical enabler but must be designed with both patients and providers in mind. Currently, providers submit exception requests through proprietary forms, phone calls, and faxes.
- **Specific request:** CMS should develop a technology platform standardizing electronic submission of step therapy information and exceptions. Require payers to provide in plain language: patient-specific UM requirements; cost-sharing information; clear step therapy protocol descriptions and evidence basis; effective dates. Require publicly available aggregated data on exception requests, medications subject to step therapy, health indications, results, denial reasons, and appeal overturn rates.

**Topic 4: Continuity of Care Across Plans and Benefit Categories**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Modify (mandatory rulemaking requested)
- **Rationale:** Patients are forced to repeat step therapy when switching insurance, when plans change formularies, when treatment crosses medical/pharmacy benefit, and at the start of every plan year. Removing patients from effective biologics creates immunogenicity risks. For liver transplant recipients, even brief interruptions risk graft rejection. Cross-benefit continuity is particularly problematic: IV induction (medical benefit) approved but subcutaneous maintenance (pharmacy benefit) denied; payer views patient as "new" to therapy because pharmacy and medical benefit data are not interoperable.
- **Specific request:** Mandatory rulemaking requiring: new payers to honor prior payer's step therapy completion and grant immediate access to prescribed medication; plans to recognize their own step therapy completion across benefit categories and multiple plan years. Specific standards: continuity even if prior payer required fewer steps; even if payer expands/alters fail-first protocols; even if new timelines are longer; even if new plan's preferred treatments differ within the same therapeutic class.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S15 (medical vs. pharmacy benefit distinction):** Directly addresses cross-benefit continuity—biologics transitioning from IV induction (medical benefit) to subcutaneous maintenance (pharmacy benefit); non-interoperable pharmacy/medical benefit data enables payers to incorrectly treat patients as "new" to therapy. Urges recognition of established therapy history across benefit categories.
- **S16 (specific reason for denial):** Recommends exception denials include patient-specific clinical rationale (citing national medical society guidelines, peer-reviewed literature), covered alternative treatment, and description of appeal rights.
- **S20/S43 (PA timeframes):** Calls for step therapy exception decisions within 72 hours standard and 24 hours for medical emergencies.
- **Step Therapy RFI:** Comprehensive response documenting clinical harms; recommends standardized exception criteria; requests technology platform; calls for mandatory continuity of care across plan changes and benefit categories; urges public reporting of step therapy metrics.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish rigorous federal safeguards protecting patients from harmful step therapy—technological improvements (ePA) are necessary but insufficient alone.
2. Require all CMS-administered programs to establish a standard step therapy exemption request process equivalent to 42 CFR § 423.578.
3. Require step therapy exception decisions within 72 hours (standard) and 24 hours (medical emergency).
4. Mandate automatic exemptions when patient tried and failed insurer-preferred drugs; faces irreversible harm from delay; faces harm from required drug; cannot work or perform activities of daily living; is stable on current medication.
5. Develop a CMS technology platform standardizing electronic submission of step therapy exception information across all payers.
6. Require payers to provide in plain language: patient-specific UM requirements, cost-sharing, step therapy protocols and evidence basis, and effective dates.
7. Require each step therapy protocol to be based on documented clinical evidence that the insurer-required medication is as effective and safe as the prescribed medication.
8. Require exception denials to include patient-specific clinical rationale, covered alternative, and appeal description.
9. Require publicly available aggregated data on step therapy exception requests, medications subject to step therapy, indications, results, denial reasons, and appeal overturn rates.
10. Mandatory rulemaking requiring new payers to honor prior payer's step therapy completion and grant immediate access to prescribed medication.
11. Require recognition of step therapy completion across benefit categories and across multiple plan years.
12. Mandate continuity eligibility even if prior payer required fewer steps; even if payer expands protocols; even if new timelines are longer; and within the same therapeutic class.
13. Ensure interoperability of pharmacy and medical benefit data to prevent treating established therapy patients as "new."

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Step therapy protocols in CMS-administered programs routinely override physician-prescribed treatments, causing serious patient harm. Current exception processes are absent, non-standardized, or burdensome. Technology improvements alone will not reform step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Irreversible disease progression (strictures, fistulas, bowel perforations, malnutrition, liver failure, cancer); anti-drug antibody formation permanently reducing biologic effectiveness; hospitalization and ER visits; job loss and inability to perform activities of daily living; graft rejection in liver transplant recipients; 12 hours/week physician administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Patchwork of exception and appeals processes; proprietary forms and fax-based submission; no standardization of documentation requirements; non-interoperable pharmacy and medical benefit data enabling therapy restart requirements.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMA estimate: 12 hours/week per physician practice on PA/step therapy administrative burden. 85% of IBD patients subjected to step therapy reported negative health outcomes when delayed from obtaining prescribed medications (Crohn's & Colitis Foundation national survey).
- **Studies or research referenced:** 19 peer-reviewed citations including: AGA Crohn's disease guideline; ACP position paper on step therapy (Joy & Mire, 2020); Mark et al. (Am J Manag Care, 2009); Jordan et al. (Inflamm Bowel Dis, 2025) IBD patient survey; Zisman et al. (Gastro Hep Advances, 2025) on biologic denial and remission rates; Thomas et al. (BioDrugs, 2015) on TNF inhibitor immunogenicity; Brun et al. (J Intern Med, 2022) on anti-drug antibody risk; Florenzo et al. (J Dermatolog Treat, 2024) on step therapy for dupilumab; Hanauer et al. on infliximab antibody responses.
- **Examples provided:** Liver transplant recipients requiring lifelong immunosuppression facing PA/step therapy delays despite Part D protected class status; IV induction approved but subcutaneous maintenance denied as continuation of same therapy; biologic interruptions causing anti-drug antibody formation permanently eliminating therapy effectiveness.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with digestive diseases (IBD, chronic liver disease, celiac disease, GI cancers, autoimmune liver disease), liver transplant recipients, Medicaid/CHIP/ACA exchange enrollees, gastroenterologists and GI care teams, PBMs.
- **Economic implications:** Step therapy fails as cost-cutting tool—multiple studies show it increases costs through ER visits, hospitalizations, and surgeries. Anti-drug antibody formation from biologic interruptions eliminates future treatment options, creating long-term cost increases. PBMs profit from steering patients to insurer-preferred medications regardless of clinical outcomes.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports technology platform development; notes current fax/phone-based exception submission is burdensome and that standardized electronic submission is feasible.
- **Patient care impact:** Severe—documents irreversible disease progression, hospitalization, organ failure, cancer risk, and quality of life impacts. Biologic immunogenicity risk makes interruptions particularly harmful and potentially permanent.
