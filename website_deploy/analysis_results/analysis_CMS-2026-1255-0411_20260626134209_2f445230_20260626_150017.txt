## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Ad Hoc Sunshine and State Law Compliance Group, a coalition of over 85 pharmaceutical, medical device, and biotechnology manufacturers, opposes CMS's proposed definition of "failure to report" under the Open Payments CMP provision (Section II.G). The Group argues CMS lacks statutory authority to impose CMPs for failing to produce documents during audits, as Congress tied CMPs to subsection (a) reporting obligations—not audit compliance. The Group urges CMS to withdraw the proposal and instead develop comprehensive audit guidance through notice-and-comment rulemaking before imposing any penalties.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Statutory Authority for CMPs**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Section 1128G(b)(1) of the Social Security Act authorizes CMPs only for failure to submit information "required under subsection (a)"—i.e., the annual reporting of payments to covered recipients. Audit document requests are categorically different. CMS's proposed rule omits the phrase "under subsection (a)" when quoting the statute, tacitly acknowledging this limitation. Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (2024) eliminates judicial deference to CMS's expansive statutory interpretation.
- **Specific request:** Withdraw the proposed definition of "failure to report" as exceeding statutory authority.

**Topic 2: Practical Complications—Due Process and Appeal Rights**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** The proposed rule lacks an appeal mechanism for manufacturers to contest unreasonable audit requests, raising due process concerns (citing Donovan v. Lone Steer, Inc., 464 U.S. 408 (1984)). The 30-day response window is inadequate given multi-year data spanning multiple business units. Subcontracted auditors would have unchecked authority to demand any documents under penalty threat.
- **Specific request:** Add an appeal process with automatic stay of the 30-day window during disputes; require CMS oversight of subcontractor requests; recognize "commercially reasonable efforts" as satisfying compliance.

**Topic 3: Overbroad Audit Requests by Subcontractors**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** During CMS's first audit round, subcontractors—unfamiliar with pharmaceutical/device industry operations—demanded irrelevant documents (e.g., full general ledgers, board meeting minutes, fraud compliance materials) and expanded into non-Open Payments areas. General ledgers contain highly sensitive, unrelated proprietary information. More appropriate audit procedures exist (Open Payments-specific systems documentation, reconciliation processes, targeted source testing).
- **Specific request:** CMS must define audit scope, require auditor training, and not finalize CMPs without first publishing audit standards through notice-and-comment rulemaking.

**Topic 4: Absence of Comprehensive Audit Guidance**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Four years after initiating audits, CMS has still not published detailed guidance governing Open Payments audit conduct. Imposing CMPs without established standards is premature and procedurally defective.
- **Specific request:** CMS must develop and publish, through notice-and-comment: (1) defined audit scope and auditor training requirements; (2) appropriate audit standards specific to Open Payments; (3) security protocols for confidential document transfer/storage; (4) document protection processes (NDAs, destruction timelines, breach notification, insurance); (5) audit completion timelines with accountability for both parties.

**Topic 5: CMP Estimate Uses "Knowing" Violation Rate**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS's CMP estimate uses the median per-record penalty of $77,371 applicable to "knowing" failures ($14,067–$140,674 range). This unfairly presumes manufacturers would knowingly fail to report. Manufacturers invest tens of millions of dollars in compliance programs; the vast majority act in good faith.
- **Specific request:** Revise CMP estimates to reflect inadvertent violation rates; distinguish good-faith disagreements over audit scope from "knowing" failures.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S57 (define "timely"):** Commenter argues the 30-day window is insufficient for complex, multi-year audit requests spanning multiple business units and third parties; requests extension processes and stay during disputes.
- **S58 (CMP amounts):** Commenter argues CMS's estimate improperly uses the "knowing" failure penalty rate; median per-record CMP of $77,371 unfairly presumes intentional noncompliance; CMS should use inadvertent failure rates.
- **S59 (due process):** Commenter extensively argues the rule lacks adequate due process—no appeal mechanism, no stay of timeline during disputes, subcontractor authority unchecked—citing Donovan v. Lone Steer and U.S. Telecom Ass'n v. FCC.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Withdraw the proposed definition of "failure to report" as exceeding CMS's statutory authority under Section 1128G(b)(1) of the Social Security Act.
2. Before any future CMP proposal, publish comprehensive Open Payments audit standards through notice-and-comment rulemaking.
3. Define audit scope and require auditors/subcontractors to be trained on Open Payments law and industry operations.
4. Establish appropriate audit standards specific to Open Payments (not GAGAS/AICPA financial audit standards).
5. Implement security protocols for secure transfer and storage of confidential documents produced to auditors.
6. Require auditors to sign NDAs, destroy documents post-audit, provide breach notification, and maintain insurance.
7. Establish audit completion timelines with accountability for auditor delays, not only manufacturer delays.
8. Add an appeal mechanism allowing manufacturers to contest unreasonable document requests, with automatic stay of the 30-day penalty window during disputes.
9. Require CMS oversight of all subcontractor document requests before CMPs can be imposed.
10. Recognize "commercially reasonable efforts" as satisfying compliance.
11. Revise CMP estimates to reflect inadvertent (not knowing) violation rates.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CMS lacks statutory authority; proposed rule exceeds agency power; post-Loper Bright, no deference available; rule is arbitrary and capricious without adequate audit guidance in place.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Manufacturers forced to disclose highly sensitive proprietary information (general ledgers, board minutes) unrelated to Open Payments; risk of data breach; competitive harm; massive compliance burden for subcontractor-managed audit requests.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-year data across multiple business units cannot be assembled in 30 days; subcontractors lack Open Payments expertise; no established audit standards exist after four years of program operation.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
