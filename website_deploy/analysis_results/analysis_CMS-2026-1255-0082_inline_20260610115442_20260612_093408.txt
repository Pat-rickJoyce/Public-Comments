## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter strongly supports CMS's proposed prior authorization modernization, especially faster PA decisions, electronic PA APIs, and public transparency. They recommend additional protections restricting step therapy, grandfathering stable patients, and requiring specialty-matched peer review for neurological denials.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Faster PA determinations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Neurological diseases may require rapid stabilization, and PA delays can cause disease progression, missed dosing windows, and prolonged suffering.
- **Specific request:** Shorten mandated PA timeframes to reduce treatment gaps.

**Topic 2: Electronic PA APIs for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs can reduce manual faxing and phone calls that drain clinic resources.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to support standardized APIs for all drugs, including physician-administered therapies.

**Topic 3: Transparency and denial accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Public aggregate PA metrics and specific denial reasons would hold insurers accountable for arbitrary roadblocks.
- **Specific request:** Require public reporting of aggregate PA metrics and specific reasons for denials.

**Topic 4: Step therapy and specialty review protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Fail-first protocols may ignore evidence-based guidelines, cause adverse effects, and undermine patient autonomy and trust.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit step therapy when the clinician documents clinical inappropriateness or adverse-effect risk; grandfather stable patients; require neurological PA denials to be reviewed by a board-certified neurologist or condition-specific peer.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Shorten PA determination timeframes.
2. Require standardized electronic PA APIs for all drugs.
3. Publicly report aggregate PA metrics and specific denial reasons.
4. Incorporate specialty-society recommendations, including American Headache Society guidance.
5. Prohibit step therapy when clinical documentation shows the preferred drug is inappropriate or risky.
6. Grandfather clinically stable patients against mid-year formulary or insurance-switch disruptions.
7. Require neurological PA denials to be reviewed by neurologists or condition-specific peers.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a Physician Assistant specializing in neurology. Every single day, I work on the front lines of patient care, diagnosing and treating complex neurological conditions where timely, effective intervention is critical to preserving function and patient quality of life. The current administrative burdens imposed by insurers severely disrupt evidence-based medical care.Impact of Prior Authorization Delays: Prior authorization (PA) protocols create unnecessary bottlenecks that delay crucial treatments for weeks or months. In neurology, where conditions like progressive multiple sclerosis, intractable epilepsy, or chronic migraines require rapid stabilization, these administrative delays cause preventable disease progression and prolonged patient suffering. Forcing patients to undergo &quot;fail-first&quot; protocols ignores evidence-based clinical guidelines. Patients are routinely required to trial and fail older, less effective, or less tolerable medications before gaining access to the therapies originally prescribed...
