ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I work in an OBGYN office for multiple providers.  I am commenting regarding the impact on laboratory testing from Section E.<br/>Prior Authorization (PA) requirements can lead to delays in care for patients that are needing laboratory testing within a certain timeframe to help make decisions impacting their Obstetrical and Gynecologic care.  Overall, prior authorizations create barriers to health equity regardless of what a patient has for insurance coverage.  Patient&#39;s may be denied certain laboratory testing because their insurance will not cover the test whereas another patient&#39;s insurance will cover the test without requiring a PA. One insurance carrier may take a week for decision time and others take a month to render a decision.   PA&#39;s create an administrative burden for providers and their staff to answer the multitude of clinical questions and the time it takes to submit clinical proof for what a patient needs and what providers recommend according to medical standards.  If CMS would change this policy, other insurance carriers would hopefully follow.  Meaningful improvements in how insurers operate would reduce these problems.  Suggestions of one website for submissions, updating what tests should always be covered according the medical guidelines, and/or requiring all insurers to give instant decisions or a one-to-two-day turnaround. The patient and medical provider should have the clinical laboratory results as soon as possible to make informed decisions on medical patient care.  Thank you.
