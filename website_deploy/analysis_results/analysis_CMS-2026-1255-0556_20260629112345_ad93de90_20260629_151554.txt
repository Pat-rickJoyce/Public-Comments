# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Aimed Alliance; signed by Ashira Vantrees, Esq., Director of Legal Strategy & Advocacy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Other health policy organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Non-profit health policy organization focused on protecting and enhancing the rights of health care consumers and providers; cites its 2025 report on step therapy, oversight, and AI.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Aimed Alliance supports CMS's proposal to extend electronic prior authorization to prescription drugs covered under pharmacy and medical benefits and supports denial-rationale requirements. It urges CMS to clarify that once a plan determines a covered drug is medically necessary, the plan may not impose a second-stage process requiring patients to seek third-party or manufacturer assistance before standard coverage applies.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, or HL7 guides named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic PA for covered drugs under pharmacy and medical benefits**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization burdens providers and patients; standardization and electronic processes can promote consistency, fairness, transparency, and access to covered benefits.
- **Specific request:** Finalize proposed electronic prior authorization requirements for prescription drugs covered under both pharmacy and medical benefits.

**Topic 2: Denial rationales for providers and consumers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers need clear information about how a patient failed to meet criteria and what information may be needed for future submissions or appeals; consumers need to understand alternatives and appeal rights.
- **Specific request:** Finalize Medicaid provider denial-rationale requirements and require consumer notices to include alternative medication information and appeal/complaint instructions.

**Topic 3: Prior authorization approval as a singular process**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Some plans allegedly use PA approval as the first step in a multi-stage coverage process that forces patients to seek external assistance programs, leaving no formal appeal pathway and creating inequitable benefit outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that after determining a covered drug is medically necessary, a plan cannot require a beneficiary to apply to or be denied by an external assistance program before covering the drug as a standard pharmacy or medical benefit.

**Topic 4: Appeals underutilization and need for front-end fairness**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Internal and external appeals are underused by consumers and providers, so the original PA process must be fair and transparent.
- **Specific request:** Ensure PA processes are standardized, transparent, and fair at the initial determination stage.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Supports denial rationales in PA notices and asks for clearer information explaining how criteria were not met.
- **S17:** Indirectly supports actionable consumer/provider denial information, including alternative medication expectations and appeal or complaint pathways.
- **S44/S45:** Supports using electronic PA for covered drugs across pharmacy and medical benefits, though without discussing FHIR technical appropriateness in detail.
- **S66-S82:** Addresses broader patient protection issues, including alternative funding programs, appeal access, and step therapy/coverage transparency.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for prescription drugs covered under pharmacy benefits and medical benefits.
2. Finalize requirements for Medicaid plans to include denial rationales in provider notices.
3. Require consumer denial notices to explain alternative medication requirements.
4. Require consumer denial notices to include clear appeal and complaint instructions.
5. Clarify that prior authorization approval must not become a multi-stage process that delays coverage after medical necessity is established.
6. Prohibit plans from requiring beneficiaries to apply to or be denied by third-party or manufacturer assistance programs before standard plan coverage applies.
7. Design initial PA processes to be fair and transparent because appeals are underutilized.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays and abandonment; low appeal rates; inadequate consumer denial information; alternative funding programs that condition coverage after medical necessity approval.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment adherence problems, treatment abandonment, confusion about coverage rights, inequitable coverage based on income or assistance eligibility, and lack of appeal pathways.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-stage plan processes may not produce a formal coverage determination, making appeals unclear or unavailable.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Providers complete nearly 40 PA requests per week; prior authorization delays lead to adherence issues or abandonment in nearly 80% of cases; only 20% of providers appeal denied PAs; Pennsylvania 2023 data showed 3,156 internal appeals out of 2,135,041 denied claims, less than 1%.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA 2025 Prior Authorization Physician Survey; Aimed Alliance 2025 State Report: Step Therapy, Oversight & Artificial Intelligence; Aimed Alliance material on Alternative Funding Programs.
- **Examples provided:** Patients required to apply to manufacturer or third-party assistance programs after the plan has found a drug medically necessary.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Consumers, patients needing prescription drugs, providers, Medicaid plans, health plans, manufacturers/assistance programs, and advocates.
- **Economic implications:** Alternative funding pathways may shift costs away from plan benefits and prevent spending from counting toward patient out-of-pocket obligations.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports electronic PA generally but does not discuss implementation details.
- **Patient care impact:** Transparent and electronic PA could reduce delays; multi-stage post-approval coverage procedures may delay or condition medication access.

- **Tone:** Legal, consumer-protective, and policy-focused
