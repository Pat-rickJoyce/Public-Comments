    # Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Infusion Providers Alliance (IPA); signed by Elliott Warren, Executive Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents community-based, non-hospital affiliated physician practices and freestanding ambulatory infusion clinics; members operate over 1,000 sites across 45 states and administer complex biologic therapies.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

IPA strongly supports CMS-0062-P, especially expansion of electronic prior authorization to drugs covered under the medical benefit, specific denial reasons, and aligned decision timeframes. IPA urges CMS to ensure prior authorization remains focused on medical necessity rather than payer steering, and to address duplicative post-authorization documentation and claims denials that undermine administrative simplification.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; TRANSPARENCY/API-related reporting generally; GENERAL/RFI on resiliency.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No specific CFR citations; references CMS-0062-P and the 2026 proposed rule.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; FHIR alignment for medical benefit drugs is discussed generally; cybersecurity/resiliency standards are referenced conceptually.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for medical benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Manual and inconsistent PA processes delay biologic infusions and create burdens for community practices and ambulatory infusion centers with limited staffing.
- **Specific request:** Finalize expansion of electronic PA requirements to drugs and align technology/standards for medical benefit drugs with the broader PA API framework.

**Topic 2: Specific denial explanations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Clear denial rationales are needed for appeals, resubmissions, and accountability, especially where payers may be using PA to steer treatment or distribution channels.
- **Specific request:** Require specific denial explanations and improve transparency into denial patterns.

**Topic 3: Decision timeframes for drug prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delays in infused biologic treatment can cause postponed treatment, disease progression, flares, and avoidable hospitalizations.
- **Specific request:** Finalize proposed seven-calendar-day standard and 72-hour expedited timeframes and monitor payer compliance.

**Topic 4: Reliance on valid prior authorization approvals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers face claim denials and medical record requests even after valid prior authorization approvals, duplicating the medical necessity review.
- **Specific request:** Align PA approvals with subsequent claims adjudication and prevent duplicative documentation demands where care was furnished consistent with an authorization.

**Topic 5: Health care resiliency and cyber disruption**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI S66-S82
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Infusion providers were economically strained by the 2024 Change Healthcare cyberattack due to buy-and-bill drug purchasing and payment delays.
- **Specific request:** Continue focusing on cybersecurity resilience and consider impacts on providers that carry high-cost drug inventory.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports addressing technical and workflow barriers for medical benefit drug ePA by expanding electronic PA requirements to medical benefit drugs.
- **S16:** Supports requiring specific reasons for drug PA denials so providers and patients can understand determinations and pursue appeals.
- **S17:** Implied support for more transparent and reportable denial rationales, though structured codes are not discussed in detail.
- **S20/S43:** Supports aligned and shorter decision timeframes for drug PA and monitoring compliance.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the health care resiliency RFI by describing the economic and operational impact of the Change Healthcare ransomware attack on infusion providers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize expansion of electronic prior authorization requirements to drugs, including medical benefit drugs.
2. Require specific, actionable reasons for drug PA denials.
3. Finalize and monitor compliance with proposed PA decision timeframes.
4. Ensure prior authorization is not used to mandate distribution channels, non-medical switching, or payer-preferred treatment decisions.
5. Prevent duplicative post-authorization documentation and claims denials when treatment matches an approved authorization.
6. Strengthen health care resiliency policies with attention to infusion providers' buy-and-bill vulnerabilities.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative burden; treatment delays; opaque denial rationales; payer steering; duplicative documentation after PA approval; cybersecurity disruption.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disease progression, flare-ups, avoidable hospitalizations, payment delays, increased employee hours, provider financial strain.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited staffing in community practices and freestanding infusion centers; fragmented PA and claims workflows; cyber disruptions to payment systems.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** IPA members operate over 1,000 sites in 45 states; infusion in lower-cost sites typically saves Medicare more than $200 per Part B drug infusion encounter; one member experienced 8,000 medical-record denials after PA approval in 2024.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Specialty injection/infusion adverse event comparison in J Clin Pathways; JMCP study on infusion therapy outcomes and reduced costs.
- **Examples provided:** Change Healthcare ransomware attack and its effect on infusion provider cash flow and staffing.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Infusion providers, physician practices, ambulatory infusion centers, patients with chronic/complex conditions, payers.
- **Economic implications:** Electronic PA and fewer duplicative denials could reduce administrative and payment uncertainty; cyber disruptions disproportionately affect buy-and-bill providers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supportive of ePA modernization; does not raise major feasibility objections.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly positive if finalized; faster access and clearer denials may reduce treatment delays and avoidable hospital use.

RE: 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs 
Proposed Rule (CMS-0062-P) 

Dear Administrator Oz and National Coordinator Keane: 

The Infusion Providers Alliance (IPA) is pleased to provide comments on CMS’s   
2026 Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule (CMS-
0062-P). 

IPA strongly supports CMS’s efforts to modernize and streamline prior authorization 
processes for drugs, including drugs covered under the medical beneﬁt. Prior 
authorization, as well as other utilization management tools, has become a signiﬁcant 
administrative burden for community-based physician practices and ambulatory infusion 
centers. Submitting and processing authorizations can delay medically necessary 
treatment and increase costs for providers and patients.  

Speciﬁcally, CMS proposes to require impacted payers to support electronic prior 
authorization, to make decisions on requests within shorter timeframes that align CMS 
programs, and to increase transparency for the prior authorization of drugs. IPA supports 
the proposed rule. It is an important step forward toward more timely and efficient care, 
which should improve patient access by removing unnecessary administrative burdens.     

More broadly, IPA believes that prior authorization, when necessary, should be used to 
conﬁrm that a patient has a diagnosed condition for which a prescribed therapy is 
medically appropriate, rather than as a mechanism to direct treatment decisions, mandate
