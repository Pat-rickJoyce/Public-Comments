## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Smith Institute submits a historically grounded, structurally focused comment that acknowledges the proposed rule's positive provisions while arguing they are insufficient to address what the commenter characterizes as a fundamental transformation of prior authorization from a clinical safeguard to a financial barrier. The comment urges CMS to pursue structural reform including prohibition of algorithmic denials without human clinical review, mandatory gold-carding, automatic PA renewal for stable chronic conditions, and creation of a federal PA ombudsman function.

---

- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based APIs, FHIR implementation guides (referenced without specific IG names)

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prohibition on Algorithmic Denial**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (absence of prohibition); calls for new policy
- **Rationale:** The use of AI and algorithmic systems to generate PA denials without individual clinical review represents the most serious systemic failure in the current PA framework. The original justification for PA was independent clinical judgment applied to individual patient circumstances, which is entirely absent in algorithm-only denials.
- **Specific request:** Establish clear standards governing AI/algorithmic denial systems; require human clinical review of all algorithmically-generated denials; create accountability mechanisms for payers whose algorithmic denial rates exceed clinically defensible thresholds.

**Topic 2: Mandatory Gold-Carding**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (strengthen from advisory to mandatory)
- **Rationale:** Gold-carding is a proven, evidence-based reform that reduces administrative burden without increasing inappropriate utilization; the proposed rule's approach is advisory and lacks specificity.
- **Specific request:** Establish mandatory gold-carding standards with minimum approval rate thresholds and automatic exemption periods as a condition of Medicare/Medicaid plan contracts.

**Topic 3: Step Therapy Reform**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (tighten standards)
- **Rationale:** Current step therapy application in many federally-funded managed care plans requires patients to fail on contraindicated treatments or treatments already tried and failed before enrollment, violating clinical integrity.
- **Specific request:** Adopt step therapy standards consistent with state best practices, requiring respect for prior treatment history and clinical contraindications.

**Topic 4: Automatic PA Renewal for Stable Chronic Conditions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new policy)
- **Rationale:** Requiring repeated PA cycles for cancer survivors on maintenance therapy, autoimmune disease patients on established biologics, and patients with serious mental illness creates clinical risk without clinical benefit.
- **Specific request:** Establish standards requiring automatic renewal of PA approvals for stable chronic conditions, eliminating annual or more frequent re-authorization for treatments with established clinical necessity.

**Topic 5: What the Rule Gets Right**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, PA-DENIAL-REASONS, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** 72-hour expedited / 7-day standard timeframes are meaningful improvements; specific denial reason requirements restore clinical integrity; ePA/FHIR standards reduce administrative burden; transparency and reporting create public accountability.
- **Specific request:** None — these are commended as written.

**Topic 6: Presumption of Medical Necessity for Guideline-Concordant Care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new policy)
- **Rationale:** When a treating physician requests authorization for a service consistent with nationally recognized clinical practice guidelines, the insurer should bear the burden of rebutting the presumption of medical necessity.
- **Specific request:** Establish a regulatory presumption of medical necessity for guideline-concordant care.

**Topic 7: Federal PA Ombudsman Function**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new function)
- **Rationale:** No federal oversight mechanism exists to receive, investigate, and publicly report on systemic PA abuses; the proposed rule lacks an enforcement architecture proportionate to the systemic failures documented.
- **Specific request:** Create a federal PA complaint and oversight function within the Medicare Beneficiary Ombudsman framework or CMMI, with authority to refer patterns of inappropriate denial to program integrity enforcement.

**Topic 8: Commission Study of PA Net Value**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (request new study)
- **Rationale:** Given the estimated $35 billion annual administrative cost of PA, CMS should determine whether savings exceed total costs including administrative burden, delayed care, and downstream adverse outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Commission an independent study; consider mandatory service-category exemptions where PA generates net costs.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12 (technical challenges, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL):** Supports FHIR-based ePA standards as a necessary modernization but argues technical reform alone is insufficient without structural restrictions on algorithmic denial.
- **S16 (what is 'specific reason', PA-DENIAL-REASONS):** Strongly supports requiring specific, clinically-grounded denial reasons; argues this restores essential clinical integrity that generic formulaic language undermines.
- **S17 (structured codes, PA-DENIAL-REASONS):** Calls for mandatory human clinical review of all denials; algorithmic systems may support but not make final denial decisions.
- **S20/S43 (timeframes, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS):** Commends the proposed 72-hour expedited / 7-day standard timeframes as meaningful improvements reducing patient harm.
- **S29/S35 (metrics, API-USAGE-METRICS):** Strongly supports transparency and public reporting as creating meaningful accountability for payer behavior that currently has minimal external visibility.
- **S66 (general/RFI — structural reform):** Argues procedural reform alone is insufficient; recommends presumption of medical necessity, mandatory human review, gold-carding, step therapy standards, chronic condition PA renewal, federal ombudsman, and cost-benefit study.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish clear regulatory standards governing AI/algorithmic PA denial systems; require that algorithmic systems may inform but not make final denial decisions.
2. Mandate human clinical review by a licensed clinician with relevant specialty expertise for all PA denials, including initial denials, upheld appeals, and peer-to-peer decisions.
3. Create accountability mechanisms for payers whose algorithmic denial rates exceed clinically defensible thresholds.
4. Establish mandatory gold-carding standards (minimum approval rate thresholds, automatic exemption periods) as a condition of Medicare/Medicaid plan contracts.
5. Adopt step therapy standards that require respect for prior treatment history and clinical contraindications, consistent with state best practices.
6. Require automatic renewal of PA approvals for stable chronic conditions; eliminate repeated re-authorization for treatments with established clinical necessity.
7. Establish a regulatory presumption of medical necessity for services consistent with nationally recognized clinical practice guidelines, placing the rebuttal burden on payers.
8. Establish timelines and standards for real-time electronic PA integrated into EHR prescribing and ordering workflows.
9. Create a federal PA ombudsman/oversight function with authority to investigate systemic PA abuses and refer patterns to program integrity enforcement.
10. Commission an independent study of PA's net value; consider mandatory service-category exemptions where PA generates net costs.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Algorithmic/AI denials without human clinical review represent a fundamental breach of PA's legal and clinical foundation; the rule's procedural improvements are insufficient for the structural scale of the problem; chronic condition re-authorization creates clinical risk without benefit; step therapy abuse violates clinical guidelines.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** $35 billion annual administrative cost; ~41 PA requests/physician/week; 94% of physicians report PA causes care delays; ~1/3 of physicians report PA has caused serious adverse events including hospitalization, permanent disability, and death; ~25% of patients abandon prescribed treatment when PA is required.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Current PA framework lacks enforcement architecture proportionate to documented failures; proposed rule does not restrict algorithmic denial; gold-carding is advisory not mandatory.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:**
  - AMA annual surveys: 41+ PA requests/physician/week; 94% report care delays; ~1/3 report serious adverse events; ~25% patient treatment abandonment.
  - Healthcare system PA administrative cost: estimated $35 billion annually.
  - 2023 Senate inquiry: MA insurers used automated denial systems with denial rates far exceeding traditional Medicare for clinically equivalent populations.
  - Executive Order 13985: documented PA denial rates vary by demographic characteristics not explained by clinical factors.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA annual PA burden surveys; 2023 Congressional investigation into MA PA practices; EO 13985.
- **Examples provided:** Cancer survivors on maintenance therapy; autoimmune disease patients on established biologics; patients with serious mental illness on optimized regimens; specialty drugs costing $50,000–$500,000/year; historical evolution of PA from 1965 through current algorithmic systems.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Medicare Advantage beneficiaries, Medicaid/CHIP beneficiaries, all federally-funded plan enrollees, treating physicians and specialist practices, healthcare workforce broadly
- **Economic implications:** $35 billion annual PA administration costs; downstream costs of adverse clinical outcomes; potential system-wide savings from eliminating clinically unnecessary PA cycles
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports FHIR-based ePA as necessary modernization; calls for real-time EHR integration; argues technology alone is insufficient without structural reform
- **Patient care impact:** Very high — chronic condition patients at risk from repeated re-authorization; vulnerable populations disproportionately harmed; PA delays linked to hospitalization, permanent disability, and death

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "The system as currently constituted has strayed so far from its original statutory and clinical purpose that incremental modernization may be insufficient to address the patient harm, administrative dysfunction, and systemic inequity it has produced." — Context: Introducing the overarching argument for structural rather than procedural reform.
2. "What had been a clinically-oriented review process became, in many applications, a financially-oriented denial mechanism — with the burden of proof placed not on the insurer to justify denial, but on the treating physician to overcome algorithmic or administrative barriers to approval." — Context: Describing the historical transformation of PA from clinical to financial instrument.
3. "Algorithmic systems may be used to support and inform clinical review but may not serve as the final decision-making authority for any denial that affects patient access to medically necessary care." — Context: Core recommendation on human review requirement.
4. "What was designed as a clinical instrument has become, in too many applications, a financial barrier that delays necessary care, harms vulnerable patients, and imposes billions of dollars in administrative costs on the healthcare system without commensurate clinical or financial benefit." — Context: Concluding characterization of the PA system's failure.
