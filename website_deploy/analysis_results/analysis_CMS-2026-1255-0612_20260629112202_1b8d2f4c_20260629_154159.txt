## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Access to Comprehensive Genomic Profiling (ACGP) Coalition
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Industry Coalition
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Coalition of molecular diagnostics and biopharmaceutical companies focused on expanding patient access to comprehensive genomic profiling, biomarker testing, companion diagnostics, hereditary cancer panels, pharmacogenomics, and related advanced tests.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ACGP responds to the RFI on prior authorization for laboratory tests and supports evidence-based utilization management only when it does not delay or deny medically necessary genomic and biomarker testing. The coalition asks CMS to prohibit date-of-service/specimen-collection denials, require payers to accept laboratory-submitted PA requests and appeals, accept test requisition forms as documentation, improve payer/LBM transparency, and create a non-compliance reporting mechanism.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI laboratory testing; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; TRANSPARENCY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy; API-USAGE-METRICS by analogy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 C.F.R. § 422.138(b); 42 C.F.R. § 422.566(c)(1)(ii).
- **Standards referenced:** LOINC; CPT; PLA coding; LCD/NCD criteria; electronic prior authorization tools; laboratory benefit managers.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prohibit specimen-collection/date-of-service denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI laboratory testing
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker and genomic testing may be ordered after diagnosis and performed on tissue already collected; denying PA because authorization occurred after the collection date is a timing technicality unrelated to medical necessity.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit impacted payers from denying coverage solely because PA was sought or obtained after the date of service or specimen collection.

**Topic 2: Laboratory ability to submit PA and appeals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI laboratory testing
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Laboratories frequently furnish the service and hold relevant documentation, yet payer rules can prevent them from submitting or managing PA and appeals.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to accept PA requests, status checks, and appeals from laboratories as recognized providers, regardless of network status.

**Topic 3: Test requisition forms as documentation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL/RFI laboratory testing
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** TRFs often contain the clinical information needed to determine medical necessity; refusing them creates duplicative records-chasing.
- **Specific request:** Require acceptance of complete TRFs as part of the medical record and sufficient documentation when they establish applicable coverage criteria.

**Topic 4: LBM and payer transparency**
- **Provision ID:** TRANSPARENCY; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** LBM policies can be opaque, proprietary, lag clinical guidelines, impose test identifiers, or create unclear accountability between payer and LBM.
- **Specific request:** Require disclosure of coverage criteria, documentation requirements, denial rationales, responsible entities, appeal pathways, and reporting by provider type/denial reason/claim disposition.

**Topic 5: Automation must fit laboratory workflows**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; TECHNICAL-FEASIBILITY
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA tools designed for imaging, procedures, and DME may not map cleanly to lab workflows involving test, code, specimen type, diagnosis, policy, and reflex logic.
- **Specific request:** Build automation around laboratory-specific data fields and coverage rules; do not simply automate flawed policies.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Directly responds to the RFI on laboratory tests, especially impacts on patient care, TRFs, post-collection denials, LBMs, process improvement, automation, technology gaps, and workforce impact.
- **S16/S17:** Recommends granular denial rationales, documentation requirements, and accountable entities.
- **S29/S35:** Recommends reporting broken down by requesting-provider type, denial reason, and claim disposition rather than only organization-level metrics.
- **S20/S21:** By analogy, emphasizes that timing rules must not delay time-sensitive oncology testing.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prohibit denials based on date of service or specimen-collection date.
2. Require impacted payers to accept PA requests, status checks, and appeals from laboratories.
3. Require acceptance of complete TRFs as part of medical necessity documentation.
4. Require transparency into payer and LBM coverage criteria, documentation requirements, denial rationales, responsible entities, and appeal pathways.
5. Create a non-compliance reporting mechanism for clinicians, patients, and laboratories.
6. Ensure automation is built for laboratory workflows, including LOINC, CPT, PLA, specimen type, diagnosis codes, LCD/NCD criteria, and reflex logic.
7. Address post-payment recoupment, retroactive denials, standing/reflex testing, reference-lab arrangements, and situations where medical necessity depends on preliminary results.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative timing denials, inability of labs to participate fully in PA, rejection of TRFs, opaque LBM policies, and automation of flawed processes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed oncology treatment decisions, denial of medically necessary testing, provider/lab burden, appeals, financial exposure, and reduced access to clinical trials or targeted therapies.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Laboratory-specific coding and coverage complexity, third-party LBM policies, network-status issues, payer opacity, and non-lab-oriented ePA tools.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No numerical data; cites members' collective experience that post-collection denials are common among payers using collection-date policies.
- **Studies or research referenced:** References Medicare MolDX, LCDs, NCDs, and clinical guidelines generally.
- **Examples provided:** Oncology biomarker testing, companion diagnostics, minimal residual disease assays, tissue already collected before test order, LBM test-registration and identifier requirements.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing oncology testing, ordering clinicians, laboratories, genetic counselors, payers, LBMs, and biopharmaceutical/molecular diagnostics companies.
- **Economic implications:** Labs may absorb denials for good-faith testing; administrative work requires dedicated staff and vendors.
- **Technical feasibility:** Automation is feasible only if designed for lab-specific fields and workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** High, because genomic/biomarker results often determine targeted therapy, immunotherapy, or clinical trial eligibility.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Mixed / Concerned
- **Tone:** Detailed; technical; policy-focused
- **Priority level:** High
