# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Association for Behavioral Health and Wellness (ABHW); signed by Debbie Witchey, MHA, President and CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association; Payer association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** ABHW represents payers managing behavioral health insurance benefits for 200 million people in public and private sectors; focuses on mental health, substance use disorders, integration, prevention, and evidence-based treatment.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ABHW supports CMS's goals of improving medication access, streamlining PA, and expanding electronic prior authorization, but requests modifications to account for pharmacy PA complexity, bundled behavioral health/SUD services, payer-to-payer privacy risks, and plan-specific step therapy criteria. The comment recommends a stop-the-clock policy for incomplete requests, an exemption for bundled MAT services from 24-hour drug PA timeframes, tighter limits and authorization requirements for payer-to-payer data exchange, and targeted use of historical claims data for step therapy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR Part 2.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API, Patient Access API, Provider Access API, Payer-to-Payer API, NCPDP standards, historical claims data, automated adjudication tools.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Drug PA turnaround times and stop-the-clock policy**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ABHW argues pharmacy PAs often involve dosage limits, quantity limits, step therapy, site-of-care issues, diagnosis-specific criteria, and documentation needs that cannot always be resolved within shortened deadlines.
- **Specific request:** Allow plans to pause turnaround-time requirements when clinically necessary information has been requested but not yet received.

**Topic 2: Bundled behavioral health and SUD services**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Some behavioral health Medicaid MCOs cover MAT through bundled case rates that include medication plus counseling, labs, assessments, and other services, making classification as a drug PA operationally difficult.
- **Specific request:** Exempt bundled BH/SUD treatment services that include medications from the 24-hour drug PA timeframe and process them under non-drug PA API requirements and non-drug timeframes.

**Topic 3: NCPDP versus Prior Authorization API classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** ABHW states bundled MAT services are broader than pharmacy benefit transactions and may require credentialing and service review.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that bundled services are processed through the Prior Authorization API for non-drug items and services, not NCPDP pharmacy-benefit standards.

**Topic 4: Payer-to-payer privacy limits**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ABHW is concerned broad payer-to-payer exchange of structured and unstructured clinical documentation could disclose unnecessary sensitive behavioral health information, including Part 2-protected information.
- **Specific request:** Limit payer-to-payer exchange to active plan transitions, require explicit member authorization, and restrict data to the minimum necessary.

**Topic 5: Step therapy data sharing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Historical claims can reduce duplicative therapy requirements for new members, but payer criteria differ based on clinical policies, formularies, and benefit designs.
- **Specific request:** Support targeted historical claims use while preserving payer flexibility and avoiding automatic acceptance of other payers' step therapy determinations.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** ABHW requests clarification of when NCPDP pharmacy standards apply, specifically asking that bundled BH/SUD services be handled through the non-drug Prior Authorization API rather than NCPDP standards.
- **S20:** ABHW argues 24-hour drug PA timeframes may be operationally unrealistic for complex pharmacy reviews and asks for a stop-the-clock mechanism.
- **S43:** ABHW addresses alignment of drug and non-drug timeframes by requesting non-drug PA timeframes for bundled services that include medications but also broader clinical services.
- **S30:** ABHW raises privacy concerns about broad payer-to-payer exchange, especially for behavioral health and 42 CFR Part 2 information, and requests explicit member authorization and minimum necessary limits.
- **S66-S82:** ABHW responds to the step therapy RFI by supporting targeted historical claims data use for new members while opposing mandatory acceptance of other payers' determinations.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow plans to stop the PA turnaround clock when additional clinically necessary information has been requested but not received.
2. Exempt bundled behavioral health and SUD treatment services that include medications from 24-hour drug PA timeframes.
3. Apply non-drug PA timeframes of 7 calendar days standard and 72 hours expedited to bundled BH/SUD services.
4. Clarify bundled BH/SUD services should use the non-drug Prior Authorization API rather than NCPDP pharmacy-benefit standards.
5. Limit payer-to-payer data exchange to active transitions where data are needed for continuity of care.
6. Require explicit member authorization before payer-to-payer exchange of sensitive information.
7. Apply minimum necessary limits and avoid transmission of full clinical documentation when targeted data will suffice.
8. Support targeted historical claims use for step therapy, especially for new members.
9. Preserve payer flexibility to apply plan-specific clinical criteria and avoid automatic acceptance of another payer's step therapy decision.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Unrealistic timeframes for complex pharmacy PAs, increased denials from incomplete requests, operational burden for bundled BH/SUD services, sensitive behavioral health data disclosure, and loss of payer flexibility in step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** More denials, appeals, administrative burden, delayed patient access, compliance challenges, privacy breaches, and undermined program integrity safeguards.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** System changes, workflow redesign, technology investments, pharmacy benefit complexity, credentialing needs, bundled payment measurement issues, and Part 2 confidentiality requirements.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** ABHW states its member companies cover 200 million people.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Not addressed.
- **Examples provided:** Louisiana Medicaid opioid treatment program bundled payment for HCPCS code H0020, including methadone dosing/dispensing, counseling, urine drug testing, physician exams, evaluation and management visits, care coordination, and labs.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Behavioral health payers, Medicaid managed care organizations, PBMs, providers, opioid treatment programs, patients receiving MAT or behavioral health services, state Medicaid programs, and members transitioning between plans.
- **Economic implications:** ABHW anticipates significant system, workflow, and technology investments, plus costs from denials, appeals, and administrative rework if timeframes are too rigid.
- **Technical feasibility:** ABHW supports electronic PA and historical claims data use but warns implementation must reflect complex pharmacy and bundled-service workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Mixed; faster PA can improve access, but rigid deadlines or broad data sharing could increase denials, delay care, or compromise privacy.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Mixed; supportive of goals but concerned about operational feasibility and privacy.
- **Tone:** Professional, technical, and pragmatic.
- **Priority level:** High
