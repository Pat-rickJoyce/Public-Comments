# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Blood Cancer United; signed by Bethany Lilly, Executive Director, Public Policy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group; patient advocacy organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Nonprofit focused on curing blood cancer, improving quality of life for patients and families, funding research, providing patient support, and advocating for accessible and affordable healthcare.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Blood Cancer United strongly supports extending electronic prior authorization standards to prescription drugs under both medical and pharmacy benefits and extending requirements to FF-SHOP small group plans. It urges CMS to go further by requiring clearer benefit-pathway identification, faster timeframes, stronger denial explanations, continuity-of-care protections, public and disaggregated reporting, centralized endpoint directories and dashboards, and greater provider API adoption.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not specifically cited by CFR section; references CMS-0062-P and prior 2024 Interoperability rule.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization APIs; Patient Access, Provider Directory, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization API endpoints; electronic PA; EHR and provider adoption; e-prescribing.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extend electronic PA to prescription drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Prescription drugs are central to chronic and complex care, including cancer treatment, and excluding them from ePA standards forces providers and patients to manage multiple systems.
- **Specific request:** Finalize extension to drugs under both medical and pharmacy benefits; require real-time identification of the benefit pathway and consistent terminology, documentation, and workflows.

**Topic 2: Guardrails against inappropriate PA and repeat authorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Electronic modernization should not merely accelerate inappropriate denials or longstanding utilization-management barriers.
- **Specific request:** Require qualified clinician review for complex denials, monitor denial and overturn rates, prohibit unnecessary repeat PA for stable long-term therapies, make approvals valid for duration of treatment, and protect continuity when enrollees change plans.

**Topic 3: FF-SHOP extension**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Small-group FF-SHOP issuers already offering individual-market QHPs should have minimal incremental burden, while patients in SHOP plans would benefit substantially.
- **Specific request:** Finalize FF-SHOP extension and continue promoting consistent PA protections across coverage markets.

**Topic 4: Prior authorization timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Chronic illness patients are harmed by delays, and electronic efficiencies should permit shorter timeframes.
- **Specific request:** Require 72 hours for standard and 24 hours for expedited non-drug requests; limit extensions to 7 days; require notice and basis for extensions; require 24-hour responses for all QHP prescription drug PA; treat missed deadlines as deemed denials triggering appeal rights.

**Topic 5: API usage metrics and transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Usage metrics can reveal adoption and disparities, but public and disaggregated reporting is needed for accountability.
- **Specific request:** Collect disaggregated API usage data by provider type, plan, organizational size, and geography; make data public at least annually.

**Topic 6: Endpoint reporting and centralized directory**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** A CMS-maintained public endpoint directory prevents endpoint information from becoming restricted or monetized and supports researchers and advocates.
- **Specific request:** Finalize endpoint reporting for Patient Access, Provider Directory, Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and PA APIs and maintain an open centralized directory.

**Topic 7: Prior authorization reporting metrics and public website reporting**
- **Provision ID:** TRANSPARENCY; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Current reporting masks the populations and services affected by PA and is difficult to locate or compare.
- **Specific request:** Require disaggregation by service type, provider type, diagnosis, geography, income, disability, gender, race/ethnicity, and age; standardize website location, file type, layout, terminology; create a centralized searchable dashboard; add patient-experience measures.

**Topic 8: Denial explanations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Generic denial reasons like “medical necessity” do not help patients or providers correct, appeal, or understand decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed denial rationales, missing-document identification, policy/clinical criteria used, covered alternatives, plain language, appeal instructions, simultaneous notice to patients/providers, and language-access/accessibility standards.

**Topic 9: Lab tests and DMEPOS RFI**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA delays for lab testing and DMEPOS can impede diagnosis, treatment planning, independence, and access to medically necessary equipment.
- **Specific request:** Ensure patients understand required materials and network status; require timely updates for providers/labs; require clear medical necessity standards and appeals information for DMEPOS.

**Topic 10: Provider API adoption**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Provider adoption is far behind payer implementation, threatening the practical value of APIs.
- **Specific request:** Use API usage data, incentives, pilots, education, and support modeled on e-prescribing/EHR adoption to increase provider uptake, especially among small and medium practices.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S15/S21:** Strongly supports extending PA APIs to drugs and requests real-time benefit-pathway identification and workflow consistency.
- **S16/S17:** Requests stronger, more specific denial explanations including missing documentation, clinical criteria, covered alternatives, appeal instructions, and accessibility standards.
- **S20/S43:** Requests shorter PA timeframes, especially 24-hour QHP drug determinations, and enforceability through deemed denial if deadlines are missed.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports API usage metrics but requests public, annual, disaggregated reporting.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Strongly supports endpoint reporting and a centralized CMS directory.
- **S40-S42:** Strongly supports extending requirements to FF-SHOP plans, arguing burden is minimal and patient benefit substantial.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to lab/DMEPOS and provider adoption themes, requesting patient-centered protections and support for API adoption.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA standards for prescription drugs under both medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Require real-time identification of whether the medical or pharmacy benefit pathway applies.
3. Require consistent terminology, documentation, and workflows across benefit types.
4. Require qualified clinician review of complex denials and monitor denial/overturn patterns.
5. Prohibit unnecessary repeat PA for patients stable on long-term therapies.
6. Require PA approvals to remain valid for the duration of treatment and through plan transitions for an appropriate period.
7. Finalize FF-SHOP extension.
8. Shorten non-drug PA timeframes to 72 hours standard and 24 hours expedited.
9. Require all QHP prescription drug PA decisions within 24 hours.
10. Limit extensions to 7 days and require notice to patients/providers.
11. Treat missed deadlines as deemed denials that trigger appeal rights.
12. Make API usage data public, annual, and disaggregated.
13. Maintain a public CMS endpoint directory.
14. Standardize PA reporting websites, formats, and terminology and create a centralized dashboard.
15. Strengthen denial explanation content and accessibility requirements.
16. Support provider API adoption through pilots, guidance, education, and incentives.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA delays for blood cancer and chronic illness patients; inappropriate denials; repeat PA for stable therapies; unclear benefit routing; insufficient transparency; weak provider API adoption.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, medication abandonment, emergency department visits, gaps in care, repeated therapies after plan changes, inability to appeal effectively, and disparities hidden by aggregate data.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Provider lack of funding and expertise, vendor/health plan coordination challenges, inconsistent website reporting, and fragmented PA processes across coverage types.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** WEDI survey stating 90% of medical plans reported starting API implementation by January 1, 2027, while only 33% of providers indicated likely Provider Access API implementation.
- **Studies or research referenced:** JAMA Network Open study on patient experience of PA for cancer care; KFF poll identifying PA as a major burden; KFF analysis on insurer PA data; Health Affairs e-prescribing adoption study; JMIR EHR adoption systematic review; CMS early adopters press release.
- **Examples provided:** Patients stable on medications facing yearly or surprise PA; refill gaps discovered at the pharmacy; repeated step therapy after plan changes; lab/DMEPOS delays.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Blood cancer patients, chronic illness patients, caregivers, providers, QHP enrollees, small group plan enrollees, researchers, patient advocates, payers, labs, DMEPOS suppliers, and small/medium providers.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced duplicate PA and clearer reporting could lower administrative burden; provider adoption may require funding, pilots, and support.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports APIs but stresses real-time pathway routing, consistent reporting infrastructure, and provider-side technology adoption.
- **Patient care impact:** High. Comment repeatedly emphasizes that delays in cancer and chronic disease treatment can materially affect outcomes.
