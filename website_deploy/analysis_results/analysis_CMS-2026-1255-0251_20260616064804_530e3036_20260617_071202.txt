## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Surescripts; signed by Deanne Primozic, VP, Policy and Federal Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Surescripts operates a national health intelligence network including EHR vendors, PBMs, pharmacies, clinicians, health plans, and other stakeholders; reports 30.5 billion transactions in 2025; offers Real-Time Prescription Benefit and Prior Authorization Automation solutions; is a TEFCA Qualified Health Information Network participant.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Surescripts strongly supports CMS's proposal to extend and standardize electronic prior authorization for pharmacy benefit drugs using NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and Real-Time Prescription Benefit standards. The comment urges CMS to finalize pharmacy-benefit proposals as written, recognize RTPB as the more patient-specific tool for PA and coverage visibility, collaborate with ONC and NCPDP on richer RTPB versions, and address cybersecurity and resiliency through MFA, Zero Trust, encryption, TEFCA, and interagency threat-sharing.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED by background reference; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; PRIVACY-SECURITY-related GENERAL/RFI; STAKEHOLDER-COORDINATION-related GENERAL/RFI
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; RIN 0938-AV44; CMS-0057-F; CMS-9115-F; 45 CFR 170.205(u); 45 CFR 170.205(c)
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT standard version 2023011; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit Standard Implementation Guide, including v60 and unexpired versions; NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit Standard Implementation Guide, including RTPB v13 and unexpired versions; HL7 FHIR; TEFCA; MFA; Zero Trust Architecture; encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Pharmacy benefit drug standards and SCRIPT versioning**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** NCPDP standards already support pharmacy-benefit workflows and avoid disrupting current ePA, formulary, and RTPB infrastructure.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the pharmacy-benefit proposals as written, including use of NCPDP SCRIPT version 2023011 after January 1, 2028.

**Topic 2: F&B and RTPB roles in prior authorization workflow**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support/Clarify
- **Rationale:** F&B provides group-level formulary reference information, while RTPB provides patient-specific real-time coverage, cost, alternatives, and PA requirements at prescribing.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to support unexpired NCPDP F&B and RTPB versions adopted at 45 CFR 170.205 and collaborate with ONC and NCPDP on a richer RTPB path.

**Topic 3: Medicaid, CHIP, and QHP RTPB expansion**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS where QHP issuers are included
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Medicaid prescribers often lack point-of-care visibility into coverage, cost-sharing, and PA requirements, causing rejected prescriptions, delays, and administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Extend RTPB requirements to state Medicaid and CHIP FFS programs, Medicaid managed care, CHIP managed care entities, and QHP issuers on FFEs.

**Topic 4: Medical versus pharmacy benefit distinction**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Surescripts supports the proposed distinction and identifies RTPB coverage restriction codes that can signal possible medical benefit coverage.
- **Specific request:** Use NCPDP RTPB codes 816 and 817 to indicate pharmacy benefit exclusion with possible or definite medical benefit coverage.

**Topic 5: Cybersecurity and resiliency**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cybersecurity failures directly threaten patient safety when systems are disrupted by ransomware or other attacks.
- **Specific request:** Treat cyber hygiene as foundational to patient safety; promote interagency threat-sharing; mandate MFA on healthcare endpoints; use Zero Trust Architecture; encrypt ePHI at rest and in transit; support TEFCA with strong accountability.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** Surescripts supports applying NCPDP pharmacy-benefit standards beyond Part D to Medicaid, CHIP, managed care, and FFE QHP issuers.
- **S15:** Surescripts supports the proposed categories of pharmacy benefit drugs and medical benefit drugs and explains how RTPB codes 816 and 817 can identify drugs potentially covered under the medical benefit.
- **S19:** The comment indicates Medicaid implementation would address current information gaps and relies on existing PBM, EHR, and network experience, though it does not provide state-by-state readiness details.
- **S22:** Surescripts argues F&B/RTPB improve efficiency and reduce costs by reducing confusion, rejected prescriptions, and avoidable administrative burden.
- **S25:** The comment directly addresses RTPB maturity, saying RTPB v13 is growing in adoption while many providers already receive some proprietary RTPB-like PA indications.
- **S31:** The comment supports requirements for QHP issuers on FFEs but does not request an exception process.
- **S60/S61:** Surescripts supports unexpired ONC-adopted versions and asks CMS to collaborate with ONC and NCPDP on future RTPB development.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to broader RFIs on cybersecurity, resiliency, TEFCA, and related technology by recommending MFA, Zero Trust, encryption, threat-sharing, and TEFCA adoption.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize the proposed pharmacy benefit drug standards framework as written.
2. Require NCPDP SCRIPT version 2023011 after January 1, 2028.
3. Require impacted payers to support an unexpired NCPDP F&B version adopted at 45 CFR 170.205(u).
4. Require Medicaid, CHIP, managed care, and FFE QHP issuers to support an unexpired NCPDP RTPB version adopted at 45 CFR 170.205(c).
5. Recognize RTPB as the better standard for patient-specific PA, cost, and coverage information at prescribing.
6. Collaborate with ONC and NCPDP on a future path for richer RTPB functionality.
7. Use RTPB coverage restriction codes 816 and 817 to communicate pharmacy benefit exclusion and possible medical benefit coverage.
8. Treat cybersecurity as patient safety infrastructure and support real-time threat-sharing among HHS, DHS, CISA, law enforcement, and industry stakeholders.
9. Mandate MFA on healthcare system endpoints, adopt Zero Trust principles, and encrypt ePHI at rest and in transit.
10. Support TEFCA adoption for resiliency while preserving robust security controls and accountability.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Prescriber lack of real-time coverage information, pharmacy counter rejections, Medicaid information gaps, PA delays, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and fragmented standards evolution.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed medication access, avoidable administrative burden, compromised patient care during cyberattacks, and reduced resiliency.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** RTPB v13 is still in adoption; F&B files may update monthly or more frequently rather than in real time; current RTPB access may use proprietary messaging; healthcare cybersecurity requires cross-sector collaboration.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 30.5 billion transactions in 2025, up 12% from 2024; Prior Authorization Automation available to 68,000 prescribers across 42 health systems, with 119,000 more expected in 2026; 18-second median approval time; 104 medications supported; 34% automated approval rate for in-scope medications; RTPB used 1 billion times in 2025; estimated $55.1 million patient savings in 2025 and $19.5 million from January-April 2026; QHIN activity of 114,149 patient searches and 202,213 clinical documents through April 2026.
- **Studies or research referenced:** 2025 diabetes adherence study published in the Journal of Managed Care & Specialty Pharmacy; Surescripts 2025 Annual Impact Report; TEFCA; CMS Health Technology Ecosystem.
- **Examples provided:** sFTP formulary file exchange and API/on-demand formulary delivery; PBMs providing Yes/No PA signals through RTPB; EHRs automatically requesting RTPB data after drug, pharmacy, and patient fields are complete.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Prescribers, patients, Medicaid beneficiaries, pharmacies, PBMs, payers, EHR vendors, QHP issuers, health systems, state agencies, cybersecurity agencies, and QHIN participants.
- **Economic implications:** Surescripts cites patient savings from RTPB and argues standards reduce avoidable burden, rejected prescriptions, and costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment presents current broad F&B availability and growing RTPB v13 adoption as evidence of feasibility, while acknowledging ongoing adoption from proprietary to standard messaging.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved point-of-care coverage and cost visibility can reduce delays, improve adherence, and prevent care disruption; cybersecurity controls protect patient care continuity.
