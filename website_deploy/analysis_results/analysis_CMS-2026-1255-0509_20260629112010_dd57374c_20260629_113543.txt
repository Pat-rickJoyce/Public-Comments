---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

OCHIN supports CMS's goals for electronic prior authorization and interoperability but urges CMS to account for the limited capacity of rural and low-resourced providers—particularly FQHCs, CAHs, and RHCs—in setting implementation timelines, standards readiness requirements, and transition pathways. Key concerns include the operational complexity of dual FHIR/NCPDP pathways and their potential to disrupt 340B pharmacy operations, the insufficient time allowed under proposed timelines for full downstream adoption, and the need for real-world IG testing in diverse provider environments before enforcement. OCHIN requests technical assistance, funding support, and a workable X12 transition pathway during the FHIR migration.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Pathway Clarity — Medical vs. Pharmacy Benefit for Drug PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support (goals) / Modify (implementation)
- **Rationale:** The FHIR/NCPDP dual pathway distinction adds significant operational complexity, especially for safety-net providers whose drug PA workflows are tightly interconnected with pharmacy operations, 340B eligibility, prescribing, dispensing, and claims. Even small gaps in pathway clarity or data flow can disrupt 340B operations, missed opportunities, and delayed patient access—which are foundational to FQHC and CAH financial sustainability.
- **Specific request:** Provide clear, payer-specific guidance on which PA pathway applies (medical vs. pharmacy benefit) including when a drug may be covered under both; clarify whether one PA approval applies across both pathways or whether separate requests are needed; explicitly protect 340B eligibility, claims, and dispensing integrity through the transition.

**Topic 2: Implementation Guide Maturity Before Enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** An IG that has not been tested across diverse provider types, payers, EHR platforms, and operational environments creates risk rather than reducing burden. Large health systems and national payers are not representative of real-world implementation at FQHCs, CAHs, and RHCs.
- **Specific request:** Establish a clear roadmap for real-world testing, reporting, and readiness assessment before enforcement; define criteria for when standards and IGs are mature enough for broad-scale compliance; include FQHCs, RHCs, CAHs, and other limited-IT-capacity providers in pilot testing.

**Topic 3: FHIR Transition Pathway Preservation**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers should not be forced to abandon functioning X12 278 transaction pathways before FHIR-based workflows are fully tested, operationalized, and proven reliable. Premature cutover risks disrupting care, increasing manual workarounds, and undermining confidence in the broader interoperability strategy.
- **Specific request:** Maintain a clear and legally permissible transition pathway for continued use of X12 278 until FHIR-based workflows have been fully tested and proven reliable across provider types and payer environments.

**Topic 4: Implementation Timelines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The October 1, 2027 compliance date and proposed 24-month HIPAA compliance period are insufficient. After a final rule publishes, EHR vendor development alone takes 6–12 months; providers then need time to configure, test, train staff, coordinate with payers, update policies, conduct end-to-end testing, and stabilize operations. Rural and low-resourced providers manage this alongside overlapping federal requirements, workforce shortages, cybersecurity mandates, and quality reporting obligations.
- **Specific request:** Set timelines that reflect the full implementation cycle for rural and low-resourced providers; coordinate with ONC to avoid requiring parallel workflows for each new policy; allow sufficient time from when final standards and IGs are confirmed through to provider stabilization.

**Topic 5: Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Costs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Expanded API use and new payer connections require rural/low-resourced providers to manage authentication, access controls, monitoring, vendor risk, and incident response—areas where many FQHCs, CAHs, and RHCs lack dedicated cybersecurity teams and rely on vendors or shared services.
- **Specific request:** Account for cybersecurity and infrastructure costs when setting compliance timelines; pair final requirements with technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding support.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S15 (PA-DRUG-MEDICAL - medical vs. pharmacy distinction):** Flags significant operational complexity from the dual FHIR/NCPDP pathway distinction, particularly for providers with integrated pharmacy and 340B operations; requests payer-specific guidance on which pathway applies and how dual-eligible drugs are handled.
- **S12 (PA-DRUG-MEDICAL - technical challenges):** Identifies EHR vendor development cycles (6–12 months post-rule), provider workflow redesign, staff training, payer coordination, and end-to-end testing as sequential steps that collectively exceed proposed timelines.
- **S18 (FHIR-IG-REQUIRED - mandatory IG burden):** Urges IG maturity testing in rural and low-resourced settings before broad-scale compliance is required; emphasizes that an untested IG creates risk rather than reducing burden.
- **S21 (PA-DRUG-MEDICAL - timeline achievable):** States that the October 1, 2027 compliance date is insufficient for the full downstream adoption cycle needed in rural/low-resourced settings.
- **S37 (FHIR-IG-REQUIRED - implementation challenges):** Details the multi-stage implementation cycle rural providers must complete and competition with overlapping federal requirements.
- **S47 (HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS - timeline):** Asserts the proposed 24-month HIPAA compliance period does not account for the full implementation cycle; advocates for preserving X12 278 as a transition pathway.
- **S51 (HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS - non-CMS challenges):** Notes rural providers are simultaneously managing overlapping federal/state requirements, cybersecurity mandates, quality reporting, and AI tool rollouts, all competing for the same limited resources.
- **S23 (API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING - security concerns):** Notes cybersecurity infrastructure costs of expanded API connections and new payer connections, which rural providers lack capacity to manage without dedicated support and funding.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide clear, payer-specific guidance on which PA pathway (medical benefit/FHIR or pharmacy benefit/NCPDP) applies to each drug, including when coverage under both benefits is possible.
2. Clarify whether a single PA approval applies across both benefit pathways or separate requests are required, and how this interacts with 340B eligibility and claims.
3. Explicitly protect 340B eligibility determinations, claims processes, and inventory management from disruption caused by new PA pathway requirements.
4. Establish a roadmap for real-world IG testing—including in FQHCs, CAHs, and RHCs—and define maturity criteria before broad-scale enforcement.
5. Test pilot workflows in real care settings (not just large health systems and national payers) before enforcement.
6. Maintain a legally permissible transition pathway for continued use of X12 278 until FHIR-based workflows are fully tested and proven reliable at scale.
7. Set compliance timelines that reflect the full implementation cycle: vendor development, provider configuration, workflow redesign, staff training, payer coordination, end-to-end testing, and stabilization.
8. Coordinate with ONC to use a consistent implementation approach across related requirements so providers are not building separate workflows for each policy.
9. Account for cybersecurity and infrastructure costs in compliance timelines; pair requirements with technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding support for rural and low-resourced providers.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Implementation timelines insufficient for rural/low-resourced providers; dual FHIR/NCPDP pathway distinction risks disrupting 340B pharmacy operations; IGs not mature enough for broad-scale enforcement without prior real-world testing in diverse provider settings.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disruption to 340B eligibility, claims, and dispensing; missed 340B opportunities; delays in patient access to medications; increased administrative burden; premature abandonment of functioning X12 workflows; widening gap between well-resourced health systems and safety-net providers.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** EHR vendor development cycles (6–12 months post-rule); overlapping competing federal requirements; limited IT staff and no dedicated cybersecurity teams; workforce shortages; financial pressures from 340B uncertainty; lack of reserve capital for rapid implementation cycles.

---

- **Data or statistics cited:** 300+ independent provider organizations across 44 states; 44,000 providers serving rural, Tribal, and underserved communities; EHR vendor development cycles of 6–12 months or more.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** 340B program as foundational to FQHC/CAH medication access and financial sustainability; savings reinvested to expand pharmacy services and offset uncompensated care in rural communities; dual-benefit drug coverage scenario requiring PA in both FHIR and NCPDP pathways.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** FQHCs, CAHs, RHCs, and other rural/low-resourced safety-net providers; their patients (rural, Tribal, underserved communities); EHR vendors serving these organizations; 340B-participating entities.
- **Economic implications:** New requirements create cybersecurity infrastructure costs, workflow redesign costs, staff training costs, and vendor upgrade costs that rural organizations cannot absorb on tight timelines; 340B program disruption directly threatens financial sustainability of safety-net providers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Insufficient without adequate IG maturity testing in real-world rural/safety-net settings; EHR vendor development cycles alone consume much of the proposed compliance window; dual pathway distinction requires reliable payer-specific guidance to be operationally feasible.
- **Patient care impact:** Disruption to 340B and PA workflows risks delaying needed medications for patients in rural and underserved communities; delays in drug PA worsen management of chronic conditions; premature FHIR cutover could disrupt care.
