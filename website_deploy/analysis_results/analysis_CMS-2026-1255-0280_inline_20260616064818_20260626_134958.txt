## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter opposes prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing, arguing they cause treatment delays, abandoned care, administrative burden, compromised clinical decision-making, and unexpected patient costs. The comment is not directly tied to the proposed drug prior authorization API provisions but is relevant to the rule's broader RFI/general issues around prior authorization burden and potential expansion to services such as labs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; potentially GENERAL/RFIs S66-S82 concerning broader prior authorization topics such as lab/DME.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratory Prior Authorization Delays**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that waiting for payer approval delays vital test results, including genetic and infectious disease screening, and can postpone life-saving treatment.
- **Specific request:** Reduce or eliminate laboratory prior authorization barriers that delay diagnosis and treatment.

**Topic 2: Administrative Burden and Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter says complex payer protocols, phone calls, paperwork, and minor coding or clerical errors consume clinical staff time and can trigger automatic denials.
- **Specific request:** Simplify laboratory PA workflows and reduce denial risk from administrative errors.

**Topic 3: Patient Harm and Financial Exposure**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that PA friction may force less appropriate alternatives, contribute to adverse health events, increase hospitalizations, and expose patients to surprise bills.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from delayed, denied, or unexpectedly uncovered laboratory services.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** The comment generally responds to broader RFI issues involving prior authorization burden beyond drugs, especially laboratory testing. It urges CMS to consider delays, abandonment, staff burden, and patient financial risk when evaluating PA policies.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Reduce or eliminate laboratory prior authorization requirements that delay clinically indicated testing.
2. Streamline payer protocols to reduce manual paperwork, phone calls, and staff burden.
3. Prevent automatic denials caused by minor coding or clerical errors.
4. Protect patients from surprise out-of-pocket costs when PA denials occur.
