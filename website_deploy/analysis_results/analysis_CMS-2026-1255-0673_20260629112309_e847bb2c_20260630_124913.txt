# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Consensus Cloud Solutions; signed by Steve Tolle, Chief Healthcare Solutions Officer
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Operates a large cloud-based digital fax and healthcare document exchange network; offers the Conductor platform with intelligent document processing (IDP), structured data extraction, routing, and downstream API integration.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Consensus Cloud Solutions strongly supports CMS's goals of reducing prior authorization burden, accelerating decisions, and improving transparency, but argues that direct FHIR API implementation is not realistic for much of the provider ecosystem. It asks CMS to recognize IDP-enabled digital fax intermediaries as a valid compliance pathway, phase requirements based on provider readiness, avoid retiring fax until FHIR attachment workflows mature, and condition FHIR replacement of X12N 278 on conformance testing and real-world readiness.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0057-F; no specific CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR R4; Da Vinci implementation guides; Da Vinci PAS; Da Vinci CDex; X12N 278; HIPAA Administrative Simplification; payer Prior Authorization APIs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Recognition of Digital Fax and IDP Intermediaries**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Many providers still rely on digital fax because it is universal, supports diverse documentation types, and requires no bilateral API credentialing or endpoint registration. Smaller and rural providers often lack FHIR-capable EHRs or IT staff.
- **Specific request:** Formally recognize qualified IDP-enabled digital fax intermediaries that generate FHIR-compatible outputs to payer Prior Authorization APIs as a valid electronic PA compliance pathway.

**Topic 2: Provider FHIR Readiness and Phased Compliance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A framework that assumes all providers can implement direct FHIR APIs would exclude independent practices, rural clinics, behavioral health providers, home health agencies, and post-acute facilities.
- **Specific request:** Establish a phased compliance framework calibrated to provider size, EHR FHIR certification status, and technical readiness, with intermediary-mediated pathways for smaller and rural providers.

**Topic 3: Unstructured Clinical Documentation Gap**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization, especially for specialty drugs, biologics, infused therapies, and complex procedures, depends heavily on narrative notes, lab results, imaging interpretations, letters of medical necessity, and step therapy histories that are not naturally discrete FHIR resources.
- **Specific request:** Do not retire fax-based clinical documentation exchange until CDex and related standards demonstrate tested end-to-end interoperability across representative EHR and payer systems.

**Topic 4: Machine-Readable Documentation Requirements**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; TRANSPARENCY-related provisions
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers currently guess or over-submit supporting documentation because payers do not consistently specify exact clinical document needs in machine-readable form.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to publish machine-readable prior authorization documentation requirements through their Prior Authorization APIs.

**Topic 5: X12N Replacement and HIPAA Administrative Simplification**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Replacing X12N 278 with FHIR should be based on operational readiness, testing tools, payer sandboxes, and end-to-end conformance results rather than publication of implementation guides alone.
- **Specific request:** Establish conformance testing infrastructure before mandating X12N replacement and clarify HIPAA obligations for digital fax intermediaries, clearinghouses, and IDP platforms.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Consensus identifies major technical challenges for medical-benefit drug PA, especially the need to exchange unstructured documentation for specialty drugs and biologics.
- **S18:** The commenter warns that mandatory FHIR IG use can burden providers that lack FHIR-capable EHRs, IT staff, or API implementation capacity.
- **S37:** Consensus describes implementation challenges for smaller, rural, behavioral health, post-acute, and independent providers and proposes intermediary-based compliance.
- **S44:** The comment supports the direction of FHIR-based prior authorization but says FHIR documentation exchange is not mature enough to replace fax for unstructured attachments.
- **S46:** Consensus urges CMS not to replace X12N 278 until conformance testing, sandboxes, and demonstrated end-to-end testing exist.
- **S47:** The commenter recommends phased compliance tied to provider capacity and readiness rather than a uniform direct-FHIR expectation.
- **S49:** The comment emphasizes small and rural provider impact, warning of a compliance cliff for smaller practices without an intermediary pathway.
- **S50:** Consensus specifically argues Da Vinci CDex remains immature, inconsistently implemented, and lacking sufficient conformance testing.
- **S51:** The comment explains that non-CMS provider participants may be unable to implement direct FHIR connections and need intermediary support.
- **S54:** Consensus asks for ONC-managed conformance testing tools, payer-published sandbox environments, and demonstrated testing before mandating replacement of X12N 278.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Recognize IDP-enabled digital fax intermediaries as a valid compliance pathway for electronic prior authorization.
2. Define minimum capabilities and create a certification pathway for qualified intermediaries.
3. Phase compliance based on provider size, EHR FHIR certification status, and technical readiness.
4. Keep intermediary-mediated pathways available for smaller and rural providers during the transition.
5. Condition any fax sunset on demonstrated maturity of CDex and related FHIR attachment standards.
6. Commission an annual readiness assessment with ONC participation before advancing any fax phase-out.
7. Require payers to publish machine-readable clinical documentation requirements through Prior Authorization APIs.
8. Establish conformance testing infrastructure before mandating replacement of X12N 278.
9. Require payer sandboxes and demonstrated end-to-end testing results.
10. Clarify HIPAA Administrative Simplification obligations for digital fax intermediaries, clearinghouses, and IDP platforms.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Direct FHIR API requirements may exclude less-resourced providers; FHIR attachment workflows are not mature; clinical documentation remains heavily unstructured; X12N replacement may be premature; intermediary obligations under HIPAA need clarity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Compliance cliff for smaller practices, increased inequities in administrative capacity, hybrid workflows that add complexity, continued reliance on fax without formal recognition, and disruption of existing provider-payer exchange.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of FHIR R4 or Da Vinci support in many EHRs, limited provider IT staffing, inconsistent CDex support, absent document type standardization, lack of machine-readable documentation requests, and lack of conformance testing.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Providers, especially smaller and rural practices; payers; technology intermediaries; EHR vendors; clearinghouses; patients whose access depends on complete PA documentation.
- **Economic implications:** The commenter argues an intermediary approach avoids forcing smaller providers to buy new EHR systems, infrastructure, or technical staff, though no cost estimates are provided.
- **Technical feasibility:** Direct FHIR is feasible for some large systems but not the full provider ecosystem; IDP-enabled fax-to-FHIR translation is presented as a scalable bridge.
- **Patient care impact:** Without inclusive pathways, patients served by smaller or less-resourced providers may face continued delays or administrative exclusion from electronic PA benefits.
