# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Health Level Seven (HL7) International; signed by Rachel Dunscombe, Chief Executive Officer, and Caroline Macumber, Board Chair.
- **Stakeholder type:** Other - standards development organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** HL7 describes itself as a global authority on healthcare interoperability; input came from HL7 leadership, Policy Advisory Committee, Patient Empowerment and Payer/Provider Information Exchange Work Groups, FAST, and Da Vinci-related communities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HL7 strongly supports CMS's move toward FHIR-based API exchange, required implementation guides, medical benefit drug prior authorization through FHIR, pharmacy benefit drug prior authorization through NCPDP, and adoption of FHIR as a HIPAA standard for prior authorization. It asks CMS to refine timing and versioning, clarify implementation dates, recognize FAST Security and FAST National Directory, support endpoint and trust metadata discovery, address electronic appeals and 24-hour clock questions, and invest in real-world testing, consent, resiliency, and step therapy continuity.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by appeal/denial transparency implication; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.215(j)-(n); 45 CFR 162.1301; 45 CFR 162.1201.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR; US Core; SMART App Launch; OpenID Connect; Da Vinci PDex, PDex US Drug Formulary, PDex Plan Net, CRD, DTR, PAS, CDex, ATR; FAST Security IG; FAST Identity IG; FAST National Directory/NDH IG; FAST Consent; NCPDP RTPB and SCRIPT; X12N 278; X12N 270/271; OAuth; UDAP; TEFCA; SEMVER; SVAP; CDS Hooks.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Overall FHIR/API direction**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** HL7 views CMS-0062-P as a foundational step toward scalable API-based exchange that can reduce burden and improve care.
- **Specific request:** Continue advancing required FHIR APIs while coordinating trust, consent, directory services, testing, and operational validation.

**Topic 2: Medical and pharmacy benefit drug PA standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 supports using CRD, DTR, PAS, and CDex for medical benefit drugs and NCPDP RTPB/SCRIPT for pharmacy benefit drugs, arguing bifurcation uses the best tools for each context.
- **Specific request:** Adopt FHIR-based medical benefit drug PA, retain NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drug PA, and consider an end-of-Q1 2028 compliance date rather than October 1, 2027.

**Topic 3: Mandatory IGs, versioning, and expiration**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 supports requiring IGs but warns that version availability, expiration terminology, and compliance/effective dates must be clear. It says Da Vinci IGs do not always mature on an annual cadence.
- **Specific request:** Do not require specific IGs before January 1, 2028; allow relevant versions through that date; clarify expired/unexpired terminology; align IPPS Promoting Interoperability requirements; adapt SVAP to HL7 consensus timing and permit voluntary upgrades.

**Topic 4: Recommended IGs and trust infrastructure**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PRIVACY-SECURITY theme
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 supports ATR, CDex, and FAST Security as recommended IGs and says scalable interoperability requires organizational trust, onboarding, certificates, authorization, and directory services beyond endpoint URLs.
- **Specific request:** Evaluate and recognize FAST Security for trusted onboarding, cross-network authentication, federated trust, organizational identity, and security metadata; consider FAST National Directory and FAST Identity as implementation guidance.

**Topic 5: Prior authorization communications, appeals, metrics, and timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS by metrics implication
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 identifies an electronic appeals gap and asks how 24-hour limits apply when additional information is needed. It also says drug PA metrics should be calculated consistently.
- **Specific request:** Clarify when the 24-hour clock starts for incomplete requests; support electronic/automated appeals; request both average and median PA metrics; clarify calendar-day versus business-day calculations.

**Topic 6: API endpoint reporting and endpoint discovery**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 supports a standardized endpoint discovery mechanism but says simple URL publication is insufficient for scalable trusted exchange.
- **Specific request:** Require or formally recognize FAST National Directory for endpoint registration and updates; publish trust framework participation, OAuth authorization server metadata, digital certificates, dynamic client registration endpoints, and security capability metadata.

**Topic 7: HIPAA prior authorization standards**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** HL7 supports defining PA under HIPAA and replacing X12N 278 and PA-related X12N 270/271 use with FHIR, arguing FHIR and Da Vinci IGs can reduce burden and improve DSMO coordination.
- **Specific request:** Adopt FHIR, US Core, SMART App Launch, CRD, DTR, PAS, and CDex for applicable HIPAA prior authorization transactions; maintain standards through the DSMO process and align versioning to semantic versioning, ONC, and SVAP.

**Topic 8: RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, testing, certification, and step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 says FHIR notifications, FAST Security, national sandbox testing, right-sized certification, and payer-to-payer step therapy data can improve care and reduce burden.
- **Specific request:** Use Da Vinci Unsolicited Notification and FHIR subscriptions; promote FAST Security for resiliency; prioritize end-to-end, cross-network, in situ, security, and multi-party testing; use national sandboxes; right-size certification; and require prior payer step therapy information to support continuity.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** HL7 supports requiring FHIR IGs to ensure interoperable implementation of required APIs.
- **S2/S3:** HL7 asks CMS and ONC to clarify required versions, expiration dates, compliance dates, and use of available versions through January 1, 2028.
- **S18/S37:** HL7 supports mandatory IGs but recommends timing changes and testing infrastructure to reduce implementation burden.
- **S12/S21:** HL7 supports adding medical benefit drugs to the Prior Authorization API and says October 1, 2027 is reasonable, though end of Q1 2028 may better fit renewal and budget cycles.
- **S14/S25:** HL7 supports NCPDP RTPB and SCRIPT for pharmacy benefit drug electronic PA.
- **S15:** HL7 expressly supports bifurcating medical benefit drugs through FHIR and pharmacy benefit drugs through NCPDP standards.
- **S20/S43:** HL7 asks CMS to clarify how 24-hour decision limits apply when the initial PA request is incomplete.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** HL7 supports endpoint reporting but recommends FHIR-based directory infrastructure, automated endpoint updates, independent endpoint discoverability, and publication of trust and security metadata.
- **S29/S34:** HL7 suggests drug-related PA metrics should include both average and median and clarify calendar days versus business days.
- **S44-S56:** HL7 supports adopting FHIR as the HIPAA standard for PA transactions, replacing X12N 278 and PA-related X12N 270/271 use, adopting CDex for attachments, and using DSMO, SEMVER, and SVAP-aligned maintenance.
- **S50/S60-S65:** HL7 addresses standards maturity, versioning, ONC incorporation by reference, FAST Security, FAST Identity, FAST National Directory, and SVAP flexibility.
- **S54:** HL7 recommends end-to-end, cross-network, security, and multi-party testing, including FAST Security Connectathon activities and a national sandbox.
- **GENERAL/RFI S66-S82:** HL7 responds to RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, payer API implementation, testing/certification, and step therapy continuity.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Continue advancing FHIR-based APIs and required IGs while building trust, consent, directory, testing, and operational infrastructure.
2. Use FHIR/Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS, and CDex for medical benefit drug PA and NCPDP RTPB/SCRIPT for pharmacy benefit drug PA.
3. Consider end-of-Q1 2028 compliance for medical benefit drug PA and mandatory IG use.
4. Clarify compliance dates, effective dates, expired/unexpired version terminology, and available IG versions.
5. Adapt SVAP to HL7 consensus timing and support voluntary standards upgrades.
6. Recommend ATR, CDex, and FAST Security and consider FAST Identity and FAST National Directory as supporting infrastructure.
7. Use FAST National Directory for endpoint registration, updates, and discovery of trust and security metadata.
8. Support electronic or automated appeals and clearer patient/provider appeal pathways.
9. Clarify how 24-hour PA timeframes apply when additional information is needed.
10. Collect both average and median drug PA metrics and clarify calendar versus business-day calculations.
11. Adopt FHIR standards for HIPAA prior authorization transactions and CDex for PA attachments.
12. Prioritize end-to-end, cross-network, real-world, and security testing, with national sandbox support.
13. Right-size health IT certification for payer and provider systems.
14. Use payer-to-payer data to avoid forcing patients to repeat failed step therapy.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Scalable trust, versioning uncertainty, endpoint discovery, incomplete PA requests, appeals gaps, consent infrastructure, cybersecurity, real-world testing, and avoiding arbitrary standards timelines.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Fragmented onboarding, inconsistent security, expanded cybersecurity risk, implementation burden, confusion over required IG versions, and repeated step therapy after payer transitions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Annual SVAP cadence misaligned with IG maturity, lack of national directory readiness detail, contract turnover affecting endpoints, insufficient end-to-end testing, and lack of automated trust infrastructure.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None quantified.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Da Vinci Project submitted comments; HL7/FAST work group and accelerator input; SEMVER.org.
- **Examples provided:** Endpoint metadata, OAuth authorization servers, certificates, dynamic client registration, FAST Security Connectathon testing, national sandbox, and payer-to-payer step therapy transfer.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, providers, payers, vendors, covered entities, clearinghouses, SDOs, CMS, ONC, and trust network participants.
- **Economic implications:** HL7 frames its recommendations as reducing burden and avoiding unnecessary certification or implementation cost, but does not provide dollar estimates.
- **Technical feasibility:** HL7 sees FHIR, Da Vinci IGs, FAST Security, FAST National Directory, and related testing as feasible but needing coordinated implementation and clear version governance.
- **Patient care impact:** Improved PA automation, appeals, patient access, consent, event notification, and step therapy continuity could reduce delays and improve patient experience.
