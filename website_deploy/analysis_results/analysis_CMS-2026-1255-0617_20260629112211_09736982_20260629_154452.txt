# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Oral Health Interoperability Alliance (OHIA)
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Technology and standards coalition / Other
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** OHIA describes itself as a bipartisan, multi-stakeholder coalition including dental practice management and EDR vendors, imaging and AI developers, clearinghouses, revenue cycle platforms, HIEs, QHINs, payers, public health agencies, standards experts, and patient advocates. Members participate in HL7 standards development, IG testing, and real-world implementation.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

OHIA supports CMS's standards-based prior authorization and API direction but asks CMS to explicitly include dentistry and stand-alone dental plans in the interoperability framework. It recommends dental-specific implementation pathways, RTPBC use for dental prescribing benefit classification, dental testing in payer API/ONC certification, flexible FHIR versioning, attention to CDT/CDex/radiographic attachments, and a transition away from DDE portals calibrated to dental market readiness.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS/stand-alone dental plan RFI; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** References CMS-0062-P sections I.C, II.B, II.E, II.F, II.H, and RFIs; no specific CFR citations emphasized.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; HL7; Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS; CDex; Consumer Real-Time Pharmacy Benefit Check (RTPBC) IG v2.0; NCPDP RTPB Standard v13; CDT code set; FHIR Subscriptions; SMART Health Cards; SMART Health Links; DDE; X12 278; Oral Health FHIR Implementation Guide.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Explicit inclusion of dentistry**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** Dentistry is unevenly represented in federal interoperability policy, and ambiguity could leave dental PA dependent on portals and manual workflows.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly confirm that dental payers, dental providers, dental practice management systems, and stand-alone dental plans are within the interoperability and PA modernization framework.

**Topic 2: Stand-alone dental plans**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** SADPs remain exempt from Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs even though HIPAA FHIR-based PA proposals partially close the gap.
- **Specific request:** Include SADPs in the interoperability ecosystem with a defined implementation pathway and a three-year transition period where appropriate.

**Topic 3: Dental prescribing and RTPBC**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Dental workflows may not have mature NCPDP-based infrastructure, and benefit classification should occur before PA initiation.
- **Specific request:** Recognize Consumer RTPBC IG v2.0 as the mechanism for benefit classification queries and include dental-specific drug categories in RTPBC implementation guidance.

**Topic 4: Payer API conformance and ONC certification**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Current conformance approaches do not reliably detect dental workflow issues such as CDT handling, dental attachments, and dental PA scenarios.
- **Specific request:** Include dental-specific code sets, workflows, attachments, treatment sequencing, and prior authorization scenarios in payer API conformance testing and ONC certification pathways.

**Topic 5: HIPAA FHIR-based PA adoption**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** OHIA supports adoption of CRD, DTR, and PAS as the HIPAA PA standard but notes dental workflows vary across Medicaid, commercial dental, portal, and pre-determination environments.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly name dentistry in final rule/guidance, establish a distinct dental timeline where needed, and include structured clinical data, radiographic attachments through CDex, and CDT support.

**Topic 6: Versioning and oral health IG pathway**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Dental implementers could be forced to build to outdated specifications if final rules lock fixed versions while oral health IG work continues.
- **Specific request:** Adopt current ballot-reconciled IG versions at final rulemaking and establish a sub-regulatory update process, including for CRD, DTR, PAS, CDex, and oral-health-related FHIR IGs.

**Topic 7: DDE portal exception**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS
- **Position:** Modify / Support transition away
- **Rationale:** Portals perpetuate payer-specific workflows and disproportionately burden small dental practices.
- **Specific request:** Initiate rulemaking to narrow and ultimately eliminate the DDE exception with a readiness-calibrated transition timeline.

**Topic 8: Event notifications and step therapy dental use cases**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Dental providers need access to broader care coordination information and step therapy-like dental benefit history.
- **Specific request:** Include dental providers as eligible recipients for event notifications; treat FHIR Subscriptions as the long-term approach; include dental benefit history, prior authorization history, CDT history, and documentation in Payer-to-Payer exchange.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S2/S3:** OHIA supports required FHIR IG adoption but asks for flexible versioning and sub-regulatory updates so dental implementers use current specifications.
- **S8/S9:** OHIA references SMART Health Cards, SMART Health Links, and FHIR Subscriptions as tools to reduce portal lookups and support event notifications.
- **S12/S15:** OHIA addresses dental prescribing and benefit classification, recommending RTPBC before PA initiation.
- **S23/S24/S26/S33:** OHIA supports payer API conformance/certification that includes dental code sets, attachments, and scenarios.
- **S44-S56:** OHIA supports CRD/DTR/PAS as HIPAA electronic PA standards and asks CMS/HHS to explicitly include dentistry, with dental-specific implementation timelines where needed.
- **S60-S65:** OHIA supports updated standards and flexible version pathways for oral health-related FHIR IGs.
- **S66-S82:** OHIA responds to RFIs on event notifications, DDE portals, and step therapy by requesting dental-specific use cases and Payer-to-Payer support.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Confirm dental payers, providers, practice management systems, and stand-alone dental plans are in scope.
2. Establish a defined SADP implementation pathway with a three-year transition period where appropriate.
3. Recognize RTPBC IG v2.0 for benefit classification before PA initiation in dental prescribing workflows.
4. Include dental-specific drug categories in RTPBC implementation guidance.
5. Include CDT codes, dental workflows, attachments, and dental PA scenarios in payer API conformance testing and ONC certification.
6. Adopt CRD, DTR, and PAS as HIPAA PA standards while explicitly naming dentistry.
7. Use current ballot-reconciled IG versions and create a flexible sub-regulatory update pathway.
8. Address structured clinical data, radiographic attachments through CDex, and CDT support.
9. Narrow and ultimately eliminate the DDE exception through readiness-calibrated rulemaking.
10. Include dental providers in event notification policy and support FHIR Subscriptions as the long-term model.
11. Confirm dental benefit history and PA history are in scope for Payer-to-Payer exchange.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Ambiguity about dental inclusion, two-tier interoperability, outdated standards, insufficient dental conformance testing, ongoing portal dependence, and lack of dental-specific data exchange.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Dental PA may remain manual while medical PA becomes automated; small and rural dental practices may face avoidable burden; oral-systemic care coordination may suffer.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Dental market readiness, limited FHIR support in dental practice management systems, inconsistent CDT handling, attachments/radiographs, legacy portal workflows, pre-determination practices.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No numeric statistics cited.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No external studies cited; comment relies on coalition expertise, HL7 participation, Connectathon-developed test data, and real-world implementation experience.
- **Examples provided:** Dental sedation/anesthesia CDT code revisions; dental radiographic attachments; implants, orthodontics, periodontal procedures, and step therapy-like benefit sequencing.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Dental payers, SADPs, dentists, DSOs, dental vendors, clearinghouses, payers, patients, ONC, CMS, HL7 participants.
- **Economic implications:** Portal dependence and payer-specific processes impose burden, especially on small practices; calibrated timelines could reduce implementation shock.
- **Technical feasibility:** OHIA believes standards-based dental adoption is feasible with explicit scope, testing, version flexibility, and dental-specific use cases.
- **Patient care impact:** Better dental interoperability can improve whole-person care coordination, reduce PA delays, and preserve continuity across dental/medical workflows.
