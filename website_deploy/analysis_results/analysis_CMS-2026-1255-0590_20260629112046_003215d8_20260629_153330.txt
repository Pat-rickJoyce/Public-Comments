- **Author/Organization name:** Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; Maya Bermingham, Senior Vice President, Regeneron Public Policy & Government Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Other / pharmaceutical manufacturer / biotechnology company
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Regeneron is a biotechnology company developing and commercializing medicines for serious diseases, including eye diseases, inflammatory diseases, cancer, cardiovascular/metabolic diseases, neurological diseases, hematologic conditions, infectious diseases, and rare diseases.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Regeneron responds primarily to CMS's Step Therapy RFI and urges CMS to rescind the Medicare Advantage Part B step therapy regulation at 42 CFR 422.136. It argues that MA step therapy for Part B drugs violates the Medicare statute by allowing restrictions not found in Original Medicare, and asks CMS to issue interim sub-regulatory guidance prohibiting MA plans from requiring patients to fail on more than one drug before accessing the physician's selected therapy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS not directly; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL indirectly through Part B drugs and step therapy; REGULATORY-AUTHORITY theme.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 422.136; SSA §§ 1832(a)(1), 1852(a)(1), 1852(c)(1)(G), 1852(c)(2)(B), 1861(s)(2); 42 CFR 422.4(a)(1)(ii); 42 CFR 422.136(b)(11); 5 USC 553; 84 FR 23832; 84 FR 23853; 84 FR 23860; 84 FR 23864; 90 FR 20478; 91 FR 19890; 91 FR 20013; references to Loper Bright, Chevron, Whitman, Erringer, Flagstaff, and Chao.
- **Standards referenced:** Step therapy technology generally; no FHIR IG, NCPDP, or API standards discussed in detail.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Rescind MA Part B step therapy regulation**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose current regulation / rescind
- **Rationale:** Regeneron argues that 42 CFR 422.136 permits MA plans to impose step therapy restrictions on Part B drugs not imposed in Original Medicare, contrary to the statutory requirement that MA plans provide the same Part A and B benefits as Original Medicare.
- **Specific request:** Initiate notice-and-comment rulemaking to rescind 42 CFR 422.136.

**Topic 2: Statutory authority and post-Loper Bright review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose / legal challenge
- **Rationale:** The commenter says disclosure provisions about utilization management do not create substantive authority to withhold a covered Part B drug, and that the regulation cannot survive the best-reading standard after Loper Bright.
- **Specific request:** Apply the Administration's regulatory review directive to identify and repeal unlawful regulations, including 42 CFR 422.136.

**Topic 3: Interim guidance limiting multiple-step protocols**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify pending rescission
- **Rationale:** Rulemaking will take time, and protocols requiring failure on multiple drugs impose the greatest burden and harm. The regulation does not specify the number of steps, creating room for interpretive guidance.
- **Specific request:** Issue sub-regulatory guidance clarifying that MA plans may not require a patient to try and fail more than one drug before covering the physician's originally prescribed therapy.

**Topic 4: Patient harm and provider burden from step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose harmful step therapy
- **Rationale:** Multiple-step protocols substitute plan preferences for physician judgment, delay effective treatment, and may be especially dangerous for progressive diseases.
- **Specific request:** Recognize the excessive burdens and risks of multiple-step policies and act immediately to curtail them.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to the Step Therapy RFI by arguing that technology cannot fix the underlying legal and policy flaw of MA Part B step therapy. It requests rescission of 42 CFR 422.136 and interim guidance barring more than one required step.
- **S52 (regulatory authority, by analogy):** Although not responding to HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS, the comment raises statutory authority concerns under the MA statute and administrative law.
- **S53/S55 (phasing/enforcement, by analogy):** Requests interim sub-regulatory guidance while notice-and-comment rescission proceeds.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Rescind 42 CFR 422.136 through notice-and-comment rulemaking.
2. Recognize that MA plans must furnish the same Part B drug benefits as Original Medicare and should not impose step therapy restrictions absent from Original Medicare.
3. Issue immediate sub-regulatory guidance clarifying that the current regulation does not permit multiple-step step therapy protocols for Part B drugs.
4. Specifically prohibit MA plans from requiring patients to fail on more than one drug before receiving the physician's first-choice therapy.
5. Explain in guidance that multiple-step policies impose excessive burdens, risks, and negative health consequences, particularly for progressive disease.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Lack of statutory authority for MA Part B step therapy, inferior MA benefit compared with Original Medicare, multiple-step protocols, plan intrusion into physician judgment, and delays to life-transforming or life-saving drugs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed access, ineffective initial treatment, treatment abandonment, severe side effects, disease progression, irreversible damage, and provider burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Notice-and-comment rescission will take time; MA enrollment is large and growing; current protocols already impose harm.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** KFF reporting that 54% of eligible Medicare beneficiaries were enrolled in MA in 2025 and CMS February 2026 enrollment data showing just over 35 million MA enrollees; CBO projection that MA share will reach 64% by 2034; 2025 AMA survey reporting 75% of physicians saw ineffective initial treatment due to step therapy, 95% reported PA delays necessary care, and 79% reported PA can lead to treatment abandonment; Avalere data on multiple-step requirements for rheumatoid arthritis drugs in MA.
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF Medicare Advantage enrollment analyses, CBO projections, AMA Prior Authorization Physician Survey, Avalere Health analysis, and a 2018 AMA coalition letter on Part B step therapy.
- **Examples provided:** MA plans requiring failure on two or more alternatives before covering the physician-selected drug; patients with autoimmune disorders and progressive blinding eye diseases where fail-first delays can cause irreversible damage.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** MA enrollees, Original Medicare beneficiaries by comparison, physicians, patients with serious and progressive diseases, MA plans, CMS, pharmaceutical manufacturers, and providers.
- **Economic implications:** Regeneron focuses less on direct cost estimates and more on statutory benefit parity, treatment delay, and provider burden; rescission may reduce administrative burden and treatment delays.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment argues technology improvements are insufficient; legal/policy rescission and guidance are needed.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly negative under current multiple-step protocols; rescission/interim guidance would improve access to physician-selected Part B drugs.
