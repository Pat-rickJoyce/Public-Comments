## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Maine Primary Care Association (MPCA), signed by Darcy Shargo, MFA, Chief Executive Officer.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association / Healthcare Provider representative (Community Health Centers/FQHCs).
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents Maine Community Health Centers and CHC lookalikes; provides HRSA UDS data and operational experience from safety-net primary care, Medicaid, pharmacy/340B, health IT, and cybersecurity contexts.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MPCA supports CMS's interoperability and electronic prior authorization goals, especially extension of ePA to drugs, centralized API endpoint reporting, denial transparency, and improved PA timeframes. It urges CMS to pair these mandates with implementation flexibility, vendor accountability, technical assistance, cybersecurity funding, CHC-specific testing, and PA reform measures such as gold-carding, automatic approvals for missed deadlines, and step therapy continuity.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Interoperability API implementation support for CHCs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs can reduce fragmented payer workflows, duplicative documentation, and care delays, but CHCs lack the staffing, budget, cybersecurity capacity, and vendor leverage to absorb unfunded implementation burdens.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate CMS and ONC timelines, require readiness validation, establish minimum implementation requirements across MCOs and QHPs, clarify providers are not penalized for vendor failures, provide funding and technical assistance, and publish a consolidated provider-side regulatory impact analysis.

**Topic 2: Electronic prior authorization for drugs and benefit classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support with clarification.
- **Rationale:** Medication access is critical for CHC patients with chronic disease, behavioral health needs, and SUD; dual medical/pharmacy pathways can create confusion for clinic-administered drugs, long-acting injectables, vaccines, contraceptives, specialty biologics, and 340B workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification information through Provider Access APIs/CRD Coverage Information, clarify whether a PA approval under one benefit applies to alternate payment pathways, coordinate with OPA on 340B implementation, and provide pharmacy workflow guidance.

**Topic 3: Prior authorization reform beyond digitization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** ePA alone may accelerate inefficient or inappropriate utilization management without reducing burden or improving access.
- **Specific request:** Pair ePA with PA reduction, prescription-drug gold-carding, sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories, proactive PA expiration alerts, automatic approval when deadlines are missed, and stronger oversight of persistent delays or inappropriate denials.

**Topic 4: Denial reasons, decision timelines, and public reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support and expand.
- **Rationale:** Clear denial reasons and uniform timeframes help CHCs correct submissions, appeal denials, and prevent delays for medically underserved patients.
- **Specific request:** Require actionable denial explanations, centralized CMS website reporting, denial rates by service/medication category, first-pass approval rates, provider touchpoints, overturns tied to unclear notices, ePA functionality metrics, and data quality metrics.

**Topic 5: API endpoint reporting and API usage metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with enhancements.
- **Rationale:** Central endpoint publication reduces the burden on small CHC IT teams and improves vendor/app integration; meaningful usage metrics are needed to determine whether APIs work in practice.
- **Specific request:** Publish machine-readable API endpoint files centrally, require timely updates and annual verification, expand metrics to include functionality/usability, successful and failed requests, structured vs unstructured data, workflow impact, calls per query/user, and API maintenance cost.

**Topic 6: Retaining formulary information and federal support**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Provider access to real-time formulary data is important because payer websites are not sufficiently reliable at point of care.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removal of drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs and prioritize safety-net providers for available federal matching funds.

**Topic 7: Open Payments CMP definition and 340B intersection**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Support with coordination.
- **Rationale:** Stronger audit documentation and reporting accountability improve transparency and public trust, but CHCs face complex manufacturer, wholesaler, TPA, and contract pharmacy relationships.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with HRSA so Open Payments enforcement and 340B compliance structures do not create duplicative or conflicting safety-net burdens.

**Topic 8: HIPAA PA standards and CDex guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with transition safeguards.
- **Rationale:** Transitioning from X12 to FHIR can improve interoperability, but CHCs and clearinghouses may need to run X12 6020 for claims attachments and FHIR/CDex for PA attachments simultaneously.
- **Specific request:** Extend February 28, 2024 National Standards Group enforcement discretion through CMS-0062-P transition; require all needed documentation in the initial CDex request; cap solicited attachment requests at two before a decision; and set payer response timeframes after receiving attachments.

**Topic 9: ONC standards harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify/clarify.
- **Rationale:** CMS-0062-P payer deadlines and ONC certification timelines may not align, creating a period when payer APIs exist but CHCs lack certified provider-facing modules.
- **Specific request:** Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance, publish a formal harmonization assessment before the final rule, and ensure HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P requirements.

**Topic 10: RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, payer APIs, step therapy, labs, and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify/expand.
- **Rationale:** CHCs need structured, workflow-integrated event notifications, cybersecurity shared services, API certification, step therapy continuity, and reduced PA barriers for labs and DMEPOS.
- **Specific request:** Include Section 330 CHCs as ADT notification recipients under §482.24(d), route notifications to organizational NPI Type 2 identifiers, align ADT with FHIR event notification models, fund cybersecurity shared services through HCCNs, require payer API certification and sandboxes, honor step therapy approvals for at least 12 months/treatment episode, and streamline lab/DMEPOS PA.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S2/S3/S18/S37:** Supports required IGs and standardized API use but asks CMS to coordinate versioning, timelines, vendor readiness, testing, and provider protections to avoid CHC disruption.
- **S12:** Identifies major technical challenges for CHCs, including vendor readiness, EHR/PMS integration, 340B workflows, benefit classification, and cybersecurity.
- **S14/S19/S22/S25:** Supports NCPDP-based pharmacy benefit ePA but requests realistic readiness support, OPA coordination, and evaluation of pharmacy system interoperability barriers.
- **S15:** Requests real-time benefit classification information and clarification about cross-benefit PA approvals when drugs can be covered under either medical or pharmacy benefits.
- **S16/S17:** Supports specific, actionable denial reasons including missing information and resubmission/appeal instructions.
- **S20/S43:** Supports 24-hour covered outpatient drug decisions and aligned decision timeframes, but requests automatic approval when payers miss deadlines.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports centralized machine-readable API endpoint publication, prompt updates after changes, annual verification, and easy access for safety-net providers and vendors.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports expanded API usage metrics and recommends additional functionality, data quality, workflow, and transaction success/failure measures with usable public trend reporting.
- **S44/S46/S47/S48/S49/S50/S53/S54/S55:** Supports FHIR-based HIPAA transition but asks for enforcement discretion, staged transition support, guardrails on CDex attachments, testing, and safeguards against provider burden/cost shifting.
- **S57/S58/S59:** Supports defining failure to report for Open Payments CMPs and asks CMS/HRSA to coordinate audit and compliance expectations with 340B operations.
- **S60/S61/S62/S63/S64/S65:** Supports standards harmonization but requests CMS-ONC alignment, a harmonization assessment, and protection from certification/timeline gaps.
- **S66-S82:** Provides RFI responses on ADT/event notifications, cybersecurity resiliency, payer API implementation, step therapy, laboratory tests, and DMEPOS.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify that providers are not penalized for vendor, clearinghouse, or EHR failures outside their control.
2. Coordinate CMS deadlines with ONC certification and require readiness validation/testing before enforcement.
3. Provide CHC-focused technical assistance, cybersecurity workforce support, and dedicated health IT funding.
4. Publish a consolidated provider-side RIA covering CMS-0057-F, CMS-0053-F, and CMS-0062-P.
5. Require real-time benefit classification for drugs through Provider Access APIs and clarify cross-benefit PA validity.
6. Coordinate with OPA to address 340B contract pharmacy, TPA, EHR, and pharmacy management system implications.
7. Require proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.
8. Pair ePA with PA reduction, prescription-drug gold-carding, and sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories.
9. Require automatic approval when PA requests are not decided within required timeframes.
10. Publish centralized, standardized, machine-readable API endpoint and PA metric data on CMS's website.
11. Expand public reporting to include service/medication categories, first-pass approval rates, provider touchpoints, overturned denials due to unclear notices, and ePA functionality/data quality metrics.
12. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
13. Extend enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute through the transition.
14. Establish CDex guardrails: initial complete documentation request, maximum two solicited attachment requests, and payer response deadlines.
15. Issue CMS-ONC joint implementation guidance and harmonization assessment before finalizing.
16. Amend ADT CoPs to include Section 330-funded CHCs as organizational recipients using NPI Type 2 routing.
17. Support CHC cybersecurity shared services through HCCNs.
18. Require payer API certification/validation with public conformance results and CHC-specific testing sandboxes.
19. Mandate step therapy continuity for at least 12 months or the treatment episode, with indefinite continuation for chronic disease medications unless the prescriber changes therapy.
20. Streamline DMEPOS PA and require payers to eliminate PA categories with high overturn rates.
21. Accept standardized, LOINC-aligned test requisition forms as sufficient lab PA documentation and support real-time pre-collection lab PA through CRD.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Unfunded safety-net implementation burden; vendor and payer readiness; cybersecurity exposure; medical/pharmacy benefit ambiguity; 340B workflow disruption; insufficiently granular reporting; misaligned CMS/ONC timelines; ePA without PA reform.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed medications, delayed specialty referrals, treatment abandonment, increased denials, financial strain, cybersecurity breaches, increased provider burden, wasted specimens, repeat testing, DMEPOS delays, and widened disparities for low-income, rural, homeless, agricultural worker, Medicaid, and uninsured patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC IT staffing, small budgets, EHR/PMS integration gaps, vendor dependency, lack of payer API certification, PBM/pharmacy complexity, overnight/batch workflows, cybersecurity costs, certification timeline gaps, multiple payer portals, 340B data fragmentation, and limited rural broadband/transportation access.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CHC national and Maine chronic disease counts; 94% of physicians report PA delays care; MA plans made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024 with over 4 million full/partial denials; CHCs serve over 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, 6 million uninsured people, 20.5 million low-income individuals, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and 1.5 million homeless individuals; only two-thirds of CHCs report a cybersecurity committee/leader; 78.9% report 1-10 health IT FTEs; proactive PA recertification outreach positive in 87% of cases vs 25%; 2024 Change Healthcare breach affected 77% of CHCs and delayed care for 62% of CHC patients; 73 FHIR implementation challenges identified in a scoping review; DMEPOS disputes are 47.5% of coverage appeals and 82% of MA PA appeals are at least partially successful.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA PA survey; KFF MA PA reporting; MGMA PA burden report; NACHC health IT and cybersecurity reports; peer-reviewed ePA studies by Lauffenburger and Salzbrenner; Aremu medication adherence study; pharmacy interoperability and EHR access literature; step therapy and Medicaid denial studies; ADT/postpartum navigation and discharge planning studies; FHIR implementation scoping review; HIE impact study.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine, long-acting injectable antipsychotics, vaccines, injectable contraceptives, specialty biologics, 340B contract pharmacy workflows, postpartum ADT handoffs, CHC cybersecurity impacts after Change Healthcare, wheelchair procurement delays, and lab specimen collection before PA finalization.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs/FQHCs, medically underserved patients, Medicaid and CHIP beneficiaries, uninsured and low-income patients, rural communities, agricultural workers, homeless individuals, CHC IT staff, EHR vendors, PBMs, pharmacies, TPAs, MCOs, QHPs, HRSA/OPA, HCCNs, HIEs, and CMS/ONC.
- **Economic implications:** MPCA expects ePA to reduce burden if implemented well but warns that cybersecurity, vendor upgrades, training, workflow redesign, API integration, 340B coordination, and unreimbursed PA staff time could impose substantial unfunded costs on CHCs with thin or negative margins.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible only with aligned certification, vendor readiness, payer API certification, testing sandboxes, machine-readable endpoint reporting, realistic timelines, and technical assistance.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong potential to improve medication access, care coordination, chronic disease management, maternal-child transitions, and laboratory/DME access; poor implementation could increase delays, denials, repeated testing, medication interruptions, and disparities.
