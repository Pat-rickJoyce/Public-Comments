# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

- **Author/Organization name:** Massachusetts Health & Hospital Association (MHA)

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This attachment appears to comment on CMS-4212-P, the Medicare Advantage CY2027 policy and technical rule, rather than directly on CMS-2026-1255-0001. Its most relevant content for this docket concerns prior authorization burden, denial and appeals accountability, prior authorization data reporting, network adequacy, and CMS oversight of Medicare Advantage plans.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; API-USAGE-METRICS by analogy to PA reporting; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy to denial accountability and overturn rates; not directly responsive to PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, or HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 C.F.R. 422.62(b)(23); 42 C.F.R. 422.116(b)(2); 90 Fed. Reg. 54965; CMS-4212-P.
- **Standards referenced:** None from the CMS-2026-1255-0001 interoperability standards map.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Medicare Advantage Prior Authorization Burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA reports excessive and inconsistent MA prior authorization, long turnaround times for post-acute discharges, inappropriate denials, non-transparent coverage criteria, and discharge backlogs.
- **Specific request:** Strengthen federal MA oversight and align response times to reduce delayed transitions and care coordination barriers.

**Topic 2: Prior Authorization Data Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Oppose elimination / Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA says robust data collection is necessary to hold plans accountable for denial rates, overturn rates, inappropriate coverage criteria, and average PA adjudication time.
- **Specific request:** Do not eliminate existing PA reporting mechanisms until CMS specifies alternative ways to collect equivalent accountability data.

**Topic 3: Appeals and Complaints Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA argues appeals and complaints measures are beneficiary protection tools, not merely administrative indicators, because delayed appeals and unmanaged complaints affect timely medically necessary care.
- **Specific request:** Retain appeals and complaints measures in MA Star Ratings.

**Topic 4: Network Adequacy and Continuity of Care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA states inadequate PAC networks and provider terminations can delay discharge, prolong hospital stays, increase readmission risk, and disrupt continuity of care.
- **Specific request:** Expand affected enrollee SEP protections and add IRFs, LTCHs, and HHAs to network adequacy evaluation.

**Topic 5: CMS Oversight of MA Coverage Practices**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** MHA warns that MA plans may reclassify coverage determinations as payment disputes to avoid CMS oversight.
- **Specific request:** Prevent MA plans from using payment-dispute characterization to evade federal coverage and beneficiary protection rules.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S29:** Although not directly addressing API usage metrics, the comment strongly supports robust PA metric collection, including denial rates, overturn rates, and adjudication time.
- **S34:** The comment supports ongoing PA data collection and reporting mechanisms sufficient for plan accountability.
- **S35:** The comment implies that reported PA data should support oversight and accountability, especially where plan practices delay care or create inappropriate denials.
- **S66-S82:** The comment is generally responsive to broader prior authorization and administrative-burden RFIs, especially around post-acute care, appeals, denials, and oversight.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Retain or replace prior authorization data reporting requirements with equally robust alternative data collection.
2. Collect denial rates, overturn rates, average authorization decision times, and information on inappropriate internal coverage criteria.
3. Retain appeals and complaints measures in MA Star Ratings.
4. Expand SEP affected-enrollee protections to beneficiaries with scheduled care at the time of termination notice.
5. Add IRFs, LTCHs, and HHAs to MA network adequacy facility-type standards.
6. Prevent MA plans from reclassifying coverage issues as payment disputes to avoid CMS oversight.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** MA prior authorization delays, inappropriate denials, weak appeals accountability, inadequate PAC networks, elimination of PA reporting, oversight evasion.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Discharge backlogs, prolonged hospital stays, avoidable decline, readmissions, clinician burden, higher system costs, reduced beneficiary protection.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Inconsistent plan practices, restrictive criteria, inadequate networks, administrative requirements, plan policies that shift disputes away from CMS oversight.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CHIA figure that more than 33% of Massachusetts Medicare beneficiaries were enrolled in MA plans as of March 2025; MHA monthly Throughput Report finding upwards of 2,000 patients waiting for discharge at a given time.

- **Examples provided:** Delayed PAC discharge authorizations; plan removal of peer-to-peer discussions; automated E/M downcoding programs; reclassifying inpatient coverage disputes as payment disputes.

Attention: CMS-4212-P

Re: CMS-4212-P, Medicare Program Contract Year 2027 Policy and Technical Changes to the Medicare Advantage Program, Medicare Prescription Drug Benefit Program, and Medicare Cost Program

The Massachusetts Health & Hospital Association (MHA) appreciates the opportunity to submit these comments on behalf of our member hospitals and health systems. As our population ages, the number of Massachusetts residents receiving primary medical insurance from Medicare is increasing, with a significant portion of that growth occurring in Medicare Advantage (MA) enrollment. According to the Massachusetts Center for Health Information Analysis (CHIA) as of March 2025, more than 33% of all Medicare beneficiaries in Massachusetts were enrolled in MA plans.

CMS proposed a number of key changes and updates in its proposed CY2027 rule that affect MA plans, as well as the providers that contract with these plans and the patients they serve. Many of these changes are helpful to both members and providers; however, others have the potential to add to the present challenges many of our hospitals continue to encounter with certain MA plans. This includes excessive and inconsistent prior authorization requirements, disproportionately long turn-around-times for prior authorization for post-acute care discharges, inappropriate denials for medically necessary care covered under Original Medicare, restrictive and non-transparent coverage criteria, and inadequate provider networks...
