## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to adopt patient-centered prior authorization protections for diagnostic testing, especially cancer and other time-sensitive care. The comment requests safeguards against timing-based denials, repeat specimen collection, delayed expedited review, and surprise financial liability caused by payer paperwork delays.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to expedited review timing; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by implication where denials arise from authorization timing rather than clinical necessity
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Timing-based denials for diagnostic tests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not lose coverage for critical testing solely because prior authorization was still pending when a specimen was collected.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit insurers from denying critical tests based only on authorization timing when delay is outside the control of patients, clinicians, or laboratories.

**Topic 2: Expedited review for time-sensitive care**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology and other urgent diagnostic situations require faster administrative review to avoid treatment delays.
- **Specific request:** Establish mandatory expedited review pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic tests.

**Topic 3: Patient and provider financial protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients, physicians, and labs should not face surprise bills or financial penalties because of insurance paperwork delays.
- **Specific request:** Ban financial penalties tied to prior authorization processing delays beyond stakeholder control.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

The comment does not expressly respond to any numbered solicitation. It is most relevant to general RFI topics S66-S82 concerning prior authorization burden, resiliency, and patient access, and indirectly to S20/S43 on expedited timeframes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prevent denials of critical tests solely because authorization remained pending when blood was drawn.
2. Protect patients from repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is already available.
3. Require expedited review pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic tests.
4. Prohibit surprise bills or financial penalties caused by payer paperwork delays.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Timing-based denials, redundant procedures, delayed cancer diagnostics, and surprise financial liability.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Painful repeat blood draws, delayed cancer care, and unfair costs for patients, doctors, and labs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer paperwork delays and authorization processes that do not align with clinical timing.
