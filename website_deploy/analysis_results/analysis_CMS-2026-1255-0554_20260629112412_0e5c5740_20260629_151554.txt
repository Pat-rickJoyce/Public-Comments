# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Physicians of Southwest Washington (PSW) and MultiCare Connected Care (MCC).
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; clinically integrated/value-based networks.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** CINs in Washington State serving over 400,000 individuals across independent practices, health systems, and rural health systems.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PSW and MCC support electronic prior authorization for drugs but warn that the rule creates near-term operational, technical, and financial risk for delegated, value-based, and health plan-aligned entities. They request phased implementation, standardized payer behavior and data elements, safeguards for shortened decision timeframes, authoritative endpoint reporting, and accountability frameworks that align responsibility with operational control.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR Prior Authorization APIs; NCPDP SCRIPT; formulary and benefit standards; real-time prescription benefit; legacy HIPAA X12 transactions; standards development organizations and FHIR accelerators.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization in delegated models**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA can reduce delays and rework, but CINs and delegated entities may be accountable for authorization outcomes without control over payer APIs, benefit logic, data quality, or timelines.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly recognize delegated entities and align accountability with operational control over API reliability, data quality, and transaction performance.

**Topic 2: Concurrent standards and workflow integration**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Moving from legacy X12 to FHIR-enabled workflows while also supporting NCPDP standards creates integration, monitoring, and exception-handling burdens.
- **Specific request:** Require end-to-end integrated workflows across payers, delegated entities, pharmacies, PBMs, prescribers, and care teams.

**Topic 3: Standardization across lines of business**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Extending NCPDP capabilities beyond Part D is useful, but inconsistent data requirements and response behaviors will perpetuate multi-payer variation.
- **Specific request:** Require consistent minimum data elements, response behavior, and workflow expectations across Medicaid, CHIP, QHPs, and other covered lines.

**Topic 4: Medical versus pharmacy benefit ambiguity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Infused drugs, specialty therapies, and high-cost medications may be adjudicated differently depending on benefit design, creating misclassification and denial risk.
- **Specific request:** Specify minimum data standards and semantics for consistent benefit-pathway determinations and computable policy/documentation requirements.

**Topic 5: Decision timeframe safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Shorter timelines can improve access, but without safeguards may result in premature denials or avoidable additional information requests.
- **Specific request:** Define completed request, allow clinically appropriate extensions, require specific actionable denial rationales, and account for dependencies outside delegated entities' control.

**Topic 6: Implementation timeline and reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Current timelines are aggressive given multi-standard support and payer-readiness variation.
- **Specific request:** Adopt phased implementation and safe-harbor periods; publish an authoritative endpoint directory; require reporting on availability, latency, and error rates.

**Topic 7: Risk-bearing arrangements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** In value-based and risk-bearing arrangements, utilization management accountability may already exist and duplicative PA can add friction.
- **Specific request:** Allow reduced or alternative PA requirements in risk-bearing models while maintaining patient protections and transparency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Supports FHIR-based PA for medical benefit drugs but identifies operational challenges for delegated entities and benefit pathway ambiguity.
- **S14:** Supports expansion of NCPDP SCRIPT/formulary/RTPB capabilities beyond Part D, with consistent minimum requirements across programs.
- **S15:** Responds directly by warning that ambiguity between medical and pharmacy benefit pathways is a major operational risk.
- **S17:** Requests specific, actionable, authorization-traceable denial rationales.
- **S20/S43:** Supports shorter timeframes but asks CMS to define completed requests and permit clinically appropriate extensions.
- **S21:** Raises concern that proposed implementation timelines are aggressive and recommends phased implementation and safe harbors.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports endpoint reporting and asks CMS to publish an authoritative endpoint directory with operational performance details.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Supports usage metrics and urges reporting on availability, latency, and error rates.
- **S44-S47:** Addresses transition from X12 to FHIR-enabled workflows and supports a real-world phased transition.
- **S53/S54:** Supports phased implementation, safe harbors, and operational testing/readiness expectations.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Recognize downstream burden on delegated entities, CINs, IPAs, and risk-bearing organizations.
2. Align accountability with operational control over payer APIs and data quality.
3. Require end-to-end integrated prior authorization workflows.
4. Standardize data elements, semantics, response behavior, and workflow expectations across payers and lines of business.
5. Clarify medical versus pharmacy benefit determination logic for specialty and infused drugs.
6. Pair shortened decision timelines with safeguards, completed-request definitions, extensions, and actionable denial rationales.
7. Adopt phased implementation and safe-harbor periods.
8. Publish an authoritative payer endpoint directory.
9. Require operational API metrics including availability, latency, and error rates.
10. Permit evolution or reduction of PA requirements in risk-bearing arrangements.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Misalignment between regulatory accountability and operational control; multi-payer variation; implementation complexity; benefit-pathway ambiguity; aggressive timelines.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Increased denials, rework, training burden, administrative burden, misclassification of drugs, patient delays, and financial risk for delegated organizations.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer API variation, data-quality problems, benefit determination logic outside delegated control, concurrent FHIR/NCPDP/X12 support, and differing payer workflows.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Serves over 400,000 individuals across the Pacific Northwest.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** Infused drugs, specialty therapies, and high-cost medications may be adjudicated under different benefit pathways depending on plan design.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CINs, delegated entities, IPAs, risk-bearing organizations, providers, care teams, patients, payers, pharmacies, and PBMs.
- **Economic implications:** Requires investment in integration, monitoring, exception handling, training, and operational support.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if phased and standardized; risky if payer variability and API performance are unmanaged.
- **Patient care impact:** Well-implemented ePA may speed access to high-cost therapies; poorly implemented requirements could increase denials and delays.
