# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Disability Rights Education and Defense Fund (DREDF); signed by Silvia Yee, Policy Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National cross-disability law and policy center with more than 40 years of advocacy, training, education, legislation, and public policy work advancing the civil and human rights of people with disabilities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DREDF responds to the rule's RFI on prior authorization for DMEPOS items, focusing on medically necessary wheelchairs and mobility devices. It supports eliminating unnecessary PA delays and applying prior authorization reporting standards to DMEPOS suppliers, while emphasizing that deeper industry problems around repair, maintenance, market consolidation, poor service, and lack of transparency must also be addressed.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI; API-USAGE-METRICS and API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING by analogy to PA reporting and transparency standards; patient access issues related to DMEPOS prior authorization.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; Federal Register paragraph 110 defining providers and suppliers to include suppliers of DMEPOS items.
- **Standards referenced:** No FHIR IG, NCPDP, HL7, or HIPAA transaction standard is substantively discussed. The comment focuses on DMEPOS prior authorization and reporting accountability.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior authorization delays for wheelchair replacement, repair, and maintenance**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Wheelchairs and other mobility devices are medically necessary, foundational supports for independent living, breathing, sitting, and activities of daily living. Delays in PA for replacement, repair, and maintenance leave wheelchair users without critical equipment.
- **Specific request:** CMS should eliminate unnecessary delays in PA requirements for wheelchair replacement, maintenance, and repairs across Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurance.

**Topic 2: DMEPOS supplier accountability and reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** DREDF welcomes CMS's statement that providers and suppliers include DMEPOS suppliers and argues the wheelchair supply industry needs greater transparency and accountability.
- **Specific request:** Apply prior authorization reporting standards and requirements fully to DMEPOS suppliers and contractors.

**Topic 3: Repair-part stocking and stacked delays**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Wheelchair providers often do not stock even basic replacement parts, ordering them only after a repair request and potentially after PA approval, compounding delays.
- **Specific request:** CMS should recognize and address routine repair and maintenance delays, including supplier practices that unnecessarily delay access to basic parts.

**Topic 4: Market consolidation and service quality in complex rehabilitation technology**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** DREDF argues that private-equity-backed consolidation, Medicare competitive bidding, low competition, high staff turnover, and poor service have contributed to long repair delays, technical errors, and unsafe outcomes for wheelchair users.
- **Specific request:** CMS should address DME industry accountability beyond PA process reform, including supplier responsibility for foreseeable maintenance and repair needs.

**Topic 5: Right to repair as complementary policy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** State and proposed federal right-to-repair laws could help users and independent technicians access parts, tools, and software needed for powered wheelchair repairs.
- **Specific request:** CMS should view right-to-repair policies and reasonable supplier timelines for parts as complementary to PA reform.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82 (General/RFI - DMEPOS prior authorization):** DREDF responds to the DMEPOS RFI by supporting prior authorization reforms for wheelchair replacement, repair, and maintenance, while warning that PA streamlining alone will not fix supplier nonperformance, market consolidation, and poor repair infrastructure.
- **S29-S35 (Metrics/public reporting, by implication):** The commenter supports applying reporting standards and accountability requirements to DMEPOS suppliers and contractors so that delays and supplier performance problems become visible.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Eliminate unnecessary prior authorization delays for medically necessary wheelchair replacement, repair, and maintenance.
2. Apply prior authorization reporting standards and requirements fully to DMEPOS suppliers and contractors.
3. Increase transparency and accountability for wheelchair suppliers whose practices delay repairs and replacements.
4. Address supplier failure to stock routine repair parts and the resulting stacked delays.
5. Recognize that right-to-repair laws and supplier obligations to keep or obtain parts on reasonable timelines complement PA reform.
6. Examine how market consolidation, private equity ownership, and competitive bidding have affected service quality and patient safety.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Wheelchair users experience delays, frustration, lack of accountability, lack of transparency, poor customer service, limited technical expertise, repair-part shortages, and unsafe equipment outcomes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Loss of mobility and independence, inability to work or meet family obligations, prolonged periods without medically necessary equipment, physical injury, unsafe repairs, skin-integrity risks, and in one cited case death after allegedly improper service.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Supplier refusal to stock basic parts, PA stacking delays, insurer requirements, supply-chain shortages, staffing shortages, reimbursement pressures, industry consolidation, reduced local competition, and insufficient supplier accountability.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** DREDF's 2025 wheelchair user report; two private-equity-backed companies reportedly servicing over 50% of the market; DME market valued at almost $60 billion in 2022 with projected 5.7% compound annual growth from 2023 to 2030; Medicare competitive bidding estimated to reduce federal costs by 35%; Numotion subject to at least 30 personal injury lawsuits across 18 states since 2015.
- **Studies or research referenced:** DREDF report, "Expensive, Frustrating, Demoralizing: Wheelchair Users' Device Purchasing Experiences"; National Disability Rights Network and Private Equity Stakeholder Project report on private equity in DME; reporting on Numotion and National Seating & Mobility; Trustpilot, Reddit, and Better Business Bureau consumer complaints.
- **Examples provided:** A DREDF colleague waited more than six weeks for a power-chair tire and eight weeks for batteries; examples of wrong parts, improper fitting, billing errors, and unsafe repairs; state right-to-repair laws in Colorado, California, Washington, and Nevada; proposed federal Wheelchair Right to Repair Act.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Wheelchair users, people with complex rehabilitation needs, people with disabilities, DMEPOS suppliers, complex rehabilitation technology providers, Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries, private insurance enrollees, caregivers, independent repair technicians, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** The comment links supplier consolidation and private equity incentives to reduced competition, poor service, and reluctance to keep capital tied up in repair inventory. It also notes reimbursement and competitive bidding pressures but rejects them as excuses for failing patient users.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment does not address FHIR or NCPDP feasibility. It frames DMEPOS PA reform as feasible but incomplete unless repair logistics, parts access, and supplier accountability are addressed.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays in wheelchair replacement and repair directly threaten mobility, independence, work, family responsibilities, health, bodily integrity, and safety.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive of applying PA reform/reporting to DMEPOS, but critical of current supplier practices and industry structure.
- **Tone:** Professional, advocacy-focused, critical, and evidence-based.
- **Priority level:** High.
