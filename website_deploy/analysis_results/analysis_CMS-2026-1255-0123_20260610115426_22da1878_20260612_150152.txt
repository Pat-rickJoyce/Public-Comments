**1. COMMENTER INFORMATION**

- **Author/Organization name:** EVRESA LLC; John Gray Rodriguez, Founder & Chief Executive Officer
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Florida-based architectural infrastructure company developing governance proof infrastructure for AI-influenced healthcare decisions, including deterministic enforcement of human authority, cryptographic attestation of decision provenance, and tamper-evident audit infrastructure.

**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

EVRESA supports CMS-0062-P's use of NCPDP and FHIR-based standards, compressed drug prior authorization timeframes, specific denial reason requirements, and HIPAA Administrative Simplification changes. Its central recommendation is that CMS recognize, in preamble or sub-regulatory guidance, a complementary governance proof infrastructure layer for automated or AI-assisted prior authorization workflows, focused on decision provenance, human authority attestation, auditability, and beneficiary protection.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI topics including resiliency and event notifications.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR Parts 162, 170, and 156; 45 CFR 170.205(b), (c), and (u); 45 CFR 170.215; 42 CFR Parts 431, 438, and 457; 42 CFR 422.122(a); 42 CFR 431.80(a); 45 CFR 156.223(a); 42 CFR 438.242; 42 CFR 457.1233; 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11); 15 U.S.C. 272(c).
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, RTPB; HL7 FHIR Da Vinci CRD v2.2.1, DTR v2.2.0, PAS v2.2.1; NIST AI Risk Management Framework; ONC HTI-1 DSI requirements; FDA SaMD/IMDRF framework.

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

**Topic 1: NCPDP Standards for Pharmacy Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** EVRESA says NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB are well established in pharmacy claims processing and will bring consistency across Medicaid FFS, Medicaid managed care, CHIP, and FFE QHP drug PA workflows.
- **Specific request:** Finalize adoption of the NCPDP standards with the proposed October 1, 2027 compliance date.

**Topic 2: FHIR Da Vinci Implementation Guides**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** EVRESA views mandatory Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS as necessary to align the ecosystem around standardized electronic PA for medical-benefit drugs.
- **Specific request:** Elevate the Da Vinci IGs from recommended to required standards under 45 CFR 170.215.

**Topic 3: Drug PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with governance caution
- **Rationale:** EVRESA supports compressed timeframes as beneficial for access, but argues they will increase reliance on automation, rules-based routing, and AI-enabled tools.
- **Specific request:** Finalize timeframes while recognizing governance proof infrastructure as a relevant implementation consideration.

**Topic 4: Specific Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons are foundational to beneficiary appeal rights and provider ability to correct documentation deficiencies.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the requirement and consider linking denial reasons to evidence, policy, reviewer identity, timing, and audit records.

**Topic 5: HIPAA Administrative Simplification for PA Standards**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** EVRESA says adopting FHIR-based prior authorization standards under HIPAA would create uniformity across covered entities and align privacy/security with interoperability.
- **Specific request:** Finalize HIPAA PA standards and recognize governance proof as a complementary layer above the transaction standard.

**Topic 6: Governance Proof Infrastructure for AI-Influenced PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** The standardized API record may show what was decided, but not necessarily who decided, the reasoning, human authority, or whether an adverse determination reflected algorithmic inference.
- **Specific request:** Acknowledge governance proof infrastructure in final rule preamble, FAQs, or implementation guidance, without mandating a particular vendor or architecture.

**Topic 7: Resiliency and Tamper-Evident Auditability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** EVRESA cites the February 2024 Change Healthcare cybersecurity event as evidence that concentrated transaction infrastructure needs integrity and resiliency mechanisms.
- **Specific request:** Encourage cryptographic attestation, tamper-evident audit chains, fail-closed behavior, and shadow-path detection where automated workflows are used.

**5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES**

- **S1:** Supports making relevant FHIR IGs required rather than recommended, especially Da Vinci CRD, DTR, and PAS.
- **S18:** Acknowledges mandatory IGs advance standardization, while arguing that implementation should also account for governance and auditability where automation is used.
- **S20:** Supports 24-hour drug PA timeframes for Medicaid/CHIP but notes compressed timeframes may increase reliance on automation and thus increase need for governance proof.
- **S21:** Treats the October 1, 2027 date as reasonable for drug PA implementation while recommending sub-regulatory guidance during the implementation period.
- **S25:** Supports RTPB as part of the pharmacy benefit drug standards framework.
- **S29/S35:** Discusses integrity of publicly reported PA metrics and recommends governance proof infrastructure to support substantive operational performance rather than reporting artifacts.
- **S44:** Supports FHIR-based PA standards as appropriate for HIPAA Administrative Simplification.
- **S45:** Supports broad HIPAA covered entity applicability, framing uniformity as beneficial.
- **S46:** Implied response that governance proof should operate above transaction standards and not replace existing transaction infrastructure.
- **S47:** Notes HIPAA compliance timing should allow implementation and guidance development.
- **S48/S49:** Says its recommendation imposes no direct compliance cost because it requests recognition, not a mandate; adoption should remain discretionary.
- **S50/S54/S55:** Recommends coordination with NIST, ONC, and FDA-related frameworks on governance, testing, certification, transparency, and oversight for AI-influenced workflows.
- **S52:** Frames the request as within CMS's interoperability and beneficiary protection objectives and not as broad AI regulation.
- **S66-S82:** Responds generally to RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, and payer API implementation through companion filings and by emphasizing communication provenance, resiliency, and auditability.

**6. RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. Recognize governance proof infrastructure, including decision provenance, human authority attestation, and communication provenance, in preamble language, FAQs, or implementation guidance.
2. Coordinate with NIST to align CMS-0062-P implementation with AI Risk Management Framework principles.
3. Treat beneficiary AI protection as a distinct regulatory consideration in automated prior authorization environments.
4. Engage state Medicaid agencies on governance proof infrastructure patterns that complement standardized PA transactions.
5. Encourage documentation of decision provenance, evidence reviewed, timing, escalation, and human authority when automated or AI-enabled tools support PA workflows.
6. Preserve flexibility by recognizing an architectural pattern without mandating a specific vendor, product, or proprietary implementation.

**7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS**

- **Primary concerns:** Faster standardized transactions may not prove decision authority, reasoning, human review, or integrity when automated tools are involved.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Pharmacist licensure exposure, payer compliance defensibility risk, insufficient beneficiary protection, cybersecurity and auditability gaps, and pharmacist-physician coordination burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of governance infrastructure, multi-stakeholder workflow complexity, state implementation variation, AI-enabled decision support adoption, and need for interagency coordination.

**8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE**

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative original data; relies on regulatory and implementation experience.
- **Studies or research referenced:** NIST AI RMF v1.0; ONC HTI-1 Final Rule; FDA SaMD and IMDRF frameworks; OMB Memorandum M-25-21; CMS-0057-F implementation experience.
- **Examples provided:** Pharmacist professional accountability, payer appeal defense, beneficiary protection in adverse determinations, 340B documentation integrity, Change Healthcare cybersecurity event, GLP-1 PA automation pressures, and public PA metric reporting.

**9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT**

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, providers, pharmacies, pharmacists, PBMs, state Medicaid agencies, beneficiaries, technology vendors, ONC/NIST/FDA/CMS, and entities using AI-enabled PA tools.
- **Economic implications:** EVRESA argues its recommendation creates no direct compliance cost because it seeks recognition rather than a mandate; voluntary adoption could reduce audit, appeal, and documentation reconstruction costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Presents governance proof as an overlay above pharmacy management systems, FHIR APIs, NCPDP standards, PBM infrastructure, and clearinghouses, using cryptographic attestation and tamper-evident audit chains.
- **Patient care impact:** Potentially improves beneficiary protection by ensuring adverse PA decisions remain tied to human authority, evidence, uncertainty escalation, and auditable records.

Executive Summary Prior authorization is the decision boundary where a payer approves, delays, or denies access to a covered drug or service before care is delivered. As electronic prior authorization becomes faster and more automated through CMS-0062-P, the policy question is not only whether the transaction is transmitted correctly, but whether the decision itself remains human-authorized, evidence-linked, policy-bounded, and auditable.

CMS-0062-P modernizes the transaction rails for prior authorization through standardized electronic standards (NCPDP, FHIR, Da Vinci). EVRESA LLC supports this direction...
