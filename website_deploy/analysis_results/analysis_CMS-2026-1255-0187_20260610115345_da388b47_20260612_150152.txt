## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter proposes Episode Intelligence Authorization(TM), a national prior authorization model using one upfront authorization for an entire episode of care, starting with behavioral health. The model combines FHIR-native criteria, public society guidelines, governed AI with licensed clinician sign-off, and a phased CMMI demonstration intended to reduce PA events, appeals, and avoidable behavioral health hospitalizations.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0057-F; no CFR citations specified.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR; FHIR PA API; FHIR PlanDefinition; CQL; APA, SAMHSA, ASAM, WOCN, AHRQ, and ASCO guidelines; Maryland HB 1563; Washington SB 5395.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Single episode authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Procedure-by-procedure PA fragments care, creates multiple delay events, and is clinically unsuitable for behavioral health patients in crisis.
- **Specific request:** Replace separate authorizations for evaluation, medication, inpatient, PHP, IOP, outpatient care, and LAI administration with one day-zero authorization covering a 30-, 60-, or 90-day episode.

**Topic 2: Governed AI risk stratification**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer AI can generate adverse decisions at scale without transparent logic, but AI may be useful if explainable, auditable, and governed by licensed clinicians.
- **Specific request:** Require AI risk stratification to use validated instruments, maintain a full FHIR decision trail, comply with state AI PA reporting rules, and require licensed PMHNP or physician sign-off.

**Topic 3: Society guideline engine and FHIR-native criteria**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Proprietary criteria such as InterQual and MCG create opacity, licensing costs, and unauditable denial logic.
- **Specific request:** Mandate FHIR criteria using APA/SAMHSA/ASAM for behavioral health, with criteria expressed as FHIR PlanDefinitions and CQL, and exclude proprietary black-box criteria from the pilot.

**Topic 4: Phased CMMI demonstration and national standard**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Behavioral health is urgent because CMS does not target BH PA in MA audits, PA-related harms are documented, and federal MHPAEA enforcement is described as uncertain.
- **Specific request:** Authorize a CMMI behavioral health episode authorization demonstration in 2026-2028, evaluate outcomes, and expand to wound care and oncology in 2029-2030 through potential rulemaking.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16-S17:** Calls for denial logic to be transparent, auditable, and tied to published clinical guidelines.
- **S20:** Requests less than 24-hour episode authorization turnaround at intake for behavioral health episodes.
- **S44-S46:** Supports FHIR-native prior authorization but argues the standard should include clinical criteria and decision trails, not only data exchange.
- **S48-S50:** Discusses cost and maturity benefits of eliminating proprietary licensing and using FHIR PlanDefinition/CQL tied to society guidelines.
- **S53:** Proposes phased implementation: 2026 CMMI authorization and validation, 2027 live pilot, 2028 evaluation, and 2029-2030 national expansion.
- **S54-S55:** Recommends AI governance, licensed clinician sign-off, auditable FHIR trails, and regulatory reporting for adverse decisions.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to general PA reform RFIs with a broad episode-based authorization model across behavioral health, wound care, oncology, and other episodes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Launch a CMMI demonstration for Episode Intelligence Authorization(TM) in behavioral health.
2. Use one day-zero authorization to cover a 90-day behavioral health episode.
3. Select a defined cohort of Medicare Advantage plans for the pilot.
4. Mandate FHIR criteria using APA, SAMHSA, and ASAM guidelines.
5. Require governed AI with licensed clinician sign-off.
6. Maintain auditable FHIR decision trails.
7. Evaluate denial rates, appeals, hospitalizations, and administrative events against controls.
8. Expand the model to wound care and oncology in Phase 2 and consider national rulemaking.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Proprietary criteria are opaque; procedure-by-procedure PA causes repeated delay events; payer AI may be unaccountable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Behavioral health relapse and harm, preventable hospitalization, delayed care, unauditable denials, and patient crisis escalation.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Need for CMS authorization, plan selection, FHIR criteria validation, AI governance, clinician sign-off workflows, and outcome measurement.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 52.8 million MA PA determinations in 2024; 80.7% of appealed denials overturned; 73% of Americans say PA delays are a major problem; proposed targets of at least 50% reduction in PA administrative events, less than 24-hour episode authorization turnaround, and at least 30% reduction in avoidable BH hospitalizations.
- **Studies or research referenced:** GAO-25-107342; Murphy et al. 2026 American Journal of Medicine systematic review; KFF 2026 MA PA analysis; KFF Health Tracking Poll; Commonwealth Fund 2026; Maryland HB 1563; Washington SB 5395.
- **Examples provided:** Behavioral health 90-day episode, wound care 60-day episode, oncology NSCLC biomarker pathway, PHQ-9/GAF/ASAM risk instruments.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Behavioral health patients, MA plans, Medicare and Medicaid programs, clinicians, CMS/CMMI, payers, regulators, vendors, and specialty societies.
- **Economic implications:** Potential reduction in administrative events and proprietary criteria licensing costs; possible savings from fewer avoidable hospitalizations.
- **Technical feasibility:** The model depends on FHIR-native criteria modules, CQL, validated risk instruments, AI audit trails, and clinician governance workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Potentially high positive impact if episode authorization reduces repeated delays for behavioral health patients in crisis.
