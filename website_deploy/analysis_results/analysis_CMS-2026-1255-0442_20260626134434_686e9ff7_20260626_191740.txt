- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Former EDI programmer implementing HIPAA 837, 835, 270/271 and related transactions; co-author of ACA Administrative Simplification provisions including the 835 remittance mandate; creator/architect of the CAQH Index; former EVP at Emdeon/Change Healthcare; former President and Chief Strategy Officer at OptimizeRx; founder of RxUtility, which embeds real-time drug pricing into clinical and consumer workflows.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

RxUtility supports CMS-0062-P, including electronic prior authorization for drugs, NCPDP RTPB, FHIR replacing X12 278 for prior authorization, denial reason reporting, and the October 1, 2027 compliance date. The commenter argues that the final rule must focus on enforceable patient outcomes rather than nominal technical compliance, and asks CMS to add functional performance standards, daily and usable formulary data, consumer-facing RTPBC with all price points, public payer-level outcome metrics, third-party access rights, and stronger enforcement.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR Part 162; Section 6062 of the SUPPORT Act; ACA Section 1104; CMS-9915-F; CMS-9115-F; CMS-0057-F; CMS-0062-P. No specific CFR section from the proposed rule text is cited beyond HIPAA Part 162 context.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP RTPB; consumer RTPBC; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit (F&B); CARIN Consumer RTPBC FHIR IG; FHIR; X12 278; HIPAA 837, 835, 270/271, 276/277; RxNorm; Da Vinci DTR; Inferno testing; machine-readable files under Transparency in Coverage.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Keep Core Rule Requirements and Compliance Date**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** RxUtility says ePA for drugs is overdue, NCPDP RTPB creates the data rail for transparency, FHIR replacing X12 278 is structurally significant, mandatory denial reasons create an audit trail, and the standards are mature enough for October 1, 2027.
- **Specific request:** Hold the October 1, 2027 compliance date, retain 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard timeframes, retain FHIR replacement of X12 278, and retain mandatory denial reasons with public reporting.

**Topic 2: Mandate Functional Outcomes, Not Only Formats**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues Inferno conformance testing can verify endpoint existence and standards conformance but not whether the endpoint works in real prescribing workflows.
- **Specific request:** Add functional standards for RTPB response completeness, latency under two seconds at the 95th percentile, scenario testing across specialty drugs/step therapy/chronic disease, and third-party access for HIPAA-compliant patient-authorized apps without commercial gatekeeping.

**Topic 3: Fix Machine-Readable Formulary and Benefit Data**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; TRANSPARENCY-related GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** RxUtility says formulary files are too stale, inconsistently coded, and operationally inaccessible, repeating problems seen in Transparency in Coverage machine-readable files.
- **Specific request:** Require daily refresh for changes affecting cost-share or PA status, standardized NCPDP restriction vocabulary without proprietary extensions, RxNorm-coded therapeutic alternatives for every PA-required or non-preferred drug, and open third-party access on the same terms as payer portals.

**Topic 4: Add Consumer RTPBC Through the Patient Access API**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers would get real-time benefit information under the proposed rule but patients would not, despite existing technical capability and the CARIN consumer RTPBC standard. The commenter gives a personal example of being unable to find prescription copays through her health plan and finding lower cash prices only outside the plan workflow.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to expose consumer-facing RTPBC via the Patient Access API on the October 2027 timeline, including patient-specific cost at preferred and non-preferred pharmacies, formulary tier, PA status, lower-cost alternatives, coupon availability, manufacturer direct price, and cash price.

**Topic 5: Enforcement and Public Outcome Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** RxUtility argues prior interoperability and transparency mandates have produced nominal compliance without enforcement or patient benefit. It cites the lack of TiC drug MRF enforcement and says visibility itself can be an enforcement mechanism.
- **Specific request:** Issue a compliance status report on payer MRF adherence; publish annual payer-level metrics for PA decision timeframe compliance, RTPB response completeness, therapeutic alternative return rate, structured denial reason rate, first-fill abandonment, and TiC MRF accuracy.

**Topic 6: Direct Third-Party Access and Intermediary Disclosure**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING-related GENERAL; PRIVACY-SECURITY-related GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter predicts payers may build compliant endpoints but restrict access through rate limits, approval queues, commercial agreements, preferred partners, or conflicted intermediaries.
- **Specific request:** Require payer endpoints to be available to any HIPAA-compliant application with patient authorization without prior commercial agreement; allow security-based rate limits but prohibit commercial gatekeeping; require disclosure of intermediary infrastructure used for RTPB compliance.

**Topic 7: Medical vs Pharmacy Benefit Ambiguity for Part B Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT-related GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The commenter explains that many high-cost drugs are covered under Medicare Part B when administered in clinical settings, while some biologics can fall under Part B or Part D depending on route of administration. Prescribers may not know which pathway applies at the point of prescribing.
- **Specific request:** Name the Da Vinci DTR framework explicitly in the final rule as a parallel requirement for medical benefit prior authorization on the same October 2027 timeline.

**Topic 8: Future Rulemaking on Cash Prices and Deductibles**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that patients who pay lower cash prices should not lose deductible or out-of-pocket maximum credit, because current rules steer patients toward higher-cost insured channels.
- **Specific request:** CMS and HHS should address in future rulemaking that cash prices paid out of pocket count toward deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Supports mandatory denial reasons and argues they should function as part of a public audit trail.
- **S17:** Recommends structured FHIR coded denial reasons rather than free text and proposes measuring structured denial reason rates.

- **S34:** Recommends annual public reporting by impacted payer.

- **S48:** Provides a cost-benefit argument centered on patient harm, prescription abandonment, avoidable hospitalizations, and lack of useful transparency.
- **S50:** Argues RTPB, consumer RTPBC, and related standards are ready; the remaining barrier is policy and enforcement.
- **S54:** Recommends functional certification and scenario testing beyond Inferno endpoint conformance, including reference round-trip testing.
- **S55:** Calls for enforcement mechanisms with actual consequences, MRF compliance review, outcome metric publication, and treatment of metric non-reporting as a violation.
- **S66-S82:** Addresses general RFIs through comments on step therapy/non-medical switching, consumer drug price access, and broader patient-facing interoperability outcomes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Hold the October 1, 2027 compliance date.
2. Retain 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard prior authorization decision timeframes for drugs.
3. Require functional performance standards for RTPB, including response completeness, latency, scenario testing, and direct third-party access.
4. Require RTPB responses to include patient-specific cost, formulary tier, PA flag, and at least one lower-cost therapeutic alternative or a structured coded reason for missing data.
5. Require daily updates to formulary data when cost-share or PA status changes.
6. Standardize coverage restriction vocabulary using a constrained NCPDP value set without proprietary extensions.
7. Require therapeutic alternatives in RxNorm for every PA-required or non-preferred drug.
8. Require consumer-facing RTPBC through the Patient Access API by October 2027.
9. Require consumer RTPBC responses to include insurance cost, preferred/non-preferred pharmacy pricing, coupons, manufacturer direct price, and cash price.
10. Prohibit commercial gatekeeping for HIPAA-compliant patient-authorized applications accessing mandated endpoints.
11. Require disclosure of RTPB intermediary infrastructure.
12. Publish annual payer-level outcome metrics and treat non-reporting as a compliance violation.
13. Issue a compliance status report on TiC drug MRF adherence before the final rule takes effect.
14. Explicitly name Da Vinci DTR as a parallel medical benefit prior authorization requirement.
15. Address in future rulemaking whether cash prices paid out of pocket count toward deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Nominal compliance without patient benefit; non-enforcement; consumer exclusion from RTPBC; stale and unusable formulary data; commercial gatekeeping; hidden intermediary control; standards that pass conformance testing but fail real workflows.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Prescription abandonment, preventable hospitalization, disease exacerbation, lower cancer survival, treatment interruption, unaffordable medications, inability to comparison shop, repeated unsuccessful therapies, and continued opacity in drug pricing.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Weak enforcement, payer resistance, inaccessible machine-readable files, inconsistent coding, endpoint throttling, commercial agreements, missing fields, ambiguous coverage restriction codes, and conflicted intermediary infrastructure.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** CAQH Index found only 29% electronic provider-to-payer eligibility transactions by 2010; electronic remittance adoption below 60% by 2016; TiC drug MRFs have had zero civil monetary penalties in four and a half years; more than five billion RTPB queries run for prescribers since 2019; AMA 2024 survey says 93% of physicians report PA delays care and 82% say it can lead to abandonment; ASTRO 2024 survey reports 92% PA-related treatment delays; about 33% of patients with PA approval do not return to pick up medication; 40% of specialty prescriptions are never filled; 60% abandonment when out-of-pocket cost exceeds $500; Medicare and beneficiaries paid nearly $47 billion for Part B drugs in 2022.
- **Studies or research referenced:** September 2025 American Journal of Medicine systematic review; AMA 2024 Prior Authorization Physician Survey; ASTRO 2024 Prior Authorization Survey; IQVIA 2024/2026 data; BrightInsight/Claritas Rx 2024; PLOS ONE 2023 biosimilar switching meta-analysis; JMCP 2025 study on non-medical switching; Frontiers in Pharmacology 2019 nocebo effects; MedPAC and ASPE Part B drug spending sources.
- **Examples provided:** Personal BCBS Tennessee/CVS Caremark pricing search; losartan and estradiol insurance vs cash price comparisons; TiC MRF schema and file-size problems; four compliance games; Part B drug categories and split benefit pathway drugs such as Humira, Stelara, and Actemra.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, prescribers, pharmacies, payers, PBMs, technology vendors, third-party apps, manufacturers, CMS, Congress, and health plans subject to RTPB/F&B requirements.
- **Economic implications:** The comment emphasizes patient savings from cash/coupon/direct prices, reduced abandonment, avoided waste from unusable MRFs, and the cost of non-enforcement; it also argues public metrics are a low-cost enforcement tool.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter considers the standards mature and technically feasible, but says feasibility must be judged by functional workflow performance, data completeness, latency, access rights, and usable machine-readable data.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment links prior authorization and cost opacity to delayed care, abandonment, hospitalization, worse cancer outcomes, and inability to afford medicine.
