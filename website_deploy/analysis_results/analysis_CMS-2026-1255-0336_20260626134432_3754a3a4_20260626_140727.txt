ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
CMS Proposed Rule: Interoperability Standards & Prior Authorization for Drugs 
Comments
1. Applicability to QHPs (Non-FFE/SHOP)
It is unclear whether the rule applies to QHPs not offered on the Federally Facilitated 
Exchange or SHOP. This creates uncertainty in compliance planning and resource 
allocation. CMS should clarify applicability for off-exchange individual and small group 
plans.
2. Interaction with State Requirements (Michigan SB 247)
The relationship between the CMS proposal and existing Michigan electronic prior 
authorization requirements is unclear, including whether federal requirements expand 
beyond current state mandates. CMS should clarify alignment and any preemption of 
state requirements.
3. Implementation Timeline
The proposed timeline may not allow sufficient time for system development, vendor 
coordination, and testing. Additional time may be needed to ensure readiness and 
compliance.
4. Technology & Cost Burden
Significant system modernization is required, including API development and workflow 
redesign. These changes are expected to increase vendor costs and internal IT 
investment.
5. Prior Authorization Workflow & Configuration Impact
Differences in prior authorization timelines across Medicare and QHP products will 
require system reconfiguration. Plans may need to separate QHP and large group 
products within shared tools, increasing operational complexity.
6. Fraud, Waste & Abuse Risk
Greater reliance on automated approvals may reduce documentation rigor and increase 
risk of inappropriate approvals, leading to increased costs and reduced oversight.
7. Lack of Auto-Approval Clarity
The rule does not define criteria for automated approvals, which may lead to 
inconsistent implementation and operational risk.
8. Metric Reporting Concerns
Public reporting of authorization metrics may be misinterpreted and could create 
unintended incentives to alter utilization management practices.
9. Vendor Dependency
Pharmacy implementation depends on PBM capabilities, while medical workflows 
require additional EHR enhancements, limiting direct control over readiness.
