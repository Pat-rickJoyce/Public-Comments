## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Illinois Public Health Association responds to the RFI on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing (Section III.E), urging CMS to protect patients from care delays caused by rigid PA timing requirements. The comment highlights that health plans often deny coverage when PA is not approved before specimen collection, forcing vulnerable patients—especially cancer patients—to undergo unnecessary repeat blood draws. IPHA makes five specific requests to reform laboratory PA requirements.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Retroactive Authorization for Already-Collected Specimens**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Health plans deny coverage when PA is not secured before specimen collection date; viable specimens must be discarded when administrative reviews lag, forcing repeat blood draws on vulnerable patients.
- **Specific request:** Require health plans to allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected.

**Topic 2: Prohibition on Timing-Based Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Denials based solely on PA timing—rather than medical necessity—are administratively driven and harm patients who need medically appropriate care.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.

**Topic 3: Protection from Unnecessary Repeat Procedures**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** When valid specimens are discarded due to administrative PA delays, patients must undergo unnecessary, repeated blood draws—adding clinical burden without medical benefit.
- **Specific request:** Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.

**Topic 4: Expedited Review for Oncology Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Cancer patients and those with rare diseases face time-sensitive treatment decisions; delays in diagnostic and biomarker testing can have life-threatening consequences.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.

**Topic 5: Patient Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (requesting new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Administrative delays that are not the patient's fault should not result in surprise costs to patients.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative in nature.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require health plans to allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.
3. Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.

5. Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative.

The Illinois Public Health Association (IPHA) appreciates the opportunity to provide comments on the CMS proposed rule CMS-2026-1255 - RFI for Prior Authorization and Access to Laboratory Testing. We are asking that CMS take appropriate steps to protect patients from critical care delays and denials caused by rigid prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing.

IPHA's mission is to lead in the enhancement and support of the public health system and the practice of public health, with an emphasis on ensuring the health of our population and the communities where people live, work, and play. Our members represent local health departments, universities, community-based providers, and health professionals including community health workers.

While IPHA commends CMS's efforts to modernize prior authorization, current rules cause severe, unintended clinical consequences. Many health plans deny coverage unless prior authorization is approved strictly on or before the exact date a specimen is collected. When administrative reviews lag, viable specimens must often be discarded, forcing vulnerable patients—especially those battling cancer or rare diseases—to undergo unnecessary, repeated blood draws...
