# Comment Analysis: CMS-2026-1255-0635_20260629112213_41f4c9fd

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION
- **Author/Organization name:** Bamboo Health
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Event notification and care coordination technology vendor that routes alerts to authorized recipients using hospital event messages, attribution/roster matching, and workflow-triggered notification delivery.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Bamboo Health supports expanding electronic event notifications but urges CMS to preserve HL7 v2 ADT as the current production-ready real-time push standard while piloting FHIR approaches. It recommends a tiered data model, stronger patient matching and attribution, provider directory modernization, permissible sharing guidance for behavioral health data, and TEFCA evolution toward operational real-time push workflows.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT
- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR Part 2, 2020 CMS Interoperability and Patient Access final rule event notification requirements
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 v2 ADT, HL7 2.5.1, FHIR Subscriptions, HL7 Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG, HL7 Da Vinci Member Attribution List IG, TEFCA, MLLP over VPN, HTTPS web services

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS
**Topic 1: Preserve HL7 v2 ADT while modernizing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 v2 ADT is widely deployed, real-time, push-based, and production-ready; FHIR push notifications are not yet mature or uniformly deployed.
- **Specific request:** Maintain HL7 v2 ADT as the operational backbone while piloting FHIR Subscriptions and Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications in targeted use cases.

**Topic 2: Additional event notification data elements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Additional data can improve routing and actionability but overloading notifications could degrade real-time performance and burden hospitals.
- **Specific request:** Use a tiered model with limited minimum fields and enhanced profiles for care management, transitions, behavioral health follow-up, and value-based workflows where available and lawful.

**Topic 3: TEFCA for event notifications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** TEFCA may expand reach and reduce duplicative agreements but is currently primarily query-based and lacks standardized real-time push infrastructure.
- **Specific request:** Develop a TEFCA use case for real-time push-based event notifications covering routing, authorization, endpoint discovery, patient matching, delivery confirmation, and technical routing entities.

**Topic 4: Privacy, behavioral health, and Part 2**
- **Provision ID:** PRIVACY-SECURITY / GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** State law and 42 CFR Part 2 fragmentation leads to over-redaction or suppression of behavioral health event data.
- **Specific request:** Provide federal guidance on permissible encounter-level sharing and adopt tiered data segmentation to allow basic event notifications while protecting sensitive details.

**Topic 5: Directories, attribution, and patient matching**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Notification value depends on routing to the right authorized recipient; stale rosters and directories reduce match and delivery accuracy.
- **Specific request:** Establish minimum practices for recipient directory maintenance, attribution files, roster updates, endpoint maintenance, delivery confirmation, and standardized roster/attribution exchange.

**Topic 6: Measurement and incentives**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Send rate alone does not show whether notifications reached authorized recipients or were timely/actionable.
- **Specific request:** Adopt objective measures for send rate, confirmed delivery rate, timeliness, recipient breadth, and closed-loop acknowledgment, with graduated enforcement and technical assistance.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES
- **S66-S82:** Provides extensive responses to the electronic event notification RFI, including data elements, standards, TEFCA, CoPs, directories, patient matching, attribution, measurement, and incentives.
- **S8/S9/S24/S33:** Discusses FHIR Subscriptions, Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications, endpoint/directory modernization, and automated discovery as future-state mechanisms but cautions against premature mandates.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS
1. Recognize real-time event notifications as foundational care coordination services.
2. Preserve HL7 v2 ADT as the current production-ready real-time push standard.
3. Advance FHIR through phased pilots rather than broad premature mandates.
4. Ensure TEFCA evolves to support real-time push-based workflows.
5. Improve patient matching, attribution data, provider directories, roster standards, and endpoint maintenance.
6. Clarify permissible behavioral health and encounter-level sharing, including 42 CFR Part 2 issues.
7. Adopt tiered notification data profiles and avoid overburdening hospitals.
8. Create qualified notification arrangements and recognize technical routing vendors.
9. Measure send rate, confirmed delivery, timeliness, recipient breadth, and closed-loop acknowledgment.
10. Use outcome-based CoP requirements rather than prescribing a single technology before market readiness.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS
- **Primary concerns:** Premature disruption of HL7 v2 ADT infrastructure.; TEFCA does not yet provide standardized real-time push event infrastructure.; No universally required production-ready FHIR ADT push specification exists today.; Patient matching, attribution, directories, and Part 2 uncertainty impede routing.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Forced migration could impose cost, strain resource-limited hospitals, disrupt stable ADT feeds, and degrade real-time performance.; Suppression of behavioral health event data could delay interventions and increase readmission or adverse events.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Fragmented HIE/vendor/network participation, variable standards implementation, custom onboarding, limited rural/CAH resources, EHR vendor readiness, legal/consent fragmentation, stale rosters.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
- **Data or statistics cited:** No formal statistics cited; provides operational observations about hospital supplemental fields and HL7 v2 ADT deployment.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Recommends a national fill-rate study; no study results cited.
- **Examples provided:** ADT event types A01, A03, A04, A08.; MLLP over VPN and HTTPS web services as common connectivity models.; Bamboo Health matching against customer rosters and delivering workflow-triggered notifications.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT
- **Affected stakeholders:** Hospitals, Critical access hospitals, Rural emergency hospitals, ACOs, Payers, PAC providers, Behavioral health providers, EMS, Pharmacies, Social service providers, HIEs, Event notification vendors, Patients
- **Economic implications:** Avoiding premature migration protects existing investment and prevents unnecessary re-engineering; better routing and automation may reduce downstream care coordination costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Current HL7 v2 ADT workflows are feasible and mature; FHIR push models require pilots, conformance tools, and broader deployment before mandates.
- **Patient care impact:** Timely notifications support transitions, outreach, post-discharge care, behavioral health follow-up, and value-based care performance.
