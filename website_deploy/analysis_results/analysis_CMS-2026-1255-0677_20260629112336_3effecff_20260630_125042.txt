# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Consumers' Checkbook / Checkbook; contact Eric Ellsworth.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Other (consumer information and plan/provider choice organization)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Checkbook states its mission is helping consumers make educated, high-value choices of service providers and describes extensive experience collecting, cleansing, and publishing provider directory data from QHP JSON files for plan shopping tools.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Checkbook supports CMS's interoperability goals and specifically supports centralized CMS collection and publication of impacted payers' API endpoints in the National Provider Directory rather than decentralized self-publication. The comment also urges CMS to develop structured payer, network, plan, and formulary identifiers, collect Transparency in Coverage file URLs, require structured prior authorization denial reason codes, report denial rates by reason code, and align QHP prior authorization timelines with other impacted payers.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations. The comment references CMS-0062-P, CMS-0057, CMS-9882P, ACA Transparency in Coverage reporting, Transparency in Coverage machine-readable files, and ERISA-regulated/self-insured plans.
- **Standards referenced:** National Provider Directory; NDH IG Endpoint Profile; FHIR APIs; FHIR Formulary resource; X12 claim adjustment reason codes, group codes, claims status codes/category codes, remittance advice remark codes, provider adjustment reason codes; CCIIO Machine Readable URL PUF; ONC Lantern.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Centralized API Endpoint Collection and Publication**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Checkbook argues that a centralized, time-driven CMS collection process would make endpoints easier for app developers, patients, and researchers to discover; create payer accountability; and enable CMS monitoring similar to ONC Lantern.
- **Specific request:** CMS should collect and publish impacted payer API endpoints in the National Provider Directory and should not rely on self-publication through the NDH IG Endpoint Profile as the primary approach.

**Topic 2: Voluntary Endpoint Submission for Non-Impacted Lines of Business**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Many payers use the same endpoints across plans and lines of business, but app developers cannot confidently use them for non-impacted lines without a centralized assertion.
- **Specific request:** CMS should request voluntary submission of API endpoints for non-impacted market segments, including ERISA-regulated plans, to broaden consumer access and app coverage.

**Topic 3: Payer, Network, Plan, and Formulary Identifiers**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that consumers and apps need open, structured identifiers to connect a health plan product to endpoints, provider networks, treatments, services, formularies, and price transparency data.
- **Specific request:** CMS should continue developing open, well-structured payer, network, and plan identifiers across all markets and coordinate with the Department of Labor to extend reach into self-insured ERISA plans.

**Topic 4: Transparency in Coverage URL Collection**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** TIC file discovery is burdensome, especially because payers often do not consolidate TIC files across plans and lines of business. Centralized URL collection would allow directories, TIC files, provider network participation, and prices to be linked and validated.
- **Specific request:** The National Provider Directory should collect TIC machine-readable file URLs for impacted payers and seek voluntary submissions for non-impacted lines of business.

**Topic 5: Structured Prior Authorization Denial Reason Codes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Free-text explanations alone would lead to inconsistent categorization and vague public debate, as seen in ACA denial reporting. Structured codes would support apples-to-apples analysis and accountability.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require well-structured denial reason codes alongside free text, using X12 code sets where possible and augmenting them as needed for prior authorization.

**Topic 6: Prior Authorization Timeline Alignment for QHPs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** A single standard across market segments would make consumer rights easier to understand and act upon.
- **Specific request:** CMS should align prior authorization timelines for QHPs with other impacted payers.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** The comment responds directly by supporting clear explanations for prior authorization denials and recommending that explanations include structured denial reason codes.
- **S17:** The comment directly supports structured codes and recommends using existing X12 denial/remittance/status code frameworks, augmented where necessary for prior authorization.
- **S23:** The comment supports centralized endpoint publication and argues it improves discoverability and accountability; it does not identify security objections to publication.
- **S24:** The comment recommends that CMS collect payer API endpoint URLs and also voluntarily collect endpoint information for non-impacted lines of business.
- **S26:** The comment opposes relying primarily on self-publication through the NDH IG Endpoint Profile and favors centralized CMS/NPD collection and publication.
- **S27:** The comment supports a regular, time-driven collection process so endpoint information is updated and stale URLs can be corrected.
- **S29:** The comment proposes reporting aggregated prior authorization denial rates broken out by structured reason codes, which is a usage/accountability metric for PA processes.
- **S33:** The comment supports publishing API endpoint information through the National Provider Directory, analogous to the CCIIO Machine Readable URL PUF.
- **S40:** The comment indirectly supports broader market reach by recommending voluntary endpoint and identifier participation for non-impacted market segments and lines of business.
- **S43:** The comment supports aligning QHP prior authorization timelines with other impacted payers to simplify consumer understanding of rights.
- **S66-S82:** The comment raises broader interoperability issues around network, plan, payer, formulary, and TIC identifiers that support consumer choice beyond the narrow drug PA provisions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Collect and publish impacted payers' API endpoints centrally in the National Provider Directory.
2. Do not rely on decentralized self-publication through the NDH IG Endpoint Profile as the primary endpoint discovery mechanism.
3. Use centralized endpoint collection to enable ongoing CMS monitoring and conformance testing similar to ONC Lantern.
4. Request voluntary endpoint submissions for non-impacted market segments and lines of business, including ERISA-regulated plans.
5. Develop open, structured payer, network, and plan identifiers across all markets.
6. Coordinate with the Department of Labor to maximize use of identifiers in self-insured ERISA markets.
7. Improve formulary identification and support access to the correct plan-specific formulary, ideally through FHIR Formulary resources.
8. Collect TIC machine-readable file URLs through the National Provider Directory.
9. Link TIC files, provider directory data, and identifiers to validate directory data and enable price/network tools.
10. Require structured prior authorization denial reason codes alongside free-text explanations.
11. Require reporting of aggregated denial rates by reason code.
12. Align QHP prior authorization timelines with other impacted payers.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Endpoint self-publication would reduce discoverability and accountability; network/plan/payer/formulary identifiers are inadequate; TIC URLs are hard to discover; free-text denial explanations would be inconsistent; QHP-specific timelines confuse consumers.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** App developers and consumers may be unable to find correct endpoints or TIC files; consumers may not be able to determine provider compatibility, treatment coverage, formulary status, or cost; denial data may be vague and non-comparable.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Stale or undiscoverable URLs, payer non-responsiveness without CMS accountability, lack of identifiers across non-impacted markets, poor formulary identification, inconsistent denial categorization, and lack of a regular endpoint update process.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics cited.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal studies cited.
- **Examples provided:** Checkbook cites its use of the CCIIO Machine Readable URL PUF for QHP JSON files and contrasts it with State-Based Marketplaces where URLs are not centrally submitted and require direct payer outreach. It also references experience with ACA Transparency in Coverage denial reporting to show variability when code-based categories are absent.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Consumers, patients, app developers, researchers, payers, QHP issuers, ERISA/self-insured plan sponsors, CMS, Department of Labor, price transparency vendors, providers, and entities using the National Provider Directory.
- **Economic implications:** Centralized endpoint and TIC URL discovery would reduce duplicative URL discovery work and improve market efficiency for consumer tools. Structured identifiers and denial codes could reduce administrative ambiguity and improve accountability.
- **Technical feasibility:** The commenter frames the approach as feasible by analogy to the existing CCIIO URL PUF and ONC Lantern, and recommends using existing X12 code sets and FHIR resources where possible.
- **Patient care impact:** Better endpoint discovery, identifiers, formularies, and PA denial transparency would help consumers understand coverage, access correct information, compare plans/providers, and exercise rights under prior authorization timelines.
