# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Ketan Dayma, MD; SUNY Upstate Medical University, New York
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; Academic
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Physician and radiologist; work focuses on validation and governance of artificial intelligence in clinical decision-making; conducting research on concordance between payer coverage determinations and specialty-society clinical criteria, including NCCN and ACR criteria.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Dr. Dayma strongly supports CMS's effort to standardize and accelerate electronic prior authorization, but warns that faster electronic transport alone will not make determinations accountable. He recommends requiring each automated or AI-assisted ePA determination to include a structured, machine-readable reference to the publicly accessible clinical criterion used, aligned with Medicare Advantage coverage-criteria policy and emerging state AI utilization-management laws.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR § 422.101(b)(6); CMS-4201-F; CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR Da Vinci standards; Prior Authorization API; NCCN Drugs & Biologics Compendium; ACR Appropriateness Criteria; NCDs/LCDs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Clinical basis of ePA determinations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The rule standardizes electronic transport of prior authorization decisions but does not require disclosure of the clinical criteria supporting approvals or denials. A denial transmitted quickly by API is still opaque if it does not identify the criterion applied.
- **Specific request:** Require every ePA determination to include a structured reference to the specific clinical criterion or guideline applied, and require internal criteria to be publicly accessible.

**Topic 2: Extending publicly accessible criteria requirements to drug PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS already requires Medicare Advantage internal coverage criteria to be based on current evidence and publicly accessible when Medicare coverage criteria are not fully established. The commenter argues this principle should also govern drug prior authorization under the new ePA pathways.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that the § 422.101(b)(6) publicly accessible internal coverage criteria standard applies to drug PA determinations made through this rule, including automated or algorithm-assisted determinations.

**Topic 3: AI-assisted and algorithmic prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** AI-assisted PA could scale opaque proprietary logic unless determinations are traceable to an auditable clinical criterion. The commenter does not oppose automation but wants the basis of automated decisions reconstructable by clinicians, patients, auditors, or courts.
- **Specific request:** Require machine-readable criterion references for automated or algorithmically assisted determinations; when no public criterion exists, attribute individualized determinations to a licensed clinician reviewer.

**Topic 4: Structured FHIR criterion-mapping element**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A free-text explanation would be less interoperable, comparable, or auditable than a structured data element. Structured criterion data would also make public PA metrics more meaningful.
- **Specific request:** Define a structured FHIR data element in the Prior Authorization API identifying guideline source, identifier, version, and date.

**Topic 5: Alignment with state AI utilization-management laws**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** States are adopting AI-in-utilization-management transparency and accountability laws. A federal ePA standard without criterion mapping could conflict with or duplicate state requirements.
- **Specific request:** Design criterion mapping so one structured determination record can support both federal ePA requirements and state AI utilization-management transparency/auditability requirements.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** The comment supports FHIR-based ePA infrastructure for covered outpatient and Part D drugs, while requesting added clinical-accountability data in the workflow.
- **S16:** The comment responds directly to what makes a denial reason specific, arguing that the reason should identify the precise publicly accessible clinical criterion applied.
- **S17:** The commenter favors structured, machine-readable criterion references rather than unstructured explanations.
- **S29:** The comment argues that public PA metrics become more meaningful when paired with structured criterion data that allows comparison across payers and services.
- **S37:** Identifies an implementation gap: standards may move decisions faster but still leave determinations opaque unless clinical criteria are mapped into the data exchange.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to broader RFIs on automation, accountability, transparency, and AI-assisted utilization management.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require each ePA determination to reference the specific clinical criterion applied.
2. Extend the § 422.101(b)(6) publicly accessible criteria principle to drug prior authorization under the rule's ePA pathways.
3. Require AI-assisted or algorithmic determinations to be traceable to a publicly accessible criterion.
4. Attribute determinations to a licensed clinician reviewer when individualized clinical judgment is used and no public criterion applies.
5. Create a structured, machine-readable FHIR data element for clinical-criterion references, including source, identifier, version, and date.
6. Align federal ePA criterion mapping with state AI utilization-management transparency and auditability laws.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Electronic prior authorization may automate opaque payer logic if determinations do not disclose the clinical criterion applied.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Faster but unexplained denials; proprietary or vendor-controlled AI logic; limited auditability for patients, clinicians, regulators, and courts; conflict with emerging state AI transparency requirements.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of structured clinical criterion fields in the Prior Authorization API and reliance on free text or payer attestations.

Thank you for the opportunity to comment on CMS-0062-P. I am a physician and radiologist at 
SUNY Upstate Medical University in Syracuse, New York, where my work focuses on the 
validation and governance of artificial intelligence in clinical decision-making. I am currently 
conducting a research project on the concordance between payer coverage determinations and

against, publicly accessible clinical criteria, not proprietary, undisclosed, or purely subjective 
logic. Building the electronic pipes without specifying the clinical substrate that flows through 
them risks automating opacity at scale.

each.
