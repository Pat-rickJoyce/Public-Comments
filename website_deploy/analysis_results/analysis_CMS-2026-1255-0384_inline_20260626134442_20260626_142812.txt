ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
At the beginning of 2026, my Botox treatment for chronic migraine was denied despite having been on this therapy for approximately two years with documented benefit. Botox had become an important part of my migraine management plan and had helped reduce the severity and frequency of my attacks. Because of the denial, my provider was required to complete a new prior authorization and appeal process, which significantly delayed my scheduled treatment. During this interruption in care, my migraine condition worsened dramatically. I ultimately developed status migrainosus, requiring hospitalization for three days due to an uncontrolled migraine attack that would not break with outpatient treatment. This disruption in an established and effective treatment plan had a serious impact on my health and daily functioning. My symptoms became severe enough that I was unable to maintain my employment, and I ultimately lost my job while dealing with this medical crisis. For patients with chronic migraine, delaying effective preventive treatment can have significant consequences. Botox was not a new or experimental therapy for me—it was an established treatment that was helping manage a disabling neurological condition. The delay caused by the denial and appeals process contributed to a significant decline in my health and quality of life.
