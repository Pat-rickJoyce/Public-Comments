# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Color of Gastrointestinal Illnesses (COGI); signed by Melodie Narain-Blackwell, President.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National patient advocacy organization focused on populations disproportionately affected by inflammatory bowel disease, digestive disorders, and associated chronic illnesses.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

COGI supports CMS's direction on prior authorization modernization and transparency, emphasizing that reforms must reduce treatment delays and protect continuity of care for patients with chronic gastrointestinal diseases. The commenter asks CMS to ensure technology supports individualized clinical decision-making rather than replacing the judgment of treating providers, especially in step therapy and chronic disease management.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI on step therapy; PATIENT-ACCESS-related issues.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Interoperability and technology/data sharing generally; no specific FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Support for prescription drug prior authorization reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support.
- **Rationale:** Patients with Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, chronic liver diseases, GI cancers, and other GI disorders often rely on complex medication regimens; delays can cause disease progression, exacerbations, ED visits, hospitalizations, and increased costs.
- **Specific request:** Finalize reforms in a way that meaningfully reduces delays in treatment and supports timely access to therapies recommended by providers.

**Topic 2: Denial transparency and public metrics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons and public metrics can improve accountability and reveal whether PA policies are causing denied or delayed access to recommended care.
- **Specific request:** Require specific reasons for denials and public reporting of PA metrics that patients, providers, and policymakers can use.

**Topic 3: Continuity of care for chronic disease therapies**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** Patients stable on established therapies should not face repetitive authorization or disruptions due to administrative processes or coverage transitions.
- **Specific request:** Reduce repetitive authorization for established therapies; recognize documented treatment history, prior failures, adverse events, and patient response; protect continuity during plan transitions.

**Topic 4: Technology and step therapy should preserve clinical judgment**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI on step therapy
- **Position:** Clarify / modify.
- **Rationale:** Patients with GI illnesses often have complex histories that cannot be captured fully by utilization management protocols; treatment decisions depend on provider judgment, severity, adverse events, preferences, and response patterns.
- **Specific request:** Ensure technology streamlines administration but does not substitute for individualized provider decision-making or undermine the patient-provider relationship.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14:** Supports reforms to improve prior authorization for prescription drugs and reduce delays for chronic GI therapies.
- **S16/S17:** Supports requiring specific reasons for denials to improve accountability and patient/provider understanding.
- **S29/S35:** Supports public reporting of PA metrics to help stakeholders understand access impacts and identify unintended consequences.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy/data-sharing RFI by urging CMS to preserve clinical judgment, recognize treatment histories, and avoid one-size-fits-all protocols.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize reforms that improve prior authorization efficiency and transparency for prescription drugs.
2. Require specific denial reasons.
3. Publicly report prior authorization metrics in a way useful to patients, providers, and policymakers.
4. Prioritize continuity of care for patients stable on chronic therapies.
5. Reduce repetitive prior authorization for established treatments.
6. Recognize documented treatment history, including prior medication failures and adverse events.
7. Protect patients during coverage transitions so plan changes do not interrupt medically necessary treatment.
8. Ensure step therapy and technology-enabled UM support, not replace, treating provider judgment.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delays in therapy initiation, interruptions to ongoing treatment, repetitive authorization, and step therapy protocols overriding individualized care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disease progression, symptom exacerbation, preventable complications, reduced quality of life, ED visits, hospitalizations, stress for patients/families, and provider burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Chronic disease complexity, personalized medicine, incomplete capture of treatment history in protocols, and coverage transitions.
