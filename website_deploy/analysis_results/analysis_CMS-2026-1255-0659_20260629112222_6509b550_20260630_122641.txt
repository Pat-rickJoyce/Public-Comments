# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Minority Quality Forum (NMQF); signed by Gary A. Puckrein, PhD, President and CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group; Research Organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** 501(c)(3) research and advocacy organization focused on reducing patient risk and improving quality of care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NMQF opposes prior authorization as a policy tool, including the proposed drug prior authorization framework in CMS-0062-P. Rather than recommending specific technical changes to FHIR, NCPDP, APIs, or timelines, NMQF argues that health care financing and access should be redesigned so prior authorization is not part of the access algorithm.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL. The comment also references non-drug PA rules generally.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior Authorization for Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** NMQF states that prior authorization is based on budgetary and expenditure concerns rather than patient needs and outcomes. It argues PA delays care, increases health and financial risk, distorts quality metrics, and creates or perpetuates bias.
- **Specific request:** CMS and stakeholders should move away from prior authorization rather than focus on making it more efficient.

**Topic 2: Health Care Financing and Access Design**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** NMQF frames drug PA as part of a broader administrative problem in U.S. health care and argues that access systems should prioritize patient outcomes over utilization control.
- **Specific request:** Design a financing and access system that does not include prior authorization in the decision algorithm.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Responds indirectly by rejecting the premise of implementing electronic PA for drugs, including medical-benefit drugs.
- **S14:** Responds indirectly by rejecting prior authorization for drugs generally, including pharmacy-benefit drugs.
- **S66-S82:** Provides a general policy response that prior authorization should not be optimized but eliminated from the access system.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Stop treating prior authorization as an acceptable tactic for managing care access.
2. Redesign financing and access systems so prior authorization is not part of care approval algorithms.
3. Prioritize patient needs, outcomes, quality of life, and risk reduction over expenditure-control processes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Prior authorization delays care, centers budgetary concerns over patient needs, distorts quality measurement, and embeds bias or preference.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Avoidable health risk, financial risk for patients and families, delayed access to care, and inequitable access effects.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Not addressed in technical terms; NMQF objects to the policy premise rather than the implementation details.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
