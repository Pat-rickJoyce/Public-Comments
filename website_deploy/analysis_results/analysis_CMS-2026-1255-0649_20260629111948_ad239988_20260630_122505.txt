## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Alliance for Health Information Operations and Standards (AHIOS); signed by Bart Howe, President.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association; Technology Vendor/Health Information Management outsourcing sector.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** AHIOS represents the release of information (ROI) industry. It states that member organizations process 60 million ROI requests annually and focus on secure disclosure of protected health information under HIPAA, state law, and industry best practices.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AHIOS responds primarily to the rule's cybersecurity and resiliency RFI rather than the drug prior authorization provisions. It supports interoperability and patient access, but asks CMS and HHS partners to add stronger privacy guardrails for third-party applications, plain-language authorizations, revocation mechanisms, encryption, multi-factor authentication, privacy-preserving record linkage, voluntary TEFCA participation, and credentialing service providers.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL. The comment relates to the rule's general RFI topics on cybersecurity, resiliency, interoperability, patient access, and TEFCA, rather than specific PA-DRUG, FHIR-IG, NCPDP, or HIPAA prior authorization standard provisions.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** HIPAA generally; HIPAA Security Rule; 45 CFR 164.508(c)(3).
- **Standards referenced:** TEFCA; APIs and digital access tools; encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit; multi-factor authentication; Privacy-Preserving Record Linkage (PPRL); credentialing service providers (CSPs).

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Third-Party App Privacy and Patient Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** AHIOS argues that third-party apps may rely on broad click-wrap authorizations, then reuse, sell, or insecurely store data after it leaves HIPAA-covered entities and business associates.
- **Specific request:** CMS should work with HHS partners to require standardized, plain-language authorizations, limit sharing to intended endpoints when selected by patients, and require simple authorization revocation mechanisms.

**Topic 2: Cybersecurity Baseline Practices**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Rising breaches and fraudulent record requests make health data exchange vulnerable to both hacking and traditional scams amplified by AI and digital pathways.
- **Specific request:** CMS should promote encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication, collaborative cybersecurity solutions, and stakeholder engagement before mandating requirements.

**Topic 3: HIPAA Gaps Outside Covered Entities**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify.
- **Rationale:** AHIOS believes patient trust is undermined when PHI moves to non-HIPAA covered entities without clear limits, transparency, or accountability.
- **Specific request:** CMS should use its authority and coordinate with other agencies to address regulatory gaps where health apps or commercial entities receive PHI through patient authorization.

**Topic 4: TEFCA and Trust Frameworks**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** AHIOS supports trusted exchange but says TEFCA is not yet ready to be the sole pathway for all purposes and could create resilience risks if mandated as a single conduit.
- **Specific request:** TEFCA should remain voluntary, include federal oversight to preserve competition and innovation, and require transparency when non-HIPAA third-party apps seek patient authorization.

**Topic 5: Secure Interoperability and Requester Authentication**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modification.
- **Rationale:** Interoperability can improve patient access only if data are accessed by authorized parties, for authorized purposes, and with privacy expectations preserved.
- **Specific request:** CMS should support credentialing service providers to authenticate requesters, verify commercial third-party applications, and reduce fraudulent PHI requests.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** AHIOS responds to the general RFI category on cybersecurity, resiliency, interoperability, and TEFCA. It recommends stronger third-party app privacy safeguards, plain-language authorizations, encryption, MFA, PPRL, voluntary TEFCA, trusted partner accountability, and credentialing service providers.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require standardized, plain-language authorizations for third-party applications receiving PHI.
2. Require or encourage clear patient options to share data only with the original intended endpoint, including in TEFCA-related exchange contexts.
3. Create simple, accessible authorization revocation mechanisms.
4. Promote encryption of ePHI at rest and in transit.
5. Promote multi-factor authentication for sensitive patient data access.
6. Consider Privacy-Preserving Record Linkage as a secure data linkage strategy.
7. Address HIPAA gaps when PHI leaves covered entities and business associates for non-HIPAA covered apps or commercial entities.
8. Keep TEFCA voluntary and maintain multiple secure exchange pathways.
9. Apply federal oversight to TEFCA to preserve competition and transparency.
10. Expand use of credentialing service providers to authenticate requesters and reduce fraudulent record requests.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Erosion of patient privacy, broad third-party app authorizations, sale or reuse of PHI, inadequate transparency, fraudulent record requests, cyberattacks, and over-centralized exchange infrastructure.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Loss of patient trust, unauthorized disclosure, compromised medical records, reduced data exchange due to distrust, impacted patient outcomes, and financial or operational burdens from poorly designed security mandates.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Regulatory gaps outside HIPAA, patient confusion about third-party requests, inconsistent security obligations, lack of clear authorizations, and insufficient requester authentication.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AHIOS members process 60 million ROI requests annually. The comment cites 725 large health data breaches reported to HHS OCR in 2023, affecting more than 133 million records.
- **Studies or research referenced:** HIPAA Journal healthcare data breach statistics are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Broad click-wrap app authorizations, third-party patient directives used to request PHI, fraudulent record requests, and TEFCA exchange involving non-HIPAA third-party applications.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, hospitals, clinics, ROI vendors, HIPAA covered entities, business associates, third-party applications, commercial health data platforms, TEFCA participants, CMS, ONC, and other federal agencies.
- **Economic implications:** AHIOS warns against costly or counterproductive mandates, while emphasizing that cybersecurity prevents financial losses and continuity disruptions.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment presents encryption, MFA, PPRL, CSPs, and trust frameworks as feasible tools, but asks CMS to coordinate with stakeholders before imposing requirements.
- **Patient care impact:** AHIOS argues secure access to records supports care delivery and patient outcomes, while privacy failures and distrust can reduce exchange and harm access.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Concerned.
- **Tone:** Professional, security-focused, and cautionary.
- **Priority level:** High.

3. "TEFCA should remain voluntary." - Context: AHIOS's position on resiliency and exchange pathway diversity.
4. "APIs and other digital access tools must be accompanied by rigorous standards" - Context: Discussion of interoperability and HIPAA Security Rule interaction.
