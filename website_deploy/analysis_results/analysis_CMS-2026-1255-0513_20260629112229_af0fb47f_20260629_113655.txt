## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Avalon Healthcare Solutions, a laboratory benefit manager (LBM), supports CMS's interoperability goals for prior authorization but responds specifically to the RFI questions on laboratory tests, urging CMS to recognize laboratory testing as a specialized benefit category requiring distinct infrastructure. Avalon identifies critical gaps in current PA workflows for lab tests—including inadequate CPT coding specificity, lack of real-time PA determination at order entry, insufficient TRF content standards, and site-of-service cost transparency—and recommends policies that promote prospective provider engagement, specific test identification coding (e.g., DEX Z-Codes), data clearinghouse completeness requirements, and consumer education on lab cost variation.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Lab Test Coding Specificity (CPT Code Inadequacy)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** More than 40,000 genetic tests are associated with a single CPT code (81479), making meaningful PA or payment integrity review impossible. Retrospective review of a single 81479 claim requires up to 90 minutes vs. fewer than 5 minutes for claims with specific molecular CPT codes.
- **Specific request:** Require a process like the MolDX program's DEX Z-Code system for all Medicare Advantage plans.

**Topic 2: Test Requisition Form (TRF) Standards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Broadly accepting TRFs without minimum content standards risks enabling panel stuffing and prevents meaningful medical necessity review for complex genetic tests.
- **Specific request:** Require minimum TRF content standards including specific test identification (DEX Z-Code or equivalent), diagnosis codes, and a clinical indication field.

**Topic 3: Data Clearinghouse Completeness**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Z-Codes are captured in the CPT comment/narrative field and are frequently stripped by claims adjudication systems during transmission.
- **Specific request:** Require data clearinghouses to transmit all data fields submitted by laboratories.

**Topic 4: Prospective Provider Engagement / FHIR-Based PA at Order Entry**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Post-collection PA submissions occur frequently due to provider unawareness, speed-to-result lab workflows, and urgent clinical situations. Avalon's experience shows 40% of PAs are auto-approved with appropriate documentation.
- **Specific request:** Promote FHIR APIs enabling real-time PA determination at order entry and provider-facing PA requirement lookup tools integrated into EHR and LIS workflows.

**Topic 5: Clinical Utility Requirements for Panel Tests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Panel stuffing adds clinically unnecessary tests; ~$2 PMPM in lab spending attributable to tests with limited/no clinical utility; ~30% of $82 billion in annual U.S. lab spending is wasteful.
- **Specific request:** Require PA processes to address panel composition and alignment with professional/societal standards.

**Topic 6: Site-of-Service Cost Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** HOPD lab tests reimbursed at 300–400% of Medicare's independent diagnostic fee schedule (up to 5.7x for common tests) with no improved clinical quality.
- **Specific request:** Implement site-neutral payment policies; require payer member education on lower-cost options; prohibit contractual terms forbidding such education.

**Topic 7: Lab Result Data Integration**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI on lab tests)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Neither PA systems nor claims adjudication systems receive lab result data, creating upcoding risk. Non-diabetic low-cardiovascular-risk patients 14x more likely to receive GLP-1 treatment than high-risk patients, suggesting lab data not informing prescribing.
- **Specific request:** Enforce clearinghouse data field transmission requirements; integrate lab result data into PA review and risk adjustment workflows.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

This comment responds primarily to the RFI on laboratory tests (S66–S82 range):

- **S66–S82 (General RFI / Lab tests and DMEPOS):** Responds to all CMS RFI questions on laboratory tests: how PA requirements impact care and provider burden; impacts of TRF acceptance; frequency of post-collection submission denials; LBM impacts on PA processes; PA improvement opportunities; how automation could improve lab PA; and technology gaps for lab PA.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Encourage payers to leverage LBMs with specialized laboratory benefit management capabilities.
2. Require minimum content standards for TRFs (specific test identification, diagnosis codes, clinical indication).
3. Promote FHIR APIs enabling real-time PA determination at point of order entry.
4. Require clinical utility standards for laboratory tests.
5. Implement site-neutral payment policies for lab tests.
6. Educate consumers on lower-cost lab testing options.
7. Require DEX Z-Code system (or equivalent) for all Medicare Advantage plans.
8. Require data clearinghouses to transmit all claim data fields submitted by laboratories.
9. Require PA processes to address panel composition and clinical utility for large panel tests.
10. Implement transparent benefit design tying out-of-pocket costs to site-of-service pricing.
11. Prohibit contractual terms forbidding member education on lower-cost lab options.
12. Integrate lab result data into PA review workflows and risk adjustment processes.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CPT coding inadequacy for genetic/molecular tests (40,000+ tests under CPT 81479); TRF acceptance without content standards enabling panel stuffing; post-collection PA submissions due to structural workflow issues; site-of-service price variation unaddressed by PA processes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Without coding specificity, retrospective review requires up to 90 minutes per 81479 claim; automated PA matching and FWA detection impossible; Z-Codes stripped by clearinghouses during transmission.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Current CPT framework insufficient for meaningful lab PA; EHR workflows prioritize speed over PA compliance; clearinghouse systems do not preserve all claim data fields.

---

- **Data or statistics cited:** 14 billion annual U.S. lab tests; $82 billion in spending (~30% wasteful); 40,000+ genetic tests under CPT 81479; 90+ million members managed by Avalon; 35% increase in genetic testing spend, 22% increase in utilization (2025 analysis of ~37M members); 40% of PAs auto-approved with appropriate documentation; 75% call volume reduction; 15% reduction in incomplete PA rejections; 39% averted cost savings in 6M-member genetic test population; 9.3% averted savings in 9M-member routine test population; ~$2 PMPM from tests with limited/no clinical utility; HOPD rates 300-400% (up to 5.7x) of independent diagnostic fee schedule; non-diabetic low-CV-risk patients 14x more likely to receive GLP-1 treatment.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Avalon Healthcare Solutions 2025 Lab Trend Report (5th Annual); Lab economics 17(11); Avalon internal analysis 2025.
- **Examples provided:** CPT code 81479 aggregating 40,000+ genetic tests; GLP-1 prescribing misalignment with lab risk data.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Health plans, laboratory benefit managers, ordering providers, patients, data clearinghouses, EHR vendors, CMS.
- **Economic implications:** ~30% of $82 billion annual lab spending is wasteful; ~$2 PMPM in clinically unnecessary tests; HOPD rates 300-400% of independent diagnostic rates; LBM programs demonstrate significant averted costs.
