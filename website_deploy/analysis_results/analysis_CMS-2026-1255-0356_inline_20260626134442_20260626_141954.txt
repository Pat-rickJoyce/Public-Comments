## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Dr. Bo Wang, a medical oncologist, strongly supports CMS's PA modernization efforts and responds specifically to the RFI on laboratory testing. Drawing on clinical experience, he describes timing-based PA denials that delay or deny medically necessary oncology tests even when specimens are already collected, forcing repeat procedures and treatment delays. He urges CMS to prohibit timing-based denials, prevent forced repeat specimen collection, establish expedited oncology lab review pathways, require transparent denial explanations in electronic PA systems, and mandate public reporting of lab PA denial rates.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prohibit Timing-Based Denials for Laboratory/Pathology Tests**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Denials occur even when tests are clinically appropriate, physician-ordered, and performed on valid specimens—solely because authorization was pending at time of specimen collection. These denials do not improve care or reduce inappropriate utilization.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials based solely on timing of specimen collection when a medically necessary test was ordered and a valid specimen already obtained.

**Topic 2: Prevent Forced Repeat Specimen Collection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring repeat blood draws or tissue sampling due to administrative delays causes physical discomfort, emotional distress, treatment delays, increased costs, and avoidable procedural risk.
- **Specific request:** Prevent insurers from requiring repeat specimen collection solely because PA was pending when the specimen was collected.

**Topic 3: Expedited Review for Oncology Laboratory Testing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS (analogous for lab)
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology lab tests—molecular diagnostics, biomarker testing, genomic profiling—directly affect diagnosis, staging, treatment selection, and monitoring; delays adversely affect outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited review pathways for oncology-related laboratory, pathology, molecular diagnostic, and biomarker testing.

**Topic 4: Transparent Denial Explanations in Electronic PA Systems**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Clinically meaningful explanations are needed to allow providers to understand, appeal, and address denials effectively.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to provide transparent, clinically meaningful explanations for lab testing denials, accessible through electronic PA systems.

**Topic 5: Patient/Provider/Lab Financial Protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer administrative delays—not clinical decisions—should not expose patients, physicians, or laboratories to financial penalties, claim denials, or surprise billing.
- **Specific request:** Protect patients, physicians, and laboratories from financial consequences resulting from payer administrative delays.

**Topic 6: Public Reporting on Lab PA Denial Rates**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Transparency about denial rates, turnaround times, and appeal overturn rates enables accountability and identifies systemic problems.
- **Specific request:** Require reporting and public transparency on lab and pathology testing PA denial rates, turnaround times, and appeal overturn rates.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

This comment responds to the RFI on laboratory testing (Section III.E) rather than numbered solicitations S1–S82. Most directly relevant to:
- **S16/S17 (denial reasons):** Urges transparent, clinically meaningful denial explanations in electronic PA systems.
- General support for broader interoperability and electronic PA provisions.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prohibit denials based solely on timing of specimen collection when a valid specimen is already obtained and the test was medically necessary.
2. Prevent insurers from requiring repeat specimen collection due to administrative PA delays.
3. Establish expedited review pathways for oncology laboratory, pathology, molecular diagnostic, and biomarker testing.
4. Require transparent, clinically meaningful denial explanations available through electronic PA systems.
5. Protect patients, physicians, and labs from financial penalties arising from payer administrative delays.
6. Require public reporting of lab PA denial rates, turnaround times, and appeal overturn rates.

- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative processing lag creates timing gap between specimen collection and PA approval.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT

Re: CMS-0062-P – Medicare and Medicaid Programs; Interoperability, Electronic Prior Authorization, and Patient Access Proposed Rule

To the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services:

I am Bo Wang, MD, a practicing medical oncologist, and I appreciate the opportunity to provide comments on CMS-0062-P. I strongly support CMS's efforts to modernize prior authorization processes, improve interoperability, and reduce administrative barriers that delay medically necessary care. I am particularly encouraged by CMS's Request for Information regarding prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing and their impact on patient care.

In oncology, laboratory and pathology testing are not ancillary services—they are essential components of diagnosis, staging, treatment selection, and ongoing disease monitoring. Modern cancer care increasingly relies on molecular diagnostics, biomarker testing, genomic profiling, pathology review, and serial laboratory assessments to guide evidence-based treatment decisions. Delays in obtaining these tests can directly delay diagnosis, postpone treatment initiation, and adversely affect patient outcomes...
