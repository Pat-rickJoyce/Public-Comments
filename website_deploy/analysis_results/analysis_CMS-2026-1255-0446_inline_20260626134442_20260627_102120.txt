## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Individual commenter; name not provided in extracted text.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; Individual.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** The commenter states they have been a provider in practice for 26 years and are also the daughter of a patient whose care was delayed by Medicare prior authorization.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter strongly supports CMS-0062-P and asks CMS to finalize and strengthen it so clinical decisions remain centered on patients and treating clinicians rather than insurance delay tactics. They support electronic prior authorization and interoperability for drugs but request stronger timeframes, deemed approval for missed deadlines, clearer submission rules, specific denial explanations, fast appeals, laboratory testing protections, and detailed public reporting.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED at a general interoperability level; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFI topics on appeals, diagnostic/laboratory testing, and payer accountability.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None.
- **Standards referenced:** Standardized electronic workflows and electronic prior authorization generally; no named FHIR IGs, NCPDP standards, or HL7 standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Finalize and strengthen electronic prior authorization for drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Standardized electronic workflows, real-time exchange of coverage and documentation requirements, and shorter timeframes can reduce burden and accelerate care.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the rule but strengthen it to prevent vague criteria, repeated information requests, unclear denials, and slow appeals from continuing to delay care.

**Topic 2: Decision timeframes and deemed approval**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PA delays interrupt medication access, postpone diagnoses, worsen outcomes, and particularly harm time-sensitive care.
- **Specific request:** Require 24-hour decisions for all drugs and time-sensitive care, including oncology and diagnostic/laboratory testing; at minimum require 24 hours for expedited requests and no more than 72 hours for standard requests; deem requests approved or covered when payers miss deadlines.

**Topic 3: Complete submissions and clock rules**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Payers can undermine deadlines by restarting review clocks through duplicative, non-clinical, or undisclosed documentation demands.
- **Specific request:** Clearly define a complete PA submission and start the review clock once required information is submitted through the standardized process.

**Topic 4: Denial explanations and appeals**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Vague denials and slow appeals can function as denial by delay.
- **Specific request:** Require denials to identify the exact policy or clinical criterion, explain why submitted documentation failed, state missing information, and provide plain-language reconsideration or appeal instructions; require simple electronic appeals decided quickly by qualified clinicians with relevant expertise.

**Topic 5: Laboratory testing protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not face repeat blood draws or financial penalties because payer paperwork lagged behind medically necessary specimen collection.
- **Specific request:** Bar denials of medically necessary tests solely because authorization was pending on the date of blood draw or specimen collection.

**Topic 6: Transparency and public reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Accountability requires meaningful plan- and service-specific reporting.
- **Specific request:** Publicly report approval rates, denial rates, turnaround times, appeal rates, appeal outcomes, overturn rates, and common denial reasons.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S21:** The commenter supports electronic prior authorization for drugs and says standardized workflows and real-time exchange can reduce burden and improve timely care, but only if paired with stronger anti-delay safeguards.
- **S16/S17:** The comment asks for clear, specific, actionable denial explanations identifying the exact policy or criterion, reasons documentation failed, missing information, and appeal steps.
- **S20/S43:** The comment recommends 24-hour decisions for all drugs and time-sensitive care, with 24-hour expedited and 72-hour standard decisions at minimum, plus deemed approval if a payer misses the deadline.
- **S29/S34/S35:** The commenter recommends public reporting of approval, denial, turnaround, appeal, appeal outcome, overturn, and common denial reason metrics in plan- and service-specific form.
- **S66-S82 (General/RFI topics, including laboratory/diagnostic testing and appeals):** The comment asks CMS to strengthen protections for laboratory testing, diagnostic testing, oncology, appeals, and payer accountability.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize and strengthen CMS-0062-P.
2. Require 24-hour PA decisions for all drugs and time-sensitive care, including oncology and diagnostic/laboratory testing.
3. Set 24-hour expedited and 72-hour standard timelines at minimum.
4. Deem requests approved or covered when payers miss required timelines.
5. Define a complete PA submission and prohibit clock restarts based on duplicative, non-clinical, or undisclosed documentation demands.
6. Require clear, specific, actionable denial explanations.
7. Create simple, electronic, timely appeals reviewed by appropriately qualified clinicians.
8. Protect laboratory testing from denials caused solely by pending authorization at the time of specimen collection.
9. Require plan- and service-specific public reporting of PA and appeal metrics.
