## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This brief inline comment on FR Section II.A.2 (API denial of access criteria, page 19950) expresses appreciation for CMS's cybersecurity focus while requesting clearer guidance on what constitutes an "unacceptable level of security risk" for denying app API access. The commenter recommends CMS support industry-developed application attestation or vetting frameworks and require patient-facing transparency when health information is transmitted to non-HIPAA-covered applications.

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** FR Section II.A.2, FR Page 19950
- **Standards referenced:** HIPAA (referenced as the protection standard consumers may or may not have for third-party apps)

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Access Denial Criteria — Need for Clear Guidance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** The proposed rule states payers must use "objective, verifiable criteria" to deny app access, but does not specify what those criteria are, leaving organizations uncertain whether their determinations will be regarded as consistent and in good faith.
- **Specific request:** CMS provide clear guidance on criteria for identifying unacceptable security risk; support recognized application attestation or vetting approaches such as voluntary industry-developed code-of-conduct frameworks.

**Topic 2: Patient Transparency for Non-HIPAA Apps**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PRIVACY-SECURITY
- **Position:** Modify (add requirement)
- **Rationale:** Patients may not understand when their PHI is transmitted to apps that lack HIPAA protections, creating an informed consent gap.
- **Specific request:** Require patient-facing transparency disclosures when health information will be transmitted to applications not subject to HIPAA protections.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S23:** Commenter addresses security concerns related to API access, recommending clearer criteria for denying app access and supporting industry vetting frameworks to help payers make consistent, good-faith security determinations.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide clear guidance on the criteria organizations may use to identify an unacceptable level of security risk when denying app API access.
2. Support recognized application attestation or vetting approaches, such as voluntary, industry-developed code-of-conduct frameworks.
3. Require patient-facing transparency disclosures when health information will be transmitted to applications not subject to HIPAA protections.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Ambiguity in "objective, verifiable criteria" for API denial leaves payers uncertain about good-faith compliance; patients may not know when their data goes to non-HIPAA apps.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Without clear guidance, organizations may be uncertain whether their security determinations will be regarded as consistent and in good faith.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Diversity of operational environments makes uniform criteria challenging without regulatory guidance.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
FR Section: II.A.2 (API requirements – denial of access)
FR Page: 19950

Source Text
"…the only reason impacted payers may deny an app or developer API access is if it would present an unacceptable level of risk to the security of PHI on the payer's system. These risks include, for example, insufficient authentication or authorization controls, poor encryption, or reverse engineering. The payer must make that determination using objective, verifiable criteria that are applied fairly and consistently across all apps and developers."

Proposed Comment
I appreciate the focus on strengthening cybersecurity practices and the importance of patient access to health information. I encourage consideration of implementation flexibility and clear guidance on the criteria organizations may rely on to identify an unacceptable level of security risk, so that organizations can decline access to demonstrably insecure applications with confidence that such determinations will be regarded as consistent and in good faith. I respectfully suggest that CMS consider supporting recognized application attestation or vetting approaches, such as voluntary, industry-developed code-of-conduct frameworks, and consider patient-facing transparency that clearly informs individuals when their information will be transmitted to an application that may not be subject to HIPAA protections...
