## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter urges CMS to adopt patient-centered prior authorization protections for critical tests, especially time-sensitive cancer diagnostics. The comment seeks to prevent denials and financial penalties when authorization delays are outside the control of patients, clinicians, or laboratories.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Timing-based prior authorization denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that a critical test should not be denied solely because authorization was pending on the date the specimen was collected.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit timing-based denials tied to insurer processing delays.

**Topic 2: Expedited review for oncology diagnostics**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cancer and other time-sensitive diagnostic testing can affect treatment decisions and should not be slowed by ordinary prior authorization queues.
- **Specific request:** Establish mandatory expedited review pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic tests.

**Topic 3: Patient and provider financial protections**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients, doctors, and labs should not bear surprise bills or penalties caused by administrative delays beyond their control.
- **Specific request:** Ban financial penalties resulting from insurance paperwork delays.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20:** The comment implicitly responds by requesting expedited handling for urgent diagnostic testing, particularly oncology-related testing.
- **S43:** The comment supports aligning prior authorization timeframes with clinical urgency so testing and treatment are not delayed.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prevent denials of critical tests solely because authorization was pending when blood was drawn.
2. Prohibit redundant blood draws when a valid specimen already exists.
3. Require expedited prior authorization review for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics.
4. Ban surprise bills and financial penalties caused by payer paperwork delays.
