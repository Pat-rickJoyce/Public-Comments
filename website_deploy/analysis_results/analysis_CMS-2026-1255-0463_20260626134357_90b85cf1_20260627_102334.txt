## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Alliance for Caregiving (NAC); signed by Jason Resendez, President & CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group (representing family caregivers)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** NAC is a nonprofit coalition focused on family caregiver research and policy advocacy; produces the authoritative "Caregiving in the U.S." research series (most recent edition July 2025, conducted with AARP since 1997).

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The National Alliance for Caregiving broadly supports the proposed rule's provisions to streamline prior authorization for drugs, framing its entire comment from the perspective of the 63 million family caregivers who become de facto PA navigators when medications are delayed or denied. NAC supports shorter PA decision timeframes, ePA, specific denial reasons, API usage metrics, cross-program consistency, and FHIR-based standardization — all on the grounds that these measures reduce the administrative burden that currently falls on families. NAC adds a targeted recommendation that CMS explore timeframe requirements for provider-to-patient/caregiver notification after PA approval to ensure expedited approvals translate into timely medication access.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** When authorization is delayed, family caregivers must manage escalating symptoms at home while fielding calls with plans and pharmacies, often while balancing work and other caregiving responsibilities. Predictable, expedited timelines reduce uncertainty for caregivers of patients whose conditions deteriorate rapidly.
- **Specific request:** Support shorter and more aligned PA decision timeframes as proposed; explore adding a corresponding timeframe for provider notification to patients/caregivers or prescription transmission to the dispensing pharmacy after approval, to ensure expedited PA decisions translate into timely medication access.

**Topic 2: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Phone-based and paper-based authorization processes consume hours caregivers cannot afford; create gaps in treatment caregivers must manage at home; contribute to emotional toll. Streamlined electronic workflows giving clinicians real-time PA information at the point of prescribing reduce instances where caregivers learn at the pharmacy that a treatment cannot proceed.
- **Specific request:** Extend electronic PA requirements to drugs as proposed.

**Topic 3: Specific Denial Reasons and PA Data in Access APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support (strongly)
- **Rationale:** Vague denials are particularly harmful to family caregivers who become de facto PA navigators. When denial notices lack clear explanations, caregivers spend days deciphering payer reasoning, contacting providers, and attempting appeals without adequate information.
- **Specific request:** Require specific denial reasons and detailed PA data to flow through patient, provider, and payer-to-payer APIs.

**Topic 4: API Usage Metrics and CMS Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Nominal compliance with interoperability requirements will not improve the caregiver experience unless the technology meaningfully reduces treatment delays; oversight of real-world API use will help CMS identify where plans continue to impose harmful friction.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to report API endpoints and annual usage metrics to CMS.

**Topic 5: Scope of Impacted Payers**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Family caregivers and those they support do not self-select into a single coverage category; coverage transitions are common during serious or chronic illness. Radically different PA barriers across programs add complexity and can disrupt treatment continuity at moments of greatest vulnerability.
- **Specific request:** Apply protections consistently across Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, and Qualified Health Plans.

**Topic 6: Standardized FHIR-Based Transactions**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Administrative fragmentation places caregivers in the position of bridging disconnected systems on behalf of family members, generating significant stress and contributing to worse health outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Support adoption of standardized FHIR-based approaches for PA-related transactions as proposed.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20:** NAC supports shorter and more aligned drug PA decision timeframes; notes expedited timelines reduce uncertainty for caregivers managing patients whose conditions can deteriorate rapidly.
- **S16/S17:** Strongly supports specific denial reasons and PA data flowing through APIs; vague denials burden caregivers navigating appeals without adequate information.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Supports API endpoint reporting and annual usage metrics to ensure nominal compliance translates into real-world reduction of treatment delays.
- **S40/S41:** Supports cross-program consistency; PA barriers vary across programs, disrupting caregiving continuity for those transitioning between coverage types.
- **S44:** Supports FHIR-based standardization for PA transactions as a means to reduce administrative fragmentation caregivers currently bridge.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Support shorter and more aligned PA decision timeframes as proposed.
2. Explore adding a timeframe requirement for provider notification to patients/caregivers or prescription transmission to the dispensing pharmacy after PA approval.
3. Extend electronic PA requirements to drugs as proposed.
4. Require specific denial reasons and detailed PA data in patient, provider, and payer-to-payer APIs.
5. Require impacted payers to report API endpoints and annual usage metrics to CMS.
6. Apply interoperability and PA protections consistently across Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, CHIP, and Qualified Health Plans.
7. Support adoption of standardized FHIR-based approaches for PA-related transactions.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Risk that expedited PA decision timeframes will not translate into timely medication access if no corresponding notification/dispensing timeframe is established; nominal API compliance without meaningful use; inconsistent PA barriers across programs disrupting caregiving continuity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Without post-approval notification safeguards, families may continue to experience treatment delays even after authorization is approved; vague denials leave caregivers unable to navigate appeals effectively.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** None specific; comment is primarily supportive.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
On behalf of the National Alliance for Caregiving (NAC), we appreciate the opportunity to comment on this proposed rule. NAC is a nonprofit coalition dedicated to conducting research, developing national programs, and advocating for policies that support family caregivers. Our flagship research series, Caregiving in the U.S., produced in partnership with AARP and first conducted in 1997, is widely regarded as the most authoritative ongoing data resource on family caregiving in America. The most recent edition, released in July 2025, found that 63 million Americans, nearly 1 in 4 adults, are providing ongoing care for an older adult, someone with a serious illness, or a person with a disability, an increase of 20 million since 2015.

Family caregivers are central to the health care system, yet they remain largely invisible to it. They coordinate medications and treatments, communicate with providers across settings, manage symptoms at home, and serve as the primary buffer between patients and a fragmented system. Prior authorization (PA) processes for drugs impose a disproportionate burden on these caregivers, who too often become the de facto appeals coordinators, documentation gatherers, and crisis managers when a treatment is delayed or denied...
