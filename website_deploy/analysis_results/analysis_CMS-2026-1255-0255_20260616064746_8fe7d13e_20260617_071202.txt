## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Vertex Pharmaceuticals Incorporated; signed by Samantha Ventimiglia, Senior Vice President, U.S. Public Affairs
- **Stakeholder type:** Other - pharmaceutical/biotechnology manufacturer
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Vertex develops therapies for serious diseases including cystic fibrosis, sickle cell disease, transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia, acute pain, and rare/complex conditions with limited treatment alternatives.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Vertex supports CMS's goals of modernizing prior authorization and reducing administrative burden, but warns that efficient electronic PA infrastructure could also increase PA volume unless paired with safeguards. The company recommends transparency benchmarks, tailored frameworks for specialized therapies, expedited pathways, step therapy exceptions, appeals modernization, gold carding, therapy-level exemptions, research-ready PA data, and broader commercial market alignment.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL/RFIs on step therapy, appeals, gold carding, and data use
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; 91 Fed. Reg. 19890 (April 14, 2026)
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization tools, interoperability standards, automated PA workflows, artificial intelligence. No specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Guardrails on electronic prior authorization expansion**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Standardized electronic PA may speed decisions but could lower the marginal cost of applying utilization management and increase PA volume, denials, and appeals.
- **Specific request:** Aggregate and publicly report PA volume and denial-rate trends by drug class and payer, set benchmarks, and investigate material increases after implementation.

**Topic 2: Tailored PA frameworks for specialized therapies**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Gene therapies, one-time potentially curative treatments, stable maintenance therapies, and urgent therapies do not fit routine annual PA cycles.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit repeat or renewal PA for stable patients, require payers to accept multi-year approvals when patients switch plans, and create a sub-24-hour pathway for certain urgently needed therapies.

**Topic 3: Step therapy safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy may be clinically inappropriate where there is only one approved therapy, contraindicated alternatives, risk of irreversible progression, or therapies involving novel mechanisms.
- **Specific request:** Use interoperability infrastructure so patients switching plans do not restart completed step therapy, define exceptions criteria, and require plans to monitor and report utilization and outcomes.

**Topic 4: Appeals modernization and AI oversight**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Automated denials could increase in speed and volume while appeals remain slow and manual.
- **Specific request:** Align appeal timelines with automated PA timelines, create interoperability requirements for internal and external appeals, expedite appeals for rare diseases and urgent circumstances, and ensure qualified physicians make final determinations when AI supports review.

**Topic 5: Gold carding, therapy-level exemptions, and market alignment**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** PA data infrastructure can identify high-performing providers and therapies with consistently high approval rates.
- **Specific request:** Establish clear gold carding standards, define therapy-level exemption criteria, incentivize high-value PA programs, and coordinate with Labor and Treasury to encourage commercial market adoption.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20/S43:** Vertex asks for a sub-24-hour pathway for urgently needed therapies and faster appeals aligned with automated PA decision timelines.
- **S16/S17:** The comment addresses denial and appeal processes by seeking appeals modernization, qualified physician final determinations, and appropriate AI oversight, though it does not specify structured denial codes.
- **S29/S35:** Vertex recommends public reporting of PA volume and denial-rate trends by drug class and payer, centrally accessible through CMS, plus de-identified longitudinal PA and claims data for research.
- **S48:** The comment addresses cost-benefit indirectly by recommending analysis of whether timely access reduces downstream utilization and total cost of care.
- **S51/S53:** Vertex raises concern about standards alignment outside CMS-covered payers, including ERISA-governed commercial plans, and recommends coordination to avoid a two-tier system.
- **S66-S82:** The comment responds to broader RFIs on step therapy, gold carding, interoperability data, and market alignment with detailed recommendations.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Publicly report PA volume and denial-rate trends, disaggregated by drug class and payer, on a central CMS website.
2. Establish PA volume and denial-rate benchmarks and investigate material increases after implementation.
3. Prohibit repeat or renewal PA for stable patients on existing therapies.
4. Require payers to accept multi-year PA approvals when patients switch plans.
5. Create a sub-24-hour PA pathway for urgently needed therapies.
6. Establish clear step therapy exception criteria and prevent patients from restarting completed step therapy after plan changes.
7. Require plans to report step therapy utilization, delays, abandonment, appeals, and outcomes.
8. Align appeals timelines with automated PA timelines and support interoperable internal and external appeals.
9. Create expedited appeals for rare diseases, small patient populations, and urgent clinical circumstances.
10. Require qualified physician final determinations and appropriate monitoring when AI is used in appeals.
11. Establish gold carding standards and therapy-level PA exemption criteria.
12. Make de-identified longitudinal PA and claims data available for research and evaluate downstream cost and outcome impacts.
13. Coordinate with the Departments of Labor and Treasury to encourage commercial market alignment.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Increased PA volume, higher denial rates, burdensome appeals, repeat authorizations for stable patients, step therapy harm, inadequate urgent pathways, and fragmented standards across markets.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed access to critical therapies, complications, avoidable utilization, opioid substitution risk in acute pain, irreversible disease progression, treatment abandonment, and greater provider/patient burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Current appeals processes are manual; ERISA commercial plans are outside the rule; annual PA cycles do not fit certain therapies; automated workflows may scale denials faster than appeals.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics provided.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Gene therapies and one-time curative treatments; stable long-term maintenance therapies; acute pain, infections, and rapidly progressing conditions requiring sub-24-hour decisions; cystic fibrosis patients forced through inappropriate step therapy; commercial ERISA plan misalignment.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with serious, rare, complex, or urgent conditions; providers; payers; pharmaceutical manufacturers; researchers; CMS; commercial plans; Departments of Labor and Treasury.
- **Economic implications:** Vertex argues timely access may reduce downstream utilization and total cost of care; broader PA expansion could shift burden to appeals and provider administration.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment assumes CMS's standardized electronic PA infrastructure can support reporting, appeals, step therapy continuity, and research data uses if CMS adds requirements.
- **Patient care impact:** High; the comment emphasizes timely access to medically necessary therapies and risk of irreversible harm where PA or step therapy delays occur.
