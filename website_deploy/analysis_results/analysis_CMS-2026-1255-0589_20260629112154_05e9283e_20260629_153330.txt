## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Kevin B. Kimble, Esq., Executive Director, SCLC Global Policy Initiative (SCL-GPI)
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / civil rights and policy organization
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Policy arm of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, with a six-decade civil rights history focused on Black Americans and marginalized communities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SCL-GPI supports CMS-0062-P as a needed reform to a PA system it views as slow, opaque, and inequitable. It urges CMS to finalize ePA, adopt the most aggressive operationally achievable timeline, provide technical assistance to smaller providers, require 24-hour decisions for all drugs, strengthen plain-language denial notices, publish meaningful PA metrics, and prohibit or tightly restrict step therapy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specifically cited beyond CMS-0062-P.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; APIs generally; no named FHIR IG or NCPDP standard cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization infrastructure**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Paper- and fax-based PA is a structural barrier that falls hardest on safety-net hospitals, FQHCs, and independent Black-owned practices.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA requirements on the most aggressive timeline that is operationally achievable and invest in technical assistance for smaller providers.

**Topic 2: Coverage determination timelines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A 72-hour non-urgent drug timeframe remains inadequate for serious chronic or acute conditions in communities where delays escalate into emergencies.
- **Specific request:** Require drug PA decisions within 24 hours for all drugs, not only urgent requests.

**Topic 3: Denial oversight and plain-language justifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen
- **Rationale:** Fast timelines without accountability may produce faster denials, and generic denials undermine appeal rights.
- **Specific request:** Establish rigorous oversight for elevated denial rates; require comprehensive, specific, plain-language justification for every denial, including initial denials, simultaneously to provider and patient.

**Topic 4: Payer metrics and public reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** API usage metrics alone do not show whether patients receive treatment, and aggregated data obscures disparities.
- **Specific request:** Pair API reporting with denial rates, appeal approvals, and time from PA request to treatment; compile annual public reporting; require Part D public reporting at plan level and by brand/generic, therapeutic area, initial denials, and step therapy.

**Topic 5: Step therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose / modify
- **Rationale:** Fail-first requirements delay effective care and compound inequitable burdens.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit MA plans from using step therapy; collect and release detailed data on MA PA for Part B drugs; require plans to honor prior step therapy completion across plan changes; require transparent disclosure, robust exceptions, and accessible appeals.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14:** Supports ePA requirements across medical and pharmacy benefits with technical assistance for smaller providers.
- **S16/S17:** Requests comprehensive, specific, plain-language denial reasons for every denial, including initial denials.
- **S20/S43:** Requests 24-hour decisions for all drug PA requests.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Requests meaningful public reporting that includes denial rates, appeal success, and time to treatment rather than API usage alone.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy topics by urging prohibition in MA, public data on Part B drug PA, portability of completed steps, clear disclosures, and robust appeals/exceptions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for medications across both medical and pharmacy benefits.
2. Implement requirements on the most aggressive timeline that is operationally achievable.
3. Provide meaningful technical assistance for smaller providers.
4. Require 24-hour decisions for all drug PA requests.
5. Monitor expedited timelines for denial-rate abuse.
6. Require specific, comprehensive, plain-language denial explanations to provider and patient.
7. Pair API metrics with patient/provider outcome measures.
8. Publish annual public reports with plan-level Part D PA data and granular breakouts.
9. Prohibit MA step therapy or impose strong patient protections and public reporting.
10. Require step therapy completion to be honored after plan changes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Systemic inequity in PA, paper/fax barriers, excessive timelines, denial opacity, aggregated metrics, and step therapy.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed effective care, emergency escalation, inability to appeal, hidden disparities, and repeated fail-first requirements after coverage changes.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Smaller providers, safety-net hospitals, FQHCs, and independent Black-owned practices may lack resources for implementation.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Black Americans, marginalized communities, safety-net hospitals, FQHCs, independent Black-owned medical practices, patients, providers, MA enrollees, Part D enrollees, policymakers, and advocates.
- **Economic implications:** Administrative barriers and delayed treatment may escalate costs; technical assistance needed to prevent benefits accruing only to large health systems.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports aggressive but operationally achievable implementation, with provider assistance.
- **Patient care impact:** Expected positive impact from faster decisions, clear denials, and fewer step therapy delays; risk remains if faster timelines produce faster denials.

likely to serve underresourced communities: safety-net hospitals, Federally Qualified Health Centers, and 
the dwindling number of independent, Black-owned medical practices operating under sustained financial 
stress.
