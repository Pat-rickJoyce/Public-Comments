## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** David Rocha
- **Stakeholder type:** Individual

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

David Rocha strongly supports rapid migration of prior authorization, drug cost/coverage, and related payer workflows from X12, portals, flat files, CDA, PDFs, and faxes to FHIR APIs and current FHIR implementation guides. The comment urges CMS/ONC to require additional IGs, regulate delegated entities, downstream entities, payer technology vendors, and value-added resellers as execution actors, and develop a stronger national medicines terminology strategy centered on RxNorm, SNOMED CT, LOINC, NDC, and FHIR terminology services.

- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.215(a); 45 CFR 162.1302; 45 CFR 162.923(b).
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, Da Vinci CRD, DTR, PAS, CDex, PDex, PDex Drug Formulary, RTPBC, ATR List, FAST Security/Tiered OAuth, CARIN, Gravity, Bulk FHIR, USCDI/USCDI+, RxNorm, Prescribable RxNorm, RxClass, SNOMED CT, SNOMED CT US drug extension, LOINC, NDC, NCPDP, X12N 278, CDA/C-CDA, HL7 connectathons, IHE plugathons, NUVA, CPT, HCPCS, ICD-10, TEFCA.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Require additional FHIR IGs and accelerate FHIR adoption**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that HL7 testing and SDO work demonstrate readiness for ATR List, CDex, FAST Security, and related IGs, and that continued CSV, portal, PDF, fax, V2, CDA, and X12 workflows prevent computable interoperability.
- **Specific request:** Require ATR List, CDex, FAST Security/Tiered OAuth, RTPBC, and current Da Vinci/CARIN/Gravity IGs more broadly and as soon as possible.

**Topic 2: Drug ePA, RTPBC, and real-time medication information**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Real-time cost, coverage, formulary, and prior authorization status information would support patients, clinicians, PBMs, and delegated entities and reduce administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Require RTPBC rapidly, require states and delegated entities to support PDex Drug Formulary where applicable, and ensure drug ePA workflows expose real-time status.

**Topic 3: API endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Publicly reportable Endpoint resources would help make payer and delegated-entity APIs discoverable and support accountability.
- **Specific request:** Finalize endpoint reporting using FHIR Endpoint resources and appropriate use-case details.

**Topic 4: HIPAA prior authorization standards and transition from X12 to FHIR**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter believes industry should transition from X12N 278 and portal/direct-data-entry workflows to FHIR APIs to reduce administrative and clinical burden.
- **Specific request:** Adopt FHIR for prior authorization, move away from X12 for referral certification/prior authorization workflows, implement CDex quickly, and revisit the direct data entry exception at 45 CFR 162.923(b).

**Topic 5: Terminology modernization for medication ePA**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug ePA requires consistent semantic representation across payers, providers, PBMs, pharmacies, vendors, and delegated entities. RxNorm, RxClass, SNOMED CT, LOINC, NDC, CPT, HCPCS, ICD-10, and FHIR terminology services need better alignment.
- **Specific request:** Have CMS, ONC, and NLM assess RxNorm API functions for drug ePA, create a US national medicines terminology aligned with the SNOMED CT drug model, and expose terminology services through FHIR.

**Topic 6: Delegated entities, vendors, VARs, and execution-layer accountability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues current rules assume non-delegated plans and fail to account for delegated UM vendors, PBMs, specialty pharmacy administrators, FDRs, TPAs, clearinghouses, VARs, and other intermediaries that execute real-world prior authorization functions.
- **Specific request:** Treat delegated and downstream entities, payer technology vendors, clearinghouses, VARs, and third-party intermediaries as first-class FHIR execution actors subject to API and IG requirements.

**Topic 7: Urgent and rare disease drug ePA use cases**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Life-saving therapies and rare disease drugs may require faster-than-72-hour prior authorization decisions and structured capture of questionnaire, assessment scale, and clinical justification data.
- **Specific request:** Conduct connectathons for urgent medications and rare disease therapies, encode assessment scale scores with SNOMED CT or LOINC, and address intellectual property constraints around proprietary clinical forms.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require ATR List, CDex, FAST Security/Tiered OAuth, RTPBC, and relevant Da Vinci, CARIN, and Gravity IGs.
2. Transition prior authorization and referral certification workflows from X12, portals, PDFs, faxes, CDA, and flat files to FHIR APIs.
3. Require API endpoint reporting using FHIR Endpoint resources.
4. Treat delegated UM vendors, PBMs, specialty pharmacy administrators, FDRs, TPAs, clearinghouses, payer tech vendors, VARs, and third-party intermediaries as first-class FHIR execution actors.
5. Create a US national medicines terminology aligned with the SNOMED CT drug model and modernize RxNorm, RxClass, NDC, LOINC, and FHIR terminology service use.
6. Conduct connectathons for urgent medications, rare disease drug ePA, delegated entity workflows, and execution-layer interoperability.
7. Revisit the HIPAA direct data entry exception to reduce portal-based transactions.
8. Provide funding or support for smaller IPAs, CINs, ACOs, MSOs, and MCOs to upgrade to FHIR capabilities.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed access to urgent or life-saving treatments, incomplete real-time PA status visibility, vendor lock-in, continued administrative and clinical burden, and weak semantic interoperability.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Legacy portals, electronic PDFs, faxes, CSVs, spreadsheets, V2/CDA systems, non-open terminology access, proprietary assessment-scale IP, VAR training gaps, and limited resources among small entities.
