## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** DirectTrust (Scott Stuewe, President and CEO)
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor / Professional Association (ANSI-accredited SDO, non-profit 501(c)(6) alliance of 100+ member organizations)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** ANSI-accredited Standards Developing Organization; operates EHNAC-governed accreditation programs; stewards national trust frameworks, certificate policy, trust bundle governance, national provider directory, and Direct Secure Messaging; over 8 billion Direct Secure Messages exchanged through their network

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DirectTrust generally supports CMS-0062-P's direction toward FHIR-based interoperability and trust frameworks, offering targeted input in six areas where their trust community infrastructure is directly relevant. Their central theme is that standards alone do not produce interoperability—conformance testing, accreditation, and shared accountability are essential—and they advocate for CMS to explicitly recognize existing, production-tested trust framework infrastructure (including DirectTrust's own) rather than building parallel federal capacity. They also argue strongly for preserving Direct Secure Messaging as a recognized pathway for ADT event notifications alongside FHIR-based alternatives.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: FAST Security IG and Trust Communities (§ II.A.5)**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** DirectTrust operates a production-scale accredited trust community for B2B identity and authentication, with accredited CAs already issuing certificates for FAST Security IG implementers including TEFCA. Payers can leverage this existing infrastructure today.
- **Specific request:** CMS should explicitly recognize accredited trust communities including DirectTrust as available B2B authentication infrastructure for payer API implementations; reference NIST 800-63 as minimum identity assurance baseline; create a trust community recognition framework so conformance can be demonstrated through accredited trust community participation rather than ad hoc payer-by-payer onboarding.

**Topic 2: Payer API Endpoint Reporting / NDH IG Alignment (§ II.E)**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support (alternative proposal)
- **Rationale:** DirectTrust operates the first NDH-compliant national provider directory and has a decade of experience with endpoint discovery. Identity-bound, lifecycle-governed, federated endpoint directories are more secure and sustainable than centralized registries; without verified identity, endpoint registries create attack surfaces.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the NDH IG alternative proposal; require verified organizational identity (NIST 800-63) as precondition for endpoint registration; develop the endpoint registry through federation with existing accredited directory operators rather than building parallel centralized infrastructure.

**Topic 3: HIPAA FHIR Adoption for PA and Eligibility, Including CDex (§ II.H.10)**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** FHIR-based standards adoption aligns with the broader interoperability direction; however, C-CDA and FHIR will remain complementary during transition. CDex attachment workflows can also be supported via Direct Secure Messaging as a transport, allowing organizations across the technical maturity spectrum to participate.
- **Specific request:** Preserve C-CDA interoperability during the transition period; anchor CDex implementation expectations to recognized trust framework infrastructure; recognize Direct Secure Messaging as an available transport for CDex solicited/unsolicited attachment exchange.

**Topic 4: Electronic Event (ADT) Notifications (§ III.A RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (current framework) / Modify (expansion approach)
- **Rationale:** Direct Secure Messaging is the nationally deployed transport for ADT notifications today, with documented Medicaid savings (~$8.7M in Texas in 2024). Mandating a single transport before national maturity would orphan working infrastructure and burden rural, behavioral health, and post-acute providers. FHIR-based notifications (Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG) are a complementary future-state pathway, not yet at scale.
- **Specific request:** Affirm Direct Secure Messaging as a recognized ADT notification pathway; recognize DirectTrust Event Notifications via Direct IG as an implementation baseline; position Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG as complementary future-state; maintain transport-neutral, payload-flexible approach as recipient scope expands.

**Topic 5: Health Care Resiliency (§ III.B RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Accredited point-to-point trust frameworks using X.509 certificates provide cryptographic mutual authentication and distributed architecture that avoids concentration risk. Identity assurance addresses root causes of most healthcare breach vectors. DirectTrust/EHNAC accreditation provides ongoing accountability complementary to HIPAA.
- **Specific request:** Recognize accredited point-to-point trust frameworks as foundational to federal health care resiliency policy; reference NIST 800-63 for cross-organizational ePHI movement; recognize independent accreditation as a complementary cybersecurity accountability mechanism; adopt a portfolio approach valuing architectural diversity.

**Topic 6: Improving Payer API Implementation (§ III.C RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Stakeholder reports of non-conformant payer APIs are consistent with DirectTrust member experience. Existing accreditation infrastructure (DirectTrust/EHNAC) is a mature, tested model. Expanding ONC certification to payer APIs would create cross-rule tension given ONC's signaled contraction of the certification program under HTI-5.
- **Specific request:** Adopt tiered accountability model recognizing accreditation as one evidence pathway alongside direct CMS testing; require publication of conformance testing artifacts, sandbox availability, and version transparency; coordinate with ONC for consistency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** DirectTrust supports FAST Security IG recommendation and calls for explicit CMS recognition of accredited trust communities as implementation pathways; does not directly address require vs. recommend framing but supports the IG's adoption.
- **S23:** DirectTrust specifically addresses endpoint registry security, noting that endpoints registered without identity verification create attack surfaces for impersonation and data exfiltration; recommends NIST 800-63 verified identity as precondition.
- **S24:** Supports NDH IG alignment for endpoint reporting; recommends federated model building on existing accredited directory operators rather than duplicative centralized infrastructure.
- **S44:** Supports adoption of FHIR-based standards including CDex for HIPAA PA and eligibility transactions, with three specific observations on C-CDA transition, trust framework layering, and Direct Secure Messaging as a CDex transport.
- **S66/S67:** Advocates for transport-neutral ADT expansion, affirming Direct Secure Messaging with documented outcomes ($8.7M Medicaid savings in Texas 2024); positions Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG as future-state complement.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Explicitly recognize accredited trust communities (including DirectTrust) as available B2B authentication and authorization infrastructure for payer API implementations consistent with the FAST Security IG.
2. Reference NIST 800-63 as the minimum identity assurance baseline for B2B connections involving PHI.
3. Create a trust community recognition framework so FAST Security IG conformance can be demonstrated through accredited community participation rather than ad hoc payer-by-payer onboarding.
4. Finalize the NDH IG alternative proposal for payer API endpoint reporting.
5. Require verified organizational identity (NIST 800-63) as a precondition for endpoint registration in CMS-recognized directories.
6. Develop the endpoint registry through federation with existing accredited directory operators rather than building parallel centralized infrastructure.
7. Finalize FHIR-based standards for PA and eligibility transactions while explicitly preserving C-CDA interoperability during the transition period.
8. Anchor CDex and related IG implementation expectations to recognized trust framework infrastructure including FAST Security IG and accredited trust communities.
9. Encourage Direct Secure Messaging as an available transport for CDex solicited and unsolicited attachment exchange during the transition.
10. Affirm Direct Secure Messaging (under DirectTrust trust framework) as a recognized pathway for ADT notifications under existing CoP and any expanded scope.
11. Recognize the DirectTrust Event Notifications via Direct IG as an established implementation baseline.
12. Position Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG as a complementary future-state pathway; maintain transport-neutral, payload-flexible approach as recipient scope is expanded.
13. Recognize accredited, identity-verified point-to-point trust frameworks as a foundational element of federal health care resiliency policy.
14. Recognize independent accreditation (DirectTrust/EHNAC) as a complementary cybersecurity accountability mechanism strengthening the HIPAA baseline.
15. Adopt a portfolio approach to resiliency explicitly valuing architectural diversity including distributed trust models.
16. Adopt a tiered accountability model for payer API conformance that recognizes third-party accreditation as one evidence pathway alongside direct CMS testing.
17. Require publication of conformance testing artifacts, sandbox availability, and version transparency from impacted payers.
18. Coordinate with ONC to ensure consistency across federal policy on payer API accountability.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Risk that CMS will build duplicative federal infrastructure instead of recognizing existing, production-tested trust framework infrastructure; risk of mandating a single transport (FHIR-only) for ADT notifications before national maturity, orphaning working Direct Secure Messaging infrastructure; cross-rule tension if ONC certification is expanded to payer APIs while HTI-5 signals contraction.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Mandating a single transport for ADT notifications before mature alternatives exist would shift burden to rural, behavioral health, post-acute, and social service environments where Direct is often the only available secure electronic transport; endpoints registered without identity verification create attack surfaces for impersonation and data exfiltration.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** C-CDA and FHIR will remain complementary during the transition period; CDex presumes trust framework infrastructure that is not always made explicit; cross-organizational FHIR push between unaffiliated entities still requires a trust framework.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Over 8 billion Direct Secure Messages exchanged through DirectTrust network; Texas Health Services Authority 2025 Annual Report documents ~$8,679,116 in 2024 Medicaid savings attributable to HIETexas EDEN (ADT via Direct) participants relative to non-participants; fewer potentially preventable hospital readmissions and ED visits among participants.
- **Studies or research referenced:** THSA 2025 Annual Report (pages 9-10); Texas Healthcare Learning Collaborative Medicaid data analysis.
- **Examples provided:** DirectTrust CAs already issuing certificates for FAST Security IG implementers in TEFCA; DirectTrust as first NDH-compliant national provider directory; NCPDP alignment with Direct as a transport for pharmacy-directed ADT notifications.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Impacted payers (MA, Medicaid, QHP issuers), healthcare providers (especially rural, behavioral health, post-acute), health IT vendors, TEFCA QHINs, Certificate Authorities, pharmacies, EMS, social service organizations, payers receiving ADT notifications.
- **Economic implications:** Recognition of existing accredited infrastructure avoids duplicative federal capacity costs; DirectTrust trust framework provides cost-effective, already-deployed B2B authentication; Medicaid savings (~$8.7M in Texas alone from ADT notifications via Direct) support continued investment in Direct-based infrastructure.
- **Technical feasibility:** DirectTrust argues its infrastructure is production-ready and nationally deployed; FHIR-based alternatives (Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications) lack the cross-organizational deployment, identity-bound trust framework recognition, and recipient ecosystem reach of Direct today.
- **Patient care impact:** ADT notifications via Direct have demonstrated measurable reductions in potentially preventable hospital readmissions and ED visits; expanding recipient scope (ACOs, payers, EMS, behavioral health, social services, pharmacies) would extend these benefits; preserving transport-neutral approach protects care coordination in underserved environments.
