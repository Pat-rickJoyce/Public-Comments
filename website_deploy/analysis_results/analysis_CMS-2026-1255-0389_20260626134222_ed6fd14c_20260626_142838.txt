## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Veradigm supports the goals of advancing electronic prior authorization and reducing administrative burden but raises significant technical feasibility and implementation timeline concerns as a major EHR vendor. Key issues include: the bifurcated FHIR/NCPDP pathway creates unworkable workflow complexity for EHR developers; existing Da Vinci IGs were not designed for drug PA and need clarification; the three-month gap between payer compliance date and ONC certification is operationally infeasible; third-party aggregator dependencies are unaddressed; and the cumulative regulatory burden of overlapping requirements is unsustainable. Veradigm urges extended timelines, consistent standards across all entities, and phased implementation.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Bifurcated FHIR/NCPDP Drug PA Pathway Creates Unworkable Workflow Complexity**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose (as designed); Modify
- **Rationale:** EHR vendors cannot reliably automate determination of whether a drug is pharmacy benefit vs. medical benefit at the point of prescribing without additional payer-supplied signals. Coverage categorization varies plan by plan and can change mid-year.
- **Specific request:** (1) Acknowledge in final rule that determination cannot be automated without payer-supplied signals; (2) explore whether CRD could serve as routing mechanism; (3) solicit HL7 Da Vinci community input on IG modifications needed for drug-specific workflows.

**Topic 2: Existing Da Vinci IGs Were Not Designed for Drug PA Under Medical Benefit**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Oppose (CMS's characterization); Clarify
- **Rationale:** CMS states adding drugs covered under medical benefit "requires no change to the existing standards" — Veradigm disagrees. CRD, DTR, and PAS IGs use CPT codes, SNOMED-CT, and procedure-based coverage logic. Drug PA introduces NDC codes, drug-specific formulary logic, and coverage indicators not in the Da Vinci workflow profiles.
- **Specific request:** Clarify whether existing IGs are sufficient; condition medical benefit drug requirements on publication of drug-specific implementation guidance.

**Topic 3: NCPDP Compliance Timeline – Three-Month Gap Is Infeasible**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE
- **Position:** Oppose (current timeline); Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer compliance Oct 1, 2027; ONC certification Jan 1, 2028 — only three months later. EHR vendors must identify endpoints, build/validate integrations, test end-to-end flows, deliver certified software, and train providers. Three months is wholly inadequate.
- **Specific request:** Move payer compliance to April 1, 2027 (nine-month lead), or explicitly acknowledge Jan 1, 2028 ONC cert is a minimum threshold with a reasonable ramp-up period.

**Topic 4: Third-Party Aggregator Dependency Is Unaddressed**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, VENDOR-ECOSYSTEM
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** EHR vendors work through third-party aggregators (Surescripts, Optum, Change Healthcare, Doctor First) for NCPDP connectivity. Aggregator compliance is not within EHR vendor control. Requires platform updates, new/amended contracts, testing, and revenue-sharing renegotiation.
- **Specific request:** Acknowledge aggregators as a critical, regulated-adjacent ecosystem component; consider regulatory guidance or safe harbors for aggregator readiness.

**Topic 5: F&B Flat File Update Frequency – Bi-Weekly Minimum Required**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Monthly updates insufficient; PBMs adjust pricing, tiering, and formulary frequently. Stale data causes incorrect cost information, formulary substitution errors, and unnecessary PA requests.
- **Specific request:** Require F&B flat file updates at minimum bi-weekly; PA requirement indicators updated on same schedule; no material lag between effective policy change and publication.

**Topic 6: F&B and RTPB Data Conflict – Single Source of Truth Needed**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Overlapping benefit indicators in both F&B and RTPB create data conflicts EHR must resolve at point of prescribing; without regulatory designation, inconsistent product-level design decisions create variation in prescriber experience.
- **Specific request:** Designate F&B as primary source of truth for benefit/coverage information (including PA requirements); RTPB carries pricing/cost-sharing. Require consistency and synchronization between F&B and RTPB indicators.

**Topic 7: Provider Access API for Drug PA Status Data**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with condition
- **Rationale:** Provider Access API appropriate for PA status retrieval outside real-time prescribing workflow. But ONC has not finalized certification criteria for Provider Access API.
- **Specific request:** Condition full implementation on finalization of ONC certification criteria; coordinate ONC/CMS alignment on timelines.

**Topic 8: Explicit Standards, Certification, and Governance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Explicit standards listing reduces ambiguity and implementation inconsistencies.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly list all required standards (FHIR, US Core, SMART App Launch, Bulk Data, OpenID Connect). Recommend ONC certification for payer APIs and delegated vendors.

**Topic 9: ATR List IG and API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** ATR List IG addresses patient attribution challenges.
- **Specific request:** Strongly support ATR List IG adoption. Require structured endpoint documentation linking endpoints to specific plans.

**Topic 10: PA Transparency, Timeliness, and Data Access**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with caveats
- **Rationale:** Electronic denial reasons improve provider efficiency and transparency; timely responses important but overly prescriptive requirements should be avoided given regulatory variability.
- **Specific request:** Support all PA data via APIs (status, dates, denial reasons, documentation). Encourage unified PA framework across drugs and non-drug services.

**Topic 11: Cumulative Regulatory Burden**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Five overlapping regulatory initiatives simultaneously (FHIR ePA APIs, NCPDP SCRIPT 2023011, NCPDP RTPB/F&B, HIPAA Claims Attachments, ONC HTI-4 criteria) draw on same engineering and compliance resources and technically interact.
- **Specific request:** Acknowledge cumulative burden in final rule preamble; extend payer compliance dates; coordinate with ONC on certification criteria lead time; establish phased incremental go-live.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports requiring Da Vinci IGs but urges clarification that IGs may need modification for drug PA use cases.
- **S2/S3:** Recommends concurrent use of current and updated IG versions through Jan 1, 2028; requires sufficiently tested and stable standards.
- **S12:** CRD/DTR/PAS designed for procedure-based workflows; drug PA under medical benefit introduces NDC codes and formulary logic not currently in Da Vinci profiles; requests IG modification or supplemental guidance.
- **S14/S22:** Payer compliance Oct 1, 2027 inadequate given ONC certification three months later; recommends April 1, 2027 or acknowledged ramp-up period.
- **S15:** Highlights fundamental workflow complexity of benefit pathway determination; recommends CRD as routing mechanism.
- **S19/S25:** Aggregator dependency is critical, unaddressed; recommends regulatory guidance or safe harbors.
- **S21:** Supports 2027 timeline goals but requires adjustments for feasibility; recommends phased approach.
- **S23/S24:** Supports endpoint reporting; recommends structured plan-linked documentation.
- **S37:** Cumulative regulatory burden is major implementation challenge; recommends explicit acknowledgment in final rule.
- **S60/S61:** Recommends explicit standards listing and ONC certification for payer APIs.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Acknowledge in final rule that pharmacy vs. medical benefit determination cannot be automated without payer-supplied signals; explore CRD as routing mechanism.
2. Clarify whether existing Da Vinci IGs accommodate drug PA without modification; condition requirements on drug-specific IG guidance.
3. Move NCPDP payer compliance to April 1, 2027, or acknowledge January 1, 2028 ONC cert is minimum with ramp-up period.
4. Acknowledge aggregators as critical NCPDP ecosystem component; consider regulatory guidance or safe harbors.
5. Require F&B flat file updates at minimum bi-weekly; include PA requirement indicators on same schedule.
6. Designate F&B as primary source of truth for benefit/coverage; require RTPB/F&B synchronization.
7. Condition drug PA status via Provider Access API on ONC certification criteria finalization; align ONC/CMS timelines.
8. Explicitly list all required standards; recommend ONC certification for payer APIs.
9. Support ATR List IG adoption; require structured endpoint documentation.
10. Allow concurrent use of current and updated IG versions through January 1, 2028.
11. Require all regulated entities to adopt same standards, versions, and timelines.
12. Acknowledge cumulative regulatory burden in final rule preamble.
13. Establish phased implementation timeline for incremental go-live.
14. Pursue unified PA framework across drugs and non-drug services.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Bifurcated FHIR/NCPDP pathway creates unresolvable workflow complexity; Da Vinci IGs insufficient for drug PA without modification; three-month gap between payer compliance and ONC certification is infeasible; aggregator dependency unaddressed; cumulative overlapping requirements unsustainable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Stale F&B data causes incorrect cost information, formulary substitution errors, and unnecessary PA requests; F&B/RTPB data conflicts require inconsistent EHR-level resolutions; vendors cannot build against undefined standards.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer-by-payer benefit categorization variation; mid-year formulary changes; aggregator contract renegotiation; EHR vendor resource conflicts across five overlapping initiatives.
