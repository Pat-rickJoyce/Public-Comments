## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** COPD Action Alliance (CAA), signed by Sarah Hoffman, Senior Director.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Coalition of nearly thirty member organizations representing COPD patients, respiratory care providers, and affected communities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CAA supports CMS's proposed interoperability and prior authorization rule, especially extension of ePA to drugs, clearer decision timelines, and public reporting. It urges CMS to ensure modernization means more than faster electronic denials by adding patient protections for COPD: expedited reviews, continuity of therapy, safeguards against timeline resets, plain-language transparency, and strong step therapy guardrails.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed.
- **Standards referenced:** General interoperability/electronic prior authorization; no specific FHIR IG, NCPDP standard, or CFR provision cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization for COPD medications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** COPD treatment often involves long-acting bronchodilators, inhaled corticosteroids, combination inhalers, pulmonary rehabilitation, and biologics; delays can increase exacerbation risk and cause permanent loss of lung function.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA expansion and ensure the framework works coherently across pharmacy and medical benefit pathways for complex COPD care.

**Topic 2: Decision timelines and expedited review**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Interruption of treatment can create rapid clinical deterioration in COPD, and plans may use repeated documentation requests to suspend or reset timelines.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited review pathways for high-risk interruptions and close loopholes that allow plans to reset or suspend decision clocks through administrative barriers.

**Topic 3: Continuity of care for stable patients**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Stable COPD regimens can take months to establish and can be disrupted by formulary changes, new PA requirements, plan transitions, aging into Medicare, moves, or job changes.
- **Specific request:** Require stable patients to retain access during transitions and pending appeals; give meaningful weight to prior approvals when patients move between plans; protect patients from reauthorization for already-approved therapies.

**Topic 4: Denial and status transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support and expand.
- **Rationale:** Uncertainty about coverage affects adherence, daily planning, and mental well-being; clinicians should not learn about formulary or UM changes only after a patient is turned away.
- **Specific request:** Provide plain-language status updates, clear denial explanations, appeal instructions, timely electronic notice to in-network providers of coverage policy changes, and public reporting usable by patients during open enrollment.

**Topic 5: Step therapy reform**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Fail-first protocols are clinically risky in COPD because inadequate treatment can cause exacerbations and permanent lung function loss, and some protocols conflict with GOLD 2026 individualized care guidance.
- **Specific request:** Recognize prior failures across health plans, require accessible clinician-deferential exceptions with urgent fast tracks, cap required steps, disclose requirements in plain language during open enrollment, and require protocols aligned with specialty guidance and reviewed by pulmonary clinicians.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Responds indirectly by noting COPD therapies span multiple classes and benefit pathways and require workflows that do not disrupt timely clinician-directed treatment.
- **S15:** Requests coherent operation across pharmacy and medical benefit pathways for COPD and comorbidity management.
- **S16/S17:** Supports plain-language denial explanations and clear appeal rights/instructions.
- **S20/S43:** Supports clearer decision timelines but asks CMS to prevent plans from resetting/suspending clocks and to create expedited pathways for rapid deterioration risk.
- **S29/S35:** Recommends public reporting that enables real comparisons across plans, including therapeutic category metrics, appeal overturns, timelines, reauthorization frequency, and step therapy prevalence.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the step therapy RFI with COPD-specific guardrails, continuity protections, prior-failure recognition, exceptions processes, and specialty-guideline alignment.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize extension of electronic prior authorization requirements to drugs.
2. Establish expedited review pathways when treatment interruption risks rapid clinical deterioration.
3. Prevent plans from suspending or resetting decision timelines through repeated documentation requests.
4. Protect patients from re-obtaining authorization for therapies already approved.
5. Ensure PA rules account for COPD comorbidities and operate coherently across pharmacy and medical benefit pathways.
6. Treat continuity of care as a core patient protection during plan transitions and appeals.
7. Require plain-language status updates, denial explanations, and appeal instructions.
8. Require timely electronic provider notice when coverage policies change.
9. Publish comparable PA metrics by therapeutic category, including denial/approval rates, appeal overturn rates, decision timelines, reauthorization frequency, and step therapy prevalence.
10. Require step therapy recognition of prior treatment failures across health plans.
11. Require accessible, urgent exceptions processes that defer to treating clinicians.
12. Cap the number of required step therapy steps.
13. Require plain-language step therapy disclosure during open enrollment.
14. Require step therapy protocols to be publicly posted, regularly updated, aligned with specialty guidance, and reviewed by pulmonary experts.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Digitization without substantive reform; unjustified denials; delayed COPD treatment; fail-first requirements; coverage disruptions for stable patients; inadequate public metrics.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Exacerbations, hospitalizations, accelerated disease progression, treatment abandonment, serious adverse events, mental stress, and irreversible loss of lung function.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative documentation cycles, payer coverage policy changes, formulary changes, plan transitions, benefit pathway fragmentation, and lack of patient-facing transparency.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** COPD affects an estimated 30 million Americans; it is the fifth leading cause of death and a major cause of admissions and ED visits; a 2025 AMA survey found 95% of physicians report PA delays care, 79% say it can lead to treatment abandonment, and 26% report serious adverse events; 79% of physicians in an AJMC survey identified step therapy as a significant barrier.
- **Studies or research referenced:** NCHS Data Brief on COPD; 2025 AMA Prior Authorization Physician Survey; AJMC article on utilization management and physician burnout; GOLD 2026 Report referenced for COPD treatment guidance.
- **Examples provided:** COPD patients requiring bronchodilators, inhaled corticosteroids, combination inhalers, biologics, pulmonary rehabilitation, and stable regimens disrupted by formulary or plan changes.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** COPD patients, respiratory clinicians, providers managing pulmonary and cardiovascular comorbidities, health plans, and patient advocacy organizations.
- **Economic implications:** The comment does not quantify implementation costs, but emphasizes avoidable hospitalizations, ED use, and treatment disruption consequences.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports electronic PA, but stresses that electronic transmission must be paired with substantive patient protections and meaningful metrics.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly focused on preventing delays, exacerbations, hospitalizations, irreversible lung function loss, and treatment abandonment.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS
