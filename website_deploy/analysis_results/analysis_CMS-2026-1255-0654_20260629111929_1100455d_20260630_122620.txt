# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** ChenMed; contact Lisa Ard, Managing Director of Market Insights and External Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Value-based primary care organization serving high-risk Medicare beneficiaries, including dually eligible and chronically ill seniors; takes full risk for total cost of care and outcomes.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ChenMed responds to the rule's event notification RFI by asking CMS to make hospital event notifications more clinically useful, timely, routable, and measurable. It recommends mandatory clinical payloads, real-time attribution verification, explicit inclusion of ACOs/MSOs/risk-bearing value-based entities as recipients, ONC certification support, and outcome-based enforcement metrics.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFIs; related to ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE for certification coordination, but not the specific 45 CFR 170.215 version updates.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations mentioned directly; references hospital Conditions of Participation, Medicare Promoting Interoperability Program, HIPAA TPO, and TEFCA.
- **Standards referenced:** ADT notifications, ICD-10, FHIR APIs, FHIR Subscriptions, TEFCA/QHINs, certified EHR functionality, interoperable provider directories.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Minimum clinical payload for event notifications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Minimal ADT pings are too late, incomplete, and misrouted to support safe transitions or avoidable readmission prevention.
- **Specific request:** Require reconciled medications, ICD-10 diagnoses, pending results with responsible provider, disposition, and attending contact information in every ADT/discharge notification.

**Topic 2: Real-time attribution and provider identity verification**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Notifications often fail because hospitals cannot identify the accountable PCP, ACO, MSO, or risk-bearing entity at the point of care.
- **Specific request:** Replace reasonable-effort language with mandatory, auditable PCP and attributed-entity verification at ED registration, inpatient admission, and discharge.

**Topic 3: Required recipients for value-based care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Risk-bearing value-based care organizations are clinically and financially accountable for patients but lack a uniform route to receive hospital alerts.
- **Specific request:** Add ACOs, MSOs, full-risk providers, and other documented value-based entities as required notification recipients under hospital CoPs.

**Topic 4: Certification, PI measures, and enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Current requirements focus on technical capability rather than whether useful notifications reach the right recipient quickly.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with ONC on certification criteria and add PI/outcome metrics for delivery confirmation, attribution completeness, and clinical content completeness.

**Topic 5: TEFCA and directory-enabled routing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify.
- **Rationale:** TEFCA and a national provider directory could simplify routing, but smaller VBC organizations may face legal, technical, and cost barriers.
- **Specific request:** Create a streamlined TEFCA participation pathway or ACO participant tier and fund connectivity grants for VBC and community-based providers.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** ChenMed responds extensively to the event notification RFI. It recommends richer notification content, mandatory attribution verification, broader recipient rules for value-based entities and PAC providers, certification criteria, TEFCA support, and outcome-based compliance metrics.
- **S9:** The comment indirectly references FHIR Subscriptions as a possible acknowledgment mechanism for notification delivery confirmation, but it does not address the Subscriptions backport proposal itself.
- **S60-S65:** The comment indirectly urges CMS/ONC certification alignment for event notification functionality, though it does not address the specific ONC standards update versioning questions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require a minimum clinical payload in hospital ADT and discharge notifications.
2. Require pending results to be transmitted to the identified PCP or attributed organization within 24 hours of finalization.
3. Require real-time PCP and attributed-entity verification at registration, admission, and discharge.
4. Add ACOs, MSOs, full-risk providers, and other risk-bearing value-based entities as required notification recipients.
5. Clarify that notifications to attributed PCPs and VBC entities are permitted under HIPAA TPO without additional patient authorization.
6. Define ONC certification criteria for subscription-based alerts, endpoint registration, delivery acknowledgment, and reporting.
7. Add PI measures for Notification Delivery Confirmation Rate, Attribution Completeness Rate, and Clinical Content Completeness Rate.
8. Use TEFCA and provider directories to route alerts, with simplified onboarding and grants for smaller VBC/community providers.
9. Provide CMS guidance, blueprints, technical assistance, and enforcement tied to measurable outcomes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Late or incomplete notifications, misrouting, lack of attribution data, unclear authorization rules, and capability-only compliance.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Unsafe care transitions, preventable readmissions, manual workarounds, alert fatigue, privacy concerns from indiscriminate distribution, and reduced effectiveness of value-based care.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Heterogeneous hospital EHRs, legacy interfaces, customized workflows, fragmented attribution files, uncertain HIPAA interpretations, limited PAC connectivity, TEFCA onboarding burden, and missing certified directory/query functionality.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Proposed targets of at least 90 percent delivery confirmation, at least 90 percent attribution completeness, and 100 percent clinical content completeness; examples of 48-72 hour delays for PAC discharge information.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None cited.
- **Examples provided:** HIE feeds, point-to-point interfaces, payer alerting systems, faxed discharge summaries, manual calls, pending lab/imaging/culture results, SNF/home health handoffs, and batch payer attribution rosters.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** High-risk Medicare beneficiaries, dually eligible and chronically ill seniors, PCPs, hospitals, ACOs, MSOs, full-risk providers, payers, PAC providers, behavioral health providers, pharmacies, EMS, social service providers, EHR vendors, HIEs, and QHINs.
- **Economic implications:** More reliable notifications may reduce avoidable readmissions and support risk-bearing entities; implementation may require EHR configuration, interface updates, TEFCA onboarding, and technical assistance funding.
- **Technical feasibility:** ChenMed views richer notifications as feasible if data is auto-populated from structured EHR fields and supported by certification, FHIR APIs, directories, and event-driven workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Timely, complete notifications would improve transitions, medication reconciliation, pending-result follow-up, PAC admissions, and care coordination.
