## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Talbert House Health Center dba Centerpoint Health; signed by Shonda E. Wallace, Chief Executive Officer
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider / Other (Federally Qualified Health Center and safety-net provider)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Centerpoint Health is a Section 330-funded nonprofit community health center and FQHC serving underserved residents in Warren and Butler Counties, Ohio. It reports that 95.88% of patients have incomes below 200% FPL, more than 70% are enrolled in Medicaid or managed Medicaid, and it operates with limited internal IT capacity.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Centerpoint Health broadly supports CMS's interoperability and prescription drug ePA proposals because they could reduce administrative burden and improve medication access for medically underserved patients. The commenter asks CMS to add practical safeguards: realistic timelines, vendor accountability, cybersecurity and funding support, benefit-classification transparency, 340B/pharmacy workflow coordination, centralized endpoint publication, expanded API metrics, retention of formulary information in APIs, HIPAA/X12-to-FHIR transition support, CDex limits, gold-carding, and CMS-ONC harmonization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS/federal matching support; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.315(g)(31)-(g)(33); 90 FR 37130; CMS-0053-F; CMS-0062-P; February 28, 2024 CMS National Standards Group X12 278 enforcement discretion.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, X12 278, X12 6020, FHIR/CDex, C-CDA, PDF/text/FHIR resource attachments, HL7 Da Vinci CRD/DTR/PAS IGs v2.2, CRD Coverage Information extension, Provider Access API, Patient Access API, Payer-to-Payer API, Prior Authorization API, ONC HTI-4 and HTI-5 certification criteria.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API interoperability requirements for safety-net providers**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs could reduce duplicative documentation, improve visibility into PA requirements, and streamline care coordination for CHC patients, but CHCs lack internal IT capacity and depend on vendors.
- **Specific request:** Pair API requirements with realistic timelines, coordinated national standards, vendor accountability, readiness testing, and technical/funding support for CHCs.

**Topic 2: Prescription drug ePA and benefit classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** ePA can improve point-of-prescribing visibility and medication adherence, but CHCs may face dual medical/pharmacy benefit workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification through Provider Access APIs using the CRD Coverage Information extension; clarify whether one PA approval applies across medical and pharmacy benefit pathways or whether separate determinations are required.

**Topic 3: Pharmacy, 340B, and operational coordination**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Prescription drug ePA must operate across EHRs, pharmacy management systems, TPAs, contract pharmacies, payer APIs, and 340B accumulation workflows, many of which are not fully interoperable.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with the Office of Pharmacy Affairs and provide technical assistance, guidance, and funding support for CHCs implementing drug ePA.

**Topic 4: PA expiration alerts and PA reduction**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ePA alone may accelerate an inefficient process if PA volume and step therapy complexity remain high.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts; pair ePA mandates with PA reduction requirements, gold-carding for prescription drugs, and sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 5: Communications, denial reasons, and decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Uniform timelines and actionable denial explanations are critical for underserved patients and resource-constrained CHCs.
- **Specific request:** Finalize 24-hour outpatient drug PA decisions, standard and expedited timeframe alignment, specific denial reasons, centralized public PA metrics, stronger oversight, and automatic approval when payers miss required deadlines.

**Topic 6: API endpoint reporting and publication**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Centralized endpoint information reduces the burden of manually searching payer websites and helps providers, EHR vendors, and app developers avoid disruptions from outdated endpoints.
- **Specific request:** Publish endpoint information on CMS's website in machine-readable files, require updates within one week of changes, require at least annual verification, and prioritize ease of access for safety-net and rural providers.

**Topic 7: API usage metrics and PA information in APIs**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Metrics can improve transparency, accountability, and targeting of access-gap interventions.
- **Specific request:** Report Medicaid/CHIP managed care Patient Access API metrics by program and plan; expand metrics to denial rates, extensions, appeals, successful API requests, authorization failures, malformed queries, missed calls, structured versus unstructured responses, workflow impact, calls per query, queries per user, and API maintenance costs.

**Topic 8: Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose removal / Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer websites are not reliable real-time sources, PBM formulary exclusions change quickly, and CHCs lack staff to perform manual lookups across many payers.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 9: HIPAA PA standards transition and CDex guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Moving from X12 to FHIR is beneficial, but CHCs and clearinghouses will face a dual-standard burden during the transition.
- **Specific request:** Provide transition support, extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion for FHIR PA API use as an X12 278 substitute through the CMS-0062-P transition, support CDex for PA attachments, require all needed documentation in the initial CDex request, limit solicited attachment requests to two per PA transaction, and set maximum payer response times after CDex attachment receipt.

**Topic 10: ONC standards harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS-0062-P payer deadlines may precede availability of certified provider-facing technology, and HTI-5 changes could remove supporting certification capabilities needed for ePA workflows.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC should issue joint guidance, ensure HTI-5 does not undercut CMS-0062-P, publish a CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before the final rule, and clarify that providers are not non-compliant when certified technology does not yet exist.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize API and drug ePA requirements with realistic timelines and safety-net implementation support.
2. Clarify providers will not be penalized for failures caused by vendors, clearinghouses, or EHR systems outside provider control.
3. Coordinate CMS timelines with ONC certification and require readiness validation before enforcement.
4. Standardize PA data fields, IG timelines, and operational requirements across Medicaid MCOs and QHP issuers.
5. Provide CHCs with technical assistance, cybersecurity support, IT workforce development, and dedicated health IT funding.
6. Require real-time benefit classification via Provider Access APIs using CRD Coverage Information extension.
7. Clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to another pathway for dual-covered drugs.
8. Coordinate with OPA on 340B and pharmacy operations and support EHR/PMS/payer API integration.
9. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications through FHIR-based alerts.
10. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction, prescription drug gold-carding, and sunset provisions for high-approval PA categories.
11. Finalize specific denial reason requirements and centralized public PA metrics.
12. Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
13. Publish API endpoints centrally in machine-readable files with prompt updates and annual verification.
14. Expand API metrics to include functionality, usability, errors, workflow impact, and costs.
15. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
16. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds if available.
17. Extend X12 278/FHIR PA API enforcement discretion through the transition period.
18. Add CDex guardrails: complete initial documentation requests, at most two solicited attachment requests, and maximum post-attachment payer response times.
19. Issue CMS-ONC joint guidance and a harmonization assessment before the final rule.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CHC resource constraints, vendor readiness, cybersecurity exposure, payer inconsistency, dual medical/pharmacy workflows, 340B operational complexity, PA delays, insufficient denial transparency, API usability, removal of formulary data, and timing gaps between payer mandates and certified provider technology.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Interrupted medication access, delayed treatment, avoidable hospitalizations, abandoned treatment, worsened chronic disease outcomes, increased staff burden, increased costs for CHCs, and operational disruption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** No internal IT staff, reliance on Tech Advisors, reduced referral staffing, eClinicalWorks/Pioneer non-interface, EHR/PMS limitations, terminology/value-set misalignment, competing vendor priorities, cybersecurity costs, and thin operating margins.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 95.88% of Centerpoint patients below 200% FPL; more than 70% enrolled in Medicaid/managed Medicaid; AMA survey finding 94% of physicians report PA delays patient care; average PA wait about three days with nearly one-third waiting more than one week; MA plans made nearly 53 million PA determinations in 2024 with more than four million denials; CHCs serve more than 16 million Medicaid beneficiaries, 6 million uninsured people, 20.5 million low-income people, 1.1 million agricultural workers, and 1.5 million homeless people; 2025 local data include 339 diabetes patients, more than 600 hypertension patients, and more than 100 heart disease patients; over 65% of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy arrangements; proactive PA outreach positive outcomes of 87% versus 25% without notification; nearly half of CHCs have fewer than 90 days cash on hand and one in four report negative 5% operating margins.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA PA article; KFF Medicare Advantage PA data; MGMA prior authorization landscape; HRSA UDS data; NACHC reports and prior comments; Aremu et al. on medication adherence; Pharmacy Times on pharmacy interoperability; Krauss et al. and Murthi et al. on EHR/pharmacy integration; Vishwanath et al. on proactive PA recertification outreach; Lauffenburger et al. and Salzbrenner et al. on ePA burden/medication filling; CMS WISeR and enforcement discretion materials.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine may be billed under pharmacy benefit when dispensed through a pharmacy or medical benefit when administered in clinic; Centerpoint uses eClinicalWorks while the pharmacy uses Pioneer and the systems do not interface; CHCs lack capacity to manually search every payer website for API endpoints.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHC patients, Medicaid/CHIP managed care enrollees, underserved and rural patients, homeless individuals, agricultural workers, CHC providers/staff, pharmacies, 340B contract pharmacies, TPAs, EHR/PMS vendors, payers, app developers, CMS, ONC, and state Medicaid/CHIP programs.
- **Economic implications:** Without support, CHCs may absorb unfunded IT, cybersecurity, vendor, training, workflow redesign, and PA labor costs. With support, ePA and central endpoint/metric publication could reduce manual burden and avoid costly care delays.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS coordinates payer, provider, vendor, pharmacy, ONC certification, and HIPAA transition requirements; risky if certification deadlines, vendor capabilities, and payer rollout schedules remain misaligned.
- **Patient care impact:** High. The comment links timely drug PA, formulary visibility, benefit classification, PA history retention, and proactive notifications to medication adherence, continuity of care, chronic disease management, and fewer preventable complications.
