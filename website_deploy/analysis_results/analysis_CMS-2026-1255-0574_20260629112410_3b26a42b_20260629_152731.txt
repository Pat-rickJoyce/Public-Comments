# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Cancer Society Cancer Action Network (ACS CAN); signed by Anna Schwamlein Howard, Vice President, Public Policy Advocacy
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** ACS CAN is the nonprofit, nonpartisan advocacy affiliate of the American Cancer Society and advocates for evidence-based policies to reduce the cancer burden.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ACS CAN broadly supports CMS's proposal to extend 2024 interoperability and prior authorization requirements to prescription drugs, emphasizing that cancer patients need timely access to outpatient and physician-administered therapies. The organization recommends broader applicability to Part D and state-based exchange QHPs, stronger denial specificity, shorter and aligned PA timeframes, penalties for missed timeframes, plan-level and category-level metric reporting, continued formulary data access, caregiver access clarification, and strong oversight of step therapy, AI, laboratory testing, and DMEPOS prior authorization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING indirectly; GENERAL/RFIs on step therapy and laboratory/DMEPOS
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; 91 Fed. Reg. 19890 (April 14, 2026)
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API; Patient Access API; Provider Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR); NCPDP SCRIPT standards; CARIN Consumer Real-Time Pharmacy Benefit Check (RTPBC) IG; real-time coverage information.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Real-time patient drug cost and coverage information**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PATIENT-ACCESS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Cancer patients may have multiple concurrent drugs with varying coverage rules, PA requirements, and out-of-pocket costs.
- **Specific request:** Support patient access to clear, timely, real-time drug cost and coverage information through the Patient Access API; consider CARIN RTPBC IG support.

**Topic 2: Medical-benefit drugs in the Prior Authorization API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Excluding drugs creates inconsistent requirements by drug type and payer, increasing burden and delaying cancer treatment.
- **Specific request:** Finalize inclusion of medical-benefit drugs by October 1, 2027; extend requirements to SBE/SBE-FP QHP issuers and Part D sponsors; apply the same PA standards to any drug covered by an MA plan.

**Topic 3: Pharmacy-benefit drug electronic PA standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Unified prescription drug PA requirements will reduce confusion and administrative burden while aligning with existing Part D standards.
- **Specific request:** Finalize NCPDP SCRIPT standards and related formulary/benefit and real-time coverage information requirements.

**Topic 4: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Generic denials prolong the time patients wait and prevent providers from quickly addressing issues.
- **Specific request:** Require clear, specific denial reasons for all drug PA denials and prohibit generic statements such as “failed to establish medical necessity” without additional detail.

**Topic 5: PA decision and notification timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Standardized and shorter timeframes reduce patient confusion, provider burden, and treatment delays.
- **Specific request:** Support 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug PA timeframes; require MA organizations to meet a 24-hour outpatient drug PA standard in future rulemaking; shorten non-drug service timeframes to align with drug timeframes; extend standardized timeframes to QHP issuers on SBEs and SBE-FPs; require penalties and oversight for missed timeframes.

**Topic 6: Public PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Public metrics improve transparency, but issuer-level or aggregated reporting can obscure plan variation and inappropriate PA use.
- **Specific request:** Publish metrics on CMS's website and payer websites; require plan-level reporting; report metrics by category of item or service; use the data for oversight.

**Topic 7: FF-SHOP Patient Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Small group QHP members should have consistent data access and payer-to-payer continuity, especially cancer patients with complex care.
- **Specific request:** Finalize Patient Access API and Payer-to-Payer API requirements for FF-SHOP issuers; require plain-language enrollee education and opt-out explanation.

**Topic 8: Drug PA data in Access APIs and caregiver access**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / GENERAL API updates
- **Position:** Support with clarification
- **Rationale:** Including drug PA information across APIs promotes consistency and reduces confusion.
- **Specific request:** Clarify how individuals can designate caregivers for Patient Access API information and require written communication options for enrollees who prefer them.

**Topic 9: Formulary data in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / GENERAL API updates
- **Position:** Oppose removal
- **Rationale:** Removing formulary data would force providers to search multiple sources, increasing burden and delaying care.
- **Specific request:** Reconsider proposal to remove drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 10: Step therapy and AI oversight**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / RFI Step Therapy
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy and AI-enabled utilization management can increase denials, burden, and delays for cancer patients whose treatments are tailored.
- **Specific request:** Require CMS staff review and human oversight of technological tools; conduct rigorous oversight and enforcement; require step therapy override processes; monitor embedded step therapy in PA requirements.

**Topic 11: Laboratory tests and DMEPOS PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / RFI Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Laboratory tests and DMEPOS are essential for cancer diagnosis, treatment selection, monitoring, mobility, daily functioning, and symptom management.
- **Specific request:** Hold PA for laboratory tests and DMEPOS to the same standards as other services; monitor implementation and exercise oversight when PA delays approvals.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S6 / S8 / related patient benefit IG questions:** Supports giving patients real-time drug cost and coverage information through the Patient Access API and discusses CARIN RTPBC IG value.
- **S12:** Supports incorporating medical-benefit drugs into the Prior Authorization API and says this will reduce burden and treatment delays.
- **S15:** Calls for consistent standards for all drugs and urges extension to Part D and SBE/SBE-FP QHP issuers to reduce medical/pharmacy benefit fragmentation.
- **S21:** Supports the October 1, 2027 compliance timeline for drug PA because impacted payers are already implementing non-drug requirements.
- **S28:** Urges any drug covered by an MA plan to be subject to the same PA requirements and supports Part D alignment in future rulemaking.
- **S14 / S22 / S25 / S31:** Supports NCPDP SCRIPT and pharmacy-benefit standards and formulary/real-time coverage information requirements.
- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons and asks CMS to require more detail than generic medical-necessity language.
- **S20 / S43:** Supports 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited drug PA timeframes and asks CMS to align non-drug timeframes with the shorter drug timeframes.
- **S29-S35:** Supports public PA metrics, numeric counts, plan-level reporting, category-level reporting, CMS publication, and oversight use.
- **S40-S42:** Supports FF-SHOP Patient Access and Payer-to-Payer API requirements, stating burden should be limited because many issuers already participate in FFEs.
- **S66-S82 / Step Therapy RFI:** Supports technology-enabled step therapy efficiency with caution; requests human oversight, override processes, and monitoring of embedded step therapy.
- **S66-S82 / Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS RFI:** Recommends applying the same PA standards to laboratory tests and DMEPOS and monitoring for delayed treatment approvals.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize extension of Prior Authorization API requirements to medical-benefit drugs.
2. Extend drug PA requirements to Part D sponsors and QHP issuers on SBEs and SBE-FPs.
3. Apply the same PA standards to any drug covered by an MA plan.
4. Finalize NCPDP SCRIPT and real-time pharmacy benefit/coverage information requirements.
5. Require detailed, specific denial reasons for all drug PA denials.
6. Align PA timeframes across programs and shorten non-drug service timeframes to match drug timeframes.
7. Establish penalties and enforcement for payers that miss required PA timeframes.
8. Publish PA metrics on CMS's website and payer websites at the plan level and by service category.
9. Finalize FF-SHOP Patient Access and Payer-to-Payer API requirements.
10. Clarify caregiver access under the Patient Access API and preserve written communication options.
11. Retain formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
12. Require human oversight and safeguards for AI and other UM technology.
13. Require step therapy override processes and monitor embedded step therapy.
14. Apply comparable PA standards to laboratory tests and DMEPOS and monitor payer implementation.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Drug PA delays, inconsistent standards across payer/program types, generic denial reasons, inadequate non-drug PA timeframes, insufficient enforcement, aggregation of metrics, removal of formulary data, and AI/step therapy risks.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, disease progression, provider administrative burden, patient confusion, delayed appeals, worse outcomes for cancer patients, and additional patient costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Fragmented payer scope, lack of plan-level data, lack of category-level metrics, absence of penalties, caregiver access ambiguity, and technology/AI oversight needs.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** ACS CAN's 2024 report found Part D plans required PA for CDK 4/6 inhibitors 97% of the time; embedded step therapy occurred in 23% of cases for Ibrance, 22% for Kisqali and Kisqali Femara co-pack, and 88% for Verzenio.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA article on AI and prior authorization denials; ACS CAN 2024 Step Therapy in Medicare Part D Oncology Drugs report.
- **Examples provided:** Cancer patients with multiple concurrent prescriptions; outpatient and physician-administered therapies; CDK 4/6 inhibitors; laboratory testing for cancer diagnosis and monitoring; DMEPOS for mobility and symptom management.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Cancer patients, caregivers, providers, MA organizations, Medicaid/CHIP programs, QHP issuers, Part D sponsors, FF-SHOP issuers, and payers using utilization management tools.
- **Economic implications:** The comment does not provide implementation cost estimates; it emphasizes reduced administrative burden and patient/provider costs associated with delays and fragmented data.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports the October 2027 timeline and API expansion; requests clarification and safeguards around caregiver access, written communication, formulary data, and technology oversight.
- **Patient care impact:** Very high. The comment repeatedly ties timely drug access, PA decisions, lab testing, and DMEPOS access to cancer outcomes and disease progression.
