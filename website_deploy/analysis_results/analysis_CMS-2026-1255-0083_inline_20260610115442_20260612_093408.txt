- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Board-certified neurologist and headache medicine specialist treating migraine, cluster headache, spinal fluid pressure headache disorders, and facial pain.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Dr. Nye supports faster prior authorization decisions and greater health plan transparency, emphasizing that PA and step therapy delay migraine care and consume substantial clinician time. She recommends requiring plans to align PA and step therapy criteria with the American Headache Society 2024 position statement so CGRP medications can be used as first-line options without mandatory failure of non-specific oral medications.

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed.
- **Standards referenced:** American Headache Society 2024 Position Statement; clinical literature on migraine treatment and CGRP therapies. No FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Faster PA decisions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delays in diagnosis and treatment can contribute to chronification of migraine and increased disability, cost, and lost productivity.
- **Specific request:** Finalize faster prior authorization decisions to prevent delays in therapeutic management.

**Topic 2: Plan transparency and denial burden**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Lack of transparency requires repeated therapeutic modification, patient outreach, and appeals; each denial takes at least 20 minutes of documentation per therapeutic.
- **Specific request:** Increase health plan transparency, including clarity around denials and PA performance.

**Topic 3: Alignment with headache specialty guidelines**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** AHS guidance supports CGRP-targeting therapies as first-line options, while step therapy requirements continue to mandate trials of non-specific oral medications.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to align PA and step therapy criteria with the AHS 2024 Position Statement and permit CGRP medications for migraine prevention and acute management as first-line options.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** The comment supports greater denial transparency because current denials require patient contact, appeal documentation, and reconsideration of treatment plans.
- **S20:** The comment directly supports faster PA decisions to prevent treatment delays.
- **S29/S35:** The request for health plan transparency relates to PA performance accountability and public reporting concepts.
- **S66-S82:** The comment addresses broader step therapy reform, specialty-guideline alignment, and CGRP access for migraine care.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Implement faster prior authorization decisions.
2. Increase health plan transparency around PA requirements and denials.
3. Require PA and step therapy criteria to align with the AHS 2024 Position Statement.
4. Allow CGRP medications for migraine prevention and acute management as first-line options.
5. Eliminate mandatory failure of non-specific oral medications before CGRP-targeted therapies.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** PA and step therapy requirements do not align with patient needs, comorbidities, family planning status, prior adverse reactions, or current headache guidelines.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Migraine chronification, increased disability, higher medical costs, lost productivity, delayed therapy, and clinician administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of transparency, appeal documentation, repeated treatment revisions, and persistent step therapy requirements despite updated evidence.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Annual conversion from episodic to chronic migraine is 2.5-3.0%; mean annual costs of chronic migraine are $8,243 versus $2,649 for episodic migraine; appeals take at least 20 minutes per therapeutic.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Dodick 2018 Lancet; Messali 2016 Headache; Ashina 2021 Lancet; Pozo-Rosich 2025 Cephalalgia; Charles 2024 Headache AHS statement; Robblee 2024 Headache meta-analysis; Moskatel and Slusky 2026 Headache.
- **Examples provided:** Neurology patients often need two to three PA-required therapeutics and frequent appeals; denied alternatives may be inappropriate due to family planning status or adverse reactions.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Migraine patients, neurologists, headache specialists, clinical staff, health plans.
- **Economic implications:** Higher chronic migraine costs, lost productivity, and clinician time spent on appeals are cited.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not addressed in technical standards terms.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster, guideline-aligned PA could reduce delays, disease progression, disability, and inappropriate medication trials.
