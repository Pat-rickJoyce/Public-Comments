## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The American Dental Association supports the rule's overarching goals of streamlining prior authorization and advancing interoperability but argues forcefully that the proposed FHIR-based standards and timelines are not ready for dental use cases. The ADA identifies four cross-cutting dental-specific concerns: (1) dental workflows are procedure-based and attachment-heavy, fundamentally different from medical; (2) dental systems rely on pre-determination rather than PA; (3) FHIR standards are not yet mature for dental data elements (CDT codes, tooth numbering, radiographs); (4) most dentists practice in small settings outside ONC certification programs. The ADA calls for mandatory pilot testing, dental-specific standards development, phased timelines, accountability mechanisms for dental software vendors, and inclusion of ADA in standards governance before any dental mandates take effect.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: FHIR API Requirements for Dental Use Cases**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** FHIR-based PA workflows are modeled on medical workflows and do not reflect dental care delivery. Current FHIR profiles do not represent tooth numbering, surface-level restorations, periodontal measurements, odontograms, or CDT-linked clinical context. Dental systems are predominantly outside ONC certification, leaving no regulatory driver to compel FHIR readiness for dental software vendors.
- **Specific request:** Require pilot testing of FHIR APIs with dental practice management systems and CDT-based transactions before mandating dental use cases; coordinate with ADA and HL7 to develop dental-specific FHIR profiles; establish cross-cutting dental workgroup within HL7.

**Topic 2: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs—Dental Prescribing**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Dental anesthesia providers prescribe drugs that may not fit "medical" or "pharmacy" benefit definitions. Office-administered drugs are typically bundled into procedural reimbursement. NCPDP RTPB is not integrated into dental anesthesia workflows. CDT/D-code workflows differ from CPT coding creating coverage and PA ambiguity.
- **Specific request:** Clarify treatment of dentally prescribed drugs (anesthesia, sedation D-codes vs. CPT 00170, procedure-bundled medications); align pharmacy PA requirements with dental prescribing workflows.

**Topic 3: HIPAA Administrative Simplification—FHIR as PA Standard**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify (phased, not opposed in principle)
- **Rationale:** Transitioning from X12 278 to FHIR requires coordinated retooling across payers, clearinghouses, and dental practice management vendors—most of which are not FHIR-enabled. CRD/DTR/PAS not validated against dental transaction patterns. Financial pressures compound the cost burden.
- **Specific request:** Allow staged adoption and later implementation dates for dental; maintain X12 278 option during transition; allow later compliance date for CDex IG; use incremental approach rather than forcing function of DDE exception elimination.

**Topic 4: Standalone Dental Plan (SADP) Scope**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify (support inclusion with conditions)
- **Rationale:** Excluding SADPs risks fragmentation given oral-systemic health connections, but immediate application premature—SADPs rely on X12, portals, and attachment workflows; FHIR infrastructure for dental still developing.
- **Specific request:** Future rulemaking should include SADPs with delayed/staged timelines, dental-specific workflow accommodations, and pilot programs before full enforcement.

**Topic 5: Dental Software Vendor Accountability Gap**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Dental practice management systems evolved outside ONC certification without Promoting Interoperability requirements or EHR incentive programs. Cloud-hosted multi-practice platforms may function as health information networks but lack corresponding accountability obligations.
- **Specific request:** Acknowledge gap in final rule; engage ONC and dental stakeholders in structured dialogue on scoped certification criteria or targeted incentives.

**Topic 6: Direct Data Entry (DDE) Exception**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Many dentists depend on the DDE exception for portal-based claims and eligibility verification. Eliminating DDE before viable alternatives are widely available would leave small practices without a practical path forward.
- **Specific request:** Do not eliminate DDE exception as a forcing function; first complete X12 transition, then support FHIR adoption; only then revisit DDE with dental community.

**Topic 7: Electronic Event Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI)
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** ED notifications can improve dental care coordination; millions of patients present to EDs annually for dental conditions receiving only palliative care. Real-time notifications enable dental providers to arrange timely follow-up.
- **Specific request:** Ensure dental providers are included as recipients of ED notifications when a care relationship exists.

- **S51:** Non-CMS covered entities (dental insurers/SADPs, dental clearinghouses, dental software vendors) face particular challenges; structured dialogue needed.
- **S60/S61/S62/S65:** ONC standards updates miss dental ecosystem; dental software outside certification; need dental-specific testing criteria.
- **S66:** Supports including dental providers in ED event notification framework.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require pilot testing of FHIR APIs with dental practice management systems and CDT-based transactions before mandating dental use cases.
2. Coordinate with ADA and HL7 to develop dental-specific FHIR profiles; establish HL7 dental workgroup.
3. Clarify treatment of dentally prescribed drugs under the proposed framework (sedation D-codes, procedure-bundled drugs).
4. Align pharmacy PA requirements with dental prescribing workflows; ensure SCRIPT/F&B/RTPB accessibility for dental providers.
5. Allow staged adoption and later implementation dates for dental HIPAA FHIR transition.
6. Maintain X12 278 option during transition; allow later CDex compliance date.
7. Include SADPs in future rulemaking with delayed/staged timelines and dental-specific accommodations.
8. Acknowledge dental software vendor accountability gap in final rule; engage ONC and dental stakeholders in structured dialogue.
9. Include dental providers as recipients of electronic event notifications when care relationship exists.
10. Evaluate how PA APIs interact with 837D dental claim; develop joint administrative simplification measures.
11. Do not eliminate DDE exception before viable alternatives are widely available.
12. Recognize ADA as key dental coding and standards expertise source in interoperability policy.
13. Provide funding, incentives, and technical assistance for small dental practices and underserved communities.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** FHIR standards not mature for dental use cases; dental software ecosystem outside ONC certification without readiness drivers; HIPAA FHIR transition imposes coordinated retooling burden without comparable EHR incentive funding; inadequate timeline for dental-specific standards development.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Risk of reverting to manual processes; delayed authorizations disrupting patient care; increased cybersecurity risks; reduced access for rural and underserved populations; DDE elimination without alternatives leaves small practices without practical path.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Most dental practice management systems not FHIR-enabled; outside ONC certification with no regulatory driver; dental clearinghouses built around X12 278; dental anesthesia prescribing not integrated with RTPB; CDT/D-code workflows not mapped to FHIR; low reimbursement squeezing practice margins; dental software vendors lack accountability obligations.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 2025 CAQH Index: dental industry could save $1.9 billion annually through automation; millions of patients present to EDs annually for dental conditions.
- **Studies or research referenced:** ADA economic research on low reimbursement rates; 2025 CAQH Index.
- **Examples provided:** IV sedatives and preoperative anti-emetics bundled into procedural reimbursement; CDT D-codes vs. CPT 00170 for dental anesthesia; multi-visit treatment planning with attachment-heavy submissions; specialty pharmacy drugs billed differently based on place of administration.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Dentists in small/solo/specialty practices; dental patients (particularly rural and medically complex); dental insurers/SADPs; dental clearinghouses; dental practice management software vendors; dental anesthesiologists.
- **Economic implications:** $1.9B annual savings potential from dental automation (CAQH); but transition costs substantial for small practices without comparable EHR incentive funding; low reimbursement rates compound cost burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** Low for current timeline—FHIR profiles lack dental data elements; dental software outside certification programs; NCPDP RTPB not integrated into dental prescribing; dental clearinghouses require major retooling.
- **Patient care impact:** Mixed—long-term efficiency gains if properly implemented; near-term risk of delayed authorizations and reduced provider participation if implementation overwhelms small dental practices.

Dear Administrator Oz:

On behalf of the members of the American Dental Association (ADA), the nation's leading authority on oral health, we appreciate the opportunity to provide comments on the proposed rule... The ADA commends the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) for seeking to streamline, clarify, and reduce the burdens of the prior authorization process for patients and providers, and for continuing to promote interoperability to facilitate the secure exchange of information between providers, patients, and payers.

Use of standardized application programming interfaces (APIs) promises more complete information at the point of care, greater efficiency through streamlined data exchange, fewer manual processes, and better coordination of benefits, which is especially important to improving timely access to clinically necessary dental services for medically complex and low-income patients. However, to achieve these goals, CMS must account for the distinct realities of dental practice, in which many providers operate in small, specialty, or single-site settings and in which existing administrative transaction standards and workflows often do not adequately support dentistry...
