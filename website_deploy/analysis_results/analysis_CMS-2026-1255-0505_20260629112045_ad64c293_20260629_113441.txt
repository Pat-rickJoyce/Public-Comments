---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The CARIN Alliance strongly supports CMS–0062–P's expansion of electronic PA to drugs and its alignment with HIPAA administrative simplification modernization. CARIN's four principal recommendations are: (1) formally name the CARIN Consumer Real-Time Prescription Benefit Check (RTPBC) IG within the Patient Access API with a time-bound adoption pathway; (2) explicitly recognize FHIR-based digital insurance cards as upstream PA infrastructure that reduces misrouting and delays; (3) pair mandatory IG conformance with open testing, transparent conformance criteria, and open-source reference tooling to prevent proprietary lock-in; and (4) ensure API endpoint/reporting requirements drive real-world usability, not just nominal availability.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Formally Name CARIN Consumer RTPBC IG in Patient Access API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support (with specific action requested)
- **Rationale:** The CARIN Consumer RTPBC IG is operational—tested through HL7/CMS Connectathons, used by third-party applications, and supported by national payers and PBMs. The primary barrier to broader adoption is now the absence of a clear regulatory signal naming it as the standard. Without explicit recognition, consumer-facing real-time benefit capabilities will develop unevenly across payers, limiting interoperability and slowing transparency goals.
- **Specific request:** CMS should formally name the CARIN Consumer RTPBC IG (STU 2.0) within Patient Access API implementations and establish a defined, time-bound pathway for broader adoption aligned with Patient Access API compliance cycles.

**Topic 2: FHIR-Based Digital Insurance Cards as Upstream PA Infrastructure**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support (with clarification request)
- **Rationale:** Many PA delays and denials stem not from medical necessity but from misidentification of coverage, outdated plan information, or incorrect routing—all of which digital insurance cards directly address by confirming active enrollment, identifying the correct payer/PBM, and determining which benefit pathway applies in real time.
- **Specific request:** CMS should explicitly recognize FHIR-based digital insurance cards as foundational upstream infrastructure that improves downstream PA performance, in the rule text or accompanying guidance.

**Topic 3: Open Standards, Open Testing, and Open-Source Reference Tooling**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Mandating IG conformance without transparent conformance criteria and open testing risks "it technically exists but doesn't work" outcomes. Proprietary implementation approaches fragment the market, slow adoption, and increase implementation costs—particularly for consumer-directed APIs requiring broad ecosystem participation.
- **Specific request:** CMS should pair required IG conformance with transparent conformance criteria and open testing; promote open-source reference implementations and test harnesses (e.g., via Inferno) for CRD/DTR/PAS and CDex; publish endpoint and capability statement information to support broad app/provider onboarding at scale.

**Topic 4: API Endpoint Reporting and Real-World Usability**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** API availability alone does not ensure adoption or usability. Variability in implementation, documentation, and response quality limits operational integration. Reporting requirements should drive actual functional use, not just nominal availability.
- **Specific request:** Publish endpoint and capability statement information enabling broad onboarding; ensure security expectations (FAST Security IG, Tiered OAuth) are implementable market-wide; consider ongoing API monitoring rather than point-in-time certification only.

**Topic 5: Support for HIPAA PA Standards Modernization**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CMS–0062–P's alignment with HIPAA administrative simplification modernization—adopting FHIR as the HIPAA standard for PA and moving away from existing transaction approaches—is supported as consistent with modern API-based exchange.
- **Specific request:** Not addressed beyond general endorsement.

**Topic 6: FHIR/NCPDP Pathway Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** The two-pathway approach (FHIR/Da Vinci for medical benefit; NCPDP for pharmacy benefit) is necessary given current market distinctions, but without active harmonization, stakeholders face fragmentation, duplicative PA efforts, and inconsistent user experiences.
- **Specific request:** CMS should support ongoing harmonization across FHIR-based and NCPDP-based workflows, including reuse of coverage logic where feasible, and pursue policy approaches that reduce divergence over time.

**Topic 7: Step Therapy (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Step therapy challenges arise from limited visibility into coverage rules, lack of longitudinal therapy history, and pharmacy/medical benefit fragmentation. Consumer RTPBC and Payer-to-Payer API access to prior therapy history can address root causes.
- **Specific request:** Require RTPB/RTPBC use at point of prescribing; reinforce Payer-to-Payer API support for historical therapy history; consider patient-authorized data sharing to build portable therapy histories.

**Topic 8: Laboratory Tests and DMEPOS PA (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Challenges for labs and DMEPOS (retroactive denials, date-of-service issues, workflow delays) mirror broader PA challenges and can be mitigated through standardized FHIR workflows and upstream real-time coverage information.
- **Specific request:** Apply FHIR-based PA workflows to labs/DMEPOS; address role-based access gaps for entities like laboratories; leverage CARIN Digital Insurance Cards and RTPBC to improve coverage identification before service delivery.

**Topic 9: Electronic Event Notifications (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Event notifications should include not only provider-directed notifications but patient-directed access to encounter/event data through the Patient Access API. Effectiveness depends on notifications being timely, actionable, and accessible across multiple actors including patients.
- **Specific request:** Ensure ADT/event notification data are accessible through patient-authorized applications via the Patient Access API; use standardized FHIR-based event representations; expand notification recipients to care coordinators, pharmacists, and other care team members.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Formally name the CARIN Consumer RTPBC IG (STU 2.0) within Patient Access API implementations and establish a time-bound adoption pathway.
2. Explicitly recognize FHIR-based digital insurance cards as upstream PA infrastructure in rule text or guidance.
3. Pair mandatory IG conformance with transparent conformance criteria, open testing (e.g., Inferno), and publicly available reference tooling.
4. Promote open-source reference implementations and test harnesses for CRD/DTR/PAS and CDex workflows.
5. Publish endpoint and capability statement information in a manner that supports app/provider onboarding at scale.
6. Align security expectations (FAST Security IG, Tiered OAuth) to ensure APIs are functional and usable, not just technically available.
7. Establish ongoing API monitoring mechanisms, not only point-in-time certification.
8. Support active harmonization across FHIR-based (medical benefit) and NCPDP-based (pharmacy benefit) PA workflows to reduce fragmentation over time.
9. Require RTPB/RTPBC use at point of prescribing to address upstream step therapy challenges; reinforce Payer-to-Payer API support for prior therapy history.
10. Apply FHIR-based PA workflows to laboratory tests and DMEPOS; leverage digital insurance cards and RTPBC for upstream coverage identification in these domains.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Absence of explicit regulatory naming of CARIN Consumer RTPBC IG will lead to uneven, fragmented consumer-facing benefit implementations; mandatory IG conformance without open testing produces nominal compliance rather than functional interoperability; FHIR/NCPDP pathway divergence creates implementation complexity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Inconsistent consumer access to real-time benefit information; variability in API implementation limiting third-party app adoption; fragmentation between pharmacy and medical benefit workflows undermining PA performance.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Lack of regulatory signal for RTPBC adoption; insufficient open testing infrastructure for CRD/DTR/PAS; proprietary lock-in risk from closed testing environments; benefit routing errors from absence of upstream digital insurance card infrastructure.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Prescription abandonment and non-adherence driven by affordability concerns as a high-value consumer use case for RTPBC; multiple national PBMs and health plans have participated in RTPBC development, testing, and implementation.
- **Studies or research referenced:** HL7 and CMS Connectathon testing demonstrating RTPBC technical feasibility; production pilot implementations by payers and PBMs; CARIN Digital Insurance Card real-world workflow evidence.
- **Examples provided:** PDex US Drug Formulary vs. RTPBC distinction (plan-level formulary data vs. patient-specific real-time cost/coverage); digital insurance card use cases (enrollment confirmation, benefit pathway routing, PA misrouting prevention); step therapy gaps arising from lack of longitudinal therapy history.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients/consumers (drug cost transparency, benefit routing), providers and pharmacies (reduced PA misrouting, less rework), payers/PBMs (harmonized RTPBC implementation), third-party app developers (clear standards, open testing), CMS (adoption measurement through metrics).
- **Economic implications:** Consumer RTPBC reduces avoidable therapy starts, denials, and appeals; open-source reference tooling reduces duplicative implementation costs across the market; FHIR/NCPDP harmonization prevents ongoing divergence costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** RTPBC is characterized as ready (operational, tested, in use); open testing infrastructure is feasible via Inferno and existing HL7 Connectathon mechanisms; FAST Security IG and Tiered OAuth are implementable.
- **Patient care impact:** Real-time consumer access to drug coverage and out-of-pocket costs reduces prescription abandonment, improves adherence, and enables informed prescribing decisions; digital insurance cards reduce PA delays caused by coverage identification errors.

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "At this stage, the primary barrier to broader adoption is not technical feasibility, but a lack of a clear regulatory signal. Without explicit identification and naming of a consumer-facing standard, implementation is likely to remain inconsistent, limiting the ability of patients and applications to access real-time, actionable benefit information." - Context: Core argument for why CMS must formally name the CARIN RTPBC IG rather than merely encouraging it.

2. "'Open source' in this context is not a philosophical preference; it is a practical strategy to improve consistency, accelerate adoption, reduce duplicative implementation costs, and prevent fragmentation that would undermine the rule's interoperability goals." - Context: Explaining the pragmatic case for open-source reference implementations alongside mandatory IG conformance.

3. "In practice, many PA delays and denials are driven not by medical necessity but by misidentification of coverage, outdated plan information, or incorrect routing issues that digital insurance cards directly help address." - Context: Establishing the upstream infrastructure argument for FHIR-based digital insurance cards as a PA improvement tool.

4. "PDex formulary provides reference data, while RTPBC provides actionable, patient-specific decision support at the point of care or consumer decision-making. These capabilities are complementary and should be viewed as foundational vs. operational layers of the same ecosystem." - Context: Clarifying that RTPBC and PDex US Drug Formulary serve distinct, non-duplicative purposes.
