## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Johnson & Johnson broadly supports CMS's proposed rule expanding electronic prior authorization requirements, emphasizing strong evidence of harm from aggressive utilization management, including data showing over 75% commercial PA denial rates in some therapeutic areas. J&J specifically responds to the step therapy and lab/DMEPOS RFIs, urging CMS to prohibit embedded step therapy and to clarify that MA plans must recognize date of test performance (not specimen collection date) as the date of service for laboratory PA purposes.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, API-USAGE-METRICS, GENERAL (step therapy and lab/DMEPOS RFIs)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 414.510(a) (date of service for lab services); 42 CFR 414.510(b) (exceptions to date of service rule)
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization; FHIR APIs (general); NCPDP standards (general); interoperability requirements (general)

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: General Support for UM Reform and Electronic PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Strong evidence of negative impact of aggressive UM on patient access; over 75% commercial PA denial rates in five therapeutic areas; 47% of new branded medication fills in Part D initially denied; proposal would meaningfully improve UM through electronic PA, timelines, clearer denial justifications, and reporting.
- **Specific request:** Electronic PA for pharmacy and medical benefit medicines; decision timelines; PA status access; actionable denial justifications; expanded reporting to CMS and public.

**Topic 2: Step Therapy Reform — Embedded Step Therapy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** "Embedded step therapy" imposes step therapy within PA requirements, hiding access barriers during plan selection and prescription filling; frequently extends beyond FDA-approved labeling; undermines informed coverage and treatment decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require timely data sharing of prior therapy history for step therapy exceptions; require full disclosure of step therapy employment; prohibit embedding step therapy within PA requirements.

**Topic 3: Laboratory Test PA — Date of Service Policy**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (lab/DMEPOS RFI)
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** MA plans are misinterpreting 42 CFR 414.510, denying PA based on date of specimen collection even when test not yet performed; denials are procedural, not medical necessity-based, and not appealable; particularly harmful for molecular testing for cancer patients.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that MA plans must recognize date of test performance as the date of service for PA purposes, not specimen collection date.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12 (technical challenges for medical benefit drug PA):** Supports; IQVIA data shows 47% of new branded Part D fills initially denied; CMS proposal would meaningfully improve UM through electronic PA, timelines, and clearer denials.
- **S16-S17 (specific denial reasons):** Supports; current denials lack actionable information preventing efficient UM navigation.
- **S20, S43 (PA timeframes):** Supports timelines; current delays average 3-5 weeks; some patients face 11+ rejections.
- **S29-S35 (API usage metrics and reporting):** Supports expanded reporting to CMS and public to increase accountability.
- **S69-S70 (step therapy RFI):** Urges timely data sharing of prior therapy history; calls for full disclosure of embedded step therapy and prohibition of the practice; highlights embedded step therapy extending beyond FDA-approved labeling.
- **S71 (lab/DMEPOS RFI):** Raises specific issue with MA plan misapplication of 42 CFR 414.510; urges CMS clarification that date of test performance governs PA for lab services not yet performed.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require electronic PA for medicines covered under both pharmacy and medical benefit.
2. Establish timelines for PA decisions.
3. Improve access to PA status and outcome information.
4. Mandate clearer and more actionable denial justifications.
5. Expand reporting requirements to CMS and public.
6. Require timely data sharing of prior therapy history to support step therapy exceptions.
7. Require full disclosure of when and where step therapy is employed.
8. Prohibit embedding step therapy within prior authorization requirements.
9. Clarify that MA plans must recognize date of test performance as the date of service for PA purposes.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Aggressive UM causing widespread denial of access to prescribed medications; embedded step therapy hides access barriers during plan selection; MA plan misapplication of date of service rules creates non-appealable administrative lab PA denials.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Over 75% commercial PA denial rates (up to 92% in immunology); 3-5 week average delays; 11+ rejections for some patients; 20-67% unable to get approval within one year; 64-82% of denied patients receive no alternative treatment; 100% of PDP covered lives face PA/step therapy for PsA drugs; embedded step therapy beyond FDA-approved labeling; non-appealable PA denials for lab tests.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** None specific to rule implementation; concerns focus on current UM practices that the rule should address.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:**
  - IQVIA (July 2025): Over 75% of commercially insured patients denied coverage; up to 92% denial rate in immunology; 3-5 week delays; 11+ rejections for some patients; 20-67% unable to get approval within one year; 64-82% of those receiving no alternative treatment
  - Avalere Health (March 2025): 100% of PDP covered lives (93% MA-PDs) face PA/step therapy for PsA single-source brand drugs; 87% PDP covered lives (51% MA-PDs) face UM for MS drugs
  - IQVIA (June 2025): 47% of new branded Part D fill attempts initially denied; 14% unapproved within one year
  - Community Oncology Alliance (2026): Over 90% of oncology professionals report UM not aligned with clinical recommendations; nearly 90% added staff for PA paperwork; nearly 90% say appeals processes inadequate
- **Studies or research referenced:** IQVIA analyses; Avalere Health reports; Community Oncology Alliance survey (2026); Avalere Health Advisory (May 2025) on commercial step therapy beyond FDA label
- **Examples provided:** Molecular testing for cancer patients (specimens collected during hospital procedures); MA misapplication of 42 CFR 414.510 creating non-appealable administrative lab PA denials.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients across all coverage types; pharmaceutical manufacturers; oncology professionals; clinical laboratories; MA organizations
- **Economic implications:** Widespread denial of access to prescribed medications; 14% of new-to-brand Part D claims unapproved within one year; between 64-82% of denied patients receive no alternative treatment; lab PA timing rules create cancer care delays
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports timely data sharing for step therapy history; no specific technical feasibility concerns raised
- **Patient care impact:** Very high; empirical data documents widespread denial rates, extended delays, and high rates of no alternative treatment; cancer patients particularly affected by lab PA denials and timing rules
