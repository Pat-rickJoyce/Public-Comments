ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
To Whom It May Concern,

I practice pain management in La Jolla, California. I care for patients with chronic pain conditions.

I support CMS's proposed rule (CMS-0062-P) to improve prior authorization processes. Prior authorization and step therapy remain significant barriers to timely, evidence-based care.

In practice, prior authorization delays frequently disrupt treatment initiation and continuity. Patients often wait days to weeks for approval despite clear clinical indications. These delays lead to worsening symptoms and avoidable urgent or emergency care utilization. Repeated reauthorization for therapies that are already effective further creates unnecessary treatment gaps and administrative burden.

Step therapy policies also delay care by requiring patients to retry medications that were previously ineffective or poorly tolerated. This is especially problematic when patients change insurance plans and are forced to repeat prior treatment failures despite clear documentation.

I support CMS's proposals to shorten decision timelines, expand electronic prior authorization, and improve transparency. Timely decisions, particularly within 24 hours for urgent requests, are essential to prevent clinical deterioration.

CMS should strengthen the rule by ensuring continuity of care across plans, limiting repeated authorization for stable patients, reducing fail-first requirements when prior failures are documented, aligning coverage criteria with current specialty guidelines, and requiring advance notice of coverage policy changes....
