# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Headache & Migraine Policy Forum (HMPF), with signatories including Alliance for Patient Access, American Migraine Foundation, Association of Migraine Disorders, Chronic Migraine Awareness, National Headache Foundation, and U.S. Pain Foundation.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Coalition.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National coalition of more than two dozen stakeholder organizations in the migraine and headache disorder community; cites clinical literature, headache society guidance, and AMA survey data.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HMPF supports CMS's effort to modernize prior authorization for drugs, especially for medical-benefit headache and migraine therapies such as botulinum toxin injections and infused CGRP-targeting agents. The commenter urges CMS to ensure electronic prior authorization reduces actual treatment delays rather than merely digitizing denials, and recommends stronger continuity-of-care, transparency, expedited review, and step therapy protections.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY-related portions of API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING/API metrics; GENERAL RFI on step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization generally; no specific FHIR IGs or NCPDP standards named.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extend electronic prior authorization to covered migraine treatments**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Many important headache therapies are administered in clinical settings and billed under the medical benefit; current authorization workflows rely on calls, faxes, forms, and peer-to-peer appeals that delay care and consume clinician time.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA expansion, but judge success by whether patients receive prescribed treatments faster and can track request/appeal status.

**Topic 2: Expedited and enforceable review pathways**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** Interruptions in chronic migraine and cluster headache therapy can cause rapid clinical deterioration. Plans may circumvent deadlines through duplicative documentation requests or ambiguous review pauses.
- **Specific request:** Guarantee expedited pathways; define when timelines start and stop; prohibit repeated duplicative documentation and unclear pauses.

**Topic 3: Continuity of care and prior approvals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Patients stable on therapy can lose access because of formulary changes, new prior authorization or step therapy requirements, insurance changes, or plan transitions.
- **Specific request:** Protect stable patients, continue coverage while appeals are pending, recognize prior approvals during transitions, and prevent new authorization solely due to dose/frequency adjustments to already-approved therapy.

**Topic 4: Transparent reporting and patient-facing status information**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify / clarify.
- **Rationale:** Patients and clinicians need plain-language, near-real-time status information and denial reasons; plan-comparison data should help beneficiaries understand whether needed care is approved.
- **Specific request:** Publicly report approval/denial rates, appeal overturn rates, timelines, repeat authorization rates, and step therapy rates, ideally by therapeutic category.

**Topic 5: Step therapy guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI on step therapy
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can conflict with current clinical evidence, including American Headache Society recognition of CGRP-targeting therapies as first-line preventive options, and forces patients to repeat failures.
- **Specific request:** Honor documented failures and prior determinations across plans; provide timely exceptions; limit number of steps; disclose protocols in plain language; update protocols to specialty guidelines; use reviewers with neurologic expertise.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Addresses technical and workflow challenges for medical-benefit drugs, noting continued reliance on faxes, phone calls, forms, and peer-to-peer appeals for clinic-administered migraine therapies.
- **S15:** Addresses medical/pharmacy coordination by asking CMS to accommodate off-label prescribing and coordinate smoothly between pharmacy and medical benefit systems.
- **S16:** Responds to denial-reason transparency by asking that denial metrics and status data convey exactly why requests are denied in patient-understandable language.
- **S20/S43:** Supports expedited decision pathways and urges CMS to prevent plans from evading timelines through documentation games or ambiguous pauses.
- **S29/S35:** Recommends public reporting metrics that are patient-meaningful, including approval/denial rates, appeal overturn rates, decision timelines, repeat authorization rates, and step therapy rates.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the step therapy RFI with detailed recommendations to honor prior failures and determinations, limit steps, update protocols to evidence, and require accessible exceptions.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA requirements for drug therapies, especially those under the medical benefit.
2. Measure success by actual patient access and request/appeal tracking, not by faster denials alone.
3. Guarantee expedited review when treatment interruption risks rapid deterioration.
4. Prevent timeline evasion through repetitive documentation demands or ambiguous pauses.
5. Keep approvals valid for clinically reasonable periods and avoid new authorization for dose/frequency adjustments absent material change.
6. Accommodate clinically appropriate off-label prescribing.
7. Embed continuity-of-care protections, including coverage during appeals and recognition of prior approvals.
8. Require real-time or near-real-time, plain-language status and denial information.
9. Publicly report plan metrics meaningful to patients and clinicians, preferably by therapeutic category.
10. Establish step therapy guardrails that honor prior failures and align with specialty guidelines.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Digitizing flawed processes; delays to migraine and cluster headache therapy; unstable continuity of care; step therapy inconsistent with clinical evidence.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Additional migraine attacks, deteriorating mental health, missed work or school, inability to care for loved ones, emergency department use, and higher downstream costs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Manual workflows, benefit-system coordination, payer documentation practices, lack of transparent status information, and lagging plan protocols.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Migraine affects more than 40 million Americans and is the second leading cause of disability globally; approximately 4 million Americans live with chronic migraine; women experience chronic migraine at roughly two to three times the rate of men; AMA survey data cited 95% of physicians reporting PA delays care, 79% reporting treatment abandonment, and 26% reporting serious adverse events.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Literature on migraine disability, ED utilization, chronic migraine prevalence, neurologist access, AMA prior authorization survey, AJMC step therapy survey, and American Headache Society CGRP-targeting therapy position.
- **Examples provided:** Botulinum toxin injections and infused CGRP-targeting agents; plan transitions forcing patients to relive failed treatment history.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Migraine and headache patients, neurologists, headache specialists, clinicians, payers, pharmacies/PBMs, and plan enrollees.
- **Economic implications:** Delayed access may increase ED visits, specialist appointments, unnecessary medication trials, and broader system costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports ePA but emphasizes operational details such as status tracking, benefit coordination, and documentation rules.
- **Patient care impact:** High; treatment delays and interruptions can worsen disease, function, mental health, and quality of life.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with strong modifications.
- **Tone:** Professional, patient-centered, evidence-based, and urgent.
- **Priority level:** High.
