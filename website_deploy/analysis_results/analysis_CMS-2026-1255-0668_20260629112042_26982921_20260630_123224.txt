## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Highmark Inc.; signed by Amy Sawyer, Director Health Policy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Highmark describes itself as part of an integrated healthcare delivery and financing system and the third-largest Blue Cross Blue Shield-affiliated organization, covering nearly 8 million members across several states.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Highmark supports CMS's overall interoperability and electronic prior authorization direction, including FHIR-enabled APIs and drug prior authorization modernization. It asks CMS to add clear version governance, preserve dual FHIR/X12 transaction paths during transition, align timelines with X12 8060, address provider readiness and affordability, and make denial/reporting requirements operationally feasible.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations are cited directly.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; FHIR implementation guides; NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP RTPB; formulary standards; Provider Directory/PlanNet IG; X12 278; X12 270/271; X12 8060; APIs; EHRs; clearinghouse translation from X12 to FHIR.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: IG version governance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring IGs without specifying versions may create fragmentation, indefinite backward compatibility expectations, and costly support for multiple concurrent versions.
- **Specific request:** Establish baseline versions, defined transition/adoption timelines, and explicit version retirement schedules.

**Topic 2: Pharmacy standards and readiness variation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacy readiness differs depending on whether utilization management is handled internally or delegated to PBMs, and real-time prescription benefit expectations may not reflect final adjudicated amounts.
- **Specific request:** Account for payer operating models and clarify the meaning of "real-time" for prescription benefit tools.

**Topic 3: Electronic PA for drugs and provider readiness**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Expanding ePA to drugs can improve access to specialty medications, but independent, dental, and professional providers may lack FHIR-enabled EHRs and face costly vendor or clearinghouse transitions.
- **Specific request:** Use phased/tiered adoption timelines, readiness assessments, targeted education, technical assistance, and funding mechanisms.

**Topic 4: FHIR as HIPAA PA standard and X12 coexistence**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Abrupt transition from X12 to FHIR would disrupt provider workflows, increase transaction failures, and delay care.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly allow FHIR and X12 278 coexistence during transition, provide use-case-based transaction-selection guidance, and align timelines with X12 8060.

**Topic 5: Eligibility transactions and PA indicators**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS/GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Providers currently use 270/271 eligibility workflows, including Type 30 inquiries, to receive benefit and PA information; the proposed rule creates ambiguity about whether PA indicators may continue in 271 responses.
- **Specific request:** Preserve flexibility, clarify whether PA indicators may continue in 271 responses, and explain how providers should be alerted to incorrect transaction types.

**Topic 6: Denials, timeframes, and metrics reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Highmark supports transparency, but fully standardized denial reason codes may not fit benefit-design and clinical-decision variation; metrics reporting will require system enhancements.
- **Specific request:** Use hybrid denial categories with plan-specific detail, provide clear definitions and standardized reporting methodologies, allow enough implementation time, and focus metrics on burden reduction and workflow efficiency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Highmark supports required adoption of FHIR IGs but says naming IGs alone is insufficient for real interoperability.
- **S2:** Highmark opposes a version-neutral approach without governance and asks CMS to designate baseline versions.
- **S3:** Highmark asks for defined transition, adoption, and retirement timelines to avoid unlimited backward compatibility.
- **S6:** Highmark notes Provider Directory/PlanNet IG limitations and asks CMS to strengthen standards based on real-world consumer and provider needs.
- **S12:** Highmark supports expanding ePA to drugs but emphasizes distinct medical and pharmacy benefit systems, workflows, and vendor platforms.
- **S14:** Highmark supports continued use of pharmacy standards but asks CMS to recognize readiness differences caused by PBM delegation and internal UM models.
- **S15:** Highmark addresses the need to distinguish medical and pharmacy benefit ecosystems because they use different workflows and systems.
- **S21:** Highmark urges phased, supportive timelines and warns that provider readiness barriers could reduce access to care.
- **S22:** Highmark notes material costs for upgrades, replacement systems, clearinghouse translation, payer system development, testing, and maintenance.
- **S25:** Highmark asks CMS to clarify "real-time" for prescription benefit tools because point-of-service estimates may not equal final adjudicated amounts.
- **S16:** Highmark supports meaningful, actionable denial explanations but cautions that fully standardized denial codes are not feasible.
- **S17:** Highmark recommends a hybrid approach using standardized categories plus plan-specific detail.
- **S20/S43:** Highmark states proposed timelines generally align with current operations but flexibility may be needed across programs with different constraints.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Highmark supports expanded metrics reporting if CMS provides clear definitions, standardized methodologies, sufficient implementation time, and metrics tied to burden reduction and workflow efficiency.
- **S44:** Highmark supports FHIR as the long-term direction for prior authorization.
- **S46:** Highmark strongly supports coexistence of FHIR and X12 278 during transition.
- **S47/S53:** Highmark asks CMS to phase and sequence implementation and align timing with X12 8060.
- **S48/S49/S51:** Highmark highlights costs and access risks for providers lacking FHIR-enabled systems, especially independent practices, professional practices, and dental providers.
- **S50/S54:** Highmark asks for clear transaction-use guidance and testing/implementation coordination to prevent market fragmentation.
- **S66-S82:** Highmark provides broader cross-cutting recommendations on provider readiness, eligibility workflows, and regulatory coordination.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish clear version governance for all required interoperability standards and IGs.
2. Designate baseline IG versions and define transition, adoption, and retirement schedules.
3. Account for different payer/PBM operating models in pharmacy standards compliance.
4. Clarify "real-time" expectations for RTPB and prescription benefit tools.
5. Strengthen Provider Directory/PlanNet IG capabilities to reflect real-world use cases.
6. Use phased or tiered adoption timelines for drug ePA and FHIR-based PA.
7. Provide readiness assessments, education, technical assistance, and funding mechanisms for providers.
8. Explicitly allow FHIR and X12 278 coexistence during transition.
9. Align CMS-0062-P timelines with X12 8060 development and adoption.
10. Clarify when FHIR APIs, X12 278, and eligibility 270/271 transactions should be used.
11. Preserve flexibility for PA indicators in 271 eligibility responses unless and until a fully operational replacement exists.
12. Use hybrid denial-reason reporting with standardized categories and plan-specific detail.
13. Standardize metrics definitions and reporting methodologies and allow adequate implementation time.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Version fragmentation, unlimited backward compatibility, provider readiness, cost, workflow disruption, overlapping regulatory timelines, eligibility transaction ambiguity, and infeasible reporting standardization.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Provider market exits, reduced network adequacy, reduced access to care, premium/member affordability impacts, transaction failures, care delays, market fragmentation, and repeated reconfiguration/testing cycles.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Non-FHIR EHR environments, legacy practice management systems, clearinghouse translation needs, PBM delegation, vendor dependencies, multiple concurrent IG versions, X12 8060 overlap, and fragmented/manual data capture.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Highmark reports nearly 8 million members and operations across Pennsylvania, Delaware, New York, West Virginia, Missouri, and Kansas.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Type 30 eligibility inquiries returning PA information in 271 responses; independent, dental, and professional practices needing vendor migration or clearinghouse translation from X12 to FHIR; operational burden caused by simultaneous CMS-0062-P and X12 8060 implementation.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, PBMs, providers, independent practices, dental providers, professional practices, clearinghouses, vendors, patients, and members.
- **Economic implications:** Highmark expects significant financial burden from system upgrades, vendor migration, dual-standard support, system development, testing, maintenance, reporting enhancements, and possible pass-through effects on premiums and affordability.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible with clearer governance, dual-standard transition, X12 coordination, and adequate implementation time; risky if CMS requires abrupt FHIR-only adoption.
- **Patient care impact:** Successful implementation could improve access to high-cost and specialty medications; rushed implementation could reduce provider participation, network adequacy, and timely care.
