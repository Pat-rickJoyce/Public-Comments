# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Datavant; signed by Alya Sulaiman, JD, CIPP/US, Senior Vice President, Regulatory Affairs and Chief Compliance and Privacy Officer
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Health data collaboration platform working with hospitals, clinics, payers, pharmaceutical companies, government agencies, and real-world data partners; privacy, security, and interoperability expertise; platform validated through third-party security and compliance assessments.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Datavant supports standards-based APIs and CMS efforts to improve prior authorization, but focuses its comment on RFIs about health care resiliency and payer API implementation. It recommends strong digital identity, risk-based cybersecurity expectations, privacy-preserving record linkage, operational API usability benchmarks, conformance testing, and flexible exchange pathways.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFIs; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; API-USAGE-METRICS; PRIVACY/SECURITY-related general issues.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** HIPAA Security Rule; HITECH; CMS-0062-P; RIN 0938-AV44; no specific CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 FHIR; NIST 800-63-3; NIST 800-63B; IAL2/AAL2; MFA; AES-256; ONC Health IT Certification Program; ONC Inferno; HIPAA Expert Determination standard.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Digital identity and authentication**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Fragmented authentication burdens patients and providers and weakens secure access to health data.
- **Specific request:** Require high-assurance NIST 800-63-3 IAL2/AAL2 credentials from established credential service providers and MFA across sensitive access points, within a cohesive federal privacy framework.

**Topic 2: AI-layer security and privacy-preserving data use**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** AI is increasingly used in prior authorization and data access workflows, creating new privacy and security risks.
- **Specific request:** Require continuous monitoring of AI data use and access controls, de-identification of training data, and human validation of AI-assisted decisions; encourage PPRL for privacy-preserving linkage.

**Topic 3: Risk-based cybersecurity training and oversight**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Uniform cybersecurity mandates can overburden smaller entities while failing to target the highest risks.
- **Specific request:** Scale training, incident response, third-party risk management, and independent audit expectations by organizational size, resources, data access, and risk profile.

**Topic 4: Technology-neutral security standards**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify
- **Rationale:** CMS should focus on security outcomes and avoid premature or overly prescriptive technical mandates.
- **Specific request:** Require strong encryption, NIST-aligned password practices, MFA, MDR, DLP, regular patching, and automated vulnerability scanning, while avoiding premature quantum-resistant encryption mandates.

**Topic 5: Payer API operational usability**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** APIs that exist on paper may fail in production through downtime, low throughput, malformed responses, weak error handling, or poor IG conformance.
- **Specific request:** Orient oversight around operational usability, not just API availability; use ONC certification where it measures real-world performance at scale.

**Topic 6: Conformance testing and performance benchmarks**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** Conformance testing identifies defects before they disrupt care, while performance benchmarks show whether APIs work reliably in production.
- **Specific request:** Require testing against finalized IGs using tools such as ONC Inferno, re-test on updated standards, and adopt transparent benchmarks for uptime, throughput, and error handling.

**Topic 7: Multiple exchange pathways**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Different relationships and use cases may need network-based exchange or point-to-point FHIR exchange.
- **Specific request:** Preserve multiple fit-for-purpose pathways rather than channeling all exchange through one national conduit.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1/S18:** Supports stronger implementation and testing of required IGs, emphasizing conformance and production performance rather than nominal compliance.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Recommends operational metrics and privacy protections, including uptime, throughput, error handling, de-identification, and monitoring of data use.
- **S50/S54/S55:** Addresses standards maturity, testing, certification, and enforcement by recommending ONC Inferno testing and certification only if it measures real-world performance at scale.
- **S60-S65:** Responds to standards-update themes by advocating technology-neutral, outcome-based security standards, NIST alignment, encryption, MFA, MDR, DLP, patching, and vulnerability scanning.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on resiliency and payer API implementation, including cybersecurity strategy, training, digital identity, PPRL, AI safeguards, and exchange pathways.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Establish a cohesive high-assurance digital identity framework for health data access.
2. Require NIST 800-63-3 IAL2/AAL2 credentials and MFA for sensitive access points.
3. Extend safeguards to AI-assisted prior authorization and data access workflows.
4. Encourage privacy-preserving record linkage for research, public health, and AI development.
5. Use risk-based, role-specific cybersecurity training and scaled audit/response requirements.
6. Maintain technology-neutral security standards focused on outcomes.
7. Shift payer API oversight from availability to operational usability.
8. Require conformance testing against finalized IGs, re-testing after updates, and performance benchmarks.
9. Preserve multiple exchange pathways, including network-based and point-to-point FHIR exchange.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Fragmented digital identity, cybersecurity burden on smaller entities, AI privacy risks, APIs that are technically available but not usable, and single-pathway exchange mandates.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Patient/provider authentication friction, re-identification risk, delayed care from unreliable APIs, and unnecessary burden from one-size-fits-all audits or security requirements.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Varying organizational risk profiles, fragmented authentication tools, production API performance gaps, evolving AI use, and differing exchange relationships.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Datavant states it works with more than 80,000 hospitals and clinics, every U.S. health insurance company, all top 20 pharmaceutical companies, government agencies, and more than 300 real-world data partners. It also states its PPRL has supported 180+ clinical trials and observational research programs covering 800,000+ patients.
- **Studies or research referenced:** All of Us Center for Linkage and Acquisition of Data and the N3C Data Enclave are cited as Trusted Research Environment examples.
- **Examples provided:** API timeout/malformed-response scenarios; digital identity providers CLEAR, Login.gov, and ID.me; role-based cybersecurity training; PPRL tokenization and re-encryption.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, providers, payers, API developers, health systems, researchers, public health agencies, third-party app developers, and smaller regulated entities.
- **Economic implications:** Risk-based requirements may reduce unnecessary burden for smaller entities; stronger API performance and identity infrastructure may require investment but improve reliability and adoption.
- **Technical feasibility:** Datavant views FHIR APIs, Inferno testing, PPRL, MFA, encryption, MDR, DLP, and vulnerability scanning as feasible, provided CMS remains outcome-oriented and risk-based.
- **Patient care impact:** Reliable APIs, stronger identity, and secure data access could reduce administrative burden and expedite treatment access while protecting privacy.
