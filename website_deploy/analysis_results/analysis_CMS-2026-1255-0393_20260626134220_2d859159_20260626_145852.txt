## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Michigan Primary Care Association (MPCA), signed by Phillip Bergquist, CEO
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (representing Community Health Centers / safety-net providers)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents Michigan's 42 Community Health Centers (CHCs) serving one in every 14 Michigan residents through 400+ locations; cites HRSA UDS data, peer-reviewed literature, AMA survey data, KFF reports, MGMA reports, and NACHC research.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MPCA broadly supports the proposed interoperability and ePA rules on behalf of Michigan's Community Health Centers (CHCs), particularly for their potential to reduce administrative burden and improve timely medication access for medically underserved patients. However, MPCA raises substantial concerns about CHC implementation capacity—citing limited IT staff, fragmented EHR systems, constrained budgets, and cybersecurity vulnerabilities—and requests CMS pair the mandates with dedicated technical assistance, funding, vendor accountability, and realistic implementation safeguards. MPCA also calls for concurrent PA reform (gold-carding, automatic approvals on timeout) and urges a consolidated provider-side regulatory impact analysis across three converging interoperability rules.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Interoperability & Implementation Support for Safety-Net Providers**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Standardized APIs would reduce CHC administrative burden and improve patient access, but CHCs operate 10+ different EHR systems, rely on small IT teams, and lack flexibility to absorb rapid implementation changes.
- **Specific request:** CMS should clarify providers won't be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse failures outside provider control; coordinate implementation timelines with ONC certification; establish readiness validation before enforcement; require uniform minimum implementation standards across MCOs and QHP issuers.

**Topic 2: Cumulative Regulatory Burden / Provider-Side RIA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Three converging interoperability rules (CMS-0057-F, CMS-0053-F, CMS-0062-P) represent the largest health IT transition for safety-net providers since Meaningful Use; none individually accounts for cumulative provider-side costs.
- **Specific request:** CMS should publish a consolidated provider-side Regulatory Impact Analysis across all three rules and ensure cumulative burden does not exceed what CHCs can absorb without dedicated support.

**Topic 3: Electronic Prior Authorization for Drugs (Medical & Pharmacy Benefit)**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Extending ePA to drugs will help CHC medication adherence outcomes, but dual benefit pathway workflows create confusion (e.g., buprenorphine, long-acting injectables, biologics can fall under either pathway). 340B contract pharmacy coordination adds complexity.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification information via Provider Access APIs (CRD Coverage Information extension); clarify whether a PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to the alternate pathway; coordinate with OPA for 340B-specific technical assistance; mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.

**Topic 4: ePA Without Concurrent PA Reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Peer-reviewed evidence shows ePA alone may increase PA volumes and time spent, without improving outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Pair ePA mandates with meaningful PA reduction: gold-carding for prescription drugs, sunset provisions for PA categories with consistently high approval rates.

**Topic 5: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Aligned, expedited timeframes (24 hours expedited drugs, 7 days standard, 72 hours expedited services) will reduce treatment delays for CHC patients.
- **Specific request:** Establish automatic PA approval for requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 6: PA Denial Reasons and Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons enable more efficient resubmission and appeals; CHCs currently experience significant back-and-forth delays.
- **Specific request:** Publish PA metrics in a centralized, standardized format on CMS' website; expand reporting to capture CHC/safety-net operational burden; establish stronger oversight for plans with persistent delay/denial patterns.

**Topic 7: API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Centralized endpoint registry directly benefits CHC small IT departments; proposed timelines are reasonable.
- **Specific request:** Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files; prioritize ease of access for safety-net/rural providers.

**Topic 8: API Usage Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Granular by-plan metrics support identification of access gaps for Medicaid patients (48% of CHC patients).
- **Specific request:** Add API functionality/usability metrics; publish aggregated trend data.

**Topic 9: Removal of Drug Formulary from Provider Access/Payer-to-Payer APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Formulary information changes rapidly; payer website information is unreliable; CHC providers serving multiple payers lack admin infrastructure for manual lookups.
- **Specific request:** Retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 10: Open Payments CMP**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Stronger audit enforcement benefits CHC patients; transparency is important for safety-net communities.
- **Specific request:** Coordinate with HRSA to clarify intersection with 340B compliance to avoid duplicative burdens.

**Topic 11: HIPAA PA Standards / X12 to FHIR Transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHCs and clearinghouses face dual-standard burden (X12 6020 + FHIR/CDex simultaneously); limited IT staff makes this especially challenging.
- **Specific request:** Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion through the CMS-0062-P transition period; implement CDex guardrails (all needed docs in initial request; max 2 solicited-attachment requests per PA; maximum payer response timeframes after CDex attachments).

**Topic 12: Gold-Carding**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support / Recommend
- **Rationale:** Reduces PA burden for compliant providers; especially valuable for CHCs with high chronic disease PA volumes.
- **Specific request:** Formal CMS guidance recommending gold-carding; extend to all items and services.

**Topic 13: ONC Standards Update / HTI-5 Alignment**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** 3-month gap between payer API compliance (Oct 2027) and certified EHR availability (Dec 2027–Jan 2028); HTI-5 may reduce supporting infrastructure CHCs depend on.
- **Specific request:** Joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance; publish formal harmonization assessment before final rule.

**Topic 14: Cybersecurity / Privacy & Security**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Concern / Recommend
- **Rationale:** API expansion increases attack surface; CHCs have small IT teams; limited cyber insurance.
- **Specific request:** Provide TA, cybersecurity/workforce support, and dedicated health IT funding; establish CHC Cybersecurity Shared Services model with HCCNs.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12:** Technical challenges significant for CHCs due to fragmented EHRs, 340B complexity, and dual-pathway workflows; real-time benefit classification via CRD needed.
- **S15:** Medical vs. pharmacy benefit distinction operationally unclear for buprenorphine, long-acting injectables; payers should expose classification info via Provider Access API.
- **S16:** Denial reasons should identify missing info, explain denial, and provide actionable resubmission guidance.
- **S17:** Structured denial codes should be paired with plain-language explanations.
- **S20:** 24-hour expedited timeframe supported; automatic approval upon timeout required for compliance.
- **S21:** Oct 2027 payer compliance is feasible if vendor certification timing gap addressed through joint CMS-ONC guidance.
- **S23:** Cybersecurity exposure significant; CHCs must not absorb unfunded costs.
- **S24:** API registry should include endpoint status, supported IGs/versions, uptime metrics, known limitations.
- **S26:** Machine-readable file format strongly recommended.
- **S27:** Weekly updates and annual verification supported.
- **S29:** Add API functionality metrics: success/error rates, structured vs. unstructured data, cost of API maintenance.
- **S30:** Privacy protections must accompany expanded usage data collection.
- **S34:** Alignment of reporting deadlines with reporting periods supported.
- **S35:** CMS should publish aggregated trend data over time.
- **S44:** FHIR is appropriate direction for PA transactions; MPCA supports moving away from X12.
- **S46:** Dual-standard burden must be relieved; extend 2024 enforcement discretion.
- **S47:** Transition timeline should include extended enforcement discretion for dual-standard period.
- **S50:** CDex guardrails needed to prevent iterative payer data requests.
- **S51:** Non-CMS covered entities (clearinghouses serving CHCs) face similar transition challenges needing support.
- **S57:** CMP "failure to report" definition supported; coordinate with HRSA on 340B intersection.
- **S60:** ONC certification versioning must sync with CMS compliance deadlines.
- **S61:** Formal CMS-ONC harmonization assessment needed before final rule.
- **S65:** Safety-net provider adoption rates should inform compliance timelines.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify providers will not be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse/EHR failures outside provider control.
2. Establish readiness validation/testing before enforcement, coordinating with ONC certification timelines.
3. Publish a consolidated provider-side Regulatory Impact Analysis across CMS-0057-F, CMS-0053-F, and CMS-0062-P.
4. Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access APIs (CRD Coverage Information extension).
5. Clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway satisfies the other.
6. Coordinate with OPA for 340B-specific technical assistance and funding support.
7. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.
8. Pair ePA mandates with gold-carding and sunset provisions for high-approval-rate PA categories.
9. Establish automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
10. Publish PA metrics in centralized, standardized, publicly accessible format on CMS' website.
11. Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files.
12. Add API functionality and usability metrics; publish aggregated trend data.
13. Retain drug formulary information in Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
14. Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion through CMS-0062-P transition period.
15. Require CDex guardrails: all docs in initial request; max 2 solicited-attachment requests; maximum payer response timeframes.
16. Issue formal CMS guidance recommending gold-carding for all items and services.
17. Issue joint CMS-ONC implementation guidance and publish harmonization assessment before final rule.
18. Provide TA, cybersecurity support, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.
19. Work with HRSA and HCCNs to establish a CHC Cybersecurity Shared Services model.
20. Coordinate with HRSA on Open Payments / 340B compliance intersection.
21. Amend CoP §482.24(d) to include Section 330-funded CHCs as designated ADT recipients; use NPI Type 2 identifiers.
22. Align ADT standards with FHIR-based event notification models (Da Vinci Notifications IG STU 1.1).
23. Require payer API certification through ONC with public conformance testing results.
24. Establish provider-payer interoperability testing sandbox with CHC-specific scenarios.
25. Establish mandatory step therapy continuation floor (12 months or current episode); indefinite for chronic disease medications.
26. Allow Standardized TRFs as PA documentation for lab tests; mandate real-time pre-collection PA for lab tests.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** CHC implementation capacity gaps (small IT teams, 10+ EHR systems, thin margins); cumulative burden of three converging rules; dual-standard burden during X12-to-FHIR transition; 3-month provider certification gap (Oct 2027 vs. Dec 2027–Jan 2028); cybersecurity exposure from API expansion; ePA alone may increase PA volumes; removal of formulary data from APIs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** 77% of CHCs negatively impacted by Change Healthcare breach; 62% of CHC patients experienced care delays; one in five CHCs had 50%+ revenue affected by delayed claims; nearly half of CHCs with fewer than 90 days of cash on hand; one in four reporting -5% operating margins.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** 10 different EHR systems across Michigan CHCs; 78.9% of CHCs have 1–10 HIT FTEs; only two-thirds have cybersecurity committee; vendor priorities drive timelines; 340B workflow complexity; payer APIs often not deployed to production.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 2024 HRSA UDS data; 53 million MA PA determinations in 2024 with 4.1 million full/partial denials; 80%+ of appealed denials overturned; 94% of physicians report PA delays care (AMA); average PA wait ~3 days; TEFCA: 21,086 organizations, 889 million documents (April 2026).
- **Studies or research referenced:** Lauffenburger JC et al. (JAMIA, 2021) on ePA impact; Salzbrenner SG et al. (JMANC, 2022) on ePA perceptions; Vishwanath S et al. (AJMC, 2021) on proactive PA outreach; Lenahan KL et al. (Health Aff, 2021) on step therapy protocols; Muralidharan B et al. (JAMA Network Open, 2025); Nopour R (FHIR scoping review, 2025).
- **Examples provided:** Buprenorphine billing under medical vs. pharmacy benefit; long-acting injectables in behavioral health; DMEPOS wheelchair delays; Change Healthcare breach impact on CHCs.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** CHCs, safety-net providers, medically underserved patients (Medicaid, uninsured, homeless, agricultural workers, low-income), EHR vendors, clearinghouses, Medicaid MCOs, QHP issuers, pharmacies/PBMs, 340B participants.
- **Economic implications:** Cumulative unfunded implementation costs; negative CHC operating margins; limited cyber insurance; need for dedicated federal funding.
- **Technical feasibility:** Significant concerns about vendor readiness, EHR certification timing gap, dual-standard transition, 340B complexity, and cybersecurity from expanded API footprint.
- **Patient care impact:** PA delays disproportionately harm underserved populations; ePA reforms could improve medication adherence; gold-carding and automatic timeout approval could further protect access.
