## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Lundbeck Pharmaceuticals LLC; signed by Devin Zatorski, Director, Federal and State Policy, Reimbursement and Strategy, Government Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Other — biopharmaceutical manufacturer.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Global biopharmaceutical company focused on brain health, with more than 70 years in neuroscience; U.S. headquarters and facilities; 172 active U.S. clinical trial sites; R&D investment of 20-25% of global revenue.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Lundbeck strongly supports CMS efforts to extend electronic prior authorization reforms to drugs and emphasizes that the rule should maximize patient access, timeliness, and accountability. It asks CMS to adopt a uniform 24-hour drug prior authorization decision deadline across impacted payers, impose meaningful consequences for missed deadlines, standardize denial reasons, use PA metrics for oversight, and pursue near-term step therapy reforms.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS indirectly through electronic PA infrastructure; GENERAL/RFI on step therapy.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 Fed. Reg. 19890, 19933-19936, 19941, 19948-19949, 20013, 20039-20040; 42 CFR 422.568(b)(3), 422.568(f), 422.572(a)(2), 422.572(f); ACA section 1311(e)(1); Social Security Act sections 1852 and 1857.
- **Standards referenced:** Prior Authorization API, NCPDP standards, payer APIs, Payer-to-Payer API; no detailed discussion of specific FHIR IG versions.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Uniform 24-hour deadline for drug prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Different deadlines across payer types create confusion and delay. Lundbeck argues that electronic PA functionality and payer access to requirements and documentation make a 24-hour deadline feasible and more aligned with patient access needs.
- **Specific request:** Require all impacted payers to respond to drug prior authorization requests within 24 hours.

**Topic 2: Accountability for missed PA deadlines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Deadlines without consequences may not change payer behavior and may still jeopardize timely access to medication.
- **Specific request:** Treat missed PA decision deadlines as automatic approvals; if CMS does not adopt that approach, treat missed deadlines as immediately appealable adverse determinations.

**Topic 3: Specific and actionable denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons help providers appeal, correct, resubmit, or choose alternatives; generic or unclear denials delay care.
- **Specific request:** Require standardized denial reason choices plus free-text fields for missing information, next steps, and actionable explanation; consider a standard denial reason form.

**Topic 4: Use PA metrics as an oversight tool**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Granular metrics can help CMS detect unintended effects of shorter timelines, including increased denials, extensions, appeals, or overturned denials.
- **Specific request:** Monitor trends and outliers, require more granular reporting by drug group or therapeutic area, and publish synthesis reports for policymakers, researchers, patients, and other stakeholders.

**Topic 5: Step therapy reforms using payer data exchange**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer-to-payer data exchange can prevent patients from repeating step therapy after changing coverage and can identify contraindications or prior failures.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to use electronic tools and payer APIs to evaluate prior therapies; require step therapy approvals/exceptions to last at least one year and not lapse due to dosage changes.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Lundbeck supports requiring specific denial reasons and asks CMS to define specificity as actionable detail, using standardized reason categories and free text for next steps.
- **S17:** The comment supports structured standardized denial reasons, supplemented by narrative information where needed.
- **S20:** Lundbeck supports a 24-hour drug PA timeframe and urges CMS to apply it uniformly across all impacted payers.
- **S21:** The commenter states that a 24-hour timeframe is feasible because the proposed electronic PA tools and NCPDP standards support faster communication and decision-making.
- **S29:** Lundbeck supports metrics and requests more granular reporting, including drug groups, therapeutic areas, and brand/generic categories.
- **S34:** Lundbeck supports public reporting over time and recommends CMS use the data to publish synthesized reports and inform future rulemaking.
- **S35:** The comment supports public reporting and aggregate analysis but emphasizes that data should be granular enough to identify access problems.
- **S66-S82 / Step therapy RFI:** Lundbeck recommends requiring payers to use payer APIs and clinical history from other payers to evaluate step therapy and preserve continuity; it also recommends one-year stability for approvals and no new PA solely for dosage changes.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require all impacted payers to respond to drug prior authorization requests within 24 hours.
2. Treat missed PA decision deadlines as deemed approvals, or at minimum immediately appealable denials.
3. Require standardized denial reasons with free-text fields for additional actionable details.
4. Use publicly reported PA metrics for CMS oversight of denials, extensions, appeals, and overturned decisions.
5. Require more granular PA metrics by drug group, therapeutic area, and generic/brand status.
6. Publish CMS synthesis reports based on PA metric trends and use findings in future rulemaking.
7. Require payers to use payer-to-payer data and APIs to determine whether step therapy requirements have already been satisfied.
8. Require step therapy approvals/exceptions to remain valid for at least one year and not lapse due to dosage changes.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Inconsistent payer deadlines; lack of accountability for missed deadlines; unclear denial explanations; insufficiently granular PA metrics; step therapy disruptions after payer changes or dosage changes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, treatment abandonment, higher emergency department and inpatient use, missed doses, increased seizures, hospitalization, disrupted psychiatric medication regimens, reduced adherence, and increased provider burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Need for payer education, provider outreach, full use of electronic PA tools, and CMS oversight to avoid unintended increases in denials or extension requests.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Lundbeck cites migraine research finding one in five commercially insured patients experienced at least two payer denials due to PA/utilization management; pediatric epilepsy research reporting 38.4% encountered PA barriers, fewer than half received the originally prescribed medicine within seven days, with documented missed doses, increased seizures, and hospitalization; antipsychotic regimen disruption associated with nearly 12% higher likelihood of discontinuing all antipsychotic treatment.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Buse et al., Headache, January 2026; Wirell et al., Pediatric Neurology, June 2018; Fung et al., Medical Care, 2013; AMA/AJMC survey discussion; CMS Multi-Payer Alignment materials.
- **Examples provided:** Denials sometimes omit basic missing information such as an unsigned form or missing patient date of birth.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients needing medication, especially neurological, psychiatric, migraine, epilepsy, and serious mental illness populations; providers and staff; impacted payers; CMS and researchers.
- **Economic implications:** Potential reduction in health care utilization from faster medication access; lower provider administrative burden; need for payer investment in meeting deadlines and reporting.
- **Technical feasibility:** Commenter argues feasibility is improved by electronic PA tools, Prior Authorization API functions, NCPDP standards, and payer-to-payer data exchange.
- **Patient care impact:** Strongly positive if adopted; faster access, clearer next steps after denials, fewer treatment disruptions, and greater continuity during payer or dosage changes.
