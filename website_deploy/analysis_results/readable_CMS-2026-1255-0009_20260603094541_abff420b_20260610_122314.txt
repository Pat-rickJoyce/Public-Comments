## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The ALS Association strongly supports CMS's proposed prior authorization modernization and drug inclusion but emphasizes that medically complex, progressive conditions like ALS require expedited default pathways, longer approval durations, specialty clinical review, AI safeguards, and comprehensive transparency. The Association recommends specific protections for patients with rare, rapidly progressive diseases to prevent treatment delays that cause irreversible harm.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Expedited Prior Authorization for Progressive Conditions**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** ALS is rapidly progressive and irreversible. Standard PA timeframes result in preventable hospitalizations, loss of respiratory/mobility function, and increased emergency care costs. The yearly cost of ALS care in the US exceeds $1 billion; delays increase downstream costs.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to treat PA requests for serious, progressive conditions (like ALS) as expedited by default. Reduce the 72-hour expedited window to real-time or near real-time decisions when clinically possible.

**Topic 2: Duration and Reauthorization Requirements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients with progressive, non-improving conditions should not repeatedly prove medical necessity for the same equipment. Repeated reauthorization creates administrative burden, delays access, and causes preventable complications.
- **Specific request:** (1) Require expedited PA renewal for previously approved items that remain clinically appropriate; (2) Ensure PAs remain valid for treatment duration when appropriate; (3) Prohibit reducing approval duration or increasing reauthorization frequency when adopting automated PA systems.

**Topic 3: Coordinated/Bundled Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** ALS patients require combinations of services. Multiple separate PA submissions create unnecessary delays. Neurologists at specialized clinics have expertise to determine appropriate strategies.
- **Specific request:** Permit providers to submit bundled PA requests for coordinated care. Process entire coordinated request on expedited basis when any component qualifies for expedited review.

**Topic 4: Transparency and Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with strengthening
- **Rationale:** Most PA denials are reversed on appeal, but medically frail patients struggle with complex processes. Vague denial language like "incomplete claim" or "failure to establish medical necessity" creates barriers.
- **Specific request:** (1) Develop clear, enforceable definition of "specific reason"; (2) Prohibit vague denial language; (3) Require plans to obtain missing information before denying; (4) Provide denial notifications directly to patients in plain, culturally/linguistically appropriate language; (5) Require streamlined, expedited appeals.

**Topic 5: Clinical Expertise in PA Decisions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Reviewers without neurodegenerative disease expertise cannot grasp ALS complexity/urgency. Generalist reviewers may not understand proactive ALS care (e.g., prescribing ventilators before emergencies). This leads to inappropriate denials and inequitable outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Require PA determinations be made by clinicians with relevant specialty expertise.

**Topic 6: Patient Access APIs and Drug PA Integration**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Patients with complex conditions need transparency in PA processes. Integrating drug PA information into Patient Access APIs would empower patients/providers at point of care, reduce delays, enhance coordination, and simplify payer transitions.
- **Specific request:** (1) Require plans to share PA details through FHIR-based APIs with clear patient instructions; (2) Integrate prescription drug PA information into Patient Access APIs for patients and providers; (3) Support real-time PA integrated from pharmacy counters to home delivery; (4) Ensure specialty pharmacies are fully integrated.

**Topic 7: Payer-to-Payer Data Exchange**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** When patients change insurers, they must resubmit PA requests, creating burdens and care delays. This especially impacts medically complex patients, leading to treatment gaps and increased anxiety.
- **Specific request:** (1) Require plans to review and consider active PA decisions from previous payers for benefit-equivalent items/services; (2) Set clear standards for payer-to-payer data exchange; (3) Integrate PA information into patient access APIs for real-time status.

**Topic 8: AI and Automated Decision-Making Safeguards**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (lack of safeguards)
- **Rationale:** The rule lacks clear safeguards for AI/automated PA tools. For rare diseases like ALS: (1) patients underrepresented in training datasets; (2) algorithms may undervalue anticipatory care; (3) automated denials may lack meaningful clinical review. This creates inconsistent, opaque decisions.
- **Specific request:** (1) Prohibit fully automated denials for life-sustaining equipment; (2) Require human clinical review of all AI-assisted determinations affecting such care; (3) Set transparency standards (clear documentation, decision explanations, straightforward appeals).

**Topic 9: Public Reporting of PA Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Stratified reporting is essential to identify disparities in access. People with rare diseases/complex needs may face higher denial rates. Transparency holds plans accountable.
- **Specific request:** Stratify public reporting by service type, diagnosis, provider type, race/ethnicity, and age. Include average time to decision, overturn rates on appeal, and denial rationale. Publish in timely, accessible, user-friendly format.

**Topic 10: Electronic Prior Authorization Implementation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Shift from manual/paper/fax to interoperable electronic workflows promises efficiency gains, reduces administrative burden, expedites decisions, enhances timely access to services/equipment/medications.
- **Specific request:** Advance interoperable electronic PA to reduce administrative costs, minimize treatment delays, improve care coordination for medically complex patients. Integrate ePA into EHR systems.

**Topic 11: Gold-Carding and Streamlined Utilization Management**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with equity safeguards
- **Rationale:** PA may be unnecessary when services are ordered by physicians with established expertise and demonstrated approval histories. However, programs must not disadvantage clinicians treating medically complex patients.
- **Specific request:** (1) Encourage MA plans to expand/adopt gold-carding programs that reduce/eliminate PA for providers consistently meeting medical necessity criteria; (2) Ensure programs don't disadvantage clinicians treating complex patients; (3) Treat ALS-related services/equipment/drugs as expedited by default.

Re: CMS-0061-P: 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule
Dear Administrator Oz:
On behalf of The ALS Association, we appreciate the opportunity to comment on CMS's proposed rule to modernize prior authorization (PA) and interoperability requirements, including the extension of these requirements to prescription drugs.
Our mission is to make ALS livable until it is cured by advancing research, supporting people living with ALS with innovative equipment and services, and advocating for policies that ensure timely access to high-quality care. We represent individuals living with ALS, their families, caregivers, and the clinicians who treat them.
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a rapidly progressive and ultimately fatal neurodegenerative disorder. Individuals affected by ALS present with significant medical complexity and frailty, requiring prompt access to multidisciplinary healthcare services, essential medications, and durable medical equipment, including advanced power wheelchairs, non-invasive ventilators, and speech-generating devices—to maintain functional abilities, independence, and their quality of life. Accordingly, ensuring timely access to these resources is critical for effective management as the disease advances.
While these comments focus on ALS, the concerns raised apply broadly to individuals with serious, progressive conditions across Medicare, Medicare Advantage (MA), Medicaid Managed Care, CHIP, Qualified Health Plans and other health payers. For individuals affected by ALS, the imposition of prior authorization and utilization management isn't just a minor administrative checkpoint, it can be the determining factor in whether they receive timely access to care, equipment, and medications that help maintain their physical wellbeing, dignity, and quality of life.
Prior Authorization as Utilization Management in Medicare Advantage
Nearly all Medicare Advantage enrollees are currently in plans that require prior authorization. While prior authorization can be an appropriate utilization management tool when applied judiciously for certain patient populations, its increasing use in MA has contributed to administrative burden and delays in medically necessary care for people living with ALS.
The Office of Inspector General determined that Medicare Advantage plans have denied prior authorization requests even though the requested services complied with Medicare coverage requirements. Some denials also occurred due to procedural mistakes or correctable issues, such as missing documentation. Instances of unwarranted denials may result in delays or impede beneficiaries' access to essential medical services.
These findings are consistent with the experience of people living with ALS. Arbitrary barriers and coverage restrictions imposed by some MA plans frequently delay or deny access to complex medical equipment, medications, and supportive services that are essential to making ALS a livable disease.
Expedited Prior Authorization for Medically Complex and Medically Frail Patients
Timely access to the health care services, medical equipment, and prescription drugs ordered by clinicians is essential for people with ALS.
Delays in prior authorization frequently result in:
•
Preventable hospitalizations
•
Loss of respiratory or mobility function
•
Delayed access to ventilators or communication devices
•
Increased reliance on emergency care
•
Delays or denials of expensive, medically complex equipment—such as ventilators, advanced wheelchairs, and speech-generating devices—that are medically necessary for ongoing care
Respiratory muscle weakness is a hallmark of ALS and often leads to respiratory failure. Timely access to ventilatory support is therefore essential for both medical necessity and quality of life. When prior authorization delays or denials block access to medically necessary equipment, patients may experience rapid health decline and require more intensive interventions, which increase overall care costs. The yearly cost of ALS care in the United States is thought to be around $1 billion. Removing unnecessary administrative hurdles could lower avoidable costs and lead to better results for patients.
CMS should require plans to treat prior authorization requests for serious, progressive conditions that are not expected to improve, such as ALS, as expedited by default...
