# Comment Analysis: CMS-2026-1255-0075

---

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Anonymous Headache Specialist
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider (physician specialist)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Headache specialist practicing in New York City; treats headache disorder patients daily; clinical experience with prior authorization and step therapy impacts

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A headache specialist supports the proposed expedited prior authorization timeframes but urges CMS to also reform step therapy requirements that delay access to first-line CGRP medications for migraine patients. The commenter provides a case example of a 31-year-old patient who lost her job due to uncontrolled migraines while being forced to fail multiple step therapy medications over a year before accessing clinically appropriate CGRP treatment, resulting in prolonged debilitation and increased ER utilization.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS (expedited PA timeframes), PA-DRUG-MEDICAL and/or PA-DRUG-PHARMACY (drug prior authorization processes), GENERAL (step therapy policies)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None
- **Standards referenced:** American Headache Society public statement on CGRP medications as first-line treatment for migraines

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Expedited Prior Authorization Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Faster PA decisions will help move treatment forward and reduce delays in patient care.
- **Specific request:** Implement the proposed expedited prior authorization timeframes as proposed.

**Topic 2: Step Therapy Requirements for Migraine Medications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Step therapy requirements force patients to fail multiple ineffective medications before accessing first-line CGRP treatments recommended by American Headache Society, causing prolonged debilitation, job loss, and increased ER visits.
- **Specific request:** Reform or eliminate step therapy requirements that contradict clinical guidelines and delay access to evidence-based first-line migraine treatments.

**Topic 3: Coverage Policies Aligned with Clinical Evidence**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Progressing treatment coverage to align with clinical evidence would decrease patient debilitation time, reduce ER visits, and lower burden on the healthcare system.
- **Specific request:** Update coverage policies to allow first-line access to medications recognized by professional societies as appropriate initial therapy.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

**S20, S43 (PA Timeframes for Drugs):** Commenter strongly supports expedited prior authorization timeframes, characterizing the proposal as "amazing" and beneficial for moving treatment forward.

**S69 (Step Therapy RFI):** Commenter opposes step therapy requirements for migraine medications, providing specific case example of patient harm from being denied first-line CGRP therapy for over a year.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Implement the proposed expedited prior authorization timeframes for drug coverage decisions.
2. Eliminate or significantly reform step therapy requirements that contradict evidence-based clinical guidelines from professional societies.
3. Allow first-line access to CGRP injectable medications for migraine patients without requiring failure of multiple other treatments first.
4. Align coverage policies with American Headache Society guidelines recognizing CGRPs as appropriate first-line therapy.
5. Consider the broader healthcare system impact: reducing step therapy delays would decrease ER visits and overall system burden.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
I am a headache specialist in New York City that treats headache disorder patients daily. In my practice I have experienced the effect of prior sighs and step therapy on my patients. Routinely I've had patients care be delayed with prolonged debilitation due to waiting for prior authorization. One example i would like to highlight is a young 31 year old who has had to leave her job that she loves because of uncontrolled migraine headaches. Ideally I'd like to get her on CGRP injectable medications which is recommended and recognized as a first line treatment for migraine headaches as put out by the public statement from the American Headache Society. However we've had to trial multiple step therapies that she's been unresponsive to and had terrible side effects for over the last year in order to fail step therapy to get to CGRPs. The current proposal to expedite prior authorization time is amazing, and will help move treatment forward but progressing treatment coverage would also decrease debilitation time and years for patients, lower ER visits and burden on the system.
