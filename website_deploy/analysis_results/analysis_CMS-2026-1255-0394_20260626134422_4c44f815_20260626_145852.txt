## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Advanced Medical Technology Association (AdvaMed), Diagnostic Payment Work Group (DPWG); signed by Zoe Guengerich, Senior Director, Payment & Healthcare Delivery Policy
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association (representing medical device, diagnostic products, and health IT manufacturers)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** AdvaMed represents world's leading innovators and manufacturers of medical devices, diagnostic products, digital health technologies, and health information systems, including hundreds of small companies; responded specifically on behalf of clinical laboratory and diagnostic testing members.

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AdvaMed's Diagnostic Payment Work Group responds exclusively to the RFI questions on laboratory testing prior authorization, identifying six systemic problems: (1) MA organizations improperly preventing laboratories from initiating PA requests despite existing CMS regulations; (2) inconsistent coverage criteria through LBMs that don't align with Medicare FFS LCD/NCD policy; (3) denial of services even when complete information is provided on TRFs; (4) post-specimen-collection PA denials based on misuse of the Medicare date-of-service policy; (5) inadequate transparency and accountability for LBM-delegated PA decisions; and (6) AI tools trained on non-CMS criteria amplifying inappropriate denials. AdvaMed urges CMS to codify laboratory PA rights, require TRF acceptance as valid documentation, clarify date-of-service rules, mandate LBM transparency reporting, and ensure AI oversight.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratories' Right to Initiate Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify / Modify (enforce existing regulation)
- **Rationale:** Some MAOs do not permit laboratories to submit PA requests despite 42 C.F.R. § 422.566 establishing labs as providers eligible to request coverage determinations; creates procedural denials unrelated to medical necessity.
- **Specific request:** New enforcement requirement for MAO compliance with 42 C.F.R. § 422.566; systemic reporting mechanisms for non-compliance.

**Topic 2: Inconsistent MA Coverage Criteria — LCDs, NCDs, and LBMs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (current LBM practices) / Modify
- **Rationale:** MAOs contract with LBMs who use proprietary criteria inconsistent with Medicare FFS coverage; billing articles and DEX™ registry supplements to LCDs are not required for consideration, creating MA/FFS coverage disparities.
- **Specific request:** Require MAOs to consider billing articles and DEX™ when making coverage decisions; require public posting of MA-developed coverage policies before adoption; require UM committees to review LBM-used criteria.

**Topic 3: Test Requisition Forms (TRFs) as Valid Medical Documentation**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support (codification)
- **Rationale:** CMS contractors (MolDX) have already clarified TRFs may establish medical necessity when complete, but MAOs continue denying NCD- and LCD-covered services despite complete TRF information.
- **Specific request:** Explicitly codify in MA regulations that TRFs are valid medical documentation; adopt MolDX clarification across MA and commercial payers.

**Topic 4: Post-Specimen-Collection PA Denials / Date of Service Misuse**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose (current practice) / Clarify
- **Rationale:** MA plans misapply 42 CFR 414.510(a) to deny PA after specimen collection, though labs may not receive specimens for up to 48 hours; inconsistent with 42 C.F.R. § 422.138(b).
- **Specific request:** Clarify that date of performance (when lab receives specimen) governs for PA; clarify all denied claims always appealable on medical necessity grounds.

**Topic 5: Appeals Process Deficiencies**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MA appeals window is 65 days vs. 120 days for Medicare FFS; collecting documentation can take up to 90 days; some plans only allow patient appeals; administrative denials not escalated to medical necessity review.
- **Specific request:** Extend MA Level 1 appeal deadline to 120 days; ensure all denials appealable on medical necessity grounds; mandate clear escalation and dispute resolution processes.

**Topic 6: Laboratory Benefit Managers (LBMs) — Transparency and Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** LBMs provide vague denial rationales, apply policies inconsistently, and redirect disputes without resolution; UM committees not required to review LBM-used criteria.
- **Specific request:** Require MAOs to report annually on PA delegated to third parties, share denied, share appealed, share overturned; CMS to compile and publish.

**Topic 7: AI in Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Conditional Support / Concern
- **Rationale:** Documented cases of MA plans training AI on inconsistent Medicare guideline interpretations leading to systematic inappropriate denials.
- **Specific request:** Require AI trained on official NCDs/LCDs/billing articles; require public reporting of AI use; consider excluding complex disease groups from AI coverage determinations.

**Topic 8: Consider Eliminating PA for NCD/LCD-Covered Services**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Recommend
- **Rationale:** High appeal overturn rate (80.7% in 2024) and time-sensitive nature of diagnostics suggest PA requirements for NCD/LCD-covered services are counterproductive.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider whether MAOs should require PA at all where defined NCD or LCD coverage policy exists.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

This comment responds exclusively to the RFI on laboratory testing (S66-S82 domain), not to numbered solicitations in the main proposed rule.

- **RFI (Lab PA impact):** PA policies preventing lab-initiated requests, LBM inconsistency, TRF denials, and DOS misuse cause delays in time-sensitive oncology and precision medicine testing; 80.7% appeal overturn rate demonstrates systemic initial denial problems.
- **RFI (TRFs as valid documentation):** Codifying TRF acceptance would align MA with Medicare FFS and reduce inappropriate denials; CMS contractors already recognize TRFs as sufficient.
- **RFI (Frequency of post-collection PA denials):** DOS policy misuse allows procedural denials after specimen collection; date of performance (not collection) should govern.
- **RFI (LBM impact):** LBMs impose extra criteria inconsistent with Medicare coverage; lack clear accountability; UM committees should be required to review LBM criteria.
- **RFI (Process improvement):** Eliminate PA for NCD/LCD-covered services; codify TRF acceptance; ensure all denials appealable on medical necessity grounds; mandate escalation/dispute resolution.
- **RFI (AI automation):** AI could improve timeliness but risks amplifying coverage misinterpretations; requires CMS oversight, mandatory reporting, and exclusions for complex cases.
- **RFI (Issues technology isn't addressing):** Lack of escalation/dispute resolution pathways; fundamental MA/FFS coverage misalignment requires regulatory enforcement, not technology.
- **RFI (Workforce and retention):** PA administrative burdens divert clinical/lab staff from patient care, contributing to burnout and reduced MA network participation.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. New enforcement requirement ensuring MAO compliance with 42 C.F.R. § 422.566 (lab PA initiation rights); systemic reporting mechanisms for non-compliance.
2. Require MAOs to consider LCD billing articles and databases (e.g., DEX™ registry) when making coverage decisions.
3. Require any MA coverage policy distinct from LCD/NCD to be publicly posted and subject to public comment before adoption.
4. Require UM committees to explicitly review criteria used by third-party entities like LBMs.
5. Explicitly codify in MA regulations that TRFs are valid medical documentation; adopt MolDX clarification across MA and commercial payers.
6. Clarify that MA plans must use date of performance (not date of specimen collection) as the relevant date for lab PA purposes.
7. Clarify that all denied claims are always appealable on medical necessity grounds, regardless of PA outcome.
8. Extend MA Level 1 appeal deadline to align with Medicare FFS (120 days).
9. Mandate clear escalation and dispute resolution processes for providers contending MA plans are not following rules.
10. Require MAOs to report annually on PA delegated to third-party entities, share denied, appealed, and overturned; CMS to compile and publish.
11. Require AI tools used in PA to be trained on official Medicare guidelines; require public reporting of AI use; consider excluding complex disease groups from AI-based determinations.
12. Consider whether MAOs should require PA at all where defined NCD or LCD coverage policy exists.
13. Require collection and public reporting of MA claim denial data by service type, compared to Medicare FFS.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** MA plans and LBMs systematically using procedural/administrative grounds to deny laboratory PA; 80.7% appeal overturn rate in 2024; 65-day MA appeal window insufficient when acquiring provider records takes up to 90 days; AI tools trained on incorrect criteria amplifying inappropriate denials at scale.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delays in time-sensitive oncology and precision medicine diagnostic testing; patients losing access to medically necessary services on technicalities; laboratories unable to meaningfully appeal administrative denials; workforce burnout from repetitive denial/appeal cycles.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Labs as ancillary providers can't acquire ordering provider documentation within short appeal windows; LBMs create accountability gaps; no systemic dispute resolution pathway short of individual claim appeals.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 53 million MA PA determinations in 2024 (KFF); 11.5% of denied PA requests appealed in 2024; 80.7% of those appeals partially or fully overturned (KFF).
- **Studies or research referenced:** KFF report (Biniek et al., Jan. 28, 2026); CMS RFI (91 Fed. Reg. 19890) acknowledging DOS policy misuse and TRF acceptance gaps.
- **Examples provided:** Humana Liquid Biopsy Policy (HUM-1151-001) improperly applying LCD criteria to NCD-covered service; LCD L38779 with complex multi-criteria billing articles; colorectal cancer screening denials despite medical necessity determinable from demographic/ICD-10 data.

---

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Clinical and diagnostic testing laboratories, MA beneficiaries, ordering physicians, MAOs, LBMs, AdvaMed members (diagnostic product manufacturers).
- **Economic implications:** Uncompensated care from procedural denials; administrative burden from duplicative appeals and documentation gathering; workforce costs from PA-related staff time diversion.
- **Technical feasibility:** Primary recommendations (LBM reporting, TRF codification, DOS clarification) are regulatory and feasible; AI oversight requires new reporting infrastructure.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays in time-sensitive oncology/precision medicine diagnostics directly affect treatment decisions; high overturn rates suggest many denied services are medically necessary.

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "Technology alone cannot resolve the fundamental misalignment between MA coverage policies and Medicare FFS rules without stronger CMS enforcement and accountability mechanisms." — Context: Responding to RFI question on issues technology is not currently addressing.

2. "The high overturn rate strongly suggests that many initial denials lack merit and that systematic improvements to the prior authorization process—not merely the appeals process—are warranted." — Context: Citing 80.7% appeal overturn rate in 2024 to argue for structural PA reform.

3. "There is no disincentive for plans to continue this practice of high denials and no recourse for laboratories and other third-party providers." — Context: Describing how the 65-day MA appeal window combined with 90-day documentation retrieval times effectively insulates plans from accountability.

4. "Improperly implemented AI tools risk amplifying existing coverage misinterpretations and denial patterns if not subject to CMS oversight and validation." — Context: Warning about AI in PA trained on inconsistent MA/Medicare guidelines.
