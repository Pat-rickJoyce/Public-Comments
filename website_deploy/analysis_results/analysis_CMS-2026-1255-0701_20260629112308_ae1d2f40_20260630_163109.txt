# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Medical Informatics Association (AMIA); signed by Eileen Koski, AMIA Public Policy Committee Chair.
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** AMIA represents more than 6,000 informatics professionals, including clinicians, researchers, public health experts, data scientists, health IT leaders, and policy experts.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AMIA supports CMS's effort to modernize drug prior authorization using FHIR-based standards, structured data exchange, payer accountability, and interoperable electronic processes. It urges CMS to publish a clear implementation roadmap, integrate ePA into point-of-care workflows, minimize payer variation, require precise machine-readable denial reasons, strengthen API and PA reporting, and add guardrails for automated and AI-enabled prior authorization.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFI on automation and reducing unnecessary PA.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; CMS-0057-F; HIPAA Administrative Simplification requirements; ONC certification policies. No specific CFR citations are provided.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR; HL7 FHIR-based Prior Authorization APIs; HL7 Da Vinci implementation guides; X12; ONC certification criteria; structured vocabularies; EHR, e-prescribing, pharmacy, PBM, and practice management system integration.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation Roadmap and Alignment**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** AMIA says CMS-0062-P creates a complex implementation environment across CMS-0057-F, HIPAA standards, ONC certification, EHR vendors, pharmacies, PBMs, payers, and clearinghouses.
- **Specific request:** Publish a roadmap with milestones, stakeholder responsibilities, testing expectations, subregulatory guidance timelines, pilots, and real-world validation; align deadlines with ONC certification and vendor release cycles.

**Topic 2: Operationally Realistic Timeline and Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** AMIA is concerned the October 1, 2027 compliance date may be challenging after other major CMS API deadlines, especially for smaller and under-resourced organizations.
- **Specific request:** Use phased implementation, good-faith flexibility where appropriate, technical assistance, and clear accountability on payers for API readiness and documentation.

**Topic 3: Point-of-Care Drug ePA Workflows**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY.
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Drug ePA can improve medication access only if clinicians can work inside EHR, prescribing, pharmacy, and practice management tools rather than payer portals.
- **Specific request:** Require workflows that let clinicians determine PA requirements, view coverage criteria, submit documentation, receive decisions, and respond to denials or requests for information within normal workflows.

**Topic 4: Two-Way Communication and Denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Without two-way communication, ePA will still require manual follow-up and disconnected processes.
- **Specific request:** Support payer questions, provider responses, information requests, escalation, appeals, reconsideration, human clinical expertise, and denial reasons that are explicit, precise, substantiated, clinically meaningful, and machine-readable.

**Topic 5: Required FHIR Standards and Reduced Optionality**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS.
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** AMIA strongly supports modern FHIR-based standards but warns that optionality, payer-specific implementation, inconsistent vocabularies, and uneven testing could fragment workflows.
- **Specific request:** Minimize optionality, distinguish required from optional implementation elements, require strong conformance testing, harmonize CMS and ONC requirements, and avoid duplicative FHIR-to-X12 translation where FHIR-native workflows are better.

**Topic 6: Reporting, API Usability, and Patient-Facing Data**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS.
- **Position:** Support and expand.
- **Rationale:** AMIA says accountability requires standardized, machine-readable data on authorization status, denial reasons, approvals, expiration dates, supporting documentation, API reliability, and workflow integration.
- **Specific request:** Expand public reporting to appeals, overturn rates, information requests, API transmission failures, patient delays, burden metrics, abandonment/withdrawal rates, disparities, downtime, response times, and manual fallbacks.

**Topic 7: Automated and AI-Enabled Prior Authorization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI.
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Shorter timelines and higher volumes may drive payer use of automation and AI, creating risks of opaque or inequitable denials.
- **Specific request:** Establish transparency, human oversight, auditability, explainability, bias monitoring, accountability, and disparity evaluation requirements for automated PA tools.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** AMIA supports required FHIR-based standards but asks CMS to minimize optionality and payer-specific variation.
- **S2:** AMIA asks CMS to clearly align required standards with ONC certification and vendor release cycles.
- **S3:** AMIA recommends a roadmap for transitions, milestones, subregulatory guidance, testing, and real-world validation.
- **S12:** AMIA supports extending ePA to medical benefit drugs when integrated into point-of-care workflows.
- **S14:** AMIA supports extending ePA to pharmacy benefit drugs and stresses coordination with pharmacy systems and PBM infrastructure.
- **S16:** AMIA recommends denial explanations that identify the specific coverage criterion not met, missing documentation, step therapy or formulary requirements, and appeal/reconsideration steps.
- **S17:** AMIA supports standardized, machine-readable denial terminology to support appeals automation, analytics, quality improvement, and oversight.
- **S20:** AMIA supports shorter standardized PA timelines but warns they must not incentivize superficial review, automated denials, or more documentation requests.
- **S21:** AMIA expresses concern that the October 1, 2027 date may be challenging and recommends phased, realistic implementation.
- **S23:** AMIA addresses patient permission and opt-in/opt-out clarity for data exchange processes.
- **S24:** AMIA supports reporting API endpoints and technical documentation with accurate updates and conformance demonstration.
- **S29:** AMIA supports API usage reporting but says CMS should evaluate usability, reliability, workflow integration, and burden reduction rather than traffic alone.
- **S30:** AMIA asks CMS to use clear, uniform, accessible language where patient permission or opt-out choices apply.
- **S34:** AMIA supports API metrics for Provider Access, Payer-to-Payer, and Prior Authorization APIs.
- **S35:** AMIA recommends public metrics on successful/failed transactions, error types, downtime, response times, manual fallback workflows, provider experience, and EHR/pharmacy workflow incorporation.
- **S44:** AMIA supports FHIR-based HIPAA Administrative Simplification standards when current, practical, and superior to existing approaches.
- **S46:** AMIA asks CMS to clarify interaction with X12 and avoid unnecessary FHIR-to-X12 translation when FHIR-native workflows better reduce burden.
- **S47:** AMIA recommends realistic phasing and alignment with ONC and vendor cycles.
- **S50:** AMIA emphasizes strong conformance testing and clear required implementation elements.
- **S54:** AMIA supports formal API testing, validation, and certification processes aligned with ONC governance.
- **S66-S82:** AMIA responds generally to automation and AI-enabled PA, urging guardrails and a long-term reduction in unnecessary PA requirements.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Publish a detailed CMS-0062-P implementation roadmap.
2. Align CMS-0062-P with CMS-0057-F, HIPAA, ONC certification, and vendor release cycles.
3. Use phased implementation, technical assistance, and good-faith flexibility where appropriate.
4. Hold payers accountable for API readiness, documentation, transparency, and reporting.
5. Integrate drug ePA into EHR, e-prescribing, pharmacy, and practice management workflows.
6. Require two-way payer-provider communication for questions, denials, appeals, and reconsideration.
7. Minimize optionality and payer-specific variation in FHIR implementation.
8. Require explicit, precise, substantiated, clinically meaningful, machine-readable denial reasons.
9. Expand API and PA reporting metrics to include reliability, burden, disparities, and manual fallbacks.
10. Establish guardrails for automated and AI-enabled prior authorization.
11. Continue reducing unnecessary, duplicative, or low-value PA requirements.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Ambiguous overlapping federal requirements, unrealistic timelines, payer variation, poor workflow integration, automated denials, excessive documentation, and digitizing inefficient manual processes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Provider burden, patient medication delays, inappropriate automated denials, increased requests for information, inequitable outcomes, and shifted costs to providers.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** EHR and vendor release cycles, PBM readiness, payer API availability, inconsistent documentation, conformance testing gaps, optional standards, X12/FHIR interaction, and under-resourced organizations.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AMIA states it represents more than 6,000 informatics professionals; no quantitative implementation cost or outcome data are provided.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No specific studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** Point-of-care prescribing workflows, payer-specific portals, prescription renewal authorization workflows, appeal/reconsideration workflows, clinical data such as allergies, side effects, diagnoses, medication history, and problem-list data.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Providers, patients, payers, EHR developers, pharmacies, PBMs, clearinghouses, practice management vendors, smaller/rural/safety-net organizations, CMS, ONC, and researchers.
- **Economic implications:** AMIA warns that costs should not be shifted to providers and that duplicative workflows or outdated standards could impose undue administrative burden.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if phased, aligned with ONC certification, supported by strong conformance testing, and integrated into existing health IT workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Potentially faster medication access and better transparency, but only if faster timelines do not lead to inappropriate denials or increased documentation burdens.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive with substantial implementation modifications.
- **Tone:** Professional, technical, collaborative.
