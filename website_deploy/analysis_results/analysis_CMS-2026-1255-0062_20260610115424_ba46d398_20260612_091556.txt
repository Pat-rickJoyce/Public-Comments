# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** MassHealth, Commonwealth of Massachusetts Office of Medicaid; signed by Mike Levine, Undersecretary for MassHealth.
- **Stakeholder type:** Government
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** State Medicaid and CHIP program covering almost two million Massachusetts residents, including 45% of children and 60% of residents with disabilities.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MassHealth supports CMS's interoperability goals and the use of NCPDP standards for pharmacy-related prior authorization, but asks CMS to reduce implementation risk. Its main requests are a 24-month implementation extension, preservation of NCPDP as the authoritative pharmacy benefit framework, clarification that requests for additional information can satisfy the 24-hour response expectation as a distinct category, and confirmation that PA metrics for drugs should be aggregate or contextualized rather than misleading drug-level rates.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING by implication; IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR sections cited in the body; references CMS-0062-P and related HIPAA Administrative Simplification transition from NCPDP D.0 to F6.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP Telecommunication Standard D.0 and F6; NCPDP Formulary & Benefit files; Real-Time Benefit Tools (RTBT); FHIR-based APIs.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Additional implementation time**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The proposed rule overlaps with the NCPDP D.0 to F6 transition, creating simultaneous interoperability, API, reporting, and pharmacy systems work that may strain state and vendor capacity.
- **Specific request:** Provide an additional 24 months for implementation.

**Topic 2: Preserve NCPDP framework for pharmacy benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify/Clarify
- **Rationale:** Pharmacy benefit operations already rely on mature NCPDP standards, and duplicative API requirements could create separate pharmacy API environments, reconciliation problems, data governance risk, and confusion about authoritative sources.
- **Specific request:** Continue excluding pharmacy benefit drugs from API-based PA and coverage documentation requirements where NCPDP systems govern the exchange; if API requirements touch pharmacy benefit drugs, specify that NCPDP systems remain authoritative.

**Topic 3: Incomplete submissions and the 24-hour timeline**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** States need consistent interpretation of whether requesting additional information is a timely response or whether incomplete requests must be treated as denials and resubmitted.
- **Specific request:** Allow timely requests for additional information to be treated as a distinct response category when the initial submission lacks needed information.

**Topic 4: Aggregate rather than drug-specific PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Drug-specific approval or denial rates can be misleading without clinical, utilization, indication, and documentation context.
- **Specific request:** Confirm that reporting is aggregate for all drugs requiring PA; if drug-level reporting is finalized, permit narrative context and therapeutic-class reporting.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S19:** Identifies state readiness concerns arising from overlapping NCPDP F6 transition work and proposed interoperability/API/reporting requirements.
- **S20:** Requests clarification on how incomplete PA submissions interact with the 24-hour drug PA response timeframe.
- **S22:** Describes costs and complexity from duplicative pharmacy API environments, integration of pharmacy systems into medical-benefit APIs, and vendor capacity constraints.
- **S25:** Supports reliance on the established NCPDP pharmacy ecosystem, including F&B and RTBT, as the mature operational framework.
- **S29:** Supports aggregate approval, denial, appeal, and timing metrics but opposes misleading drug-specific metrics without context.
- **S34:** Does not focus on frequency, but asks CMS to clarify the level and form of reporting.
- **S35:** Supports reporting in aggregate and asks for narrative context if more granular data are required.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Add 24 months to the implementation timeline.
2. Preserve the separate NCPDP-based framework for pharmacy benefit drugs.
3. Avoid duplicative API requirements where NCPDP standards already govern pharmacy benefit exchanges.
4. Clarify that NCPDP systems remain authoritative for pharmacy benefit coverage, formulary, and PA information if APIs touch pharmacy benefit drugs.
5. Treat timely requests for additional information as a distinct response category under the 24-hour PA timeframe.
6. Confirm that drug PA metrics are aggregate, not drug-specific.
7. If drug-level reporting is required, allow narrative context and therapeutic-class reporting.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Compressed timeline, overlapping NCPDP F6 transition, duplicative API requirements, unclear treatment of incomplete submissions, and misleading drug-level metrics.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Implementation risk, strained state and vendor capacity, inconsistent pharmacy data, inaccurate access metrics, unnecessary resubmissions, and possible patient access disruptions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Pharmacy systems modernization, vendor capacity, synchronization of dynamic formularies and coverage rules, data governance, reconciliation across systems, and reporting interpretation.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** MassHealth covers almost two million residents, including 45% of Massachusetts children and 60% of residents with disabilities.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** NCPDP D.0 to F6 transition beginning August 2027 with compliance in April 2028; high-cost medication PA approval rates that may appear high because PA directs requests to eligible members; high denial rates where documentation or indication criteria are not met.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** State Medicaid agencies, Medicaid members, providers, pharmacies, PBMs, pharmacy IT vendors, health IT vendors, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Duplicative APIs and overlapping system changes would increase state and vendor costs and implementation risk.
- **Technical feasibility:** MassHealth views NCPDP pharmacy infrastructure as mature but sees duplicative FHIR/API exposure for pharmacy benefit data as complex and risky.
- **Patient care impact:** A realistic timeline and clear standards could avoid disruptions; duplicative or rushed implementation could delay PA decisions and medication access.
