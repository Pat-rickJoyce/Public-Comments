ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
Hello I am a Medicare beneficiary living with chronic migraine.

For several years, I have successfully managed my migraines with Botox treatments administered by my physician. When I receive treatment on my established schedule every four months, my migraine frequency is dramatically reduced, allowing me to function normally and maintain my quality of life.

Recently, Medicare denied my physician's request to continue my Botox treatment on the schedule that has been working for me. As a result, I am now more than two months overdue for treatment.

The consequences have been significant. While receiving Botox on my regular schedule, I experienced only one to two migraines per month. Since my treatment was delayed, my migraine frequency has increased to approximately 15 to 16 migraines per month. I am also relying on Imitrex far more often than I did when my Botox treatments were provided on time. Before this disruption, I rarely needed rescue medication. Now I find myself using it frequently simply to remain functional...
