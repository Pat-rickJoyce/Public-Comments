# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Courtney Lawrence, Senior Vice President, Head of Government Affairs and Global Public Policy, The Cigna Group.
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer / PBM / integrated health services organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Cigna describes national commercial medical coverage, Evernorth pharmacy and specialty pharmacy services, PBM capabilities, participation in the CMS Health Technology Ecosystem, and co-leadership of the Payer Workgroup.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Cigna supports CMS's goals to modernize prior authorization, expand electronic data exchange, improve transparency, and reduce administrative burden, but urges CMS to add flexibility and a longer implementation runway. The comment's central requests are a minimum 24-month implementation period after final guidance, provider adoption requirements for ePA, flexible routing across medical/pharmacy benefit boundaries, streamlined reporting, and narrowly scoped API endpoint reporting with cybersecurity protections.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P generally; no detailed CFR citations beyond references to Sections II.B, II.C.2, II.C.3, II.C.6-II.C.7, II.E, and RFI sections.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR, Da Vinci prior authorization approaches, NCPDP, HL7 Da Vinci Member Attribution List IG, Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG, TEFCA, NIST, API endpoint metadata, OAuth-like authorization concepts, EHR/ePA integration.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Implementation timeline**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Drug PA API implementation requires multi-system build, integration, testing, and coordination with other interoperability obligations. A rushed timeline could create instability and disruption for patients and providers.
- **Specific request:** Establish at least 24 months after publication of the final rule and associated guidance, and use phased implementation that prioritizes mature capabilities.

**Topic 2: Dual standards and integrated benefit workflows**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Support with modifications.
- **Rationale:** Cigna supports FHIR for medical benefit drugs and NCPDP for pharmacy benefit drugs, but says providers often cannot determine benefit classification and complex regimens may span both benefits.
- **Specific request:** Allow a provider-friendly single entry point with internal routing, and create exceptions for integrated regimen- or protocol-based PA requests that span medical and pharmacy benefit drugs.

**Topic 3: Provider adoption of ePA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Payer API investments will not deliver efficiency if providers are not required to use or integrate ePA and continue fax/phone/portal workflows.
- **Specific request:** Move beyond attestation-based measures and incorporate ePA utilization into quality programs or Conditions of Participation.

**Topic 4: Drug PA decision timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify / clarify.
- **Rationale:** Incomplete submissions can require additional clinical information; compressed clocks may increase denials and resubmissions if the timeline runs before a complete request is available.
- **Specific request:** Start decision clocks only when minimum necessary clinical information is received; allow clock-stops/tolling while awaiting additional information; clarify interaction with state-specific Medicaid and CHIP timelines.

**Topic 5: Denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with flexibility.
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons are important, but payers must comply with varied state notice requirements and clinical nuance.
- **Specific request:** Preserve flexibility in format and presentation and provide audit guidance.

**Topic 6: Reporting and endpoint transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Overlapping federal and state metrics can be duplicative; overly broad endpoint reporting may expose system architecture or proprietary information.
- **Specific request:** Standardize metrics and denominators, consider phased reporting, preempt duplicative state reporting where appropriate, limit endpoint reporting to base URLs, capability statements, and supported standards, and protect sensitive information.

**Topic 7: RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, payer APIs, step therapy, lab tests, and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Mixed / modify.
- **Rationale:** Cigna supports standardization, FHIR-aligned notify-and-query models, TEFCA improvements, NIST-aligned cybersecurity, testing over certification for payer APIs, and pragmatic automation of step therapy, labs, and DMEPOS.
- **Specific request:** Use phased guidance, avoid payer API certification, enable standards-based testing, support lab coding solutions, and balance DMEPOS access with program integrity.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S15/S21/S28:** Supports dual medical/pharmacy standards but requests provider-friendly routing and exceptions for integrated regimen-level PA; requests at least 24 months after final guidance.
- **S16/S17:** Supports specific denial reasons but requests flexibility for clinical nuance and state-law variation.
- **S20/S43:** Requests decision timeframes begin only once a complete request is received and allow tolling when more information is needed.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports endpoint visibility but recommends limiting reporting to essential technical metadata and safeguarding proprietary/cybersecurity-sensitive information.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Requests streamlined metrics, standardized definitions, and phased reporting to reduce duplication and improve comparability.
- **S44-S56:** Addresses HIPAA implications indirectly through concern about FHIR requirements, workflow transformation, and the need for practical timelines and flexibility.
- **S60-S65:** Supports updated standards with phased adoption, guidance, and testing; warns against fragmented or premature mandates.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on event notifications, resiliency, payer API implementation, certification, step therapy, labs, and DMEPOS with recommendations for standardized, phased, and outcomes-based approaches.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide at least 24 months after final rule and guidance before compliance.
2. Permit a single provider entry point for drug PA with payer/PBM/EHR routing to the correct benefit pathway.
3. Create exceptions allowing integrated regimen/protocol PA across medical and pharmacy benefits.
4. Require stronger provider-side ePA adoption through performance-based requirements or quality programs.
5. Begin PA decision clocks only after complete clinical information is submitted and allow tolling.
6. Preserve flexibility in denial-reason formats and issue clear audit guidance.
7. Streamline and standardize reporting metrics, reducing duplicative state and federal obligations.
8. Limit endpoint reporting to essential metadata and protect cybersecurity/proprietary information.
9. Use conformance/interoperability testing rather than payer API certification.
10. Support FHIR-aligned event notification, TEFCA payer use cases, lab coding improvements, and DMEPOS program integrity.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Implementation timeline, provider non-adoption of ePA, rigid dual-standard boundaries, fragmented reporting, endpoint security, certification burden.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Misrouted drug PA requests, duplicate submissions, delayed therapy, incomplete clinical review, increased administrative burden, rushed deployments, cybersecurity exposure.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-system integration, EHR adoption, provider workflow integration, state-law variation, PBM/payer/vendor coordination, standards variability, lab coding gaps.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Cigna reports reducing overall medical PA volume by about 15% since June 2025 and expects standardization for more than 70% of PA volume by the end of 2026; it cites CMS fraud figures for DMEPOS in the RFI appendix.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Federal cybersecurity/resiliency analyses and CMS healthcare fraud takedown are referenced; details are mostly summarized rather than deeply analyzed.
- **Examples provided:** Oncology regimens involving injectable and oral therapies across benefit structures; acute myeloid leukemia, endometrial cancer, tumor-agnostic therapies, lab test coding, and DMEPOS fraud examples.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, PBMs, EHR vendors, providers, Medicaid/CHIP programs, QHPs, patients, pharmacies, labs, DME suppliers, clearinghouses, app/API developers.
- **Economic implications:** Significant implementation and reporting costs; potential savings from automation and reduced duplicate submissions; potential increased costs from certification or fragmented workflows.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if phased, tested, and flexible; risky if mandates are compressed or rigid.
- **Patient care impact:** Cigna argues integrated and automated workflows can reduce delays, while rigid benefit-split processes can delay specialty and oncology treatment.

- **Tone:** Professional, technical, collaborative.
- **Priority level:** High.
