## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Joint Commission responds to CMS RFIs on electronic event notifications and health care resiliency. It reports generally consistent compliance with event notification requirements but recommends additional guidance on admission notifications, and it emphasizes cyber resiliency as a patient safety issue requiring operational continuity, preparedness, and clinical workflow planning.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI — electronic event notifications for value-based care and care coordination; GENERAL/RFI — increasing health care resiliency.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None specific beyond the proposed rule and docket reference.
- **Standards referenced:** None. The comment references event notification requirements and cyber readiness but does not cite FHIR, NCPDP, X12, or implementation guides.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Additional support for hospital event notifications**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Joint Commission surveyors generally find consistent compliance with event notification requirements, but when gaps occur they are most often related to admission notifications rather than discharge or transfer notifications.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider additional guidance or technical assistance focused on admission notifications.

**Topic 2: Cyber resiliency as patient safety and care continuity**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Sudden loss of access to electronic systems and patient information can threaten patient safety and disrupt care. Cyber resilience should include clinical continuity, operational response, leadership coordination, and staff preparedness, not only IT security and recovery.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider practices that help health care organizations maintain clinical continuity during cyberattacks and should view recovery capability as equally important to preventive security measures.

**Topic 3: Joint Commission resources for cyber readiness**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Clarify
- **Rationale:** Joint Commission has launched a voluntary self-assessment tool and plans Cyber Resilience Readiness certification to evaluate organizational readiness for cyber-related technology outages.
- **Specific request:** CMS should consider Joint Commission's CRR framework and related expertise as it evaluates policies to improve cyber resiliency.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82 / Electronic Event Notifications RFI:** Joint Commission says surveyors generally see consistent compliance, but CMS could provide additional support focused on admission notifications.
- **S66-S82 / Health Care Resiliency RFI:** Joint Commission recommends emphasizing clinical continuity during cyber events, including operational readiness, leadership coordination, staff preparedness, and community coordination.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Provide additional guidance, technical assistance, or support on admission notifications.
2. Recognize cyber-related technology outages as patient safety risks that require continuity-of-care planning.
3. Promote practices that help HCOs sustain clinical operations during cyberattacks.
4. Consider operational readiness, leadership coordination, staff preparedness, and clinical workflow impacts in cyber resilience policy.
5. Consider Joint Commission's voluntary cyber self-assessment and forthcoming Cyber Resilience Readiness certification as resources or models.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Admission notification compliance gaps; patient safety threats caused by cyberattacks and digital outages; rural HCO vulnerability due to constrained resources.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disruption to patient care, increased patient mortality, longer length of stay, increased procedural complications, and loss of access to electronic systems/patient information.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Resource constraints, especially for rural HCOs; need for organization-wide operational readiness and staff preparedness.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 93% of organizations reported at least one cyberattack in the past year; 72% reported disruption to patient care; health systems experiencing cyberattacks reported an average 29% increase in patient mortality; 53% reported longer length of stay; 54% reported increased complications from medical procedures.
- **Studies or research referenced:** ECRI blog on digital outage preparedness; Ponemon Institute 2025 report on cyber insecurity in health care.
- **Examples provided:** Joint Commission surveyor findings on admission notification improvement areas; Joint Commission cyber self-assessment and planned CRR certification.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Hospitals and other health care organizations, rural HCOs, patients, CMS, accrediting bodies, clinical staff, and leadership teams.
- **Economic implications:** Not quantified, but cyber disruptions can require organizational resources and affect operational continuity.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment supports readiness tools and certification rather than prescribing a specific technical standard; feasibility depends on organizational preparation and resources.
- **Patient care impact:** High. Joint Commission frames cyber readiness and event notification compliance as direct patient safety and continuity-of-care issues.
