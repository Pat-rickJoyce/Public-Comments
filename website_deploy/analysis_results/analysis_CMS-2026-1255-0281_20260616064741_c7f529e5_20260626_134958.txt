# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Massachusetts Health & Hospital Association (MHA)
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents Massachusetts member health and hospital systems; signed by Karen Granoff, Sr. Director, Payer Relations and Managed Care.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

MHA strongly supports CMS efforts to streamline prior authorization through interoperability, ePA standards, transparency, and consistent payer communication. It supports applying ePA standards to medical-benefit drugs, adopting FHIR APIs as the HIPAA prior authorization standard, requiring named implementation guides, and collecting PA metrics, while requesting more granular reporting, clearer public posting requirements, phased implementation flexibility, and robust stakeholder testing.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; API-USAGE-METRICS; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; TRANSPARENCY-related general reporting; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations; references CMS-1845-P, CMS-0062-P, and prior CMS-0057 ePA standards.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR APIs; HIPAA prior authorization standard; electronic prior authorization (ePA); X12 278 transaction; implementation guides.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Extend ePA to Medical-Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization causes administrative burden and patient care delays, especially for pharmaceuticals; extending CMS-0057 ePA standards to drugs covered under the medical benefit is described as a welcome improvement.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the extension of ePA standards to medical-benefit drugs.

**Topic 2: FHIR as HIPAA Prior Authorization Standard**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** MHA says naming FHIR APIs as the industry standard under HIPAA and replacing the underused 278 transaction would let providers interact with all plans consistently.
- **Specific request:** Adopt FHIR APIs as the HIPAA standard process for prior authorization.

**Topic 3: Prior Authorization Metrics and Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA supports added PA metric reporting but says contract-level reporting is insufficient to identify specific problem areas affecting patient care.
- **Specific request:** Require service-level metrics and specify where reporting must be posted on carrier websites so it is easy to find.

**Topic 4: Required Implementation Guides and Operational Readiness**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** MHA supports required IGs but warns that technical compliance alone will not reduce burden unless payer and vendor implementations support efficient provider workflows.
- **Specific request:** Ensure implementations achieve operational efficiency, allow standardized documentation submission across payers, and minimize manual work.

**Topic 5: Timeline, Phasing, and Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Providers need time for technology development, testing, workflow redesign, staff education, and payer coordination.
- **Specific request:** Provide implementation flexibility, consider phased implementation where appropriate, and coordinate testing with providers, vendors, and payers before full deadlines.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** MHA supports naming specific implementation guides as required rather than merely recommended, provided implementation is operationally efficient.
- **S18:** MHA recognizes potential provider burden from implementation and asks CMS to ensure workflows reduce manual work rather than merely achieving technical compliance.
- **S21:** MHA urges CMS to consider operational readiness and adequate provider implementation time for medical-benefit drug ePA.
- **S29:** MHA supports PA metric reporting but recommends service-level reporting rather than contract-level reporting.
- **S34:** MHA requests clear requirements for where metric reporting must be posted so the information is accessible.
- **S44:** MHA supports FHIR APIs as the industry standard process under HIPAA for prior authorization.
- **S46:** MHA favors replacing the underused X12 278 process with FHIR APIs to create a consistent process across plans.
- **S47:** MHA asks CMS to consider implementation timelines and provider readiness.
- **S53:** MHA recommends flexibility and a phased approach where appropriate.
- **S54:** MHA asks CMS to work with providers, vendors, and payers to ensure sufficient testing before full implementation deadlines.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize ePA standards for drugs covered under the medical benefit.
2. Adopt FHIR APIs as the HIPAA standard for prior authorization transactions.
3. Require PA metrics to be reported at the service level rather than only the contract level.
4. Specify where reports must appear on carrier websites so they are easy to locate.
5. Ensure IG implementation produces operational efficiency, not only technical compliance.
6. Allow implementation flexibility and phased implementation where appropriate.
7. Coordinate sufficient testing among providers, vendors, and payers before full deadlines.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Continued manual provider work, delayed care, inability to identify harmful plan requirements if metrics are too aggregated.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Technology development, testing, workflow redesign, staff education, payer coordination, vendor implementation quality.

The Massachusetts Health & Hospital Association, on behalf of its member health and hospital systems, appreciates the opportunity to comment on the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services' proposed rule regarding Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs. In general, Massachusetts hospitals and health systems strongly support CMS's continued efforts to simplify and streamline prior authorization processes through interoperability, electronic prior authorization (ePA) standards, improved transparency, and more consistent communication requirements from insurers. Prior authorization and the related processes and requirements cause significant administrative burdens and patient care delays, particularly for pharmaceuticals. Extending the ePA standards established in CMS-0057 to drugs covered under the medical is a welcome improvement.
