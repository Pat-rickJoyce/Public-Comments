## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AstraZeneca broadly supports the proposed rule as a critical and long-overdue step toward closing the regulatory gap between medical services and prescription drugs in prior authorization. The company provides highly detailed recommendations across nearly every major provision, including: supporting the October 1, 2027 implementation date; recommending a uniform 24-hour PA decision standard across all payer markets; urging strong transparency and AI guardrails for denial rationales; advocating for plan-level and point-of-sale reporting; and calling for robust safeguards for rare disease patients who face disproportionate harm from PA delays and step therapy requirements. AstraZeneca also recommends additional reforms not directly covered by the proposed rule, including prohibitions on retroactive denials, white-bagging/brown-bagging mandates, and inadequate single case agreement processes.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: PA-DRUG-PHARMACY — NCPDP Standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with enhancements
- **Rationale:** Broad adoption of NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB standards will improve transparency, reduce administrative inefficiency, and facilitate timely prescribing decisions. Critical for rare disease patients who face complex PA requirements and community pharmacy unfamiliarity.
- **Specific request:** (1) Require weekly F&B data updates to ensure accuracy; (2) clarify that all claim-level restrictions resulting in rejected/modified claims (including POS claim edits) are treated as PA transactions subject to NCPDP standards and disclosure.

**Topic 2: PA-DRUG-MEDICAL — Prior Authorization API**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support with enhancements
- **Rationale:** PA API will reduce administrative burden, improve turnaround times, and is especially important for rare disease therapies (infused enzymes, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapies) that currently use manual multi-week processes.
- **Specific request:** Adopt safeguards to prevent PA from being used to steer patients to payer-preferred (often payer-affiliated) sites of care; require site-of-care conditions to be disclosed via PA API and subject to same justification and appeal rights as denials.

**Topic 3: Implementation Timeline and Extension Pathways**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** October 1, 2027 provides a reasonable timeframe; extension pathway for state Medicaid/CHIP FFS is appropriate.
- **Specific request:** Limit extensions to no more than 6 months total; require showing of demonstrable technical/operational barriers; allow QHP issuers to request exceptions through annual certification process under 45 CFR 156.223(h).

**Topic 4: Compliance Certification and Delegated Entity Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payers could maintain nominal compliance while relying on older standards in live transactions; delegated entities (PBMs, UM vendors) administer core PA functions but may not be subject to same standards.
- **Specific request:** Require compliance certification based on live transaction data (not just system support); require transaction-level auditing; establish dedicated compliance audit mechanism extending to PBMs and UM vendors.

**Topic 5: Concurrent Review**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Concurrent review functions as de facto PA but may not be subject to same transparency/timeliness standards; particularly burdensome for rare disease patients requiring lifelong therapy with frequent reauthorizations.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that concurrent review is subject to same transparency, electronic workflow, and timeliness standards; F&B files should indicate reauthorization requirements.

**Topic 6: Access APIs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL (implicitly), API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with enhancements
- **Rationale:** Making PA request and decision data available through Access APIs reduces duplicative submissions and improves treatment planning; formulary removal from Provider/Payer-to-Payer APIs should be sequenced carefully.
- **Specific request:** (1) Phase formulary removal only after PA API is fully operational; (2) include additional data elements: decision-making entity identity, step therapy status, authorization end dates, clinical criteria version applied; (3) require near-real-time updates within 12 hours of determination; (4) define covered drug scope broadly and explicitly, preventing carve-outs for biosimilars, compounded drugs, vaccines.

**Topic 7: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify (strengthen)
- **Rationale:** Varying market-specific timeframes create complexity and risk slower routing; 72-hour standard is insufficient for oncology, rare disease, and acute infectious disease patients where delays cause irreversible harm.
- **Specific request:** Adopt uniform 24-hour standard across all impacted payers including MA and Part D; if not adopted, extend 24-hour standard to all Medicaid drugs and establish default expedited pathways for oncology, rare disease, and acute infectious disease categories; establish 5-business-day outer limit on total PA decision time; limit pends to one occurrence with documented reason; auto-approve if qualified review cannot be completed in 24 hours.

**Topic 8: PA Denial Reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with significant enhancements
- **Rationale:** Existing denial communications are vague and insufficient; rare disease specialists cannot effectively appeal without knowing specific clinical criteria; AI is increasingly used in PA decisions without disclosure or qualified review.
- **Specific request:** (1) Send denial rationales directly to both providers AND patients; (2) require: specific criterion not met, evidence standard/guideline applied, documentation needed for approval; (3) prohibit withholding criteria on proprietary grounds; (4) develop standardized denial categories with patient advocate and specialist input; (5) require disclosure of AI use; (6) require AI-influenced denials to be reviewed by specialty-trained clinician; (7) apply rationale requirements to all denial types including administrative/technical.

**Topic 9: API Usage Metrics (Payer Reporting to CMS)**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with enhancements
- **Rationale:** Contract/issuer-level aggregation masks variation; metrics should reflect actual patient access impact, not just utilization counts.
- **Specific request:** (1) Plan-level reporting; (2) add access/performance measures stratified by therapeutic area and drug type, overturn rates, time-to-decision, step therapy failure rates; (3) CMS to publish annual analysis; (4) define reportable transactions broadly to include claims edits, concurrent review, re-authorization, formulary exceptions.

**Topic 10: Public Reporting of Rx PA Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING, API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with significant enhancements
- **Specific request:** (1) Plan-level granularity; (2) stratification by drug type and therapeutic area with rare disease reported separately; (3) include POS claim rejections; (4) extend to Medicare Part D; (5) annual CMS analysis and public publication; (6) sub-reporting for delegated entities; (7) authorization duration by drug class; (8) distinguish conditional approvals; (9) require public disclosure of UM policies and clinical criteria in machine-readable format.

**Topic 11: Step Therapy (RFI)**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support with comprehensive reform framework
- **Specific request:** Transparent disclosure of step therapy protocols; honor outcomes across plan transitions; automatic exceptions on prescriber attestation; prohibit when no alternative exists; prohibit plan-year resets; prohibit mid-year imposition on stable patients; establish federal \"previously failed\" definition; use collected data to develop evidence-based guardrails.

**Topic 12: Other Prior Authorization Reforms**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Recommend additional actions
- **Specific request:** Guardrails for single case agreements; prohibit retroactive denials post-dispensing; require good-faith outreach before administrative denials; peer-to-peer review within 24 hours of denial; apply PA requirements to copay tier exceptions; prohibit white-bagging and brown-bagging mandates.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vague denial rationales harm rare disease patients; POS claim edits function as PA without transparency; nominal compliance gap (standards supported but not used in live transactions); delegated entity noncompliance undermines payer-level requirements; site-of-care steering through PA conditions serves financial rather than clinical interests; white/brown-bagging disrupts care quality and integrity.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** 90%+ of physicians report PA delays negatively impact outcomes; nearly 1 in 4 report PA led to serious adverse event; 75%+ of abandoning patients fail to initiate alternative within 90 days; practices spend ~$68,000/physician/year; aHUS patients face irreversible kidney damage within 48-72 hours; PNH patients face life-threatening thrombosis during review periods.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Delegated entity compliance gaps; live-transaction vs. nominal compliance gap; concurrent review not clearly covered; Access API data currency insufficient for time-sensitive therapies; POS claim edits excluded from current framework.

---

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** 90%+ of physicians report PA delays negatively impact outcomes (AMA 2024/2026 surveys); nearly 1 in 4 physicians report PA led to serious adverse event (AMA May 2026); 75%+ of abandoning patients fail to initiate alternative within 90 days; $68,000/physician/year on health plan interactions; 39 PA requests/physician/week (~13 hours); 40% hired dedicated PA staff; 88%+ report PA contributes to burnout; 75% of dermatology denial letters did not identify decision maker.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA 2024 and May 2026 Prior Authorization Physician Surveys; NORD 2023 barriers report; Loirat & Frémeaux-Bacchi 2011 (aHUS); Legendre et al. 2013 NEJM (eculizumab); Ismail et al. 2023 J. Managed Care & Specialty Pharmacy; Parker et al. 2005 Blood (PNH); Barbieri & Bader 2025 (dermatology denial transparency); multiple AMA Council on Medical Service reports.
- **Examples provided:** aHUS — irreversible kidney damage within 48-72 hours; PNH — life-threatening thrombosis during review; complement-mediated disease patients require lifelong therapy with frequent reauthorization; psychiatric medications PA leads to higher hospitalization/incarceration; buprenorphine-naloxone PA removal increases use; novel anticoagulant PA restriction increases stroke risk.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Rare disease patients (disproportionate), oncology patients, providers (administrative burden reduction), PBMs (compliance extension), UM vendors, pharmacies (retroactive denial risk), payers, CMS oversight
- **Economic implications:** PA delays create $68,000+/physician/year administrative cost; PA reform could reduce abandonment rates and downstream hospitalization; plan-level reporting needed to identify outlier payer behavior
- **Technical feasibility:** Supports October 1, 2027 as reasonable; sequenced formulary API removal important; near-real-time API updates technically achievable; POS edits need definitional clarification only
- **Patient care impact:** Very high — aHUS, PNH, and rare disease patients face irreversible harm; treatment abandonment rate is very high; step therapy forces repeated treatment failures; retroactive denials disrupt established therapy; white/brown-bagging compromises drug integrity

## 11. KEY QUOTES

1. "For patients with rare diseases, prior authorization delays can be particularly devastating. Many rare diseases are progressive, meaning that delays of even days or weeks can result in irreversible organ damage, permanent disability, or death." - Context: Establishing clinical urgency of PA reform for rare disease patients.
2. "Absent clear guidance, these claim-level restrictions may fall outside the scope of prior authorization requirements, despite having the same functional impact on patient access. This creates a risk that utilization management controls could be shifted into the claims adjudication process, where they are less visible and not subject to the same safeguards." - Context: Recommending POS claim edits be treated as PA transactions.
3. "It is not sufficient for payers to provide boilerplate explanations that fail to communicate the reason behind the denial." - Context: Advocating for specific, actionable denial rationales.
4. "Without greater visibility into authorization length, approval rates alone may present an incomplete or misleading picture of access. In particular, short-duration authorizations may increase the volume of repeat requests while maintaining superficially favorable approval rates." - Context: Requesting public reporting of authorization duration to reveal access barriers masked by approval rate statistics.
