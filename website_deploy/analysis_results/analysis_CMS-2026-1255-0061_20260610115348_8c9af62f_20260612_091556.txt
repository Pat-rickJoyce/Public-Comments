- **Stakeholder type:** Individual; Advocacy Group perspective

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter asks CMS to address prior authorization barriers to laboratory testing, especially oncology, biomarker, pathology, genetic, and other time-sensitive diagnostics. She supports PA modernization but urges CMS to prohibit denials based only on authorization timing, allow retroactive approvals after specimen collection, prevent repeat blood draws, expedite diagnostic pathways, and shield patients from surprise costs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy to time-sensitive care; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by implication; HEALTH-EQUITY-related general RFI issues.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** None. The comment is clinical and patient-access focused, not standards-focused.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Retroactive PA for laboratory specimens**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Lab specimens may be collected in a provider office and processed elsewhere; tying authorization to the collection date can cause denials despite clinical validity.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to allow PA approvals to be processed after specimen collection when clinically appropriate.

**Topic 2: Timing-based denials**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Oppose/Modify
- **Rationale:** Denying medically necessary tests solely because paperwork was incomplete before collection creates avoidable delays and repeat procedures.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials based only on failure to finalize paperwork before the specimen draw date.

**Topic 3: Redundant testing and patient burden**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Discarding viable specimens and requiring repeat blood draws is clinically inefficient and burdens patients with serious conditions.
- **Specific request:** Prevent forced repeat testing when a valid specimen is already available.

**Topic 4: Expedited diagnostic pathways**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics guide treatment, surveillance, and risk assessment; delayed testing can delay care decisions.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited pathways and faster turnaround for oncology, biomarker, genetic, and other time-sensitive diagnostics.

**Topic 5: Financial protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients acting in good faith should not face surprise out-of-pocket liability caused by administrative timing problems.
- **Specific request:** Insulate patients from financial liability when PA delays or timing rules cause coverage disputes.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Responds to a general RFI on laboratory testing access by urging CMS to align prior authorization rules with clinical specimen workflows and time-sensitive diagnostics.
- **S20:** By analogy, supports expedited decision pathways where delays affect urgent oncology and diagnostic care.
- **S16:** Implies timing-only denials are not clinically meaningful denial reasons and should not be permitted for medically necessary tests.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow prior authorizations for laboratory testing to be approved after specimen collection.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on paperwork not being finalized before the specimen draw date.
3. Prevent forced repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.
4. Establish expedited pathways for oncology, biomarker, genetic, and other time-sensitive diagnostics.
5. Protect patients from surprise out-of-pocket costs when they acted in good faith.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rigid PA timing rules, delays in laboratory testing, denials tied to collection date, repeat specimen collection, and patient financial exposure.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed diagnosis, delayed treatment decisions, repeated blood draws, discarded viable specimens, disproportionate burdens on cancer, rare disease, rural, and low-income patients.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative lag, mismatch between specimen collection date and processing workflow, and plan rules that do not reflect real-world laboratory operations.
