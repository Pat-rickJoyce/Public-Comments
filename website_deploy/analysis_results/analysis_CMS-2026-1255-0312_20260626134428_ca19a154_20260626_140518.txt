- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** FQHC headquartered in Washington County, Oregon; nine clinics and two school-based health centers (SBHCs); served 25,000 patients through 74,000 visits in 2025; serves low-income, communities of color, agricultural workers, and individuals experiencing homelessness; pharmacy contact Michele Taney, PharmD

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NHC strongly supports the proposed rule's electronic prior authorization and interoperability goals, viewing them as critical for the medically underserved populations CHCs serve. However, the organization raises extensive safety-net-specific concerns including: vendor readiness gaps, 340B pharmacy workflow complexity, medical/pharmacy benefit classification ambiguity, cybersecurity resource constraints, HTI-5 rule conflicts creating a compliance timing gap, and the need for dedicated federal support and technical assistance for FQHCs. NHC opposes removing drug formulary information from Provider Access/Payer-to-Payer APIs and urges automatic approval for missed PA deadlines.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: API Interoperability — Vendor Readiness and Payer Standardization**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHCs rely on limited EHR vendors and third-party tech partners; absent vendor readiness requirements, CHCs may face compliance gaps despite acting in good faith; inconsistent payer API rollouts will undermine efficiency goals.
- **Specific request:** Clarify providers won't be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse/EHR failures; coordinate CMS implementation timelines with ONC certification; require uniform minimum implementation standards across MCOs and QHP issuers.

**Topic 2: Cybersecurity and IT Resource Concerns**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** Expansion of interoperable exchange increases cybersecurity exposure; CHCs lack dedicated cybersecurity teams; unfunded compliance, vendor upgrade, and API integration costs would harm safety-net providers.
- **Specific request:** CMS should provide technical assistance, cybersecurity/IT workforce development support, and dedicated health IT funding for CHCs.

**Topic 3: Medical/Pharmacy Benefit Classification**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHCs face burden managing dual workflows; benefit classification is not always clear in real time (e.g., extended-release buprenorphine: pharmacy benefit when dispensed, medical benefit when administered in-clinic).
- **Specific request:** Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension); clarify whether PA approval under one benefit pathway applies to the alternate pathway.

**Topic 4: 340B Pharmacy Workflow Complexity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Over 65% of CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy; ePA requires coordination across EHR, pharmacy management systems, contract pharmacies, TPAs, and payer APIs — systems that aren't always interoperable; errors in 340B workflows risk medication access and financial integrity.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with Office of Pharmacy Affairs (OPA) for targeted technical assistance, implementation guidance, and funding support.

**Topic 5: Proactive PA Expiration Notifications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify (additional requirement)
- **Rationale:** Evidence shows proactive expiration outreach achieves 87% positive outcomes vs. 25% without notification.
- **Specific request:** Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications to providers via FHIR-based alerts.

**Topic 6: ePA Without Concurrent PA Reform**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Peer-reviewed evidence shows ePA implementation alone increased PA volumes and slightly decreased medication fill rates; ePA may accelerate rather than fix an inefficient process.
- **Specific request:** Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction requirements including Gold Carding and sunset provisions for high-approval-rate PA categories.

**Topic 7: PA Decision Timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Delays disproportionately affect CHC patients; proposed 24-hour standard and 72-hour QHP standard represent meaningful improvement.
- **Specific request:** Add automatic approval for requests not decided within required timeframes.

**Topic 8: Denial Reasons and Public Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support with expansion
- **Rationale:** Specific denial reasons allow CHCs to efficiently correct submissions; centralized public metrics would improve accountability.
- **Specific request:** Centralized CMS website publication; expand metrics to include first-pass approval rates, denial rates by service type, and denials overturned due to inadequate denial notices.

**Topic 9: API Endpoint Reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CHC IT departments too small to manually search each payer's website; centralized publication maximizes utility.
- **Specific request:** Publish as machine-readable files; prioritize ease of access for safety-net/rural providers.

**Topic 10: Drug Formulary Information Removal**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose
- **Rationale:** Formulary lists change rapidly; payer website access is not reliable or real-time; CHC providers serving multiple payers cannot conduct manual lookups at point of care.
- **Specific request:** CMS should reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 11: HIPAA PA Standards — X12 to FHIR Transition**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CHCs must support both X12 6020 and FHIR/CDex simultaneously during transition; limited IT staff makes dual-standard period especially burdensome.
- **Specific request:** Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover CMS-0062-P transition period.

**Topic 12: CDex Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modification
- **Rationale:** CDex creates mechanism for iterative, escalating payer data requests that increase provider burden.
- **Specific request:** (1) Require all documentation specified in initial CDex request; (2) limit to two solicited-attachment requests per PA transaction; (3) establish maximum payer response timeframes after receiving attachments.

**Topic 13: Gold Carding**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** CMS already implemented DMEPOS gold carding at 90% threshold; CHCs with demonstrated compliance should receive exemptions; reduces unreimbursed PA staff time and costs.
- **Specific request:** Formal guidance recommending payer gold carding; extend to all items/services, not just specific categories.

**Topic 14: ONC/HTI-5 Harmonization**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** HTI-5 removal of 34 certification criteria may affect ePA supporting infrastructure; EHR certification deadlines (Dec 2027-Jan 2028) create 3-month gap after payer compliance date (Oct 2027) when certified EHRs may be unavailable.
- **Specific request:** CMS and ONC issue joint implementation guidance; publish formal harmonization assessment before CMS-0062-P final rule; clarify providers not non-compliant for missing unavailable certified technology.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Clarify providers won't be penalized for vendor/clearinghouse/EHR failures outside provider control.
2. Coordinate implementation timelines with ONC certification; establish readiness validation before enforcement.
3. Require uniform minimum implementation standards across MCOs and QHP issuers.
4. Provide technical assistance, cybersecurity/IT workforce development, and health IT funding for CHCs.
5. Require payers to expose benefit classification via Provider Access API (CRD Coverage Information extension).
6. Clarify PA approval portability across dual-pathway drugs.
7. Coordinate with OPA for CHC 340B pharmacy workflow technical assistance and funding.
8. Mandate proactive PA expiration notifications via FHIR-based alerts.
9. Pair ePA mandates with PA reduction requirements including Gold Carding and sunset provisions.
10. Require automatic approval for PA requests not decided within required timeframes.
11. Publish PA metrics in centralized, standardized CMS website format.
12. Expand reporting to include first-pass approval rates, denial rates by category, and overturned denials due to inadequate notices.
13. Publish API endpoint information as machine-readable files; prioritize safety-net/rural access.
14. Add API usage metrics: successful/failed request rates, structured vs. unstructured data, workflow impact, API costs.
15. Reconsider removing drug formulary information from Provider Access and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
16. Extend February 28, 2024 enforcement discretion to cover CMS-0062-P transition period.
17. Require initial CDex requests to specify all documentation; limit to two solicited-attachment requests per PA transaction; set maximum payer response timeframes.
18. Issue formal guidance recommending payer gold carding for all items/services.
19. Publish joint CMS-ONC harmonization assessment before final rule.
20. Prioritize safety-net providers for federal matching funds to cover implementation costs.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Vendor readiness gaps; cybersecurity exposure without resources; medical/pharmacy benefit classification ambiguity; 340B workflow complexity; dual-standard transition period; HTI-5 certification timing gap creating 3-month window where payer APIs operational but certified EHRs unavailable.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** CHC compliance failures from vendor issues; unfunded cybersecurity costs; ePA without PA reform may accelerate inefficient process; 340B workflow errors; formulary data removal will harm CHC prescribing decisions.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited CHC IT staff; EHR/PMS interoperability gaps; 340B system complexity; lack of cybersecurity teams; negative sector operating margins; nearly half of CHCs with fewer than 90 days cash on hand.

---

- **Data or statistics cited:** 94% of physicians report PA delays patient care (AMA); average PA wait ~3 days, one-third >1 week; 53 million MA PA determinations in 2024 (KFF); 90%+ CHC patients below 200% FPL; 65%+ CHCs use 340B contract pharmacy; proactive PA expiration outreach achieves 87% positive outcomes vs. 25% without; ePA associated with higher PA volumes and slightly decreased fill rates.
- **Studies or research referenced:** AMA PA survey; KFF MA PA 2024; MGMA PA landscape 2025; NACHC 2025 NTTAP HIT Needs Assessment; Lauffenburger JC et al. J Am Med Inform Assoc. 2021; Salzbrenner SG et al. J Manag Care Spec Pharm. 2022; Vishwanath S et al. Am J Manag Care. 2021; 2024 HRSA UDS data.
- **Examples provided:** Extended-release buprenorphine dual-benefit classification; site-specific patient volume data; CHC sector financial stress data.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** FQHCs/CHCs; medically underserved patients (low-income, homeless, agricultural workers, communities of color, rural); 340B program participants; EHR and PMS vendors serving safety-net markets.
- **Economic implications:** CHC sector margins negative; unfunded cybersecurity/vendor costs would be severely burdensome; gold carding and automatic approval would reduce administrative costs for resource-constrained CHCs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Multiple safety-net-specific barriers: EHR/PMS gaps, 340B complexity, limited cybersecurity teams, HTI-5 timing gap, lack of FHIR integration in pharmacy systems.
- **Patient care impact:** Delays disproportionately harm CHC patients with non-clinical health factors; 340B medication access depends on smooth PA workflows; formulary data removal would harm prescribing for providers without manual lookup capacity.
