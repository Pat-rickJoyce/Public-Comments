# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** UnitedHealth Group (UHG), signed by the CEO, Optum Insight.
- **Stakeholder type:** Payer / Technology Vendor / Health Services Organization
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** UHG describes UnitedHealthcare as its health benefits business and Optum as its health services business, working with employers, providers, and governments. It also references operational experience implementing ePA, payer APIs, pharmacy infrastructure, clearinghouse connectivity, and cybersecurity controls.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

UHG supports CMS' overall effort to standardize electronic prior authorization for drugs, but urges a nuanced, scalable implementation strategy that preserves existing pharmacy infrastructure and recognizes real-world technology deployment constraints. Its core recommendations are to support unexpired NCPDP standards, permit multi-standard and network-based approaches, extend implementation to up to 24 months after the final rule, retain a uniform March 31 reporting deadline, refine endpoint reporting, and phase any HIPAA FHIR transition without disrupting X12-based workflows.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL/RFI on resiliency and payer APIs.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 FR 19890; 42 CFR 423.160; 45 CFR 170.215(a); 45 CFR 162.1301; 45 CFR 162.1201; 45 CFR 170.205(b), 170.205(u), 170.205(c).
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP SCRIPT, NCPDP Formulary & Benefit, NCPDP Real-Time Prescription Benefit, FHIR, FHIR Endpoint Resource, FHIR APIs, CRD, X12 270/271, X12 278, TEFCA, HIPAA, HITRUST, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-63-4, JSON APIs, C-CDA.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: NCPDP standards for pharmacy-benefit drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB are widely adopted, federally mandated under Medicare Part D for their functions, and can reduce burden by creating consistency across plan types.
- **Specific request:** Require impacted payers to support unexpired versions of NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB.

**Topic 2: Multi-standard pathway identification**
- **Provision ID:** MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** No single tool currently has full visibility into both pharmacy- and medical-benefit coverage. NCPDP F&B/RTPB can provide pharmacy benefit information and may indicate possible medical-benefit coverage, while X12 270/271 guidance may support medical-benefit drug information but not pharmacy benefit data.
- **Specific request:** Permit multi-standard approaches that use existing tools in tandem to determine the appropriate PA pathway.

**Topic 3: Scalable connectivity through TEFCA and existing networks**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** One-to-one FHIR integrations between payers/PBMs and health systems require separate contracting, implementation, testing, and maintenance and may slow adoption.
- **Specific request:** Allow or encourage scalable data exchange for PA through TEFCA, existing NCPDP connectivity, clearinghouses, and network-based approaches rather than relying on point-to-point connections.

**Topic 4: Compliance timeline**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE / PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** End-to-end workflow integration across payers, providers, EHRs, pharmacies, PBMs, and trading partners requires coordinated readiness and operationalization, not just deployment.
- **Specific request:** Extend the implementation timeline to up to 24 months after publication of the final rule.

**Topic 5: Public PA metrics deadline**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** State-specific Medicaid/CHIP managed care reporting deadlines would fragment reporting periods, reduce transparency, and make cross-payer comparisons harder.
- **Specific request:** Retain the uniform March 31 annual deadline for all impacted payers.

**Topic 6: Endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payer endpoint structures are complex and may vary by plan, product, benefit structure, delegated vendor, service type, and drug pathway.
- **Specific request:** Create a public repository that supports hierarchical mapping of multiple endpoints to a payer entity, plan-level distinctions, and use-case-specific endpoints; pair endpoint transparency with scalable integration strategies.

**Topic 7: HIPAA FHIR prior authorization standards**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** UHG supports FHIR-based workflows but warns that referral transactions are currently processed at scale using X12 278, while FHIR-based referral workflows have not yet been tested at comparable scale.
- **Specific request:** Use pilot testing and phased adoption before changing referral standards; preserve existing transaction frameworks where effective; maintain clear transaction boundaries.

**Topic 8: Eligibility transaction boundaries**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Existing HIPAA eligibility transactions under 45 CFR 162.1201 are intended for eligibility, coverage, and benefits and do not expressly include PA requirements. The intent of an inquiry may be unclear at scheduling or for ancillary services.
- **Specific request:** Clarify boundaries between eligibility and PA discovery, or create a distinct transaction type for determining whether PA is required.

**Topic 9: Later standards versions**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Oppose newer-than-proposed NCPDP versions
- **Rationale:** Industry is already mid-transition to the proposed NCPDP versions under Part D deadlines; requiring newer versions would obsolete ongoing implementation work and increase costs.
- **Specific request:** Do not name versions newer than NCPDP SCRIPT 2023011, F&B v60, and RTPB v13 in the final rule.

**Topic 10: Cybersecurity and resiliency RFI**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Cyber threats are increasing, but new mandates should be flexible, risk-based, harmonized, and not duplicative.
- **Specific request:** Reauthorize CISA, establish minimum cyber standards with financial/technical support, harmonize with HIPAA/HITRUST/NIST, embed security-by-design in interoperability, streamline reporting, enforce minimum necessary data sharing, and require provider/delegate identity proofing for ePA.

**Topic 11: Payer API implementation and certification RFI**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFI / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify/Oppose mandatory certification
- **Rationale:** Certification does not guarantee real-world performance, adds cost, and may stifle innovation. FHIR APIs are not plug-and-play, and multiple IG versions create implementation difficulty.
- **Specific request:** Focus on outcomes, data liquidity, existing pharmacy infrastructure, end-to-end interoperability testing, real-world audits, and voluntary or narrowly scoped certification only if CMS proceeds.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S2/S3:** Supports a measured versioning approach and cautions against supporting multiple FHIR IG versions where versions are not backward compatible; recommends coalescing to a single version with adequate adoption time.
- **S12:** Discusses technical challenges for medical-benefit drug PA, particularly need for clinical data at scale and the limitations of point-to-point FHIR connections.
- **S14:** Supports requiring NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB for pharmacy-benefit drugs for Medicaid/CHIP and QHPs.
- **S15:** Responds directly to medical-versus-pharmacy benefit pathway questions by explaining no single tool can definitively identify both pathways and recommending multi-standard approaches.
- **S21:** Recommends an implementation timeline of up to 24 months after final rule publication rather than the proposed October 1, 2027 compliance date.
- **S22:** Notes that newer NCPDP versions would create significant additional development, testing, and deployment costs.
- **S23/S24/S26/S27/S33:** Supports endpoint reporting but asks CMS to account for multiple endpoints, use cases, plan/product distinctions, and real-world integration/security needs.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Recommends retaining a uniform March 31 deadline for PA metrics and Patient Access API usage metrics to preserve comparability.
- **S44:** Supports moving toward FHIR-based prior authorization workflows where they improve efficiency and reduce burden.
- **S45/S46:** Recommends preserving X12-based referral workflows until FHIR referral workflows are pilot-tested and phased in; stresses coexistence and transition planning.
- **S47/S53:** Requests phased adoption and clear transition boundaries for HIPAA standards.
- **S50/S54/S55:** In the payer API RFI, recommends real-world audits and end-to-end testing instead of relying primarily on certification.
- **S60/S61/S63/S65:** Recommends not adopting newer-than-proposed NCPDP versions and coordinating version transitions with current Part D implementation.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on resiliency and payer APIs by recommending risk-based cybersecurity, networked exchange, clearinghouse portability, security-by-design, and outcome-based API implementation.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize support for unexpired NCPDP SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB standards.
2. Permit multi-standard approaches to determine medical- versus pharmacy-benefit PA pathways.
3. Enable scalable PA data exchange through TEFCA, existing NCPDP connectivity, clearinghouses, and health information networks.
4. Extend implementation to up to 24 months after publication of the final rule.
5. Retain the March 31 annual deadline for Medicaid/CHIP managed care PA metrics and Patient Access API usage metrics.
6. Build a hierarchical, flexible public endpoint repository that supports plan-level and use-case-specific endpoint mapping.
7. Phase any HIPAA transition to FHIR-based PA standards and avoid disrupting functioning X12 referral workflows without pilots.
8. Clarify boundaries between eligibility transactions and PA requirement discovery or establish a distinct PA discovery transaction.
9. Do not adopt NCPDP versions newer than SCRIPT 2023011, F&B v60, and RTPB v13.
10. Use voluntary, flexible, risk-based cybersecurity approaches aligned with existing frameworks.
11. Incorporate provider and delegate identity proofing as an ePA API access condition.
12. Use end-to-end real-world interoperability testing and audits rather than mandatory payer API certification.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Fragmented implementation, unrealistic timelines, point-to-point scalability, unclear transaction boundaries, premature standards version changes, duplicative cybersecurity requirements, and certification that fails to reflect real-world performance.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Increased costs, obsolete implementation work, reduced comparability of metrics, security risk from expanded endpoints, disrupted referral workflows, provider confusion, claims submission problems, and delayed adoption.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** One-to-one FHIR connections, multiple endpoint structures, health system contracting/testing burdens, EHR and trading partner readiness, clearinghouse enrollment constraints, multiple FHIR IG versions, vendor capacity, and industry transition to NCPDP Part D standards.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None as numeric health outcome data in the main proposal section, but the comment cites Part D compliance dates for F&B v60/RTPB v13 (January 1, 2027) and SCRIPT 2023011 (January 1, 2028), and references 72 hours after confirmation as a minimum cyber incident reporting interval.
- **Studies or research referenced:** CAQH CORE 2025 guidance on X12 270/271 for medical-benefit drugs; NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0; NIST SP 800-63-4; Section 405(d) of the Cybersecurity Act of 2015; Cooperative Exchange Trebuchet Pilot Cohort.
- **Examples provided:** Multiple payer endpoints by plan/product/vendor; HETS validation changes increasing provider burden; state Medicaid gateway TPID transfer limitations; point-to-point connection proliferation; clearinghouse-to-clearinghouse FHIR prior authorization pilot.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Payers, PBMs, Medicaid/CHIP programs, QHP issuers, providers, health systems, EHR vendors, clearinghouses, pharmacies, health IT developers, patients, and cybersecurity teams.
- **Economic implications:** UHG warns that newer standards mandates, certification, point-to-point connections, and duplicative reporting could impose significant development, testing, contracting, maintenance, and compliance costs.
- **Technical feasibility:** Mixed; UHG supports the technical direction but stresses that FHIR is not plug-and-play and scalable implementation depends on networks, trading partner readiness, version control, and realistic timelines.
- **Patient care impact:** Potentially positive if ePA reduces time to therapy and provider burden; potentially negative if fragmented or rushed implementation disrupts workflows.
