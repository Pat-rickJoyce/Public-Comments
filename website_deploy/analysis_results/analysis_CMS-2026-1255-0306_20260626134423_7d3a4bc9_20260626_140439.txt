## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** NAPCA (National Asian Pacific Center on Aging)
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National organization serving Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander (AANHPI) older adults; represented by Clayton S. Fong, President and CEO; headquartered in Seattle, WA

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NAPCA expresses full support for the proposed rule, emphasizing the particular benefits for AANHPI older adults who face compounded barriers from the current PA system due to limited English proficiency and heavy reliance on family caregivers. The organization highlights that structured denial reasons and electronic PA modernization will improve transparency, facilitate translation, and reduce administrative burdens for both patients and caregivers in this community.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS, PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY (electronic PA modernization), GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-2026-1255-0001; CMS-0061-P
- **Standards referenced:** Application Programming Interface (API); general reference to electronic prior authorization replacing fax processes

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: PA Denial Reasons and Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specific, structured denial reasons reduce confusion and improve transparency. Clearer explanations are more easily translated for non-native English speakers, helping AANHPI older adults understand their options.
- **Specific request:** Supports requirement as proposed; implicitly requests denial reasons be structured in a way that facilitates translation.

**Topic 2: Electronic Prior Authorization Modernization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Replacing fax with API-based electronic PA makes submission faster and supports more effective translation and communication of denial and appeal decisions.
- **Specific request:** No specific modifications requested.

**Topic 3: Health Equity for AANHPI Older Adults**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** AANHPI older adults with limited English proficiency face disproportionate barriers navigating PA requirements and appeals. One in eight AANHPI older adults is a caregiver (twice the general population rate), amplifying the administrative burden of PA delays.
- **Specific request:** No specific regulatory change requested; comment functions as testimonial support for the rule's equity benefits.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons; notes particular benefit for limited English proficiency patients whose families need clear, translatable explanations.
- **S17 (implicit):** Implicitly favors structured denial reason formats that facilitate translation and communication.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Support the proposed rule in its entirety.
2. Ensure PA denial reason requirements produce structured, specific explanations that can be effectively translated for non-native English speakers.
3. Prioritize electronic modernization of PA processes to replace fax-based systems that create greater barriers for limited English proficiency patients.

- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Disease progression and long-term health damage from PA delays; heavy caregiver burden for family members managing PA coordination.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** None cited; comment is entirely supportive.

Re: CMS-0061-P: 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule

Dear Dr. Oz,

NAPCA submits this comment in support of the proposed rule CMS-2026-1255-0001. This rule is important because it will introduce faster response times, modernization in the healthcare system, and help Asian American, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander (AANHPI) older adults achieve better health outcomes.
The prior authorization process can harm patient health by delaying access to timely, high-quality care. It is especially crucial for our elders to receive this care as quickly as possible so there aren't prolonged symptoms, disease progression, or long-term damage to their health due to delays in care.
According to KFF, seven out of ten older adults found prior authorization a major burden in accessing healthcare. These challenges may be even greater for AANHPI older adults who have limited English proficiency and may have difficulty understanding prior authorization requirements or navigating the appeals process...
