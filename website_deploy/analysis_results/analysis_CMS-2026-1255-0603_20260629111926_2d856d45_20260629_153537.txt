ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
Given the important role that evidence-based clinical guidelines play in supporting 
comprehensive, cost-effective care, NCCN encourages CMS to consider these factors when 
finalizing requirements related to explanations for prior authorization denials. Providing 
providers with clear, detailed, and evidence-based rationales for denials will better enable them 
to submit appropriate supporting documentation, pursue informed appeals, or identify alternative 
treatment approaches that are covered and guideline adherent. 
Timeframe for Communicating Prior Authorization Decisions 
CMS is proposing to shorten provider notification timeframes for prior authorization decisions 
for QHP issuers on the FFEs to align with the timeframes finalized for other impacted payers in 
the 2024 CMS Interoperability and Prior Authorization final rule. CMS proposes to require QHP 
issuers on FFEs to notify the requesting provider of their decision for standard prior 
authorization requests for non-drug items and services as expeditiously as the enrollee's health 
condition requires, but no later than 7 calendar days after receiving the request. For prescription 
drugs, CMS proposes to require QHP issuers to notify the requesting provider no later than 72 
hours after receiving a standard prior authorization request, and no later than 24 hours for 
expedited prior authorization requests. 
NCCN supports CMS' efforts to establish more timely and consistent prior authorization 
notification timeframes and recognizes the importance of reducing delays in clinical decision­
making. Timely communication of prior authorization decisions is particularly critical in 
oncology, where treatment delays can have significant and measurable impacts on patient 
outcomes. Even relatively short delays in cancer treatment have been associated with increased 
mortality risk, underscoring the urgency of ensuring that administrative processes do not create 
unnecessary hurdles for timely access to care.
