# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** erika hanson brown, Founder and First KAPA / KAPA-in-Chief, One Cancer Place.
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Long-term cancer survivor; founder of a cancer-focused nonprofit; patient advocacy experience.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

One Cancer Place urges CMS to address prior authorization rules that deny or delay laboratory testing because authorization was not finalized by the specimen collection date. The commenter asks CMS to permit retroactive approvals, prohibit timing-only denials, prevent repeat blood draws when viable specimens exist, expedite oncology diagnostics, standardize documentation, and shield patients from surprise bills.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy for expedited time-sensitive decisions; PATIENT-ACCESS theme.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** No FHIR, NCPDP, HL7, or other technical standards referenced.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Retroactive authorization for lab specimens**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues that real-world lab workflows often involve blood being drawn before authorization is finalized, especially for high-complexity biomarker and specialty diagnostic tests.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to allow prior authorization submission and approval after specimen collection when the lab did not control the draw timing.

**Topic 2: Prohibition on timing-based denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** A medically necessary test should not be denied solely because the authorization date and collection date do not align.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit denials or delays based only on the authorization not being finalized on or before collection.

**Topic 3: Avoiding redundant testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeat blood draws can delay cancer treatment decisions, clinical trial eligibility, and diagnosis, while imposing travel, work, childcare, and illness-related burdens.
- **Specific request:** Do not require patients to repeat a blood draw when a viable specimen already exists and is ready for analysis.

**Topic 4: Expedited oncology and specialty diagnostic pathways**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Time-sensitive diagnostics for cancer, rare diseases, and other serious conditions require faster turnaround because treatment decisions depend on results.
- **Specific request:** Establish expedited pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostic prior authorizations.

**Topic 5: Standard documentation and financial protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Plan-by-plan documentation variation creates administrative hurdles, and patients should not bear costs when the patient, physician, and lab acted in good faith.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to accept standard clinical documentation and protect patients from surprise bills in good-faith cases.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **GENERAL/RFI S66-S82:** The comment responds to the general RFI area concerning prior authorization and access to laboratory testing, specifically requesting safeguards for specimens collected before authorization is finalized. The exact solicitation number within S66-S82 is not identifiable from the provided text, but the substance concerns lab testing access, delays, and patient burden.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive prior authorization approvals after specimen collection when collection timing was outside the lab's control.
2. Prohibit denial or delay of medically necessary lab tests solely because authorization was not finalized by the collection date.
3. Eliminate redundant blood draws when a viable specimen already exists.
4. Establish expedited prior authorization pathways for oncology and other time-sensitive diagnostics.
5. Standardize clinical documentation accepted by health plans for medical necessity.
6. Protect patients from surprise bills when the patient, physician, and lab acted in good faith.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Timing mismatches between PA workflows and specimen collection; rejected or discarded specimens; repeat blood draws; delayed cancer care.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Treatment delays, delayed clinical trial access, emotional distress, travel burden, missed work, childcare costs, and surprise billing.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Plan-specific documentation rules, rigid collection-date authorization requirements, and slow oncology diagnostic PA turnaround.
