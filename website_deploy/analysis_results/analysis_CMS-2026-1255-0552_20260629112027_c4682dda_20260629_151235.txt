## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Insurance Watchdog Coalition (IWC); signed by Mark Merritt, Executive Director
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group / Patient advocacy organization
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** National patient advocacy organization focused on eliminating insurance barriers to medication and treatment access; cites a recent IWC-commissioned national survey.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

IWC strongly supports CMS’s proposal to require electronic prior authorization for drugs, adopt modern standards, and increase transparency, but argues the final rule needs stronger enforcement and patient protections. It recommends universal adoption, a uniform 24-hour maximum for all drug PA requests, detailed denial justifications to both patients and providers, richer plan-level metrics including Medicare Part D, binding step therapy protections, and safeguards against clock manipulation, denial substitution, and formulary avoidance.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; TRANSPARENCY; GENERAL/RFIs
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P; no specific CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Electronic prior authorization infrastructure; modern technical standards; real-time electronic exchange; drug coverage verification, PA submission, and status tracking. No specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard names cited.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Universal ePA infrastructure and enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL / PA-DRUG-PHARMACY / FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with stronger enforcement
- **Rationale:** Phone, fax, and other outdated PA methods delay access to medications; electronic exchange will help patients and providers only if adoption is universal rather than voluntary.
- **Specific request:** CMS should maintain firm implementation timelines and establish clear enforcement mechanisms for insurers and payers that fail to meet ePA standards.

**Topic 2: Drug PA decision timelines**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify / Strengthen
- **Rationale:** IWC supports 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard timelines but says delays for supposedly non-urgent drugs can still cause serious harm.
- **Specific request:** Require a uniform 24-hour maximum timeline for all drug PA requests, not only urgent requests.

**Topic 3: Denial substitution and oversight**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Faster decision timelines could cause insurers to increase denials rather than improve access.
- **Specific request:** CMS should track and report denial rates before and after implementation, at the plan level, to detect whether payers are substituting denials for delays.

**Topic 4: Detailed denial justifications to patients and providers**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify / Strengthen
- **Rationale:** Vague denial language and provider-only communications leave patients unable to understand or appeal decisions.
- **Specific request:** Require detailed denial justifications for every denial, including initial denials, delivered simultaneously to the prescribing provider and patient across all markets.

**Topic 5: Meaningful metrics and public reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS / TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** API usage metrics alone do not show whether the rule is helping patients; CMS needs outcome and experience metrics.
- **Specific request:** Require metrics on average submission-to-decision time, denial/appeal rates by drug category, payer and market, step therapy rates, brand/generic and therapeutic area breakdowns, initial denial rates, and overturn rates; publish them in an annual CMS public report.

**Topic 6: Medicare Part D plan-level transparency**
- **Provision ID:** TRANSPARENCY / API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Lack of Part D plan-level public reporting creates an accountability gap for beneficiaries who rely heavily on medication coverage.
- **Specific request:** Require Medicare Part D plans to publicly report prior authorization metrics at the plan level.

**Topic 7: Step therapy protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL/RFIs
- **Position:** Modify / Strengthen
- **Rationale:** Step therapy is a major patient access barrier and can be used to bypass PA decision clocks by rerouting requests into a separate process.
- **Specific request:** Include binding step therapy limits in the final rule, especially in Medicare Advantage, and require plans to honor step therapy already completed under prior coverage.

**Topic 8: Closing enforcement gaps**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL / PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS / PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Payers could technically comply while undermining the rule through urgency determinations, clock resets, denials, step therapy restarts, or formulary manipulation.
- **Specific request:** Clarify that prescribers determine urgency; define a complete request and prohibit clock resets; monitor formulary changes; flag avoidance patterns such as moving drugs to non-covered tiers.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S14/S21/S28:** Supports extending ePA to prescription drugs and real-time electronic coverage verification, submission, and status tracking; requests universal adoption and enforcement.
- **S16/S17:** Requests detailed denial justification for every denial, including initial denials, sent to both provider and patient.
- **S20/S43:** Supports 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard timelines but recommends a uniform 24-hour maximum for all drug PA requests.
- **S29/S30/S34/S35:** Recommends meaningful patient/provider outcome metrics and annual CMS public reporting, including denial, appeal, step therapy, drug category, payer, market, and overturn data.
- **S66-S82 / Step therapy RFI:** Argues that an RFI is insufficient and asks CMS to include binding step therapy protections, including honoring prior step therapy completion when patients change plans.
- **S55 (indirect):** Requests enforcement mechanisms against clock manipulation, denial substitution, formulary manipulation, and noncompliance with ePA standards.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Require universal, not voluntary, ePA adoption for drug prior authorization.
2. Maintain firm timelines with clear enforcement for payers that fail ePA standards.
3. Require a uniform 24-hour maximum decision timeline for all drug PA requests.
4. Track and report denial rates before and after implementation to detect denial substitution.
5. Require detailed denial justifications for every denial, including initial denials.
6. Send denial justifications simultaneously to the prescribing provider and patient.
7. Publish patient- and provider-facing outcome metrics, not just API usage metrics.
8. Require annual CMS public reporting on PA performance.
9. Require Medicare Part D plan-level PA metric reporting.
10. Finalize binding step therapy protections, especially in Medicare Advantage.
11. Require plans to honor step therapy already completed under prior coverage.
12. Clarify that prescribing providers determine urgency.
13. Define complete requests and prohibit decision-clock resets.
14. Monitor formulary changes for avoidance patterns.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Insurer abuse of PA, voluntary adoption gaps, delays disguised as standard requests, vague denials, lack of patient access to denial reasons, insufficient metrics, Part D accountability gaps, step therapy restarts, and enforcement loopholes.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Medication access delays, serious patient harm, increased denials, inability to appeal, repeated step therapy, formulary manipulation, and technical compliance that undermines policy intent.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Payer noncompliance, insufficient enforcement, unclear urgency designation, undefined complete request, clock manipulation, and lack of plan-level transparency.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** IWC-commissioned national survey found that 90% of Americans believe health insurance must be reformed to reduce costs and increase quality.
- **Studies or research referenced:** IWC survey; no external studies cited.
- **Examples provided:** Phone/fax/manual PA delays; non-urgent drug delays causing harm; patients unable to advocate when denial reasons go only to providers; step therapy restarts after health plan changes; drugs moved to non-covered tiers to avoid PA obligations.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, prescribing providers, insurers/payers, Medicare Part D beneficiaries, Medicare Advantage enrollees, advocates, Congress, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** IWC frames insurer control over formularies, pharmacy networks, PA, and benefit design as part of health care cost and quality concerns; specific implementation cost analysis not provided.
- **Technical feasibility:** IWC treats real-time ePA infrastructure as feasible if universally adopted and enforced.
- **Patient care impact:** Strong focus on avoiding medication delays and denials, enabling appeals, preserving continuity of therapy, and preventing payer workarounds that block access.

## 10. SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

- **Overall position:** Supportive but strongly pro-enforcement / critical of insurers
- **Tone:** Advocacy-focused, urgent, patient-centered, critical
- **Priority level:** High

## 12. THEMES

- **IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE:** IWC supports firm timelines and wants an even shorter 24-hour maximum for all drug PA decisions.
- **PROVIDER-BURDEN:** Manual PA processes burden providers and divert time from care.
- **PATIENT-ACCESS:** The comment is centered on medication access and preventing delays, denials, and step therapy restarts.
- **TECHNICAL-FEASIBILITY:** IWC supports ePA infrastructure and real-time exchange as a practical modernization.
- **TRANSPARENCY:** Detailed denial justifications, annual public metrics, and Part D plan-level reporting are key recommendations.
- **STAKEHOLDER-COORDINATION:** Patients and providers should both receive denial information and be able to act on PA decisions.
- **EXISTING-WORKFLOWS:** IWC criticizes phone, fax, and outdated PA workflows.
- **REGULATORY-AUTHORITY:** The comment asks CMS to enforce payer compliance and close avoidance mechanisms.
- **HEALTH-EQUITY:** Not expressly framed in equity terms, but patient protection and access across markets are core concerns.
