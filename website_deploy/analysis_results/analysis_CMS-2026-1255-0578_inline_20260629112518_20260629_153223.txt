## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Colorado Access supports CMS's interoperability and drug ePA goals but urges CMS to extend or phase the October 1, 2027 compliance deadline. The commenter warns that overlapping implementation of CMS-0057-F and CMS-0062-P could create system instability, provider workflow fragmentation, and medication access disruptions, especially for Medicaid members and safety-net providers.

    Comment Re: CMS-0062-P

Colorado Access is a nonprofit health plan serving Medicaid and Child Health Plan Plus (CHP+) members in Colorado. We administer benefits through a combination of internally managed services and delegated vendor arrangements, including pharmacy benefit management (PBM) services. As a Medicaid managed care organization, we strongly support CMS’s goals of improving interoperability, streamlining prior authorization (PA), and reducing administrative burden for providers and members.
We also support CMS’s proposal to extend electronic prior authorization (ePA) requirements to drugs, which is necessary to complete the transition to a modern, standardized prior authorization framework. We have significant concerns, however, regarding the proposed compliance deadline of October 1, 2027, and respectfully recommend that CMS extend or phase the implementation timeline.

Colorado Access is currently engaged in implementing the 2024 Interoperability and Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS‑0057‑F), including:

- Building and testing required FHIR APIs
- Implementing prior authorization decision timeframe requirements
- Developing reporting capabilities for transparency and API usage

Under the proposed CMS‑0062‑P timeline, plans would be expected to:

- Complete CMS‑0057‑F implementation, and
- Within less than one year, expand those systems to include drug prior authorization across both medical and pharmacy benefits

This creates a compressed and overlapping implementation cycle, increasing the likelihood of:

- Incomplete integration
- System instability
- Disruption to providers and member access

The success of ePA depends on provider and EHR adoption of standardized workflows, including:

- FHIR‑based integration for medical benefit prior authorization
- Real‑time prescription benefit tools for pharmacy workflows
