- **Author/Organization name:** Mutima Jackson-Anderson on behalf of Ruby A. Neeson Diabetes Awareness Foundation
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter asks CMS to protect patients, especially people with diabetes, from care delays and denials caused by rigid prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing. The comment supports modernization but focuses on the RFI for laboratory testing, recommending retroactive approvals, limits on timing-based denials, reduced redundant testing, expedited pathways, and financial protections.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P referenced generally; no CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Prior authorization and laboratory testing access**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Diabetes patients need timely, continuous access to laboratory data, and rigid prior authorization can cause critical care delays, denials, complications, care discontinuity, financial strain, and psychological burden.
- **Specific request:** CMS should mandate protections for urgent and time-sensitive laboratory testing, including retroactive approval and expedited pathways.

**Topic 2: Redundant testing and coordinated care**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repetitive blood draws are unnecessary when recent comprehensive laboratory results are available and accepted across coordinated care networks.
- **Specific request:** Require coordinated care networks and plans to accept recent lab results to avoid redundant testing.

**Topic 3: Patient financial protection**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients should not face unexpected billing or excess out-of-pocket costs because of prior authorization delays or administrative errors.
- **Specific request:** Cap out-of-pocket costs and shield patients from unexpected bills tied to PA failures.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Responds to a general RFI on prior authorization and access to laboratory testing. The commenter requests patient protections for urgent lab testing, diabetes-related monitoring, redundant testing, and billing consequences.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive approvals for necessary lab tests conducted during urgent care visits.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on strict calendar or timing constraints.
3. Require acceptance of recent comprehensive lab results to eliminate redundant testing.
4. Establish expedited pathways for medically necessary, time-sensitive continuous glucose monitoring and laboratory data.
5. Cap out-of-pocket costs and protect patients from unexpected billing caused by PA delays or administrative errors.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rigid prior authorization requirements delay or deny medically necessary lab testing for diabetes patients.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Severe complications, care discontinuity, financial strain, psychological strain, unexpected billing, and unnecessary blood draws.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Strict calendar/timing constraints, arbitrary retroactive denials, administrative errors, and lack of coordination among care networks.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** None.
- **Studies or research referenced:** None.
- **Examples provided:** Urgent care lab tests, diabetes laboratory monitoring, continuous glucose monitoring data, repetitive blood draws, timing-based claim denials.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Diabetes patients, vulnerable populations, patient advocates, health plans, laboratories, urgent care settings, and coordinated care networks.
- **Economic implications:** Recommended protections could reduce patient out-of-pocket exposure and unexpected billing, but may increase payer responsibility for urgent or retroactively approved lab claims.
- **Technical feasibility:** The comment does not address technical standards; operational changes would require payer policy and claims/prior authorization workflow changes.
- **Patient care impact:** Timelier lab access and reduced redundant testing could improve diabetes management and reduce complications and care discontinuity.
