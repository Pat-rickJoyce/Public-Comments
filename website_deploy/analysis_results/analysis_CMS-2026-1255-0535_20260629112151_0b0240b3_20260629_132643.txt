# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Arthritis Foundation; signed by Anna Hyde, Vice President of Advocacy and Access
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents and advocates for nearly 60 million American adults and 300,000 children living with arthritis; cites patient survey and focus group data.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Arthritis Foundation strongly supports adding prescription drugs to prior authorization interoperability requirements and supports electronic PA, NCPDP standards, API usage metrics, centralized API endpoint publication, and specific denial reasons. It asks CMS to apply 24-hour prescription drug PA timeframes broadly across MA, QHP, and Part D plans, expand patient notifications, strengthen enforcement, improve public metrics, use payer-to-payer APIs to preserve PA and step therapy history, and limit burdensome step therapy.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No specific CFR sections cited.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP standards; NCPDP SCRIPT; FHIR-based APIs; Patient Access API; Provider Access API; Payer-to-Payer API; National Provider Directory concept.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Electronic prior authorization and NCPDP standards**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Electronic PA and aligned NCPDP standards can help impacted payers process requests more efficiently.
- **Specific request:** Finalize ePA and NCPDP standards requirements, and encourage use of NCPDP standards across all applicable functions.

**Topic 2: FHIR pathways for Part D documentation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Current ePA standards do not fully solve electronic transmission of clinical documentation or uncertainty about where providers should submit it.
- **Specific request:** Allow providers to submit Part D PA requests through FHIR-based pathways connecting EHRs, pharmacy billing systems, and relevant systems, bridging NCPDP SCRIPT transactions.

**Topic 3: Uniform 24-hour prescription drug PA timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Arthritis patients experience delays, repeated PA, and worsening disease while waiting for decisions.
- **Specific request:** Apply 24-hour prescription-drug PA decision timeframes to MA, QHPs, and Part D plans; extend shortened notification timeframes to patients; clarify or replace ambiguous "expeditiously as health condition requires" language; make missed timeframes trigger immediate appeal opportunity.

**Topic 4: Specific denial reasons**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Vague denial language such as "medical necessity not met" does not tell providers or patients what evidence is needed for appeal.
- **Specific request:** Finalize denial-reason requirements, include specific clinical criteria and reasons, write notices in clear patient-understandable language, and extend the requirement to Part D plans.

**Topic 5: Public PA metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Public metrics can reduce opacity and help beneficiaries compare plan behavior, but aggregate contract-level MA reporting may hide plan-level variation.
- **Specific request:** Require or move toward plan-level MA reporting, display data in Medicare Plan Finder, set minimum web placement standards, add metrics on overturned denials, repeated PA, plan-transition outcomes, treatment receipt timing, and approval criteria.

**Topic 6: API usage metrics and patient education**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Usage data can show whether patients and providers are adopting Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs and can guide outreach.
- **Specific request:** Finalize API usage metrics and work with medical societies and patient organizations to improve outreach and education after metrics are available.

**Topic 7: Centralized API endpoints**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** A centralized directory reduces fragmentation, supports accountability, and helps CMS monitor endpoint availability.
- **Specific request:** Collect and publish impacted payer API endpoints through a National Provider Directory rather than relying on payer self-publication.

**Topic 8: Payer-to-payer exchange of PA and step therapy history**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Arthritis patients often repeat PA annually or after changing plans; longitudinal prior PA and step therapy data could reduce delays.
- **Specific request:** Ensure Part D plans use payer-to-payer APIs to transfer PA data; establish standards when plans disregard prior approvals; require new payers to recognize prior step therapy determinations.

**Topic 9: Step therapy limits**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Inappropriate step therapy causes physical and mental harm and can force patients to prove long-past treatment failures.
- **Specific request:** Use PA data-sharing infrastructure for step therapy transparency, require recognition of previous determinations, and limit Part B step therapy in MA.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** Supports NCPDP standards for electronic PA and related pharmacy benefit functions.
- **S19:** Does not address state readiness in detail, but supports alignment across Medicaid and CHIP prescription drug decision timing.
- **S20:** Supports 24-hour Medicaid/CHIP prescription-drug decisions and asks CMS to apply the same standard to MA, QHP, and Part D plans, including patient notifications.
- **S22:** Recognizes NCPDP implementation as useful but notes additional documentation transmission issues remain.
- **S23-S24:** Supports centralized collection and publication of API endpoint information through a National Provider Directory.
- **S29-S35:** Supports API usage metrics and public PA metrics, with recommendations for plan-level detail, placement standards, added measures, and regulator use.
- **S44-S56:** Does not directly address HIPAA PA standards.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to step therapy RFI by recommending data-sharing, recognition of previous determinations, longitudinal treatment records, and limits on Part B step therapy.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize electronic PA requirements for prescription drugs.
2. Finalize and broaden use of NCPDP standards across applicable functions.
3. Create FHIR-based Part D pathways for electronic clinical documentation submission.
4. Apply 24-hour prescription-drug PA decision timelines to MA, QHP, and Part D plans.
5. Require shortened patient notification timeframes and immediate appeal opportunity when deadlines are missed.
6. Require denial notices to include specific clinical criteria and patient-understandable reasons; extend to Part D.
7. Improve public PA metrics with plan-level data, Medicare Plan Finder availability, consistent website placement, and added repeated-PA and transition metrics.
8. Require approval criteria to be reported with PA metrics.
9. Ensure Part D payer-to-payer transfer of PA data and standards for honoring previous approvals.
10. Finalize API usage metrics and engage patient groups on education.
11. Publish API endpoints through a centralized National Provider Directory.
12. Use payer-to-payer APIs for step therapy history and require new payers to recognize prior determinations.
13. Limit MA Part B step therapy.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Long PA waits, repeated PA for stable medications, vague denials, fragmented documentation submission, insufficient plan-level transparency, patient notification delays, and step therapy repetition.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Worsening joint damage, mental health issues, comorbidities, anxiety or depression, delayed treatment, and patient/provider administrative burden.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multiple PA administrators including PBMs and third parties, incomplete electronic clinical documentation transmission, inconsistent criteria across plans, self-publication fragmentation, and difficulty retrieving old step therapy documentation.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** In Arthritis Foundation 2023 data, mean PA decision wait was 3 days; 4% received decisions within 24 hours; 37% waited 7 or more days; 73% required PA when first prescribed; 24% after formulary change while stable; 21% after insurance change; 37% repeated PA annually; PA granted 88% of the time; 70% experienced step therapy multiple times in five years; 70% reported anxiety/depression from step therapy, 50% worsening joint pain/damage, and 25% non-joint health issues. Avalere study: 53% of MA enrollees were in plans using step therapy for 10 common rheumatoid arthritis drugs in 2023; 27% faced two or more steps.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Arthritis Foundation surveys and focus groups; Avalere study on MA step therapy for rheumatoid arthritis Part B drugs.
- **Examples provided:** Patients receiving mailed updates after PA issues were resolved; stable patients repeating PA yearly; patients needing long look-back evidence for step therapy failures.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Arthritis patients, chronic disease patients, providers, pharmacies, pharmacists, payers, PBMs, Part D plans, MA plans, QHPs, CMS, and state/federal regulators.
- **Economic implications:** Electronic PA and reusable data may reduce administrative cost, but repeated PA and step therapy create patient and provider burden and may delay effective therapy.
- **Technical feasibility:** The Foundation views ePA, NCPDP, FHIR-based documentation pathways, and payer-to-payer APIs as feasible but requiring bridging between pharmacy and clinical systems.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster timelines, clear denials, and longitudinal PA/step therapy data would improve medication adherence and reduce disease worsening.
