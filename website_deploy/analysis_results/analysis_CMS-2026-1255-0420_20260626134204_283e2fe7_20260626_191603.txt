# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** PointClickCare (PCC).
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** PCC describes itself as a PAC/LTC health IT partner and references operational experience with patient event notification delivery, ACO and health plan customers, PAC/LTC workflows, and California Data Exchange Framework implementation.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PCC responds to the Patient Event Notifications RFI by urging CMS to prioritize actionable, timely, workflow-integrated notifications over increased alert volume or a single mandated architecture. The commenter recommends a small phased transition-of-care core, standards-based subscriptions, provider directory stewardship, attribution and authorization governance, TEFCA-aligned but flexible routing, ONC capability-based certification, targeted funding, and measurable delivery/use outcomes.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE. The comment also relates indirectly to FHIR-IG-REQUIRED because it discusses HL7 Subscriptions, Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications, and Da Vinci Member Attribution List, but it is not focused on the proposed prior authorization FHIR IG mandate.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR Part 2 is cited for sensitive SUD data. Hospital Conditions of Participation (CoPs) are discussed, but no specific CFR section is cited.
- **Standards referenced:** HL7 v2 ADT, Direct messaging, C-CDA, FHIR-oriented models, HL7 Subscriptions Framework, HL7 Da Vinci Unsolicited Notifications IG, HL7 Da Vinci Member Attribution (ATR) List IG, TEFCA/QHIN connectivity, CEHRT, provider directories, and patient matching standards.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Actionable event notifications over alert volume**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** More alerts are not inherently useful; recipients need computable, timely information embedded in clinical and operational workflows.
- **Specific request:** CMS should require or incentivize notifications that contain minimum actionable content, reach authorized recipients, support tasking and closed-loop workflows, and reduce alert fatigue through filtering.

**Topic 2: Phased standardized notification content**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Overly limited content reduces value, but overly expansive payloads can create burden, privacy risk, and unreliable data at the time alerts are generated.
- **Specific request:** CMS should define a small must-include transition-of-care alert core, phase in optional extensions, and prefer pointers to richer clinical content through existing exchange pathways.

**Topic 3: Routing, attribution, authorization, and directories**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Delivery failures often arise from stale directories, unclear authorization, weak attribution processes, cross-network trust gaps, and incomplete endpoint management rather than message format alone.
- **Specific request:** CMS should set outcome-oriented directory maintenance expectations, support panel/attribution-based routing, clarify acceptable authorization models, require auditability, and strengthen feedback loops for misrouting and nondelivery.

**Topic 4: Privacy and sensitive data governance**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Expanded notifications could over-disclose sensitive information, especially for behavioral health, SUD, and other protected categories.
- **Specific request:** CMS should apply minimum necessary and purpose limitation principles, support role- and relationship-based access, enable revocation, require auditability, and align sensitive-data expectations with HIPAA, 42 CFR Part 2, state laws, segmentation realities, and SAMHSA coordination.

**Topic 5: PAC/LTC recipient capacity and funding**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** PAC/LTC and other downstream providers often lack dedicated interoperability staff, face staffing shortages, and need timely, workflow-usable information for admission readiness and follow-up care.
- **Specific request:** CMS should treat PAC/LTC as first-class recipients, phase requirements, provide implementation guidance, and pair expanded obligations with funding and technical assistance.

**Topic 6: ONC certification and standards-based subscription capabilities**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Event notifications need scalable subscription management, authorization, panel/attribution routing, endpoint discovery, audit logs, and interoperable transport options.
- **Specific request:** CMS should coordinate with ONC on capability-based certification criteria without prescribing one vendor, network, or architecture.

**Topic 7: Technical pathways and TEFCA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** HL7 v2 ADT is the dominant operational baseline, while FHIR subscriptions and Da Vinci IGs can add value over time; TEFCA can improve governance and reach but does not solve directory, attribution, or workflow integration alone.
- **Specific request:** CMS should preserve multiple compliant pathways, support hybrid transition from ADT to FHIR subscription models, leverage TEFCA where helpful, and avoid premature single-architecture mandates.

**Topic 8: Measurement, incentives, and enforcement**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Compliance measured only as whether a hospital sent a notification misses actual delivery, timeliness, and use in transitions of care.
- **Specific request:** CMS should measure verified delivery to authorized recipients, time-to-delivery, delivery failure/bounce rates, workflow use, directory-related failures, and recipient reporting of nondelivery or misrouting.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S60-S65:** PCC responds indirectly where it recommends CMS/ONC capability-based certification for subscription lifecycle management, authorization, panel/attribution routing, endpoint metadata, auditability, and interoperable transport; it does not address ONC version expiration or adoption-rate questions directly.
- **S66-S82:** PCC provides an extensive response to the patient event notification RFI topics within the general solicitation group. It recommends actionable alert content, phased standardization, privacy guardrails, directory stewardship, attribution and patient matching governance, PAC/LTC support, TEFCA alignment, FHIR subscription and Da Vinci IG pathways, incentives, and reliability metrics.

Because the prompt maps event-notification RFIs only to the S66-S82 general range and does not provide a one-to-one crosswalk between CMS RFI question numbers and solicitation numbers, this analysis treats PCC's answers to the patient event notification questions as responses within S66-S82.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Prioritize actionability, delivery confirmation, and workflow integration rather than maximizing notification volume.
2. Define a small standardized must-include alert core and phase in optional extensions only after core capabilities work.
3. Prefer pointer-to-retrieve models for richer clinical data instead of embedding sensitive or changing data in every alert.
4. Establish directory maintenance, endpoint verification, correction, removal, and auditability expectations.
5. Clarify authorization, attribution, patient-panel, and revocation models for ACOs, payers, PAC/LTC providers, and other recipients.
6. Support panel-based subscriptions and event-type filtering to reduce alert fatigue and one-off hospital configurations.
7. Pair expanded requirements with funding and technical assistance for PAC/LTC and other resource-constrained downstream providers.
8. Coordinate with ONC on capability-based certification for subscription management, authorization, attribution, endpoint metadata, auditing, and multiple transports.
9. Leverage TEFCA and existing exchange infrastructure while preserving multiple compliant technical pathways.
10. Measure verified delivery to intended authorized recipients, timeliness, failure rates, and use in care-transition workflows.
11. Strengthen patient matching through better demographic data quality and a minimum patient matching dataset rather than relying on a universal patient identifier.
12. Use incentives, demonstrations, shared accountability models, playbooks, and phased requirements instead of relying solely on CoPs.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Alert fatigue, non-actionable notifications, stale directories, unclear authorization, weak attribution, uneven PAC/LTC readiness, sensitive-data over-disclosure, and one-size-fits-all technical mandates.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Missed transitions, misrouted alerts, privacy and safety risks, duplicative vendor procurements, unfunded downstream obligations, unreliable payloads, and reduced trust in event notification programs.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Heterogeneous source systems, incomplete endpoint directories, network fragmentation, cross-state trust issues, limited IT staff in PAC/LTC and behavioral health, manual triage, stale attribution files, inconsistent acknowledgments, patient matching data quality, and workflow integration gaps.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** No quantitative statistics are cited.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No formal studies are referenced.
- **Examples provided:** PCC cites California Data Exchange Framework experience, ACO and health plan use cases, PAC/LTC admission readiness, discharge planning, SNF transfer readiness, home health/hospice/LTPAC demonstrations, HL7 v2 ADT as the dominant baseline, and FHIR subscription/Da Vinci ATR List approaches.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Hospitals, CAHs, REHs, PAC/LTC providers, SNFs, home health, hospice, ACOs, payers, MA plans, behavioral health and SUD providers, social service providers, EMS, care managers, health IT vendors, exchange networks, directory operators, QHINs, ONC, CMS, and patients moving across care settings.
- **Economic implications:** PCC emphasizes implementation, integration, testing, support, onboarding, directory participation, and workflow integration costs, particularly if obligations are shifted to resource-constrained PAC/LTC providers or vendors without funding.
- **Technical feasibility:** PCC sees existing ADT-based approaches as viable baselines and FHIR subscription models as valuable future capabilities, but stresses phased adoption, hybrid paths, directory governance, authorization/attribution infrastructure, and technical assistance.
- **Patient care impact:** Reliable, timely, actionable notifications can improve admissions readiness, discharge coordination, follow-up outreach, reduced readmissions, and safer transitions of care.
