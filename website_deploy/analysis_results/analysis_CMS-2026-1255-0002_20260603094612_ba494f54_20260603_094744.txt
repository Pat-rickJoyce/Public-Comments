**1. COMMENTER INFORMATION**
- Author/Organization name: Calvient (Jonathan Minson, CEO)
- Stakeholder type: Technology Vendor
- Credentials or expertise mentioned: Healthcare technology company developing AI-powered solutions for prior authorizations, referrals, intelligent document processing, and healthcare operations automation. Platform serves federally qualified health centers (FQHCs), community health centers, and health systems across the United States.

**2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**
Calvient strongly supports CMS-0062-P's extension of electronic prior authorization requirements to prescription drugs but urges CMS to address the root cause of administrative burden: the complexity and variability of documentation requirements, not just the transmission medium. The company advocates for mandatory adoption of the Da Vinci DTR standard, real-time PA necessity determination APIs, and designing the EPA framework to support AI-augmented workflows that can dramatically reduce provider burden while maintaining human clinical judgment.

**3. REGULATORY CONTEXT**
- Specific sections/provisions addressed: CMS-0062-P (2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs Proposed Rule), building on CMS-0057-F final rule
- Regulatory citations mentioned: Medicaid managed care plans, CHIP programs, 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard decision windows
- Standards referenced: HL7 FHIR, NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB), Da Vinci Prior Authorization Support (PAS), Documentation Templates and Rules (DTR), Clinical Data Exchange (CDex), C-CDA

**4. DETAILED POSITIONS**

Topic: Compressed Decision Timeframes
- Position: Support
- Rationale: 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard windows reflect clinical reality that drug PA delays directly harm patients, especially at FQHCs
- Specific request: Finalize proposed timeframes

Topic: Mandatory Electronic PA via FHIR and NCPDP Standards
- Position: Support
- Rationale: Creates foundation for automated, interoperable PA workflows; particularly welcome Da Vinci PAS, DTR, and CDex implementation guides
- Specific request: Finalize standards adoption

Topic: Specific Denial Reasons
- Position: Support
- Rationale: Essential for providers to understand what additional documentation is needed and to mount effective appeals; vague denials waste time
- Specific request: Require payers to provide specific denial reasons

Topic: Public Reporting of PA Metrics
- Position: Support with Enhancement
- Rationale: Transparency creates accountability and enables informed decisions about payer performance
- Specific request: Ensure metrics are reported in standardized, machine-readable format

Topic: Da Vinci DTR Standard Adoption
- Position: Strongly Support with Enhancement
- Rationale: DTR is the most consequential element of the rule; enables structured, machine-readable documentation requirements that can be pre-populated from clinical records
- Specific request: Mandate DTR adoption with same urgency as PAS; place equal emphasis on requiring payers to publish drug coverage requirements in DTR format

Topic: PA Necessity Determination via API
- Position: Modify/Add
- Rationale: Significant wasted effort occurs determining if PA is required; RTPB is a step but insufficient
- Specific request: Require payers to expose real-time API endpoint for PA necessity queries

Topic: Questionnaire Consistency and Complexity Limits
- Position: Add New Requirement
- Rationale: Variability and complexity of payer questionnaires remains major burden driver even with DTR
- Specific request: Develop guidelines promoting consistency across payers, limiting questionnaire length/complexity to clinical necessity

Topic: AI-Augmented Workflows Support
- Position: Add Framework Consideration
- Rationale: AI agents already assisting providers with chart scrubbing, pre-population, status monitoring; standards should facilitate not impede
- Specific request: Design EPA to accommodate AI-augmented workflows; ensure DTR is machine-processable, PAS supports programmatic queries, CDex supports automated compilation, API rate limits accommodate high-volume processing

Topic: FQHC and Safety-Net Provider Support
- Position: Add Support Provisions
- Rationale: FQHCs serve 30+ million patients with significantly fewer resources than large health systems
- Specific request: Provide technical assistance/funding for FHIR adoption; accelerate DTR questionnaire availability timeline; monitor whether benefits reach safety-net providers

Topic: Step Therapy Processes
- Position: Add Structured Format Requirement
- Rationale: Step therapy compounds PA burden; structured formats would reduce back-and-forth
- Specific request: Require payers to publish step therapy protocols in machine-readable formats alongside DTR questionnaires

Topic: PA for Laboratory Tests and DME
- Position: Support Future Extension
- Rationale: Represent substantial and growing PA burden
- Specific request: Extend interoperability framework to lab tests and DME in future rulemaking

**5. RECOMMENDATIONS**
1. Mandate DTR adoption with the same urgency as PAS, requiring payers to publish drug coverage requirements and documentation questionnaires in structured, machine-readable DTR format
2. Require payers to expose PA necessity determination via real-time API endpoint
3. Establish standards for questionnaire consistency and complexity limits, working with payers, providers, and standards bodies
4. Design the EPA framework to support AI-augmented workflows, ensuring DTR is machine-processable, PAS supports programmatic queries, CDex supports automated compilation, and API rate limits accommodate high-volume processing
5. Address unique needs of FQHCs and safety-net providers through technical assistance/funding, accelerated DTR timelines, and monitoring of benefit distribution
6. Require payers to publish step therapy protocols in structured, machine-readable formats
7. Extend interoperability framework to laboratory tests and durable medical equipment in future rulemaking

**6. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS**

Primary Concerns:
- Electronic submission alone does not reduce administrative burden; the work of preparing submissions remains the bottleneck
- Complexity and variability of payer-specific documentation requirements consume majority of PA processing time (30-60 minutes per complex request)
- Providers cannot easily determine if PA is required for specific drug/patient/plan combinations
- Questionnaires vary significantly payer-to-payer even for same drug and clinical indication
- One payer may require 2-page form while another requires 12-page clinical questionnaire
- Chart scrubbing requires extracting data from different EHR locations, scanned documents, external records

Potential Negative Impacts:
- FQHCs and safety-net providers may lack technical capacity to benefit from electronic PA if support not provided
- Benefits may accrue primarily to large health systems with greater technical resources
- Without DTR, electronic submission still requires manual determination of documentation needs
- API rate limits and access policies may not accommodate high-volume PA processing needs

Implementation Barriers:
- Many FQHCs employ full-time staff solely for managing prior authorizations
- Physicians complete average 39-43 PA requests per week requiring 13-16 hours of staff time (AMA data)
- 93% of physicians report PA delays patient care; 89% say it contributes to burnout
- Safety-net providers have constrained staffing and patients face greatest barriers to medication access
- Small health plans have 36-month compliance timeline which may delay benefits

0062-P, the 2026 CMS Interoperability Standards and Prior Authorization for Drugs

Calvient is a healthcare technology company that develops AI-powered solutions for

healthcare system.
We write from the perspective of a technology company that works daily with providers
who are, in their own words, drowning in prior authorization requests. Our comments
are informed by direct experience automating PA workflows, processing unstructured
clinical documentation, and interfacing with the payer systems that administer these
requirements. We strongly support the direction of this proposed rule while urging CMS
to go further in addressing the root causes of administrative burden.
II. General Support for the Proposed Rule
Calvient supports the core objectives of CMS-0062-P. Extending the interoperability and
prior authorization framework to prescription drugs is overdue and essential. Drug prior
authorization represents a significant and growing share of the administrative burden
borne by providers, and the absence of electronic standards for drug PA has left
providers reliant on fragmented, manual processes that delay patient access to
necessary medications.
We specifically support the following provisions:

Compressed decision timeframes. The proposed 24-hour urgent and 72-hour
standard decision windows are appropriate and reflect the clinical reality that drug prior
authorization delays can directly harm patients. When a patient at an FQHC is
prescribed a medication that requires prior authorization, every day of delay is a day
that patient goes without treatment. The current timeframes are inadequate, and we
commend CMS for proposing meaningful compression.
Mandatory electronic PA via FHIR and NCPDP standards. The adoption of HL7
FHIR-based standards for drugs covered under medical benefits and NCPDP SCRIPT,
Formulary & Benefit, and Real-Time Prescription Benefit standards for pharmacy
benefits is a critical infrastructure investment. These standards create the foundation for
automated, interoperable PA workflows. We particularly welcome the adoption of the Da
Vinci Prior Authorization Support (PAS), Documentation Templates and Rules (DTR),
and Clinical Data Exchange (CDex) implementation guides, which we discuss in detail
below.
Specific denial reasons. Requiring payers to provide specific reasons when denying a
drug PA request is essential for providers to understand what additional documentation
or clinical justification is needed, and to mount effective appeals. Vague denials waste
provider time and delay patient care.
Public reporting of PA metrics. Transparency in approval rates, denial rates, and
turnaround times will create accountability and enable providers to make informed
decisions about payer performance. We encourage CMS to ensure these metrics are
reported in a standardized, machine-readable format to enable meaningful comparison.
III. The Fax Is Not the Problem: Addressing the True Source of
Administrative Burden
While we strongly support the move to electronic standards, we urge CMS to recognize
that the transmission medium is only one dimension of the prior authorization burden.
The fax is not the problem. The problem is the intensity, variability, and volume of
the documentation requirements themselves.
Our clients at FQHCs and health systems consistently report that the most time-
consuming aspect of prior authorization is not the act of submitting a request, whether
by fax, phone, portal, or API. It is the work required to complete the payer-specific
questionnaires and gather the clinical documentation necessary to satisfy each payer's
unique requirements. These questionnaires are often lengthy, clinically intensive, and
vary significantly from payer to payer, even for the same drug and the same clinical
indication.
Consider the workflow a provider faces today when a prior authorization is triggered:
First, the provider must determine whether a prior authorization is even required for the
specific drug, for the specific patient, under the specific plan. This determination is often
not straightforward and may require calling the payer or checking a portal.
