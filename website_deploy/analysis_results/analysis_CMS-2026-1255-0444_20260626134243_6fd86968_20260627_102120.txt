- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** AHDA describes itself as a national coalition representing patients, care partners, clinicians, researchers, patient advocacy organizations, medical societies, and other stakeholders focused on migraine, cluster headache, facial pain, and disabling headache disorders.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AHDA supports CMS's effort to modernize drug prior authorization, extend interoperability requirements to drugs, improve prior authorization transparency, establish decision timeframes, and report utilization management metrics. It asks CMS to strengthen the rule by addressing step therapy, continuity of care, recognition of prior payer determinations, clinician notification of coverage changes, evidence-based review criteria, physician-administered drug workflows, and patient-facing public metrics.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT concerns under PA-DRUG-MEDICAL/PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; GENERAL/RFI step therapy and prior payer determinations.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations are expressly cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR APIs are referenced generally through patient and provider APIs; no specific FHIR IG, NCPDP, HL7, or CFR standard names are discussed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Drug prior authorization modernization and timeframes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization delays can cause additional migraine attacks, disease progression, disability, missed work or school, emergency department use, and treatment abandonment.
- **Specific request:** Make timeframe protections meaningful by defining when clocks begin and end, limiting duplicative documentation requests, monitoring delay tactics, honoring approvals for reasonable durations, and ensuring expedited review for neurological conditions where interruption can cause rapid deterioration.

**Topic 2: Step therapy and prior payer determinations**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Step therapy can force patients to retry ineffective, poorly tolerated, contraindicated, or previously failed treatments, especially after insurance changes. AHDA cites migraine-specific evidence that payer blocking is associated with higher emergency department and inpatient admissions.
- **Specific request:** Treat step therapy as a core utilization management issue, require recognition of prior failures and prior determinations across payers, protect stable patients from repeating fail-first protocols, require timely override processes, report step therapy metrics, and set reasonable limits on required steps and repeat authorizations for dosage or frequency changes.

**Topic 3: Continuity of care for stable patients**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Patients stable after years of trial and error can relapse when coverage changes, formulary changes, new PA requirements, or new step edits interrupt therapy.
- **Specific request:** Establish grandfathering protections, streamlined or automatic renewals where no material clinical change exists, recognition of prior approvals during payer transitions, temporary continuation coverage during appeals or transitions, and limits on non-medical switching for chronic neurological disease.

**Topic 4: Actionable API transparency**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** API access is valuable only if PA information is complete, timely, usable by patients and clinicians, and interoperable in real workflows.
- **Specific request:** Require real-time or near-real-time PA status updates; plain-language patient information; actionable clinician information; specific denial reasons; appeal rights and next steps; and integration across EHR and practice management systems.

**Topic 5: Clinician notification of coverage changes**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; TRANSPARENCY-related API provisions
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Practices often learn about formulary, PA, step therapy, quantity-limit, site-of-care, or preferred-product changes only after a prescription is rejected or treatment is disrupted.
- **Specific request:** Add explicit electronic notice requirements to affected in-network clinicians and practices when plans finalize material utilization management or formulary changes, with notice sufficiently in advance of implementation when feasible.

**Topic 6: Evidence-based review criteria and appropriate expertise**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter argues denials may rely on outdated criteria and rigid pathways that do not reflect current headache medicine or individualized needs.
- **Specific request:** Encourage or require transparent medical necessity criteria, periodic updates aligned with trusted clinician guidelines, specialty consultation pathways for complex cases, and individualized review rather than rigid algorithmic denials.

**Topic 7: Physician-administered therapies and buy-and-bill risk**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Scheduling disruption for physician-administered therapies can cause missed treatment cycles, symptom escalation, provider burden, and financial risk for practices that buy and bill expensive products.
- **Specific request:** Fully capture physician-administered drug workflows, including timely review standards, clear approval duration rules, coordination between medical and pharmacy benefit systems, reduced repeat authorization for stable regimens, and protection against mid-cycle administrative disruption.

**Topic 8: Public reporting of utilization management metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Public reporting can create accountability and help patients compare plans based on access to medically necessary care.
- **Specific request:** Include approval and denial rates, appeal overturn rates, average and median decision times, additional documentation request frequency, repeat PA rates for previously approved therapies, step therapy override approval rates, and therapeutic-category breakdowns where feasible.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S16/S17:** AHDA supports specific, actionable denial information and asks that status information explain the exact reason for denial, missing documentation, appeal rights, and next steps in patient- and clinician-usable form.
- **S20/S43:** The comment supports drug PA timeframes but asks CMS to prevent plans from undermining timeframes through duplicative documentation requests, unclear clock rules, technical pauses, or repeated reauthorization.
- **S23/S24/S26/S33:** The comment supports API-based access to PA information but asks CMS to ensure the information is timely, plain-language for patients, actionable for clinicians, and interoperable across EHR and practice management systems.
- **S29/S34/S35:** AHDA supports public reporting of metrics and recommends consumer-friendly measures such as approval/denial rates, appeal overturn rates, average and median decision times, documentation request rates, repeat PA rates, and step therapy override rates.
- **S12/S15/S21/S28:** The comment addresses physician-administered and benefit-boundary issues by asking CMS to capture medical benefit drug workflows, coordinate medical and pharmacy benefit systems, and prevent authorization disruption for therapies delivered in clinical settings.
- **S66-S82 (General/RFI topics, including step therapy and prior payer determinations):** AHDA responds extensively to CMS's step therapy and prior payer determinations request by recommending recognition of prior treatment failures and prior determinations across payer transitions, limits on fail-first requirements, override processes, continuity protections, and step therapy reporting.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Treat step therapy as a core utilization management issue subject to transparency and reform.
2. Require recognition of documented prior treatment failures and prior step therapy determinations, including those made under a previous payer.
3. Protect stable patients from repeating fail-first protocols or losing effective therapy solely because of coverage transitions or formulary changes.
4. Define and enforce meaningful PA decision timeframes, including clock-start and clock-stop rules and limits on duplicative documentation requests.
5. Require timely, accessible override pathways when clinicians determine step therapy is medically inappropriate.
6. Add explicit electronic notice requirements for material formulary and utilization management changes.
7. Require PA status and denial information to be real-time or near-real-time, plain-language for patients, actionable for clinicians, and inclusive of appeal rights.
8. Promote transparent, current, evidence-informed review criteria and access to appropriate specialty expertise for complex neurological cases.
9. Ensure physician-administered drug workflows are fully covered, including approval duration, renewal, medical/pharmacy coordination, and protection against mid-cycle disruption.
10. Expand public reporting to include patient-relevant utilization management metrics, including step therapy override rates and repeat PA rates.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Treatment delays; step therapy abuse; repeated reauthorization; lack of continuity protections; abrupt formulary or utilization management changes; unclear denial reasons; outdated review criteria; insufficient accommodation of physician-administered therapies.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Additional migraine attacks, worsening symptoms, disease progression to chronic migraine, disability, missed work or school, caregiver disruption, mental health deterioration, treatment abandonment, emergency department visits, inpatient admissions, and provider financial strain.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Administrative ping-pong, duplicative documentation demands, opaque PA clock rules, non-transparent review pauses, insufficient EHR/practice-management integration, insurance churn, and lack of timely notice to clinicians.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Migraine affects approximately 39 to 40 million Americans; migraine/headache account for more than 4 million emergency department visits annually at about $4.2 billion in direct cost; about 2.5% of people with episodic migraine progress to chronic migraine annually without adequate preventive treatment; a 2026 study of 7.7 million migraine patients found 20.6% of actively treated individuals experienced payer blocking; fewer than 900 board-certified headache medicine physicians exist against a projected need of about 3,700; an AMA survey found 93% of physicians reported PA delays patient care, 82% reported PA causes treatment abandonment, and 29% reported PA contributed to a serious adverse event; only 11.5% of people with migraine and 2.7% of people with chronic migraine reach consultation, diagnosis, and minimally appropriate treatment.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Cohen et al., Headache, 2024; Rhudy et al., Headache, 2024; Lipton, Neurology, 2009; Buse et al., Headache, 2026; Charles et al., Headache, 2024; Begasse de Dhaem et al., Headache, 2020; American Medical Association, 2024; Lanteri-Minet et al., J Headache Pain, 2024.
- **Examples provided:** Patients losing stable therapy after formulary or payer changes; pharmacy rejection revealing coverage changes; staff urgently resubmitting paperwork; patients changing jobs, states, or Medicare eligibility and being forced to restart years of treatment history; practices facing buy-and-bill inventory and reimbursement risk.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with migraine, cluster headache, facial pain, and other headache disorders; caregivers; clinicians; headache specialists; medical practices; in-network clinicians; payers; pharmacies; EHR and practice management vendors.
- **Economic implications:** Delays may increase emergency department, inpatient, and downstream care costs; administrative burden consumes practice resources; buy-and-bill uncertainty can financially strain smaller and community practices and discourage stocking therapies.
- **Technical feasibility:** AHDA supports API transparency but emphasizes that technical availability is insufficient unless information is real-time or near-real-time, understandable, actionable, and integrated into clinician workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** The comment frames timely and continuous access to effective therapy as essential to preventing worsening disease, disability, avoidable acute care, and loss of function.
