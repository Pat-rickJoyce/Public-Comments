## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM); signed by Anita V. Shelgikar, MD, MHPE, President
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Represents more than 9,000 physicians and advanced practice providers and 2,400 accredited sleep centers.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AASM supports CMS's effort to modernize prior authorization, expand ePA to drugs, improve API interoperability, and increase transparency, while urging specialty-sensitive implementation. The commenter emphasizes small practice burden, sleep-medicine-specific clinical documentation, uniform drug PA timeframes, detailed denial explanations, public metrics, API testing, cybersecurity support, and continuity protections for step therapy and DMEPOS workflows.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** File Code CMS-0062-P; no CFR sections are cited.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR-based implementation guides, NCPDP transactions, standardized APIs, Patient Access API, Provider Access API, Payer-to-Payer API, ADT notifications.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Standardized ePA and Interoperability Implementation**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Required FHIR IGs and NCPDP transactions can reduce burden, but complexity may create operational challenges for specialty practices if not coordinated and integrated into existing systems.
- **Specific request:** Provide timeline flexibility for small specialty practices, avoid shifting technology burden to clinicians, and align required standards to minimize duplicative workflows.

**Topic 2: ePA for Sleep-Medicine Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Sleep clinicians prescribe drugs requiring PA for narcolepsy, hypersomnia, obstructive sleep apnea-related excessive daytime sleepiness, insomnia, restless legs syndrome, and circadian rhythm disorders.
- **Specific request:** Finalize drug ePA expansion for medical and pharmacy benefit drugs and require specialty-specific criteria and structured documentation aligned with evidence-based sleep medicine.

**Topic 3: Medical vs Pharmacy Benefit Categorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Clarify
- **Rationale:** Unclear benefit categorization can cause delays, denials, and unnecessary burden.
- **Specific request:** Ensure categorization policies reflect real-world practice and clearly identify the appropriate submission pathway.

**Topic 4: Decision Timeframes and Expedited Review**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Delays in sleep medicine treatment can affect safety, occupational functioning, driving, and long-term outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Establish clear and uniform decision timeframes for all PA drugs; maintain expedited pathways; monitor and enforce compliance; publicly report turnaround times; consider shorter timeframes for evidence-based low-risk services.

**Topic 5: Denial Transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Unclear or inconsistently applied medical-necessity criteria cause repeated submissions, delays, and administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Require denial explanations to identify the unmet clinical criterion, missing documentation, payer policy used, and alternative approval pathways.

**Topic 6: Endpoint Directory and API Access**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Sleep practices interact with many payers, and a centralized directory would reduce burden and improve adoption of interoperability tools.
- **Specific request:** Finalize payer endpoint reporting and make PA status, approval dates, denial reasons, and supporting documentation available through Patient Access, Provider Access, and Payer-to-Payer APIs.

**Topic 7: Public PA Metrics**
- **Provision ID:** API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Approval rates, denial rates, appeals, and turnaround times would help stakeholders evaluate payer performance, but more specialty-relevant measures are needed.
- **Specific request:** Add service-specific and specialty-specific denial rates, overturned appeal rates, peer-to-peer review use, automated denial rates, and downcoding rates.

**Topic 8: RFIs on Notifications, Cybersecurity, Testing, Step Therapy, and DMEPOS**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Sleep care depends on diagnostic platforms, PAP monitoring, DME suppliers, payer APIs, and continuity across payer transitions.
- **Specific request:** Include sleep specialists in ADT notification access, tailor cybersecurity support to small practices, implement API testing/certification, honor prior step therapy completion via interoperable data, and reduce unnecessary PA for evidence-based sleep testing and DMEPOS.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports required standardized APIs and nationally recognized interoperability standards while requesting coordinated specialty-sensitive implementation.
- **S12:** Supports ePA for medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit drugs and asks systems to support sleep-medicine criteria and documentation.
- **S15:** Responds directly by asking CMS to clarify medical versus pharmacy benefit categorization and submission pathways.
- **S16:** Supports specific denial reasons and asks for detailed explanations tied to clinical criteria, missing documentation, payer policy, and alternative pathways.
- **S17:** Supports structured detail sufficient to reduce resubmissions, though it does not specifically name denial code sets.
- **S20:** Recommends clear and uniform drug PA decision timeframes, especially for wake-promoting and hypersomnia/narcolepsy drugs affecting safety.
- **S21:** Supports drug ePA implementation but requests timeline flexibility for small and resource-constrained specialty practices.
- **S23:** Addresses cybersecurity and resiliency concerns for API-enabled sleep care infrastructure, including cloud diagnostics and remote PAP monitoring.
- **S24:** Supports endpoint reporting and centralized directories; also supports access to PA status, approval dates, denial reasons, and documentation through APIs.
- **S29:** Supports public PA metrics and recommends additional measures such as specialty-specific denial rates and automated denial rates.
- **S35:** Supports public reporting of metrics so patients, clinicians, employers, and policymakers can evaluate payer performance.
- **S37:** Identifies implementation challenges for smaller practices, EHR/vendor dependence, standards complexity, and workflow integration.
- **S43:** Supports harmonized decision timeframes and expedited pathways across payers.
- **S54:** Supports standardized testing, certification or validation programs, public API performance reporting, and ongoing compliance monitoring.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to RFIs on ADT notifications, cybersecurity/resiliency, step therapy, laboratory tests, and DMEPOS.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize standardized ePA APIs and interoperability requirements with coordinated implementation.
2. Provide compliance timeline flexibility where necessary for small specialty practices.
3. Prevent technology burden from shifting onto individual clinicians.
4. Include sleep-medicine-specific clinical criteria and structured documentation in ePA systems.
5. Clarify medical versus pharmacy benefit submission pathways.
6. Establish uniform decision timeframes for all drugs requiring PA and maintain expedited pathways.
7. Monitor, enforce, and publicly report payer performance on PA turnaround times.
8. Require detailed denial explanations identifying missing criteria, documentation, payer policy, and alternative pathways.
9. Finalize centralized API endpoint reporting and access to PA information through Patient, Provider, and Payer-to-Payer APIs.
10. Expand public PA metrics to include specialty-specific and service-specific measures, appeals, peer-to-peer reviews, automated denials, and downcoding.
11. Include sleep specialists in ADT notifications and provide small-practice cybersecurity assistance.
12. Establish payer API testing, certification, and performance reporting.
13. Use interoperable data to honor prior step therapy completion after payer changes.
14. Reduce unnecessary PA for evidence-based sleep testing and DMEPOS and support ePA coordination with DME suppliers.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Administrative burden, small practice implementation challenges, standards complexity, unclear benefit categorization, treatment delays, poor denial explanations, API inconsistency, cybersecurity resiliency, duplicated step therapy, and DMEPOS delays.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed sleep testing and therapy, increased crash and workplace injury risk, impaired driving and work functioning, reduced PAP adherence, and worse outcomes for sleep disorders.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Limited technical resources, reliance on EHR vendors and payers, multiple FHIR IGs and NCPDP transactions, inconsistent payer API implementation, and coordination with DME suppliers.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** AASM represents more than 9,000 clinicians and 2,400 accredited sleep centers; sleep disorders affect tens of millions of Americans.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No named studies are cited.
- **Examples provided:** PAP therapy, oral appliances, hypoglossal nerve stimulation, wake-promoting medications, insomnia therapies, polysomnography reports, home sleep apnea tests, PAP adherence data, actigraphy, validated sleep questionnaires, ADT events, step therapy after insurance changes, and DMEPOS supplies.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Sleep medicine physicians and advanced practice providers, accredited sleep centers, patients with sleep disorders, small specialty practices, EHR vendors, payers, DME suppliers, employers, and policymakers.
- **Economic implications:** Streamlined PA could reduce administrative burden and long-term health expenditures from untreated sleep disorders; implementation may strain small practices without support.
- **Technical feasibility:** AASM supports standards-based APIs but flags feasibility risks around integration, vendor dependence, API variability, and cybersecurity capacity.
- **Patient care impact:** High; delays can affect cardiovascular risk, accident risk, work and education functioning, therapy adherence, and quality of life.
