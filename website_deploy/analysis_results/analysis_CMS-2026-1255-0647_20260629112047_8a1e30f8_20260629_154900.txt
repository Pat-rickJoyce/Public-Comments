## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The commenter strongly supports CMS' vision for modernizing health data exchange and drug prior authorization but urges CMS to sequence implementation carefully. He recommends proceeding only after prior regulatory implementations are stabilized, technology systems across payers/providers/government are upgraded and validated, and overlapping timelines are coordinated to avoid wasted resources and implementation failure.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; API-USAGE-METRICS; OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP; PRIVACY-SECURITY; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Docket/proposed rule described generally; no CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR APIs, EHR technology, medical benefit vs pharmacy benefit PA systems, API-based patient data access.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Conditional support for modernization**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter says the initiative is crucial and urgent, but transformational success depends on stabilizing existing regulatory implementation and technology foundations first.
- **Specific request:** Proceed only after previous requirements/deadlines are completed, systems are upgraded and validated, and implementation is coordinated.

**Topic 2: Implementation timeline and overlapping builds**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The October 1, 2027 deadline overlaps with existing technology requirements, potentially doubling short-term costs and stretching state budgets, contractor procurement, and software development.
- **Specific request:** Avoid parallel implementation timelines that conflict with existing rules and drain implementation capacity.

**Topic 3: Provider/clinic impact**
- **Provision ID:** PROVIDER-BURDEN; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT; PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support/Concerned
- **Rationale:** Electronic systems can reduce PA time and provide coverage rules inside EHRs, but clinics may need expensive software upgrades and must navigate separate medical-benefit and pharmacy-benefit systems.
- **Specific request:** Ensure providers, including small and rural clinics, are not left behind by EHR upgrade costs or technical confusion.

**Topic 4: Patient benefits and privacy/cyber risks**
- **Provision ID:** PATIENT-ACCESS; PRIVACY-SECURITY
- **Position:** Support/Concerned
- **Rationale:** Faster PA decisions, portable records, and patient apps can improve access, but increased API data sharing raises cybersecurity and privacy risks and can create broken connections.
- **Specific request:** Strengthen implementation safeguards for cybersecurity, privacy, and reliable patient-facing data connections.

**Topic 5: Audit and penalty exposure**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Concerned
- **Rationale:** The commenter notes that failure-to-report rules may create audit and penalty risks if documents are not produced quickly enough.
- **Specific request:** Ensure audit expectations are realistic and do not add destabilizing penalty risk during implementation.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S12/S15:** The commenter notes technical confusion from two systems depending on whether drugs are paid under medical or pharmacy benefit.
- **S18/S21/S37:** The commenter raises implementation burden and timeline concerns for payers, clinics, state programs, and software developers.
- **S20/S43:** The comment references 24-hour urgent and 72-hour standard deadlines as patient access benefits.
- **S23/S30:** The commenter highlights cybersecurity, ransomware, identity theft, and patient privacy risks associated with expanded APIs.
- **S48/S49:** The comment discusses costs, especially payer build costs, ongoing maintenance, clinic software upgrades, and small/rural clinic gaps.
- **S57-S59:** The comment touches on audit/penalty risk under a failure-to-report concept.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Stabilize all prior regulatory implementations before this new rule takes effect.
2. Upgrade and validate payer, provider, and government technology systems to current standards before parallel implementation.
3. Coordinate implementation timelines to avoid overlapping and conflicting work.
4. Account for state budget and contractor procurement hurdles.
5. Support clinics facing EHR upgrade costs and prevent small/rural practices from being left behind.
6. Address cybersecurity, privacy, and app-vetting risks created by expanded APIs.
7. Ensure audit and penalty structures are workable.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Overlapping deadlines, doubled implementation costs, state budget/procurement constraints, ongoing maintenance expenses, clinic software upgrade costs, cybersecurity, privacy, broken data connections.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Inefficiency, resource dilution, small/rural clinic gaps, patient privacy exposure, cyberattack disruptions, failure to achieve real-time outcomes.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Concurrent software builds, state legislative funding, contractor hiring, certified EHR upgrades, dual benefit-specific systems.
