# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Laboratory Corporation of America Holdings (Labcorp); signed by Donald E. Horton, Jr., Senior Vice President, Global Government Relations & Public Policy.
- **Stakeholder type:** Other - clinical laboratory / diagnostics provider.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Global laboratory services company providing diagnostics and drug development laboratory capabilities; member of the American Clinical Laboratory Association.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Labcorp asks CMS to reform laboratory prior authorization because current processes delay care, create high administrative burden, and often deny medically necessary testing for documentation or timing reasons rather than clinical merit. It recommends recognizing Test Requisition Forms and structured EHR orders as valid documentation, prohibiting specimen-collection-date denials, limiting lab PA to tests that satisfy defined clinical and economic criteria, improving transparency of LBM logic, and using automation, conditional approvals, and gold carding.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL/RFI on laboratory tests and DMEPOS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy for timely decisions; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy for specific denial explanations; TRANSPARENCY; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 91 Fed. Reg. 19890, 20014 (Apr. 14, 2026).
- **Standards referenced:** Test Requisition Form (TRF); EHR-linked order entry; structured clinical inputs. No FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is specifically requested.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Patient care and provider burden from laboratory PA**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Labcorp states that requiring testing to wait for authorization can delay diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment. It says ordering providers are often expected to submit PA, but fewer than 5% of Labcorp ordering provider clients perform PA, leaving labs at payment risk.
- **Specific request:** Reform lab PA processes to reflect laboratory workflows and avoid delayed testing or uncompensated medically necessary services.

**Topic 2: TRFs as valid medical documentation**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; TRANSPARENCY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Labcorp argues TRFs and structured EHR orders already contain core documentation such as ordering provider, test, diagnosis, indication, and clinical intent, and would reduce record requests, appeals, audits, and technical denials.
- **Specific request:** Require MACs, MA plans, and commercial payers to accept TRFs or structured electronic orders as presumptive documentation of medical necessity, with additional records reserved for outliers or audits.

**Topic 3: Specimen collection timing denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Oppose current practice / Modify
- **Rationale:** Labcorp says most date-of-service denials misuse a Medicare specimen-collection billing rule and ignore that the service requiring authorization is the test, not the specimen draw. It reports that 66% of specimens are collected by ordering provider clients, outside Labcorp's control.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit payers and LBMs from denying PA requests because authorization was not sought or obtained before specimen collection; allow laboratories to submit PA between collection and timely claim submission.

**Topic 4: Laboratory benefit manager transparency and incentives**
- **Provision ID:** TRANSPARENCY; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Labcorp says LBM decision logic is proprietary, updated without standardized notice, and can create unexplained denials based on sequencing or test-selection nuances, especially in genomics.
- **Specific request:** Require clearer policies and explainable denial logic, including identification of the failed criterion and what additional data would approve a request.

**Topic 5: Criteria for when lab PA may be required**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Labcorp argues PA is often applied to routine, low-risk, low-cost, or time-sensitive services where administrative cost exceeds value and patient care can be harmed.
- **Specific request:** Establish minimum criteria: a 96-hour result delay will not harm care; specimen integrity is not affected by 96-hour delay; allowed amount exceeds $1,100; and fewer than 10% of PA-based denials are overturned on appeal. Prohibit PA if criteria are not met.

**Topic 6: Automation, gold carding, and conditional approvals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy
- **Position:** Support/Modify
- **Rationale:** Labcorp says manual PA causes delays and appeal burden, while high-performing providers remain subject to unnecessary requirements.
- **Specific request:** Enable near-real-time automated PA based on structured inputs, route potential denials for clinician review, gold-card high-performing providers and their performing laboratories, and allow conditional approvals or retrospective review for time-sensitive services.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **GENERAL/RFI S66-S82:** Labcorp responds extensively to the RFI on lab tests and DMEPOS, including patient-care impact, TRF documentation, specimen-collection timing denials, LBM effects, process improvement, and technology gaps. The exact solicitation numbers are not identifiable from the provided taxonomy, but the response squarely addresses the lab/DMEPOS RFI topics within S66-S82.
- **S16/S17 by analogy:** Labcorp requests explainable denial reasons that identify which criterion failed and what data would approve the request.
- **S20/S43 by analogy:** Labcorp emphasizes that 72-hour routine PA decisions plus 24-hour lab processing can create a 96-hour delay that is inappropriate for many lab tests.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Accept TRFs and structured EHR orders as presumptive valid medical documentation for medical necessity.
2. Reserve full chart requests for outlier cases, warranted audits, or suspected misuse.
3. Prohibit PA denials based on authorization not being sought or obtained before specimen collection.
4. Allow laboratories to submit PA requests between specimen collection and timely claim submission.
5. Establish standard criteria governing which laboratory tests may be subject to PA.
6. Prohibit lab PA when a 96-hour delay would harm care, specimen integrity would degrade, the allowed amount is $1,100 or less, or denial overturn rates are too high.
7. Require payers and LBMs to publish written justification for lab PA requirements.
8. Use near-real-time automated PA and decision trees for clear approvals, with clinician review for potential denials.
9. Gold-card high-performing ordering providers and extend those exemptions to the laboratories performing their ordered tests.
10. Require explainable decision logic and clinician-readable denial explanations.
11. Allow conditional approvals and automatic retrospective review for valid time-sensitive cases.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Delayed care, high administrative burden, technical denials, opaque LBM rules, payment risk for labs, and PA applied where it lacks clinical or economic justification.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Slower diagnoses for cancer, autoimmune disease, and infectious illness; disease progression; patients foregoing recommended services; unnecessary appeals; duplicate documentation; and reduced revenue predictability for laboratories.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Provider nonresponse to documentation requests, plan-specific forms, wet signature requirements, confidential LBM logic, inability of labs to control specimen collection timing, and manual PA workflows.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** In Q1 2026, approvals averaged 11 calendar days and denials 17 calendar days; less than 5% of Labcorp ordering provider clients perform PA; 10% of test holds were cancelled due to provider nonresponse to documentation requests; more than 90% of ordering provider clients submit orders electronically; about 2,200 PA rejections occurred in the last 90 days, mostly due to specimen collection timing; about 66% of specimens are collected by ordering provider clients; ACLA survey data cited for PA denial and appeal calculations.
- **Studies or research referenced:** ACLA member survey data; no external clinical studies cited in the extracted text.
- **Examples provided:** FEP NC and Avalon requiring clinical notes; Aetna requiring a particular form; Medicaid Alabama requiring wet signatures; payer examples include Texas Medicaid, Highmark Wholecare, Molina Healthcare, FEP NC, and Health Choice Arizona.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients, ordering clinicians, laboratories, MACs, MA plans, commercial payers, LBMs, and CMS.
- **Economic implications:** Labcorp argues PA can be economically irrational below roughly an $1,100 to $1,143 break-even point and creates administrative costs for labs, providers, and payers.
- **Technical feasibility:** Structured TRFs, EHR orders, decision trees, and explainable rule tools are presented as feasible process improvements.
- **Patient care impact:** High, because delays can postpone diagnosis, treatment selection, monitoring, and time-sensitive oncology, infectious disease, genetic, and autoimmune testing.
