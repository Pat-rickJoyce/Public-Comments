# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** American College of Cardiology (ACC)
- **Stakeholder type:** Professional Association; Healthcare Provider representative.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Cardiovascular professional society; signatory Dr. Roxana Mehran, MD, FACC, President; expertise in cardiovascular care, clinical guidelines, registries, accreditation, health IT, and digital health policy.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

ACC supports CMS's expansion of electronic prior authorization to drugs and supports more enforceable interoperability standards, public reporting, denial transparency, endpoint reporting, and Open Payments audit accountability. Its support is conditioned on practical workflow integration, adequate testing, dual-version transition periods, technical assistance, and careful HIPAA/FHIR implementation planning to avoid adding burden to clinicians and resource-constrained practices.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP; ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 45 CFR 170.205(c), 170.205(u), 170.215(j), 170.215(k), 170.215(m), 170.215(n); 42 CFR 403.902; 42 CFR 402.912(e)(2); 45 CFR 162.1301(a), 162.1301(c).

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Mandatory API and IG conformance**
- **Provision ID:** FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support with modifications
- **Rationale:** Specific IGs can reduce variation and support scalable integration, but only if they function in real clinical workflows through EHRs, e-prescribing systems, and revenue cycle tools.
- **Specific request:** CMS should explicitly require single-workflow integration where possible within certified health IT, require usability/testing/stability, and avoid treating technical endpoints alone as success.

**Topic 2: Version transitions and updated ONC standards**
- **Provision ID:** ONC-STANDARDS-UPDATE
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Requiring unexpired or newly updated IGs without adequate transition time could create instability, costly system changes, and clinician disruption.
- **Specific request:** Allow dual-version support, grace periods, and at least a 24-month transition period from the final rule date, with enforcement delayed until real-world interoperability is substantiated.

**Topic 3: Electronic drug prior authorization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support with future strengthening
- **Rationale:** Drug PA is a major source of delay and burden, and electronic PA can improve timeliness and standardization.
- **Specific request:** Maintain 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes for Part B and Part D drug requests, align inpatient incident-to drugs with the authorized service timeline, and consider future rulemaking to reduce all MA drug PA response times to 24 hours.

**Topic 4: Denial reasons and metrics transparency**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; API-USAGE-METRICS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Specific, actionable denial reasons and public metrics help physicians and patients understand payer behavior and identify high denial rates, long decision times, and appeal outcomes.
- **Specific request:** Require clinically meaningful denial reasons identifying unmet criteria, missing information, policy relied upon, policy date/version, and appeal/resubmission steps; make metrics easy to find, machine-readable, consistent, and separated by standard versus expedited requests.

**Topic 5: Endpoint reporting**
- **Provision ID:** API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING
- **Position:** Support with validation
- **Rationale:** A centralized directory of API endpoints, capability statement URLs, authentication, authorization, and registration information can reduce implementation friction.
- **Specific request:** Keep endpoint information current, standardized, and validated; collect both base endpoints and direct capability statement URLs.

**Topic 6: HIPAA FHIR prior authorization standards**
- **Provision ID:** HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS
- **Position:** Support with careful implementation
- **Rationale:** Duplicative X12 and FHIR workflows can create inefficiency, but overlapping or immature standards can also burden providers and hospitals.
- **Specific request:** Pair any final rule with detailed implementation guidance, testing resources, technical assistance, and realistic transition planning to avoid confusing parallel workflows and unfunded costs.

**Topic 7: Open Payments CMPs**
- **Provision ID:** OPEN-PAYMENTS-CMP
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** A failure-to-report definition supports audits and holds manufacturers and GPOs accountable.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the proposed definition allowing CMPs when applicable manufacturers or GPOs fail to grant timely audit access.

**Topic 8: Event notification RFI**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** FHIR-based event notifications could improve routing, filtering, and workflow, but existing HL7v2 ADT infrastructure should not be abruptly replaced.
- **Specific request:** Use a phased approach and improve provider directories, HIE/TEFCA connectivity, ONC certification criteria, and technical assistance.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1:** Supports making specific FHIR IGs enforceable because they can improve consistency and reduce variation, while stressing that workflow usability matters.
- **S2-S3:** Supports unexpired/updated versions only with dual-version support, grace periods, and substantiated real-world interoperability before enforcement.
- **S12:** Supports medical-benefit drug ePA but emphasizes single-workflow integration and real-world clinical workflow testing.
- **S14:** Supports NCPDP SCRIPT, Formulary & Benefit, and RTPB standards for pharmacy benefit drugs.
- **S16-S17:** Strongly supports specific denial reasons and recommends clinically meaningful, actionable denial content, including unmet criteria and policy/version information.
- **S20/S43:** Supports current 72-hour standard and 24-hour expedited timeframes for Part B/Part D drug requests and encourages future MA alignment toward 24-hour responses.
- **S21:** Indicates timelines should be paired with technical assistance, workflow support, and realistic implementation planning, especially for small and rural practices.
- **S23-S24:** Supports endpoint reporting including endpoint URLs, capability statement URLs, authorization/authentication details, and registration information.
- **S26-S27:** Requests standardized, current, and validated endpoint reporting.
- **S29-S35:** Supports public prior authorization metrics, numeric counts plus percentages, aggregated publication where privacy is protected, and machine-readable, consistent reporting.
- **S44-S55:** Supports the move from duplicative X12 requirements to FHIR-based HIPAA PA standards but asks for operational alignment, testing, guidance, technical assistance, transition planning, and attention to unfunded provider costs.
- **S57-S59:** Supports defining Open Payments failure to report for timely audit access and CMP accountability.
- **S60-S65:** Supports updated ONC health IT standards but cautions against mandates without a transition period allowing concurrent versions and technical guidance.
- **S66-S82:** Responds to the event notification RFI by supporting phased FHIR-based approaches while preserving existing HL7v2 ADT infrastructure during transition.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize drug ePA expansion using appropriate FHIR and NCPDP standards.
2. Require single-workflow integration within certified health IT where possible.
3. Provide adequate testing and implementation support for CDex and FAST Security IGs.
4. Allow dual-version support, grace periods, and at least 24 months for ONC IG transitions.
5. Delay enforcement of new IG versions until payers and vendors demonstrate real-world interoperability.
6. Provide technical assistance for small, rural, and resource-constrained practices.
7. Require specific, clinically meaningful, actionable denial reasons.
8. Publish prior authorization metrics that are easy to find, machine-readable, consistent, and separated by standard/expedited status.
9. Maintain and validate a centralized API endpoint directory.
10. Implement HIPAA FHIR PA standards with detailed guidance, testing resources, and realistic transition planning.
11. Finalize Open Payments failure-to-report CMP provisions.
12. Use a phased approach for FHIR-based event notifications without abruptly replacing HL7v2 ADT infrastructure.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Clinician burden, unstable IG version transitions, inconsistent payer implementation, lack of real-world workflow integration, small practice readiness, and unfunded provider costs.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Increased administrative burden, confusing parallel workflows, costly updates, EHR disruption, and delayed patient access to medications.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Vendor readiness, payer variation, third-party dependency, limited IT support in smaller practices, need for authentication/authorization infrastructure, testing resources, and CEHRT updates.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Cardiologists and cardiovascular care teams, physicians, staff, hospitals, small/rural practices, patients, payers, vendors, GPOs, manufacturers, policymakers, purchasers, and researchers.
- **Economic implications:** Upfront implementation, vendor, testing, and transition costs could be significant; standardized ePA and transparent metrics may reduce administrative burden over time.
- **Technical feasibility:** Feasible if CMS requires consistent implementation, testing, validation, transition periods, and technical assistance; risky if treated as endpoint compliance alone.
- **Patient care impact:** Faster, more transparent prior authorization and better workflow integration could reduce treatment delays and improve access to essential medications.
