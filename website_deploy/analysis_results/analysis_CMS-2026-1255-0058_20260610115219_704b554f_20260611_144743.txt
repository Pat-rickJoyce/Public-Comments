# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** National Health Care for the Homeless Council; signed by Barbara DiPietro, Ph.D., Senior Director of Policy
- **Stakeholder type:** Advocacy Group
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Membership organization representing HRSA-funded Health Care for the Homeless health centers and other organizations serving people experiencing homelessness; 300 HCH programs served over 1 million patients in more than 4,500 locations last year.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

NHCHC strongly supports more timely prior authorizations for prescription drugs, emphasizing that current processes create barriers for people experiencing homelessness and for safety-net providers. The organization supports standardization across payers to reduce administrative complexity, improve clarity, and enhance access to care for vulnerable patients.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; GENERAL
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Not addressed
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: More timely drug prior authorizations**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Prior authorization delays create barriers to care for people experiencing homelessness, who already face transportation, employment, safety, and basic-needs barriers.
- **Specific request:** Finalize policies that make prescription drug prior authorizations more timely.

**Topic 2: Standardized payer processes**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Health centers face burdens from differing payer prior authorization processes and forms; standardization would reduce administrative burden and improve clarity.
- **Specific request:** Standardize prior authorization processes across payers for both patients and providers.

**Topic 3: Protecting vulnerable populations**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** Cumbersome insurance rules can alienate patients and make care harder to access even when they have insurance and a regular provider; consequences can be severe for patients with substance use disorders at overdose risk.
- **Specific request:** Implement the rule in a way that improves access for people experiencing homelessness and other vulnerable populations.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S20:** NHCHC supports timely drug prior authorizations and highlights the access consequences of delays for vulnerable patients.
- **S21:** The comment supports implementation of drug prior authorization requirements, but does not provide technical detail on the proposed compliance timeline.
- **S43:** NHCHC's emphasis on timely prior authorization supports alignment of timeframes with patient access needs.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Adopt rules that make prescription drug prior authorizations more timely.
2. Standardize prior authorization processes and forms across payers.
3. Ensure implementation improves clarity for both patients and providers.
4. Consider the disproportionate access barriers faced by people experiencing homelessness.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Prior authorization is a barrier to care and administrative burden for providers serving vulnerable populations.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Alienation from care, difficulty accessing needed treatment, and potentially deadly consequences for people with substance use disorders at risk of overdose.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Differing payer processes and forms.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** NHCHC states that 300 HCH programs served over 1 million patients in more than 4,500 locations last year.
- **Studies or research referenced:** Not addressed.
- **Examples provided:** People experiencing homelessness may lack transportation or paid leave and may prioritize food, shelter, and safety over health care access.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** People experiencing homelessness, HCH health centers, health care providers, payers, patients with substance use disorders.
- **Economic implications:** Reduced administrative burden for health centers; no quantified cost estimate provided.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not addressed directly; the comment supports standardization rather than discussing FHIR or NCPDP implementation details.
- **Patient care impact:** More timely and standardized prior authorization could improve medication access and reduce delays for vulnerable patients.
