# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** McKesson Corporation; signed by Rich Buckley, Senior Vice President, Corporate Affairs.
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor / healthcare services organization / pharmacy and distribution stakeholder.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** McKesson describes an enterprise-wide role spanning community pharmacy, specialty care, oncology, distribution, health information technology, providers, pharmacies, payers, and technology developers.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

McKesson strongly supports extending interoperability and electronic prior authorization requirements to drugs, but urges CMS to reflect pharmacy, PBM, specialty pharmacy, provider, and intermediary workflows rather than an EHR-only model. It recommends preserving the dual FHIR/NCPDP standards framework, eliminating fax-based processes, requiring patient-specific machine-readable coverage criteria at initial eligibility inquiry, aligning payer and PBM requirements, strengthening actionable denial responses, and using phased implementation with clear glide paths.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED; API-ENDPOINT-REPORTING; API-USAGE-METRICS; PA-DENIAL-REASONS; PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; HIPAA-PA-STANDARDS; STATE/Medicaid implementation issues; GENERAL workflow and direct data entry concerns.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** CMS-0062-P. The comment refers to sections II.A, II.B, and II.C of the proposed rule but does not rely heavily on specific CFR citations.
- **Standards referenced:** FHIR APIs; NCPDP SCRIPT; NCPDP pharmacy ePA workflows; Prior Authorization API; patient-specific eligibility/coverage inquiry; structured denial codes; PBM/payer APIs; EHR-connected and intermediary tools.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Preserve dual-standard FHIR/NCPDP framework**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED
- **Position:** Support / clarify.
- **Rationale:** McKesson argues a FHIR-only approach would not support real-world pharmacy PA operations because NCPDP SCRIPT already provides mature request-response workflows for pharmacy benefit PA.
- **Specific request:** Retain NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit workflows and use FHIR where it improves medical benefit and payer API consistency.

**Topic 2: Transition fully away from fax-based PA**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; EXISTING-WORKFLOWS
- **Position:** Modify / strengthen.
- **Rationale:** Fax-based submissions and inconsistent payer processing of electronic submissions drive administrative burden despite available ePA infrastructure.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to accept and process electronic PA requests and establish a clear glide path away from fax, including near-term electronic submission/receipt followed by real-time processing and structured responses.

**Topic 3: Machine-readable coverage criteria and benefit pathway clarity**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL; PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; MEDICAL-VS-PHARMACY-BENEFIT
- **Position:** Modify / clarify.
- **Rationale:** Specialty and oncology therapies may be covered under medical, pharmacy, Medicare Part D, or multiple pathways depending on site of care, reimbursement, and affordability. Ambiguity causes delay and back-and-forth.
- **Specific request:** Require payers and PBMs to provide machine-readable coverage criteria and PA requirements at the point of initial benefit eligibility inquiry; require a 24-hour front-end response for specialty drugs to clarify available coverage pathways and PA requirements.

**Topic 4: PBM/payer alignment and broader care-team participation**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; VENDOR-ECOSYSTEM; STAKEHOLDER-COORDINATION
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** PBM-administered requirements may conflict with payer medical policies; PA work is often performed by office staff, vendors, pharmacies, specialty pharmacies, and care-team members, not only ordering clinicians in EHRs.
- **Specific request:** Require payers to oversee and align PBM criteria with payer medical policies; explicitly preserve participation by pharmacies, specialty pharmacies, delegated vendors, intermediary tools, and non-physician care-team members with appropriate permissions.

**Topic 5: Actionable denial communications**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS
- **Position:** Support with transition details.
- **Rationale:** Current NCPDP SCRIPT transactions may not consistently support all structured denial details; denials are often conveyed through unstructured attachments.
- **Specific request:** Require complete, specific, usable denial information during transition and advance standardized denial codes and response elements over time. Responses should identify missing information, denial basis, clinical/utilization-management criteria, step therapy requirements, and duration parameters.

**Topic 6: Timeframes and completeness rules**
- **Provision ID:** PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS
- **Position:** Clarify / modify.
- **Rationale:** A nominal response is not meaningful if it only initiates repeated back-and-forth or premature denial. Timeframes must reflect initial submission, missing information requests, and final determination.
- **Specific request:** Require prompt acknowledgement, specificity on missing information, and a substantive determination once a request is complete; clarify when the clock starts; use pharmacy workflow lessons to push medical benefit PA toward faster, informative responses.

**Topic 7: Phased implementation and QHP flexibility**
- **Provision ID:** IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE; FF-SHOP-REQUIREMENTS; STATE-EXTENSION-PROCESS
- **Position:** Modify.
- **Rationale:** Operational readiness, Medicaid/PBM/vendor complexity, and marketplace constraints may vary.
- **Specific request:** Adopt clear phased implementation with time-bound expectations, preserve long-term consistent electronic capabilities, and avoid an EHR-centric model.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S1-S3, S18, S32, S37 (FHIR IGs):** Supports FHIR APIs where useful, but warns against overriding mature pharmacy standards and asks for real-world workflow integration and machine-readable policy logic.
- **S12, S15, S21, S28 (medical benefit drugs):** Strong response; supports inclusion of medical benefit drugs and asks for benefit-pathway transparency and front-end coverage determination.
- **S14, S19, S22, S25, S31 (pharmacy benefit drugs):** Strong response; preserve NCPDP SCRIPT and pharmacy ePA infrastructure; avoid FHIR-only design.
- **S16-S17 (denial reasons):** Strong response; require complete, usable, structured denial information and advance standardized denial codes.
- **S20, S43 (timeframes):** Requests clarity on clock-start rules, missing information, and front-end specialty-drug pathway responses within 24 hours.
- **S40-S42 (QHP exceptions):** Supports limited phased flexibility if consistent with long-term electronic PA capability.
- **S44-S56 (HIPAA standards):** Addresses interaction of FHIR and NCPDP standards, urging preservation of dual standards and practical workflows.
- **S66-S82 / direct data entry and workflow RFI:** Addresses need to move away from fragmented fax/payer portals while preserving intermediary and EHR-connected tools that support actual care-team workflows.
- **Other solicitations:** Endpoint publication, Open Payments CMP, and ONC standards are less central than workflow and standards-choice issues.

## 7. POTENTIAL CMS ACTION ITEMS

1. Preserve NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit PA while requiring FHIR where appropriate.
2. Require payers to accept and process electronic PA submissions and phase out fax reliance.
3. Require patient-specific, machine-readable coverage criteria and PA requirements at initial eligibility/benefit inquiry.
4. Clarify coverage pathway response obligations for specialty drugs, including 24-hour front-end pathway responses.
5. Require payer oversight and alignment of PBM PA criteria with payer medical policy.
6. Ensure APIs and authorization workflows can be used by pharmacies, specialty pharmacies, delegated vendors, and care-team staff.
7. Define denial response elements and build toward standardized structured denial codes.
8. Adopt phased implementation with clear milestones.

## 8. NOTABLE QUOTES / EVIDENCE

- McKesson states that a FHIR-only model will not support real-world pharmacy prior authorization operations.
- The comment stresses that implementation should not default to an EHR-centric model because PA work is often performed by office staff, pharmacies, specialty pharmacies, or delegated vendors.
- McKesson recommends machine-readable coverage criteria at initial benefit eligibility inquiry to reduce iterative back-and-forth.

## 9. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

McKesson provides a technically detailed support-with-modifications comment from a pharmacy/technology ecosystem perspective. Its highest-priority asks are dual-standard preservation, machine-readable coverage criteria, PBM alignment, care-team workflow access, phased implementation, and actionable denial responses.
