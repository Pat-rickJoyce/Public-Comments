## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

PWSA | USA strongly supports CMS's efforts to modernize prior authorization but urges targeted protections for individuals with rare diseases, specifically Prader-Willi syndrome, where laboratory testing delays cause unique and severe harms. The submission responds to RFI III.E (laboratory tests) and is anchored in first-person caregiver experience and advocacy across the national PWS community. Six specific recommendations address retroactive authorization, timing-based denials, repeat blood draws, expedited rare-disease pathways, reduced reauthorization burdens, and financial protection for patients.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Support for PA Modernization — With Rare Disease Protections**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Support / Modify
- **Rationale:** PA modernization is needed but must account for the unique challenges of rare disease patients like those with PWS, for whom laboratory testing delays carry specific and severe clinical consequences including delayed diagnosis, missed treatment windows, and behavioral trauma from repeated blood draws.
- **Specific request:** CMS should recognize rare disease complexity and create specific protections within the PA modernization framework.

**Topic 2: Retroactive Authorization for Laboratory Testing**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Timing misalignments between PA approval and specimen collection often have nothing to do with clinical appropriateness; families cannot wait for PA finalization before collecting specimens in time-sensitive clinical situations.
- **Specific request:** Permit coverage when medically necessary specimens have already been collected in good faith before PA finalization.

**Topic 3: Prohibit Timing-Based Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Health plans currently deny medically necessary testing solely because authorization was not finalized before specimen collection, not because the testing was clinically inappropriate.
- **Specific request:** Prevent health plans from denying medically necessary testing solely because authorization was not finalized before specimen collection.

**Topic 4: Prevent Unnecessary Repeat Blood Draws**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** For individuals with PWS (who often have developmental disabilities, ASD, sensory challenges, and behavioral rigidity), a repeated blood draw can trigger significant behavioral distress, escalating anxiety, need for sedation, missed school/therapy, lost caregiver wages, and additional travel burdens.
- **Specific request:** Require plans and laboratories to honor valid specimens whenever possible; prohibit rejection of collected specimens due to administrative timing issues.

**Topic 5: Expedited Review for Rare Diseases**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Standard PA timelines are inadequate for rare genetic disorders where delays compound clinical urgency; PWS patients require monitoring for rapidly changing health conditions.
- **Specific request:** Establish accelerated authorization timelines for laboratory testing associated with rare genetic disorders and specialty care.

**Topic 6: Reduce Reauthorization Requirements**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Lifelong genetic conditions like PWS require ongoing monitoring; repetitive reauthorization for well-established diagnoses serves no clinical purpose and creates substantial administrative burden.
- **Specific request:** Eliminate repetitive prior authorization requirements for ongoing monitoring associated with lifelong genetic conditions such as PWS.

**Topic 7: Financial Protection for Patients**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL (RFI III.E)
- **Position:** Support (new requirement)
- **Rationale:** Patients and families should not bear financial liability for administrative failures outside their control.
- **Specific request:** Ensure patients and families are not held responsible for administrative failures outside their control.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S70 (RFI III.E — Laboratory Testing):** PWSA | USA directly responds to the laboratory testing RFI, urging six specific protections: retroactive authorization for in-good-faith specimen collection, prohibition on timing-based denials, prevention of unnecessary repeat blood draws, expedited review for rare diseases, elimination of repetitive reauthorization for lifelong conditions, and financial protection for patients from administrative failures.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive authorization when medically necessary specimens have been collected in good faith.
2. Prohibit health plans from denying medically necessary testing solely because authorization was not finalized before specimen collection.
3. Require plans and laboratories to honor valid specimens whenever possible; prevent administrative timing issues from forcing repeat specimen collection.
4. Establish accelerated (expedited) authorization timelines for laboratory testing associated with rare genetic disorders and specialty care.
5. Eliminate repetitive prior authorization requirements for ongoing monitoring of lifelong genetic conditions such as PWS.
6. Ensure patients and families are not held financially responsible for administrative failures outside their control.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Timing-based denials and rejection of already-collected specimens impose uniquely severe burdens on PWS patients (and rare disease patients generally) due to behavioral/developmental challenges that make repeated blood draws traumatic. Delays in genetic testing extend diagnostic journeys, delaying early interventions. Repetitive reauthorization for well-established lifelong diagnoses adds burden without clinical benefit.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Postponed treatment initiation or dose adjustments; delayed identification of endocrine abnormalities; missed detection of metabolic complications; inability to satisfy clinical trial enrollment requirements; delays in specialty medication access; behavioral distress and escalating medical anxiety from repeat blood draws; increased sedation use; lost work and school days; transportation burdens.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Multi-specialist care coordination complexity for PWS (endocrinology, genetics, sleep, GI, psychiatry, nutrition, primary care); administrative burden of repeated documentation submissions for well-known diagnoses; inadequate consideration of rare disease complexity in standard PA frameworks.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with rare genetic disorders (particularly PWS) and their caregivers; Medicaid beneficiaries; specialty care providers; laboratories.
- **Economic implications:** Repeat blood draw costs including travel, lost wages, additional sedation; financial liability falling on families from administrative failures; caregiver time burden from multi-specialist coordination plus PA administrative overhead.
- **Technical feasibility:** Not addressed (non-technical submission); recommendations are policy-level, not standards-based.
- **Patient care impact:** Central focus: laboratory testing delays cause direct clinical harm including delayed diagnosis (particularly during critical infancy developmental windows), missed treatment adjustments, and inability to enroll in clinical trials. Behavioral trauma from repeated blood draws has downstream health impacts.
