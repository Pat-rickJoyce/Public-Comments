# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Charla B. Johnson, DNP, RN, NI-BC; FMOL Health.
- **Stakeholder type:** Healthcare Provider; Technology/User Organization.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Doctor of Nursing Practice, registered nurse, nursing informatics board certification. The comment states FMOL Health uses Epic electronic medical record software and depends on vendor development for reporting and interoperability.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

FMOL Health supports extending NCPDP standards to drugs but asks CMS to remove the Prior Authorization Notification requirement for payers because the commenter views PAN as a prescriber-to-pharmacy workflow and transaction. The comment also argues that the October 1, 2027 compliance date is unrealistic and that at least 24 months after the final rule effective date is needed for development, testing, certification, and implementation.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY; IMPLEMENTATION-TIMELINE as a theme; GENERAL.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** No CFR citations are mentioned.
- **Standards referenced:** NCPDP; SCRIPT; Formulary and Benefit (F&B); Real-Time Prescription Benefit (RTPB); Prior Authorization Notification (PAN); Epic EMR.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: NCPDP Standards for Pharmacy Benefit Drugs**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The commenter supports extending NCPDP standards to drugs and recognizes the proposal's use of SCRIPT, F&B, and RTPB capabilities for prior authorization, formulary information, and real-time coverage checks.
- **Specific request:** Maintain use of NCPDP standards for drug-related electronic prior authorization, formulary, and coverage workflows.

**Topic 2: Prior Authorization Notification Requirement**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that PAN is a prescriber-to-pharmacy workflow and transaction, so applying a PAN requirement to payers such as Medicare Advantage organizations and Medicaid managed care plans would create unnecessary implementation burden.
- **Specific request:** Remove the PAN requirement for payers from the proposed pharmacy-benefit drug prior authorization standards.

**Topic 3: Compliance Timeline**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** The commenter states that October 1, 2027 is not realistic given the time required by the industry and software vendors.
- **Specific request:** Provide at least 24 months from the final rule effective date for development, testing, certification, and implementation.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S14:** The comment supports applying NCPDP standards for drugs but asks CMS to refine the applicability of the PAN requirement for non-Part D payer contexts.
- **S19:** The comment raises readiness concerns for affected organizations and vendors, stating that the proposed compliance date does not allow enough implementation time.
- **S22:** The comment identifies unnecessary implementation burden from retaining the PAN requirement for payers.
- **S25:** The comment references RTPB as part of the proposed pharmacy benefit drug API/transaction set but does not object to RTPB itself.
- **S31:** The comment does not directly discuss QHP exceptions, but its burden and timeline concerns may be relevant to non-Part D payer implementation generally.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Continue supporting NCPDP standards for pharmacy benefit drug workflows.
2. Remove the Prior Authorization Notification requirement for payers such as Medicare Advantage organizations and Medicaid managed care plans.
3. Revise the compliance timeline to provide at least 24 months from the effective date of the final rule.
4. Ensure vendor development, testing, certification, and implementation needs are accounted for before enforcement.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Unnecessary implementation burden from the PAN requirement and an unrealistic October 1, 2027 compliance date.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Payers, providers, and vendors may face avoidable workflow and implementation burden if CMS keeps PAN in scope for payer requirements.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Need for software vendor development, testing, certification, and implementation; dependence on Epic EMR development for interoperability and reporting success.
