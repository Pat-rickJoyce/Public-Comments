## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation asks CMS to protect oncology patients from laboratory and biomarker testing delays caused by rigid prior authorization timing rules. The comment recommends retroactive authorization, prohibiting denials based only on authorization timing, avoiding repeat blood draws, requiring expedited oncology testing review, and protecting patients from administrative-delay costs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL, with relevance to PA-TIMEFRAMES-DRUGS by analogy for expedited review and patient access concerns.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** Section III.E of CMS-0062-P is referenced; no CFR sections are cited.
- **Standards referenced:** Not addressed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratory and biomarker testing access**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Biomarker and specialty laboratory results guide cancer treatment selection, treatment changes, disease monitoring, and clinical trial eligibility. Delays can directly affect potentially life-saving care.
- **Specific request:** CMS should protect medically necessary oncology laboratory and biomarker testing from PA delays and denials.

**Topic 2: Authorization timing and specimen validity**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** If plans require authorization before or exactly on the specimen collection date, valid samples may be delayed, rejected, or discarded even when patients, physicians, and laboratories acted in good faith.
- **Specific request:** Require plans to allow retroactive authorization for already-collected specimens and prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.

**Topic 3: Avoid repeat draws and administrative costs**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Repeat blood draws create unnecessary patient burden and can delay urgent cancer care. Administrative timing disputes should not create surprise costs for patients.
- **Specific request:** Prevent repeat blood draws when a valid specimen exists and protect patients from costs caused by administrative delays.

**Topic 4: Expedited oncology testing review**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Oncology diagnostic and biomarker testing is often time-sensitive and needed to select therapy or determine clinical trial eligibility.
- **Specific request:** Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Responds to the general RFI topic on laboratory testing by recommending specific safeguards for oncology-related laboratory and biomarker PA workflows, including retroactive authorization, timing-denial prohibitions, expedited review, and patient cost protection.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Allow retroactive authorization when specimens have already been collected.
2. Prohibit denials based solely on authorization timing.
3. Prevent unnecessary repeat blood draws when a valid specimen is available.
4. Require expedited review for oncology-related diagnostic and biomarker testing.
5. Protect patients from surprise costs when delays are administrative.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Rigid PA timing rules for laboratory and biomarker testing in oncology.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed or discarded specimens, repeat blood draws, delayed urgent cancer care, delayed treatment selection, and impeded clinical trial access.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Health plan rules requiring approval before or exactly on the specimen collection date.

My name is Juan Valle, and I am writing as Chief Medical Officer of the Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation to urgently request that CMS protect patients from critical care delays and denials caused by rigid prior authorization requirements for laboratory testing.
The Cholangiocarcinoma Foundation (CCF) is the leading global nonprofit organization dedicated to curing cholangiocarcinoma (bile duct cancer) and improving the quality of life for patients and caregivers. Through patient support, education, advocacy, and research funding, CCF works to ensure that every patient has access to timely, evidence-based care. Prior to my current role, I was an academic medical oncologist. I continue to participate as a researcher in studies that help advance precision medicine and biomarker-driven treatment approaches for patients with biliary tract cancers. Based on both clinical experience and emerging research, we know that timely access to laboratory and biomarker testing is essential...
