## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Crohn's & Colitis Foundation mass comment campaign — 1,255 individual IBD patients, providers, and caregivers across multiple U.S. states (referenced as Attachment A to the Foundation's formal comment, CMS-2026-1255-0500/dbb96e83)
- **Stakeholder type:** Individual (mass form letter campaign organized by Advocacy Group)
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Submitters self-identify as IBD patients, caregivers, and healthcare providers; several include personal disease histories (e.g., 30-year Crohn's patient, parent of child diagnosed at age 10).

---

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This file contains 1,255 substantially identical form letters submitted via the Crohn's & Colitis Foundation's advocacy campaign, urging CMS to finalize the proposed PA rule and strengthen step therapy protections for IBD patients. All letters express support for streamlining and standardizing ePA and for implementing stronger exemptions from insurance-mandated step therapy protocols.

---

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY, GENERAL (step therapy RFI)
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** None
- **Standards referenced:** None

---

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Support for ePA Rule Finalization**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DRUG-MEDICAL, PA-DRUG-PHARMACY
- **Position:** Support
- **Rationale:** The proposed rule streamlines and standardizes PA for IBD medications; the current process causes dangerous delays for a chronic, degenerative disease.
- **Specific request:** Finalize the rule on prior authorization for prescription medications.

**Topic 2: Step Therapy Exemptions**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify (strengthen)
- **Rationale:** Insurance-mandated step therapy overrides physician treatment decisions; more than 80% of Medicare Advantage PA denials are overturned on appeal; medication delays cause more than two-thirds of IBD patients to experience negative health outcomes; nearly 50% cannot participate normally in work, school, or daily activities.
- **Specific request:** Implement and strengthen common-sense exemptions from insurance-mandated step therapy protocols.

---

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66 (step therapy RFI):** All 1,255 commenters urge stronger federal exemptions from insurance-mandated step therapy; cite IBD-specific harms including disease progression and loss of function.

---

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Finalize the proposed rule on prior authorization for prescription medications.
2. Implement and strengthen common-sense exemptions from insurance-mandated step therapy protocols.

---

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Insurance companies overriding physician treatment decisions through PA and step therapy; delays causing irreversible harm to IBD patients.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** More than 80% of Medicare Advantage PA denials overturned on appeal (implying wrongful denials); medication delays cause negative health outcomes in over two-thirds of IBD patients; nearly 50% of delayed patients cannot participate in work, school, or daily activities.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Not addressed (form letter focus is on policy, not implementation).

---

- **Data or statistics cited:** >80% of Medicare Advantage PA denials overturned on appeal; medication delays cause >2/3 of IBD patients to experience negative health outcomes; ~50% of affected patients cannot participate normally in daily activities.

- **Examples provided:** Several individual submitters add personal context: one is a 30-year Crohn's patient; one is a parent whose daughter was diagnosed with Crohn's at age 10.
