# Comment Analysis Template: CMS-2026-1255-0001

## 1. COMMENTER INFORMATION

- **Author/Organization name:** Castle Biosciences, Inc.; signed by Derek Maetzold, Founder, President and CEO.
- **Stakeholder type:** Technology Vendor / Laboratory diagnostics company.
- **Credentials or expertise mentioned:** Castle describes itself as a molecular diagnostics company providing genomic information to guide treatment decisions for patients with cancers and other high-need diseases. It discusses operational experience with tissue specimens, LCD-covered tests, Medicare Advantage prior authorization, out-of-network laboratory issues, and laboratory benefit managers.

## 2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Castle Biosciences responds to CMS's RFI on prior authorization for laboratory tests and DMEPOS, focusing on laboratory testing workflows rather than the drug PA provisions. It argues that PA is structurally mismatched to advanced diagnostics because testing must often occur before authorization can be obtained, and asks CMS to eliminate PA for NCD/LCD-covered tests or, at minimum, require TRF acceptance, laboratory-initiated PA and appeals, DOS-denial prohibitions, actionable denial rationales, and stronger oversight of MA plans and LBMs.

## 3. REGULATORY CONTEXT

- **Specific provisions addressed:** GENERAL; PA-DENIAL-REASONS by analogy; API-USAGE-METRICS by implication for denial-pattern tracking and oversight; FHIR-IG-REQUIRED only indirectly through automation/electronic workflow discussion.
- **Regulatory citations mentioned:** 42 CFR 410.32; 42 CFR 422.101(c)(1); Medicare NCDs and LCDs; Medicare Part B date-of-service policy; CMS-0062-P RFI Section E.
- **Standards referenced:** Test Requisition Forms; CLIA requirements; NCD/LCD coverage criteria; electronic prior authorization and automated workflows generally. No specific FHIR IG or NCPDP standard is discussed.

## 4. DETAILED POSITIONS

**Topic 1: Laboratory PA Burden and Patient Care Impact**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Advanced diagnostic testing often must proceed when specimens are received because delays can harm patients and compromise specimen integrity.
- **Specific request:** CMS should treat laboratory PA as a distinct workflow where retrospective authorization, denials, appeals, and reimbursement risk fall heavily on laboratories.

**Topic 2: Acceptance of Test Requisition Forms**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** TRFs include clinician attestation, diagnosis codes, and information aligned to coverage criteria, yet MA plans often demand duplicative records.
- **Specific request:** Require MA plans and other payers to accept TRFs as sufficient, stand-alone medical necessity documentation when they contain required information.

**Topic 3: Date-of-Service Denials**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Oppose
- **Rationale:** Denying PA because the specimen was collected before authorization uses a billing construct rather than a coverage standard and is operationally impossible for labs to avoid.
- **Specific request:** Prohibit DOS-based denials and mismatches between authorization date ranges and specimen collection dates when medical necessity is established before claim adjudication.

**Topic 4: Laboratory Benefit Manager Accountability**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** LBMs can create accountability gaps, especially where plans say LBMs act on their behalf and LBMs disclaim responsibility for coverage decisions.
- **Specific request:** CMS should establish accountability for MA plans and contractors, including LBMs, and examine whether LBM out-of-network requirements improperly restrict beneficiary access.

**Topic 5: Eliminate or Limit PA for NCD/LCD-Covered Tests**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify/Oppose
- **Rationale:** NCDs and LCDs already establish medical necessity criteria, so PA duplicates review and creates opportunities for inappropriate denial.
- **Specific request:** Eliminate PA for tests covered by NCDs or LCDs, or limit PA to confirming existing coverage criteria without adding inconsistent requirements.

**Topic 6: Laboratory-Initiated PA and Appeals**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Clarify/Modify
- **Rationale:** Laboratories submit claims, are positioned to respond to documentation requests, and bear financial risk, yet some MA plans require ordering physicians or PCPs to initiate PA.
- **Specific request:** Enforce laboratory ability to initiate PA requests and clarify that laboratories may pursue appeals directly.

**Topic 7: Specific Denial Rationale and Appeal Rights**
- **Provision ID:** PA-DENIAL-REASONS; GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Generic denial notices and procedural denials can prevent labs from identifying missing information, proving medical necessity, or obtaining meaningful review.
- **Specific request:** Require denial rationales tied to applicable NCD/LCD criteria and guarantee all PA and claim denials are appealable on medical necessity grounds with defined appeal timeframes.

**Topic 8: Automation With Policy Guardrails**
- **Provision ID:** GENERAL
- **Position:** Modify
- **Rationale:** Automation can speed decisions, but if it automates improper DOS rules, TRF rejection, or misaligned criteria, it worsens the problem.
- **Specific request:** Pair automation with standardized documentation, Medicare coverage policy alignment, accessible tools for out-of-network labs, denial-pattern reporting, and enforcement.

## 5. SOLICITATION RESPONSES

- **S66-S82:** Castle directly responds to the general RFI on laboratory tests and DMEPOS, answering each listed RFI question about patient care, TRFs, DOS denials, LBMs, process improvements, automation, unresolved issues, and workforce effects.
- **S16/S17:** Castle's request for specific actionable denial rationale parallels the drug denial-reason solicitations; it wants denial notices to identify missing documentation and unmet NCD/LCD criteria.
- **S29/S34/S35:** Castle does not propose a specific public metrics regime, but it supports denial-pattern tracking and oversight reporting to identify systemic misuse by MA plans and contractors.

## 6. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Eliminate prior authorization for laboratory tests already covered under NCDs and LCDs.
2. Require acceptance of TRFs as sufficient, stand-alone medical-necessity documentation when complete.
3. Permit laboratories to initiate PA requests directly.
4. Permit laboratories to initiate and pursue appeals directly.
5. Prohibit DOS-based denials, including denials based on specimen collection before authorization.
6. Prevent authorization-date-range and claim-DOS mismatches from defeating otherwise valid authorizations.
7. Guarantee all PA and claim denials remain appealable on medical necessity grounds.
8. Require appeal processes accessible to laboratories and issued within defined timeframes.
9. Require specific actionable denial rationales tied to NCD/LCD criteria.
10. Standardize documentation requirements and align PA with Medicare coverage policies.
11. Ensure automated PA systems are available to out-of-network laboratories and do not automate improper criteria.
12. Establish robust oversight and enforcement for MA plans and contractors, including LBMs.

## 7. CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS

- **Primary concerns:** Retrospective lab PA burden; TRF rejection; DOS misuse; LBM accountability gaps; out-of-network access barriers; procedural denials; generic denial notices; lack of lab appeal rights.
- **Potential negative impacts cited:** Delayed or denied reimbursement, financial losses, reduced access to innovative diagnostics, patient confusion and distress, potential compromise of specimen integrity, and diversion of resources from diagnostic innovation.
- **Implementation barriers mentioned:** Laboratory tests are often performed immediately after specimen receipt; labs may be out of network; payer/LBM portals may exclude labs; ordering physicians may be burdened by requests that labs are better positioned to handle.

## 8. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE

- **Data or statistics cited:** Castle states that authorization date range and specimen collection date mismatches result in denial of otherwise valid authorizations approximately 35% of the time for its orders requiring PA.
- **Studies or research referenced:** No external studies are cited in the extracted text; the comment relies on Castle's operational experience.
- **Examples provided:** Tissue specimens from biopsy or surgical excision; TRFs signed by treating clinicians; MA plans requiring duplicate medical records; LBM circular accountability; OON portal exclusion; DOS-based denials.

## 9. IMPACT ASSESSMENT

- **Affected stakeholders:** Patients with cancers and complex conditions, ordering clinicians, laboratories, Medicare Advantage plans, LBMs, out-of-network laboratories, CMS oversight staff, and diagnostic innovators.
- **Economic implications:** Denials and appeals impose laboratory administrative costs and financial losses and may divert resources away from new test development and access expansion.
- **Technical feasibility:** Automation can reduce manual burden only if CMS first corrects policy and enforcement failures and requires accessible standardized workflows.
- **Patient care impact:** Castle says delays or nonpayment can threaten timely access to medically necessary diagnostic testing and ultimately treatment decision-making.

ORIGINAL COMMENT EXCERPT
------------------------
June 15, 2026

Castle Biosciences, Inc. is a molecular diagnostics company focused on providing clinicians with accurate, objective genomic information to guide treatment decisions for patients with cancers and other diseases with significant unmet clinical need. Our tests are performed on tissue specimens submitted by ordering clinicians following biopsy or surgical excision, typically during or immediately after a dermatologic or oncologic procedure. Because our specimens are processed contemporaneously with the clinical encounter, and because delays in testing can compromise specimen integrity and patient care, testing must proceed upon receipt of the specimen, well before prior authorization can realistically be obtained.
